# What Snapback 5 does not do

Both Snapback 5 bets are Shelved; there is no live implementation scope.
Moving an item onto the doing-list needs a new explicit decision recorded
in the governing LLP and the journal, with the scope it replaces.

- A SQL front end over the Quarry engine: [LLP 4001](../llp/4001-snapback5-sql-front-end.rfc.md)
  was shelved on **2026-09-25** after both pre-test 1 families exceeded the
  registered refusal thresholds and found no preserving spelling for Muse's
  counters, message windows and feed. The [record](./reports/20260925-pretest1/README.md)
  stops at step 4; pre-test 2 and compiler work are not run.

The remaining list records the first bet's historical fence from
[LLP 4000 §3.2 and Non-claims](../llp/4000-snapback5.rfc.md):

- Authored `sync`, `sync to`, `sync public`, `sync last N by` or horizons.
  Declared queries, the warm set and one depth step determine residency;
  read rules still determine audiences.
- `cap N by` as a residency bound, `first 10001`, `size` or `cap 60000`
  as engine-work declarations. `at most N by` remains a product constraint.
- Authored operation eligibility markers `server` and `device`. Generated
  `runs` and `predicts` facts describe execution.
- The residency notes `N_NO_HORIZON`, `N_HORIZON_GUARD`, `N_NOW_SERVER`,
  `N_MAINTAIN_SYNCED` and `N_NOT_PREDICTABLE` in default `check` output.
- Default cost/scale notes `N_WIDE_MERGE`, `N_SEEK`, `N_SPARSE_FILTER`,
  `N_TIMELINE_PROFILE_CAP`, `N_MAINTAINED_COLUMN_STREAM` and
  `N_TIMELINE_COMPAT`. Their evidence belongs in `check --scale`.
- An app author's `createClient` versus `createApp` residency choice.
  Apps use `createApp`; headless and third-party clients keep `createClient`.
- A replacement engine, server-held page results, a second delivery
  channel, changed `complete` semantics or a different predictor.
- Engine-derived delivery receipts in this slice. The receipt table
  remains in both arms. `through` waits for the accepted shared-baseline
  contract; it is not a capability of this scaffold.

The leave policy, `online only`, product bounds, completeness classes and
disclosure notes stay. Retained history is frozen at removal; it receives
no later updates or tombstones. Index names and maintain strategies were
already removed from the paved path in Snapback 4 and are not new wins here.

There is no claim of sufficiency for every application: the proposed surface
covers the calibration brief and Workroom shape; additions enter the count.
There is no claim of delivering less data, changing the grader, brief or
protocol, or achieving a score. The hypothesis is less authoring; its
decision depends on time against the control, correctness, delivery,
non-inferiority floors and reading (A). The RFC's one registered
variance-transfer exception remains explicit. Nothing runtime-related is
implemented by this fence.

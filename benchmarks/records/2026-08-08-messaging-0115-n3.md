## Reading it

1. **The token ratio is the robust number: ~2.2–2.4x across all nine
   Snapback lanes**, tightly banded per configuration, consistent with
   run 5's N=1 (2.24x). The wall ratio is the noisy number (skill-lane
   range 2.42–4.57x): per-lane draws — new-find detours and fleet load —
   dominate wall, exactly as diary 0066 predicted ("tokens are the
   better cross-run metric").
2. **Run 5's 1.84x wall was a good draw, not the distribution center.**
   The N≥3 median says the honest standing wall number is ~2.7–2.9x on
   a fleet-loaded machine. Note the Convex control also ran fast and
   stable (10.7–12.8m) under identical load — load does not explain the
   asymmetry; per-lane discovery detours do.
3. **The zero-defect result replicated 9/9.** No Snapback lane in any
   repeat hit a defect-shaped framework loop: 27 codegen/typecheck
   passes were first-try across all nine lanes, and every refusal named
   its rule. The friction that remains is knowledge-shaped (client-API
   envelope shapes, offline choreography discoverability) and
   scaffold-shaped (web deps — fixed on main post-0.1.15, recurring
   until the next release ships).
4. **The control's profile is stable and its costs structural:** the
   emoji-field-name refusal hit 3/3 Convex lanes at runtime; offline
   was hand-rolled and disclaimed 3/3 ("genuinely DIY"); presence was
   rebuilt from durable primitives or omitted; CLI churn (deprecated
   `--local`, hidden port flags excavated from the shipped bundle)
   appeared in 2/3.
5. **Native is the most consistent Snapback carrier** (wall range
   1832–2237s, ratio 2.84–2.90x) — all proofs ran on the simulator in
   all three repeats, zero web fallbacks, zero data-plane misbehavior;
   its cost center is Apple tooling, replicated identically (iOS 26.3
   emoji-less runtime hit twice this series after run 4; scheme
   approval; permission prompts; no first-party headless tap).

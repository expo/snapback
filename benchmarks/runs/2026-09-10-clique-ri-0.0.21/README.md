two builders, no change dropped the database, forced a reset, or restarted the owner. The builders'
friction was elsewhere: the global-search closure budget refusing a page of 20 (CR1), the drain

binder-type diagnostics during M7.

**What the grok wave says about the platform** (the scorer's notes and the diary): Quarry as
product vocabulary helped most (unique handles, follow caps, merge feeds, maintained counts,
`once` columns, insert-if-absent intents, schedule-on-column expiry); M7's additive successor
was "the brochure experience the rest of the stack did not match". What hurt: the builder treated
live `useQuery` and one-shot `createClient().query` as two backends for view-once photos,
insight totals, `groups.get`, and poll tallies (M5, M8, M7 — the same class as astra's
maintained-aggregate disagreement); the web IndexedDB range store stalling mutations past
15 s forced a token-only auxiliary client for online writes and the offline flush; Expo Router
was off the paved `App.tsx` path; one-owner-per-directory and no `live:false` recipe added
operational ceremony. The grok build's authored purge/tombstone pattern (`purgeMessage` setting
`tombstone: true` on expiry) is what conflated expiry with delete-for-everyone in the grader's
d-06 — the pattern LLP 1019 D6 removes from the template.

**Score movement, 0.0.4 → 0.0.21, both families:** astra 64 → 82, grok 57 → 72. The categories
that moved least for grok are the ones the astra wave also scored lowest: client integration
(50), offline (53), queries/live (62), diagnostics (66).

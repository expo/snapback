## M8 — insights, scheduled digest, deeper offline

Built six server-maintained totals, seven UTC-day buckets including zeros, rolling 24-hour top-ten ranking, and the scheduled digest with durable per-user progress and one effect per period. The aggregate recipes were unusually direct: recorded author paths made likes/reposts/replies credit the right person without client scans. A fresh isolated backend let the ranking suite create real old posts by advancing its own dev clock rather than backdating the main database. The real-clock digest proof closed every client, restarted the backend mid-period, verified authorization and tombstone exclusion, then verified silence in the next inactive period.

The UI lifecycle still fought me more than the schema. The frozen three-client run caught a transient empty history during receipt-driven answer replacement. A bounded one-second display buffer for delivered-history metadata fixed that; authoritative answers and denials replace it, expiry uses the local server clock, media stays behind revocable asset sources, and acknowledgements use only live rows. The final choreography passed dual-offline ordering and read receipts. An additional browser-process restart preserved pending sends and ordinary viewed images, rejected view-once bytes offline, then flushed in order. Offline likes needed kept public post reads; same-account edits and reactions then passed too.

Familiarity with SQL aggregation and idempotent outboxes helped; the exact Quarry forms and compiler certificates still required published-guide lookups. Compared with a general TypeScript backend, the scheduled sweep and transactional effect deduplication removed orchestration code. Compared with Postgres tooling, the restricted aggregate paths and read lifecycle require more platform-specific thinking. I cannot turn that impression into a comparative latency claim. Scale replay is next, on its separately recorded clock and fresh instance; these green milestones do not pre-claim its outcome.

Day-2 on Snapback 4 is not “write a migration file.” It is: leave the seeded store running, change Quarry, and let the process re-adopt. The compiler names the change Additive, lists every new column and table, and rebuilds maintained aggregates. That is the whole ceremony. Compared with a SQL shop, you lose the comfort of a reviewed `ALTER` plus a backfill job you can dry-run; you gain a classification the binary will refuse to apply if you accidentally drop a column. The backfilled field the brief demanded was not a new integer I had to fill by paging chats. It was `maintain conversationMessageCounts: count messages by conversationId, deleted`. After adopt, Alice’s DM was 6 and Weekend Plans was 9, matching the starter invariant, with no script that knew those numbers.

Polls could have been a side table that never touched messages. Making them a message type with `pollQuestion` on the existing row, plus `pollOptions` / `pollVotes` and an intents-guarded `setPollVote`, is what made a poll increment the same count as a text send. The vote path copies the reaction idiom the continuous session already paid for: insert the client mutation id into `intents` if absent; a retry returns `replayed` and does not move the row. Changing your mind is a *new* mutation id. That is the global set-state ruling in code, not a comment.

The part that still felt like a product app rather than a schema trick was the offline vote. The published online client already queues `/m` while the link is down. The UI had to keep the option mounted and mark it pending without a 300 ms refresh tearing the node down. Once that was honest, the choreography’s Bob-offline-then-reconnect path reconciled exactly once. I did not switch web onto the wasm replica to make that true; I used the queue the online tier already documents. That is a narrower offline story than M8’s durable-across-restart bar, and it is the one this session actually proved.

What I would tell a team picking this backend for evolution work: additive is cheap and real. Counts that the engine can maintain are cheaper than counters you update in every mutation, and they stay correct when expiry sweeps a row. Anything that needs a filtered count (visible = not tombstoned) belongs in the maintain’s group key, not in a nightly job, because jobs may not scan expiring tables. Do not assume the web client is local-first just because native is. Prove pending in the DOM. Pin the Node the setup-identity file claims. And export the snapshot *before* you save `schema.q`.

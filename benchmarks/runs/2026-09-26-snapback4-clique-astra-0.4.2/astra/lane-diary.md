# M8 — final scale follow-up

The stronger scale browser check found a real weakness after the initial green boundary: a direct unread count scanned too much history. Maintained 128-ordinal block counts plus a boundary scan fixed the online query; an independent 400-message proof checks partial reads, own messages, deletion, and clearing the final unread range. A cold reload also showed that expiry-aware aggregate coverage must not control whether the conversation list exists. The client now reads conversation identity separately, preserving all 100 conversations offline while omitting unavailable unread totals. Background delivery acknowledgements wait until the initial snapshot is acquired. The published `why inbox` diagnostic explained the source-horizon dependency clearly. What fought me was treating badge completeness as a prerequisite for navigation, and an Expo cache that retained the wrong backend URL. No earlier Snapback implementation was consulted. Compared with separate PostgreSQL/storage/offline layers, the integrated diagnostics shortened the investigation, but an integrated model still required deliberate aggregate and UI lifetime design. The replay reset and large-store adoption contention remain explicit evidence, rather than being erased by the successful recovery.

The final M6 rerun exposed one more client-state mistake: a visible predicted message is not a settled send. I had retired its pending intent when the row appeared. The UI now keeps the pending intent until the SDK reports terminal settlement and hides the predicted duplicate. M6 and M8 choreography passed after that change, and the cold-reload two-write proof passed again. This reinforced the same lesson as view-once rendering: the backend's guarantees only reach the user when the client assigns state and lifetime precisely.

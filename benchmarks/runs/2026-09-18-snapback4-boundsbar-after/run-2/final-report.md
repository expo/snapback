Search uses a one-cursor membership query with product page size 20. Its nested search returns the whole chat, newest first. The chosen message cap is 512 per room, and its collection bound is 512: larger rooms are refused at insertion rather than silently truncated. This is a real product limitation. The returned outer shape owns continuation, so a meter-starved nested shape can be retried through the native continuation. A whole room still has to fit within one read's heap and examined limits. Broad prefixes can encounter the engine's 32-word limit. No acquisition horizon or device holdings are involved.

Unread uses the engine-owned messageCounts maintain and sums each room's count minus the member's readOrdinal, clamped at zero. The import mutation enforces consecutive ordinals starting at one; messages cannot be deleted or updated through any declared operation. This makes the count subtraction exact for the supported model. There is no per-principal counter table. Very large membership populations can exhaust the read meter and return incomplete without a continuation; the fixed 1,000-membership hub fits.

Trending stores negative likes and negative post time on the original post row. Like membership and score updates commit together, and uniqueness makes the set-state mutation idempotent. A 25-hour-lane merge orders by negative likes, negative time, then logical key ascending, with the exact open lower and closed upper time boundaries. Zero-like posts participate directly. Whole old hours are skipped; a crowded boundary hour can exhaust the meter while rejecting older or future rows. Historical posts and likes are retained. Ranking cursors are live, not snapshots, so concurrent score changes require restarting the first page.

The maintain's read audience includes members and the seed principal; its delta note is an explicit disclosure. Posts have public reads and permissive update authority so viewers' like mutations can update their scores. The declared operations accept no caller-supplied score, but this is hand-maintained application state: a future mutation with arbitrary score inputs would be unsafe. posts.negAt is immutable, while posts.negLikes is changed only by the idempotent helper. The machine-readable log declares both columns and the engine-maintained room count.

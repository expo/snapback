## Verdicts

- **astra:** Snapback 4 strongly supported Clique's authorization and durable messaging, while prediction inconsistencies, feed complexity, and qualified scale results prevented a seamless experience. Would use again for: I would use it again for membership-based messaging applications where durable offline behavior, shared authorization, and protected media justify deliberate read-model design.
- **grok:** Snapback 4 made Clique's backend a single compiled program, then left the lane on Expo/replica/Write gaps the published 0.2.6 client does not yet absorb. Would use again for: A local-first social messenger whose rules, expire, jobs, and membership sync can live in one schema, if live Expo choreography and a 1000-user signup replay are not the gating proofs.
- **muse:** A proof-carrying backend that disposes of visibility bugs at compile time and bills only in quarry expressiveness and scale-runner rate limits. Would use again for: Any social product whose hardest bugs are who-can-see-what, with headless replay-first development and schema-level jobs and effects.

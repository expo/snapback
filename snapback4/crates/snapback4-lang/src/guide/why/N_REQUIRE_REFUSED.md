# N_REQUIRE_REFUSED

A mutation's `require` does not admit a write the table's rule refuses: the delete still runs the table's delete rule as the caller. `require t.captain = viewer or exists admins[viewer] or person = viewer else NOT_CAPTAIN` before `delete memberships[team, person]` admits the member leaving, but a delete rule `exists teams[.team, captain = viewer] or exists admins[viewer]` refuses them E_RULE, never your NOT_CAPTAIN. `check` notes N_REQUIRE_REFUSED when a branch of a top-level `require` identifies the caller by a column of the deleted row (`person = viewer` where the key pins `.member` to `person`, or `m.member = viewer` on a lookup of that row) and no delete-rule disjunct's caller tests follow from that branch and the other `require`s. If that caller may delete the row, add them to the delete rule (`delete <- … or .member = viewer`); otherwise drop that branch from the require so they get your code. A delete rule naming the caller by another column of the row is not noted (the app may keep the two equal).

For an insert, update, upsert update arm or delete with one keyed table-role probe, the note also identifies a mixed caller gate: one OR branch establishes that probe while another does not. This means table authority is not established for every admitted branch and a write may still refuse. It does not prove roles disjoint: a guide may also be staff. A separate earlier require establishing the table role suppresses this note; unknown expressions and invalidated facts do not prove a contradiction.

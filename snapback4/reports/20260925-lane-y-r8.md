## Findings and implementation

**Deferred journal recovery advances an idle client.** Live clock notification
now belongs to the shared web outbox commit boundary. It collects the maximum
valid admission timestamp from the existing change batch, persists the batch,
preserves the existing committed-answer invalidation ordering, and then notifies
the clock before another serialized device call can execute. Ordinary admission,
deferred journal recovery, orphan transfer and receipt/replay upserts all pass
through this boundary. Failed saves do not notify. The orphan-only notification
and its separate maximum calculation have been removed.

This is the engineering choice for the every-path rule: use the bounded write
batch already being persisted, rather than adding a queue scan or another device
round trip for each recovery path. `raiseIssued` still leaves estimated server
time unchanged. The existing local-client listener and returned-admission floor
adoption remain in place; their registration order and synchronous journal
capture are unchanged.

**Restore preserves the order lost at a held save.** Both opening and lazy
restoration now adopt available orphan outboxes and commit their floor before
recovering the current store's journal. A destination save failure propagates
out of restore, discards the uncommitted VM, and leaves the captured journal
unadmitted. Backend adoption and store observation use the same orphan-before-
journal order when previously deferred recovery becomes possible. Background
restoration no longer repeats the orphan scan already completed by restore.
Existing identity checks, ownership claims, unresolved-orphan handling and
bounded retries remain intact.

**Returned admission time is protected by a distinguishing test.** A host may
omit the optional outbox notification. The new test uses a real web device with
that notification disabled and an execution floor above the client's clock. It
requires both the admitted timestamp and the client's resulting time to reach
the floor. Removing the existing `clock.raiseIssued(entry.now ?? 0)` now fails.

## Tests and measurements

Focused checks: **48 clock/live/recovery/regression tests** and **76 existing
navigation/durability tests**, all passing.

The new tests prove:

1. An entry at `1004000`, held in the synchronous journal of a store marked
   `backend-required`, is admitted after adoption. An idle client's clock reaches
   `1004000` with wall time fixed at `1000000`, without another mutation or reopen.
2. With host notifications disabled, a mutation's returned execution timestamp
   raises the client from `1000000` to its durable floor of `1004000`.
3. During a held destination orphan save, a waiting entry is synchronously
   journaled at `1000000`. The test snapshots only durable stores and the journal
   at that exact checkpoint, excluding the old VM, uncommitted save and ownership
   claims. Opening a fresh Wasm device from that crash image must first adopt
   orphan times `1002000` and `1004000`, then admit the waiting entry at `1004000`.
4. Rejecting that save also blocks an already-waiting admission. Repeated lazy
   restore failure leaves the captured journal byte-for-byte intact and absent
   from the destination outbox. Once saving works, recovery admits in the correct
   order and updates the same live clock. No uncommitted floor is notified.

Four deliberate mutations were rejected and restored:

- Restore journals before orphans: reload admits in the wrong order; rejected
  restoration prematurely persists the waiting entry.
- Suppress the shared clock notification: deferred replay leaves the idle clock
  behind and emits no committed timestamp.
- Ignore the returned admission timestamp: the entry uses `1004000`, but the
  caller's clock remains `1000000`.
- Notify before saving: both held-save tests issue `1004000` before commit.

Initial development-only failures were lane-owned fixture errors: the two held-
save tests initially used observation alone to initialize a fresh store, leaving
its identity private and therefore correctly preventing journal capture. The
fixture now publishes its initial snapshot before the fault checkpoint. No
production identity check was relaxed.

1. [P2] Bound capture size and accumulation.
Source: snapback4/packages/snapback4/test/helpers/maintenance-evidence.mjs:14-37, 56-71; snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/verbs/witness/retention.rs:13-15.
The helper recursively inventories every directory and copies every matching JSON/JSONL/log/lock/tmp file in full. There is no byte, entry, PID, FD, overall elapsed-time or retained-snapshot count limit. The ps/lsof command limits do not bound this filesystem work. Traces also append indefinitely outside the 5/20 retained-run policy; default snapshots have no cleanup policy. Successful fixture deletion does not bound accumulated evidence across runs. The scratch probe copied all 16,777,216 bytes of a synthetic maintenance.log. A growing failure log can therefore make capture itself consume substantial disk or synchronous test-loop time just before the enclosing guard. Add bounded log tails and inventory/PID/FD budgets with explicit truncation metadata, cap trace growth and define evidence retention/cleanup. Preserve a useful latest failure snapshot rather than silently dropping it. Evidence: helper-probe.json and helper-probe.mjs.

2. [P2] Keep stale identities from capturing unrelated process data.
Source: snapback4/packages/snapback4/test/helpers/maintenance-evidence.mjs:30-37, 43-61, 67-71.
The collector discards recorded birth identities and collects every integer pid/group from every JSON file, plus old bare maintainer PIDs. It then persists full command lines and descriptor information for the current occupants and their descendants/groups. PID reuse can therefore retain an unrelated process's arguments, including credentials. A synthetic stale maintenance-next.json pointing at our own unrelated test process captured its full synthetic token argument and FDs despite an explicitly mismatched identity. No real credential was used or discovered by this probe. Birth-check protocol owners or retain a mismatch marker without their sensitive details; limit process fields/arguments and files to what is needed, redact sensitive arguments, and write private evidence permissions (the current umask produced 0775 directories / 0664 files). This is diagnostic selection only; it need not change FL's product/test liveness semantics. Evidence: helper-probe.json and helper-probe.mjs.

3. [P2] A blocked trace sink can hold custody beyond the deadline.
Source: snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/verbs/witness/retention.rs:10-18, 200, 217, 225, 381-383.
Trace uses blocking open/write on an unchecked pathname, including while leases are held and immediately before native cleanup. Ignoring returned errors does not make a blocked open/write best effort. In an isolated synthetic fixture with retention-trace.jsonl as a FIFO without a reader, an observer with a real inherited next lease and a 1,500 ms expiry stayed blocked in wait_for_partner after four seconds, still holding that lease. With tracing disabled, the same observer expired in 1.518 seconds and released it. A direct export-cleanup probe also blocked before its work budget started. We killed only the processes launched by these probes. Reject symlinks/nonregular sinks through an appropriately nonblocking open and validation, bound emission, and ensure diagnostic emission cannot hold up deadline/cleanup processing. Add a refusing/stalled-sink control. Scope: debug binaries with tracing enabled; no release-runtime write or historical-cause claim. Evidence: trace-observer-probe.json and trace-sink-probe.json.

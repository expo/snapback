## Implemented surface

Quarry declares static POST routes and generates typed flat `Routes` and
`server()` handlers. Session authentication is the default. A provider route
declares a service seat and separate `verify`/`handle` functions. Verification
receives raw body bytes, declared secrets and a cancellation signal, not a
delegated database context. Every delivery verifies, including a retry that
would collect an already settled response. Invalid requests cannot occupy a
valid body's durable deduplication identity or collect its response.

Verification tickets are bounded, ephemeral, randomly fenced and checked
against current worker/declaration/service generation. Only successful
verification promotes the original envelope to the existing A2 durable work
record. The core strips reusable credential headers before storage/dispatch.
IDs bind route and body digest; session routes additionally bind principal
and admitting family. Changed declarations and revoked service generations
refuse old same-body work rather than minting replacement authority.

Handlers use A2's rule-checked context, frozen authority, stable write IDs and
durable steps. Responses are bounded buffered completions, not streams.
Plain application `GiveUp` records a refusal and returns bare 204; typed
infrastructure/uncertain-step failures do not masquerade as acknowledgements.
Explicit 5xx responses retry; malformed/oversized output fails terminally
after possible external effects. A pending 503 asks the provider to retry;
a disconnected client does not undo admitted work.

The [server reference](../packages/snapback4/SERVER.md) and `guide routes`
state bounds and exclusions: 64 KiB bodies, 64 headers/16 KiB, static exact
POST paths, no captures/query variants/stream forwarding, and bounded
verification/response waits. Development stays loopback by default; explicit
wildcard binding plus cleartext acknowledgement permits only declared route
ingress. Core doors and development personas retain their loopback gate.

Generated handlers share one action-registry poll loop grouped by session
lifetime, and a separate verifier loop. Both rotate 128-name chunks with at
most one-second waits when there are multiple chunks. Four registry slots
and four verifier slots are independent of 16 ingress waits, 32 legacy long
polls and 32 short requests. Legacy `runWorker` retains per-name polling by
default. The name-set selector shares the existing claim/authority path.

processes, HTTP sockets and SQLite stores. They cover exact non-UTF8 HMAC
bytes; wrong/unsigned duplicates before and after success with immediate
unchanged data/work/audit snapshots; independent Set-Cookie headers; verifier
exceptions/non-booleans; pending 503 then fresh verification and collection;
5xx retry without duplicate mutation; real SIGKILL after a completed step
and after provider acceptance before recording, including reconciliation;
terminal malformed output versus plain application refusal; session/family
isolation and revocation during work; disconnect recovery; an actual
non-loopback peer; request bounds before verification; and a supervised
32-action/129-service-route registry reaching its final route.

The billing witness imports the landed backend-neutral Workroom provider and
canonical W5 conformance actions, not a similarly named standalone double.
It checks signed duplicates, newer-before-older delivery, invalid-signature
non-mutation, provider and receiver restarts with durable backlog, 503 and
commit-then-drop faults, acknowledgements and final persisted billing state.
When a newer backlog revision commits first, the older one is acknowledged
through a recorded stale application refusal rather than adding an audit
change. Every distinct canonical event has one handler invocation.

This is the increment-A route portion: it uses the two-event conformance
backlog, not the 200-event scale probe, and excludes project/seat-limit and
past-due write-authorization assertions. It is **not** a complete W5 claim,
Workroom backend/control cell or qualification of a third-party provider.

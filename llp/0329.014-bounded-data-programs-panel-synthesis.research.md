## Summary

The founder's question, verbatim: *"the real first question isn't whether to
restrict the language. It's whether the restricted language can be made
expressive enough to be the only language — bounded fan-out, grouped counts,
multi-table derivation, bounded graph traversal. If yes, the agreed design
works. If no, you're buying offline at a price the benchmark already shows you
losing. Can we come up with something that solves both sides of this?"*

Three model families argued it for three rounds — one blind, two open — with
the orchestrator's own candidate on the table and the two same-day single-model
answers ([LLP 0329.012](./0329.012-bound-carrying-algebra-as-the-only-language.research.md),
[LLP 0329.013](./0329.013-one-read-language-proposals.research.md)) as
inputs. The panel's answer is **yes, and the restriction that buys both sides
is narrower than every earlier document assumed**:

> **The property that makes one language total in capability is not algebraic
> closure. It is three weaker properties — deterministic given
> `(snapshot, args, viewer)`, every data access mediated by a typed, bounded,
> traced read API, and one semantics executed on both server and client — plus
> one static gate: a certificate of read *shape* and *bound* computed at
> `check`. Given those, real `if` on row values, `for` over bounded
> collections, and pure computation between reads threaten no guarantee the
> product needs in year one.**

Concretely, the converged design is **bounded data programs**: TypeScript
syntax, type-checked against a custom `lib.d.ts` that simply omits `Date`,
`Math.random`, `fetch`, `while`, and recursion; reads only through `get` /
`scan(index, prefix, {take})` / `exists` / `recurse({depth, fanout})`; loops
only over collections with static cardinality; rules as single algebraic
predicates; lowered to a small IR whose relational operators (`nest`, `group`,
`count`/`sum`, `maintain`, `recurse`) are the algebra of LLP 0329.012 and
LLP 0329.013 **as library forms**. Every admitted program gets a static
certificate (shape families, examined-entry bound, VM-step bound, prediction
sites) and, on every execution, a trace (index ranges with stop reasons and
absences, viewer-visible rows plus a one-row halo, scoped verdicts for rules
over hidden inputs). The certificate is the gate; **the trace is
simultaneously the invalidation predicate, the offline closure, the OCC set,
and the completeness detector** — which is why capability is uniform without
operator poverty.

Two of the three panelists *moved* to this position during the discussion
(Fable from a builder-spelled algebra; grok from "JS control flow is Zero in
disguise"); codex held it from round 1; the orchestrator recanted one sentence
("static analysis is an aid, not the gate" — it is the gate). What remains
genuinely contested is recorded in §5 with a verdict and the cheapest
experiment for each: the bound model for *rules* (static ceiling vs.
index-served with reported cost — the crux of LLP 0329.013 §7 decision 1), the
client evaluator (TypeScript twin vs. Rust/WASM core), whether computed-key
reads are admitted with honest `partial`, and the spelling bakeoff (TS syntax
vs. SQL text over the same IR).

This is research. It ratifies nothing and authorizes no rewrite; §8 gives the
one-week spike that would falsify it.

## 1. The crux, settled as a shared fact

LLP 0329.004 F3 and LLP 0329.012 P1 assert that an arbitrary deterministic
function cannot be re-executed on a client because "the function does not
describe what data it needs." The panel's shared statement, adopted by all
three families in round 2 (codex's containment, grok's two artifacts, Fable's
prediction closure):

> A recorded execution trace — index ranges with stop reason
> (`limit` / `exhausted`), including empty ranges; the viewer-visible rows
> examined, including one halo row past each window; and dependency-keyed
> verdicts for rules over hidden inputs — permits exact local replay of that
> execution at its snapshot, and permits local re-execution after client-local
> writes **exactly while `Reads(P, D₀ ⊕ Δ) ⊆ Coverage(trace ⊕ Δ)`**. A read
> outside coverage is detected and reported `partial`; nothing is invented.

Two artifacts, named once (grok): the **snapshot closure** is the trace — a
*value* from one execution; the **program closure** is the set of range
*patterns* a program can issue — a *type*, derivable only by static analysis.
The claim in F3/P1 is false of the last execution and true of the *next*
local execution's unknown reads. The two compose as type and value with a
`⊆` assertion: the trace must fall inside the certificate's shape families, or
the compiler has a bug.

Boundary conditions the panel adopted:

- **Dependent reads on a not-yet-existing row.** Whether an offline `send`
  predicts completely is known *in advance* iff the extra reads the new row
  triggers (e.g. `users[viewer]` for the author join) are functions of the
  written fields, `viewer`, args, or fields of rows at traced sites. The
  compiler derives this **prediction closure** per `(query, mutation)` pair by
  substitution and the client hydrates it at subscribe time (Fable). Reads
  keyed by computed values (a handle parsed out of message text) are
  `partial` under prediction, visibly in the certificate (grok's `@mention`
  program is the canonical example). This is the *only* place static analysis
  beats tracing for the product, and it is derivable over bounded programs
  for the same cases it is derivable over an algebra, because read keys are
  either shape expressions or marked `computed`.
- **Rule reads over hidden rows** (a `blocks` or `admins` table the viewer may
  not read) cannot ship as rows — range extents alone leak existence
  (LLP 0120 §Noninterference). They ship as a scoped verdict bound to
  `(ruleHash, pinned inputs, principal, authEpoch)` — not to `planHash + args`,
  so a read verdict serves a compiler-equivalent write rule. A verdict has no
  deadline of its own; it dies with the epoch or the store fence, which is the
  difference from a lease. Clique's membership rule needs none: the
  membership row (or its absence range) is viewer-readable.
- **Local delete of a held row.** A full `take(N)` page whose stop reason is
  `limit` needs row N+1's content to stay complete after one delete; the
  server already examined it as the has-more probe, so a one-row halo is free
  in trace size and adds no invalidation fan-in. Two deletes go `partial`.
- **Revocation while disconnected** is unobservable in every design; the
  client shows `stale`; on reconnect the server re-evaluates rule
  dependencies before outbox replay, retracts, and the client evicts rows
  held by no range. Prediction against a stale witness is a UI claim;
  authority rejects it.
- **Shipping a trace is safe only if authorization is a property of rows.**
  Under "the function is the authorization" (Convex-style), the read set is
  not a disclosure boundary and the trace is unshippable. Row rules are
  therefore a *precondition* of the mechanism, not a separate preference.

## 2. The converged design

Ten points the panel signs. Round-3 tally: Fable signed 9, dissented on 1
(add `channel`); grok signed 8, dissented on 1 (`channel`, clock input) and 6
(halo); codex signed 7, dissented on 4 (maintenance admission semantics), 5
(resource ceilings), and 6 (two trace artifacts). Every dissent was additive
or a tightening; none reopened the computational class, the gate, the closure
mechanism, or the client shape. The text below is the post-dissent version:

1. **One language, two kinds of program** (`query`, `mutation`) and two kinds
   outside the data plane: `effect` (JavaScript with I/O, at-least-once, never
   live, never client-callable, changes data only through named mutations)
   and — added by unanimous round-3 dissent — `channel` (ephemeral fan-out
   for typing and presence, LLP 0013's shipped surface: `publish`/`subscribe`
   predicates are rule expressions over durable rows; no persistence, trace,
   retention, or prediction). TypeScript syntax; `tsc` against a custom
   `lib.d.ts`; result types inferred (the CV-4/CV-6 half of Convex's win).
   Modeled inputs: `ctx.viewer` everywhere; `ctx.now()`/`ctx.newId()` in
   mutations, bound at intent time; a query-declared clock with granularity
   (§5 D-G) — ambient clocks stay refused.
2. **Reads only through the typed API** — `get`, `scan(index, prefix,
   {order, take})`, `exists`, `recurse({depth, fanout})`. `take` is required;
   `N` is a literal or an arg with a declared max. `Unbound` is a legal
   intermediate and an illegal result.
3. **Three rules that keep static analysis alive under real control flow:**
   (i) loop domains carry static cardinality — pages, literals, and arrays
   derived from schema-bounded columns (`text({max})`); (ii) read keys are
   *shape expressions* (args, `viewer`, literals, fields of rows from traced
   sites, total scalar functions of those) or the site is marked `computed`;
   (iii) rule bodies are single algebraic predicates, so they hoist (one
   probe when an equality filter pins the rule's inputs) and review. Helpers
   are inlined — there is no FFI, so there is no opaque boundary.
4. **Row rules are the only authorization**, deny-by-default, `public(reason)`
   conspicuous and unlocking nothing. Maintained aggregates are schema
   constructs (`maintain.count/sum … by …`) with compiler-generated
   same-commit maintenance (preimage on delete, −1/+1 on group move, declared
   overflow behavior, idempotent replay). Exact-over-a-growing-population is
   *only* the maintained form; `countCapped(K)` is the paved bounded form.
   `All<T,N>` (exact-or-refuse) only on schema-capped domains, never as a
   runtime peer of `Page`.
5. **Static certificate at `check`**, emitted beside the generated client:
   `planHash, schemaHash, ruleHashes, evaluatorVersion; readSites[{index,
   prefixTemplate: arg|viewer|const|computed, take, maxCalls, hoist}];
   writeSites; maintainedAmplification; maxExamined; maxRuleProbes; maxSteps;
   maxHeap; maxOutputBytes; resultType (cardinality-typed); completeness:
   page|capped|maintained; predictionSites[{transition, reads, mode:
   closed|conditional|partial}]; indexesRequired`. Refuse above the app ceiling
   `G` (loud default), never as a function of invisible-row counts.
   Cardinality alone is not a cost proof (codex): a loop over 100 rows can
   double a string 100 times with a tiny trace, so VM steps, heap, stack,
   value bytes, closure bytes, and output are separate ceilings (codex's
   round-3 dissent: `G` names a family of ceilings — examined entries, index
   entries, CPU, memory, closure, output, rule probes, write amplification —
   not one number), metered and failing closed inside the transaction. Every
   read site also names its `table`.
6. **Two trace artifacts per execution** (codex's round-3 correction, adopted):
   the **authoritative trace** — actual dependencies including hidden rows
   and ranges — drives server OCC and invalidation and never leaves the
   server; the **client closure receipt** — authorized rows, visible ranges
   and absences with stop reasons, projected row N+1 and the authorized key of
   N+2 (or an exhaustion fact), scoped verdicts for hidden-input rules, no
   hidden range extents — drives offline execution and client invalidation.
   Each is asserted `⊆` the certificate. Exporting one trace as both is a
   noninterference bug. Maintained aggregates are admitted only with explicit
   insert/delete/preimage/group-move/retry-idempotency/online-backfill/
   overflow semantics and a proof that every contribution is visible wherever
   its aggregate is visible (else authority-partitioned or refused).
7. **The shared fact of §1**, including the prediction closure.
8. **Client = a per-principal encrypted store of delivered rows indexed by
   `(table, index)` with range coverage at one version, a verdict cache, an
   outbox, and an IR evaluator.** The eight runtime states
   (`hydrating|fresh|stale|partial`; `predicted|queued|committed|rejected`)
   are the whole offline contract. One engine-owned, store-wide
   `maxOfflineAge` (mechanism required, value an author ruling); sign-out
   destroys the store key. Cross-query row consistency is free because every
   query re-executes over one normalized store — Zero's outcome — while the
   store is bounded by traces, never a replica (DK-9's intent). The client
   cannot issue a query the model did not name.
9. **Server: one Rust IR interpreter inside the SQLite transaction; JavaScript
   not in the authoritative path.** Subscriptions indexed by range; a commit
   overlapping a traced range or rule-dependency key reruns and diffs
   (rerun-on-touch is the v1 live lowering; `maintain` is the only
   incremental path; per-operator IVM is year two). Postgres is a second
   physical lowering, program-near-DB, with trace-validated OCC at commit.
10. **The dev loop is co-equal** with the execution model (one generation
    identity across schema, server, generated client, bundler, and cache;
    `check` / `dev` / `verify`). No language fixes a Metro race; Diary 0114's
    losses were lifecycle and media.

### 2.1 Why real control flow costs nothing the product needs

Once every read goes through the typed API, an `if` or `for` can only *select
among lexically fixed read sites*. The shape family of a program is the union
over branches; the examined bound is the product of nested `take`s (an
exclusive `if` contributes the max of its branches, not the sum); a `for` over
`Page<N>` with body cost `B` is `N×B`. That is LLP 0329.012's bound calculus
applied over control flow instead of over combinators. What the algebra still
buys — per-operator IVM, single-statement SQL lowering, a closed value model
with fewer semantic corners, a smaller compiler — the panel judged year-two
properties for a SQLite-first, scale-to-zero target. What bounded programs buy
— the Convex-shaped authoring prior that won the holistic scores, the
Split/unread loops agents actually write, nested result objects with inferred
types — is the year-one product metric (the B-half of the Clique rubric is 55
of 100 points).

### 2.2 The worked delta — the unread inbox

The shape every panelist named as the most likely Clique loss:

```ts
export const inbox = query({}, ({ db, ctx }) => {
  const mine = db.scan(memberships.byUserActivity, [ctx.viewer], { order: 'desc', take: 50 });
  return mine.map(m => ({
    conversation: db.get(conversations, m.conversationId),
    latest: db.scan(messages.byConvTime, [m.conversationId], { order: 'desc', take: 1 })[0] ?? null,
    unread: db.scan(messages.byConvTime, [m.conversationId], { after: m.lastReadAt, take: 100 })
              .countCapped(),                                   // exact to 100, then "99+"
  }));
});
// certificate: examined ≤ 51 + 50×(1 + 2 + 101) = 5,251; every prefix a shape expression;
// prediction closure for markRead: memberships.byKey([conv, viewer]) — already held
```

and the exact alternative, a maintained per-`(conversation, viewer)` counter
whose `send` fan-out is bounded by a schema member cap. The diagnostic an
agent hits when it writes `count()` over an unbounded range pastes both,
prints each one's read and write cost, and never offers an effect, a wider
`take`, or a weaker rule.

### 2.3 Amendments from the archetype stress test (2026-08-29)

[LLP 0329.015](./0329.015-app-archetype-stress-test.research.md) tested the
retrofit ladder of LLP 0329.008 against four deep applications (booking,
workspace, auction, ledger) and found it does not generalize; its author reads
the result as evidence for the general-language line, and its OQ5 asks whether
this design covers the six constructs the archetypes demanded. Mapping them
honestly, and folding in the two findings that apply to *every* design:

- **K2 — structural counts (applies to this design as written).** "Visibility
  before aggregation" as a silent default returns a confidently wrong integer
  for a count that is *about a resource* (waitlist depth, auction fullness,
  subtree size) rather than *about the caller*. Amendment to point 4: an
  aggregate over rows the reader may not read is admitted **only as a declared
  disclosure** — a maintained aggregate with its own `readable` rule (a
  security item in the receipt) or an argument-scoped count over the caller's
  own rows — and a silently visibility-filtered count is refused
  (`E_STRUCTURAL_COUNT`). Codex's round-3 dissent supplied the refuse half;
  0329.015 supplies the admit half.
- **K3 — predicate-selected writes** are native: `for (r of db.scan(…, {take}))
  db.update(…)`, with `patchWhere`/`patchEach`/`deleteWhere` as library forms
  over the same IR; bound = the scan's `take`; `hasMore` at the bound is
  `E_FANOUT`.
- **K4 — `sum`, `argMax`/`topK`, per-row expressions** are loops or `sort(cmp)`
  over a `Page` (exact over the page) or maintained rows (exact over the
  population); money is i53 minor units with a deterministic remainder sink.
- **K5 — interval membership** is a schema feature (`interval({ from, to })`
  columns, `contains(t)` predicates with shape-expression `t`, an `exclusion`
  constraint), and time is handled as durable transitions written by schedules;
  rules stay time-independent; `readTime` buckets serve display only.
- **K6 — constraints bind every write by construction**, because there is no
  write path outside the language: `unique`, `cap`, `appendOnly`,
  `immutable(fields)`, `exclusion`. This is the property the retrofit line cannot
  obtain while a JS write tier exists ("E without G means the one mutation you
  did not rewrite can still patch a posting").
- **K7 — scheduled and system authority** is a kind (`schedule(mutation, { at |
  every, key, principal: 'system' })`): transactional enqueue, at-least-once with
  the schedule key as idempotency key, generation fencing, rules admitting it via
  `principal.kind === 'system'`. Read elevation is never needed to settle.
- **Tree closure** (workspace §6.2) is a schema feature — `tree({ parent, path,
  depth, maxDepth })`, host-maintained in the same commit — converting recursive
  ACL and subtree operations into one indexed read; **negation** in rules is
  admitted (denials are expressible).
- **K8** (booking): range-granular capture is shipped and predicate-complete on
  the mutation handler path — an under-claim in the corpus that supports the
  trace-as-closure mechanism being buildable.

These amendments are written as a checkable target in
[LLP 0329.014.000](./0329.014.000-bounded-programs-language-v0.spec.md); the
paper exercise that tests it against all six applications is
[LLP 0329.014.001](./0329.014.001-paper-archetypes.research.md).

## 3. The proposals, compared

Three families of design answered the question this week. The table uses the
LLP 0329.004 test plus the axes the discussion exposed.

| | **Bounded data programs** (this panel) | **P4 / bound-carrying algebra** (LLP 0329.012, grok) | **Proposal A / general closed algebra** (LLP 0329.013, Fable) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Computational class | bounded structured programs; algebra as library | relational algebra with cardinality types; relational `case` only | non-recursive relational algebra ≈ SQL-92 minus recursion, nested shapes |
| Spelling | TS syntax lowered to IR (SQL text as a possible second front end) | TS build-time builder or restricted SQL, bakeoff | SQL-shaped model file with nested-select extension |
| Four LLP 0120 shapes | yes, as loops or library operators | yes, as `nest`/`group`/`maintain`/`recurse` | yes, except recursion (closure tables) |
| Bound model | static ceiling on scans (`take` required) + rule probes counted + runtime meters | global ceiling `G` on worst-case product; `Unbound` cannot execute | index-served everything; cost dimensions reported, no ceiling |
| Read-set / closure | static shape at `check`; trace is the closure (normative) | static shape; trace as `⊆` assertion + windows | recorded read-set wake (v1); witness closure derived statically |
| Prediction closure | derived for shape-keyed reads; `partial` otherwise | not specified (gap named by Fable, codex, grok) | "witness closure … derivable because rules, queries, mutations share one language" |
| Time | banned in reads; `now`/`newId` modeled at intent time in mutations | banned | **`now(granule)` as an input relation** — the clock "writes" once per granule |
| Aggregates | explicit `maintain` schema construct; `countCapped` paved | explicit `maintain*`, `Page.count` exact over page | compiler-decided maintenance by default; visibility-before-aggregation classifier |
| Client | rows + coverage store, IR evaluator, outbox | per-operation closure cache + IR interpreter | **SQLite** running the same plans over delivered rows; local FTS free |
| Evaluator | Rust server; TS twin or WASM client (conformance corpus + hydration replay check) | Rust IR both sides | one lowering, SQLite both sides (no twin) |
| JS in data plane | no | no | no |
| Agent prior | Convex-shaped TS (measured holistic win) | new | SQL (sol-supabase lane: 7/8, B1 92) |

### 3.1 Bounded data programs (the panel's design) — good and bad

**Good.** Covers the census and the full Clique read/write census without a
second query class; keeps the Convex-shaped prior that won the holistic
scores; inferred result types; one trace mechanism serves invalidation,
closure, OCC, and completeness; the prediction closure falls out of key
provenance; the algebra is not lost — it is the library and the IR's operator
set, so LLP 0329.012's and LLP 0329.013's read shapes are all expressible
unchanged; the client is a bounded store plus an evaluator with no capability
matrix to implement.

**Bad.** It is a compiler (off-the-shelf TS parser + ~30-node lowering +
dataflow + cost model) and a language with a value model to pin (i53 or i64
integers, codepoint string order, stable sort, `Map` insertion order, no
observable `NaN`/`-0`, refused overflow) — a spec cost year one must pay. No
IVM for loops; rerun-and-diff bounded by `G` plus `maintain`. Postgres runs
the program near the DB, not as one statement. A client evaluator twin is
unavoidable unless the Rust core reaches every arm (§5 D-A). "Looks like TS
but isn't" is a real hazard mitigated only by the custom `lib.d.ts`, refusals
that carry rewrites, and the fact that the agent's prior code is mostly
*inside* the subset.

### 3.2 P4 — bound-carrying algebra (LLP 0329.012) — good and bad

**Good.** The cardinality-as-type insight (`Unbound<T>` executes nowhere) is
the single cleanest statement of why "restricted" and "expressive" are
compatible, and the panel kept it whole. The operator set, the bound
calculus, and the ceiling-as-compile-time-fact (closing the existence side
channel) all survive into the converged design. The "shapes as products, not
operators" diagnosis of LLP 0157 is right and is the reason the corpus could
not converge.

**Bad.** Refusing row-level `if`/`for` is a spelling artifact of the
build-time builder, not a guarantee requirement — all three panelists and
the document's own P4a text concede this. The B-half of the benchmark is
where a builder in which `rows.filter(r => …)` is `LANG_OPAQUE` loses, the
way Familiar lost. It lacks the prediction closure (an offline `send` whose
author join needs `users[viewer]` is `partial` after the fact). Cardinality
alone is not a cost proof. A global `G` over worst-case products refuses
legitimate boards and, applied to *rules*, refuses the ordinary two-hop
co-member rule (LLP 0329.013 §8.1). **Survives as the type system and the
operator list.**

### 3.3 Proposal A — general closed algebra, SQL-shaped (LLP 0329.013) — good and bad

**Good.** Four contributions the panel had not made and this synthesis
adopts: (a) the capability decomposition — only *prediction* forces
restriction, and only of mutations plus per-node predicates; static
derivation is not required for reactivity; (b) the measured existence proof
that agents write SQL well for reads (`sol-supabase` 7/8, schema score 92);
(c) **time as an input relation with declared granularity**, which turns
disappearing messages, edit windows, trending, and 7-day buckets into
ordinary programs instead of client-clock workarounds; (d) SQLite as the
client store, which is already on every arm and makes offline search over
delivered rows free. Its complete Clique read/write census (§2.2–2.3) is the
best single expressiveness argument in the family.

**Bad.** Reads-as-SQL is well evidenced; writes-as-restricted-DML is the
tight spot, which the document itself concedes by naming shared TypeScript
mutators (its Proposal E) as the fallback — and once mutations are structured
programs, the reason to keep reads in a separate algebra is only the
evaluator-twin argument. The nested-select SQL extension is unproven with
agents. "Index-served everything with reported cost" relaxes the evidence
model (its own §7 decision 1). Compiler-decided maintenance hides write-path
cost from the author (the panel prefers explicit `maintain` with the compiler
*suggesting* it). **Survives as the read algebra inside the IR, as the time
relation, as the SQLite store, and as a candidate second front end.**

(why writes escape), [0329.007](./0329.007-is-the-point-read-restriction-deliberate.research.md)
(the restriction is unbuilt, not forbidden), 0329.008–0329.010 (coterie
expressibility ladder, plan-translation experiment, cost of a mutation step),
and [0329.011](./0329.011-bound-read-security-contract.research.md) (the
security contract for a bound read). The panel did not see it; the
orchestrator read its summaries after round 3. Its central finding relocates
the measured loss: **74% of writes in the demo corpus escape to
`defineJsMutation` because a declarative mutation plan can read rows only by
primary key** — a uniqueness check, a membership check, any relational
invariant forces the JS handler, which forfeits prediction and retention —
and the census narrows LLP 0329.004 F2 (the read-side loss is smaller than
LLP 0120's felt-loss set suggested).

This is complementary to, not in tension with, the converged design, and if
anything it favors it over the read-algebra-first proposals: in bounded data
programs a mutation is the *same* language as a query, so `exists` and
`scan(…, {take})` inside a mutation (§2 point 2; `startDm`, `send` fan-out,
`vote` in §6) are native, and the policy question 0329.011 answers — caller
visibility by default, elevated reads only in a mechanized form, refusals
only over a domain the caller is authorized for — is the same question §5 D-C
answers with rule-mediated reads and read-witness ≠ write-authorization. Two
consequences for the founder: (a) the series identifies a **retrofit** — a
bound, visibility-composed read in mutation plans, costed in 0329.010 — that
may recover most of the measured loss *without* a rebuild and is the cheapest
test of the panel's own claim that the escape hatch, not restriction, cost
the benchmark; (b) if that retrofit is built, its read API should be the one
in §2 point 2, so a later rebuild inherits it rather than replacing it.

### 3.5 Rejected, with reasons

- **Convex clone + result cache** (LLP 0329.012 P1, LLP 0329.013 D): not
  buying offline; app-authored optimistic reducers; JS in the authoritative
  path; reach evidence at zero. The honest null hypothesis if the paper Clique
  fails.
- **Query/query hybrid** — reactive-everywhere JS reads beside a retainable
  algebraic subset (LLP 0329.004's fourth architecture): LLP 0329 F1 with
  better names; `predicted` in fixtures, `partial` in production; rejected by
  all four proposals and both single-model documents.
- **Replica engine + client query language** (Zero/Instant/Electric, LLP 0155
  move 1 read strongly): the 212k-line client is the measured cost of
  approximating it; named operations only; copy the outcomes.
- **Read models only** (Firestore posture): the aggregate insight is right and
  is folded in as `maintain`; making the author write every read model by
  hand is Proposal A with the planner deleted.
- **Sealed-trace arbitrary JS** (P9's thesis sentence as first written):
  without the static certificate as the gate it is Convex plus a miss
  detector; grok's `@mention` program shows where advance completeness is
  lost. Recanted.

## 4. Where the panel moved, and what moved it

- **Fable**: builder-spelled algebra + `when` → TS-syntax bounded programs.
  Moved by P9's observation that control flow can only select among fixed
  read sites, by its own Split and unread examples being loops, and by
  realizing that everything it wanted from the algebra (prediction closure,
  hoisting) reduces to a rule on *key provenance*. Held: TypeScript client
  evaluator (Hermes has no runtime WebAssembly — verified: the "WASM
  support" headline is Callstack's *polygen*, an ahead-of-time wasm→C→native
  build); moved toward codex on a required store-wide fence.
- **grok**: "builder constructs AST; JS control flow is Zero in disguise" →
  TS-syntax bounded programs with `maxSteps`. Moved by "a `for` over
  `Unbound` is a type error in both designs — I had identified control flow
  with Zero." Held: `check` is the gate (the orchestrator's "analysis is an
  aid" sentence is rejected and recanted); verdicts narrow in v1; one Rust
  interpreter, native and WASM.
- **codex**: held from round 1 (`P4+`: structured bytecode, `for` over
  `Page<N>`), and sharpened it — "arbitrary pure computation" is the wrong
  phrase; resource meters (steps, heap, stack, output, closure bytes, write
  amplification) are part of the certificate; `AllOrRefuse` demoted to
  schema-capped `All<T,N>`; prediction status `closed | conditional |
  mayPartial`; policy facts richly bound.
- **Orchestrator**: P9 held on substance; recanted "aid, not gate"; absorbed
  the two rules P9 lacked (derived-array loop domains; computed-key marking),
  the halo, the prediction closure, the resource meters, and the Hermes fact.

The convergence is therefore not three models agreeing from the start; it is
two models changing position under a specific argument and one model
tightening its own. That is the evidence this document offers that the
attractor is real rather than trained.

## 5. Genuine disagreements and verdicts

Each item: the positions, the panel's round-3 verdict, the orchestrator's
recommendation, and the cheapest experiment.

**D-A Client evaluator.** Round-3 verdicts: Fable and codex — TypeScript
evaluator in v1 on every arm; grok — WASM if the same bytes run in Expo Go
within the size bar, else the generated TypeScript evaluator, never a native
module (recanting its round-2 "TS client rejected": the identity is the IR
and the value model, not the embedding). Facts: Hermes has no runtime WebAssembly; LLP 0129 measured the
previous Rust/WASM evaluator at 306,890 raw / 117,353 gzip bytes against
21,295 minified / 7,615 gzip for the shipped JS evaluator; a native module
costs the Expo dev loop (custom dev client). **Recommendation:** the Rust
interpreter is the *reference semantics*; ship a generated TypeScript
evaluator on mobile behind a frozen cross-engine result-and-trace corpus and
Fable's **hydration replay check** — on every hydration the client
re-executes the operation over the shipped closure and compares to the
shipped result, so divergence is detected in production and degrades to a
result cache with a diagnostic. Use the WASM build on web where it measures
acceptably. Experiment: build the operator-subset interpreter both ways,
measure size and cold start on Hermes/JSC/V8/web, run the corpus.

**D-B Computed-key reads.** Admit + mark `computed` + `partial` under
prediction (Fable, grok, codex) vs. refuse (algebra purists) vs. require a
written `mentions` row. **Recommendation:** admit, mark, `partial`; the
diagnostic for a prediction site marked `partial` offers "write a row the
view already nests" as the rewrite. Experiment: hand-lower `thread` with
`users[author]` and with `handles(body)`; offline `send` of a first mention;
assert `predicted` vs. `partial`.

**D-C Verdicts.** Needed in v1 at all (grok: no; Fable, codex: yes, narrowly,
for hidden-input rules such as `blocks`/`admins`/entitlements)? Binding:
`(ruleHash, pinned inputs, principal, authEpoch)` (Fable) vs. codex's richer
tuple. **Recommendation:** specify the object in v1, bound as Fable states
(so a read verdict serves a compiler-equivalent write rule), introduce it the
first time the census needs a hidden-input rule, never as authority, never
as write authorization unless the compiler proves equivalence. Experiment:
write Clique's and one blocking/muting app's complete rule sets; count rules
over hidden inputs.

**D-D `maxOfflineAge` default.** Round 3 split three ways: Fable 7 d default /
30 d hard max, resetting on any authenticated contact; grok 24 h default /
7 d max (LLP 0053's current `AUTH_LEASE_DEFAULT_MS` = 24 h); codex 24 h as
both default and hard max, shortenable only. Mechanism agreed by all;
sign-out destroys the key in every version. **Author ruling.**

**D-E Halo.** Content of row N+1 plus the key of N+2 (grok, codex) vs. one
examined row (Fable). **Recommendation:** N+1 content plus N+2 stop evidence,
selected by view/transition analysis where a delete is predictable, not
applied uniformly (codex). Experiment: replay realistic edits with halo
0/1/2; measure `partial` incidence and closure growth.

**D-F Bound model for rules** (LLP 0329.012 vs. LLP 0329.013, surfaced by
the two-hop co-member rule). A static ceiling over a rule whose cost is
`window × viewer's membership count` either refuses an ordinary correct rule
or forces a `.take(N)` that changes its meaning; Postgres RLS runs it
affordably because it is index-served. **Recommendation:** two regimes, both
in the certificate: *scans* keep the static ceiling (`take` required,
product bounded by `G`); *rules* are admitted when index-served, hoisted to
one probe when pinned, and otherwise reported as a per-row probe cost with a
runtime budget that fails closed — the diagnostic *suggests* (does not
require) the schema split or the maintained closure when the estimate is
high. Refusing the ordinary rule is how an agent ends up at `public`, which
is the failure this whole family exists to prevent. This is LLP 0329.013 §7
decision 1 and is the author's call.

**D-G Time.** Banned (0329.012, panel round 1–2) vs. `now(granule)` as an
input relation (0329.013). **Recommendation:** adopt the input relation —
design it into v1 even if the scheduled-tombstone effect ships first — because
disappearing messages, edit windows, trending, and 7-day buckets are in the
Clique brief and every lane re-derived the client-clock workaround. The read
set of a program that reads `now(hour)` includes `clock@hour`; the server
re-evaluates at granule boundaries; the client re-evaluates with its own
clock while offline and is re-checked on reconnect.

**D-H Spelling.** TS syntax (panel) vs. SQL text (0329.013) vs. builder
(0329.001/.002). All three agree the IR is the contract and the spelling is a
measurement. **Recommendation:** TS syntax first, because mutations and
nested shaping need it and the prior is measured; SQL text as a second front
end for reads if the paper Clique shows agents reaching for it; settle by the
one-week census bakeoff on the same IR.

**D-I Maintenance.** Explicit `maintain` (0329.012, panel) vs.
compiler-decided (0329.013). **Recommendation:** explicit in the schema, with
the compiler *proposing* the exact construct in the diagnostic; write-path
cost must never be implicit.

effect or headless client — the correct kind boundary.

Findings from the gauntlet:

- **A fourth kind is needed.** All three families used an ephemeral channel
  for typing (Fable and grok as a formal dissent on point 1; codex as
  `ephemeral.channel` in shape 3) — `channel` (ephemeral fan-out for typing and
  presence — LLP 0013's shipped surface — authorized by rule expressions over
  durable rows, with no trace, retention, or prediction). Without it, shape 3
  has no home and agents will persist presence as rows.
- **A time input is needed and the design space has converged on it.**
  Fable's `serverNow({refresh})`, grok's `readTime: { granularityMs }`,
  codex's engine-owned `visibility({ notAfter })` fence with temporal
  invalidation, and LLP 0329.013's `now(granule)` are one construct seen from
  the query side and the schema side: a clock modeled as an
  input relation with declared granularity, in the read set as `clock@g`,
  re-evaluated at granule boundaries server-side and with the device clock
  offline. It resolves shapes 5, 6, 13, and 0329.013's trending case; the
  sweep-only design leaves expired rows readable until the effect commits.
- **`G` is a product parameter, not a constant.** Shape 1's natural read-time
  merge at 500 followees sits at 11,001 against a default of 10,240 — a
  knife-edge that would refuse a correct program by 7%. The ceiling must be
  app-level with a loud default, and the certificate must show the product
  that produced the number.
- **Nothing in the fifteen shapes needed a second query class**, an opaque
  handler, or an author-written optimistic reducer. Two shapes needed a
  schema-level construct (`maintain`, `channel`), one needed an index kind
  (tokens/FTS), and three needed the time input.

## 7. What the orchestrator recommends

Build **bounded data programs** as specified in §2, with these amendments from
the single-model documents and the disagreements above: the algebra of
LLP 0329.012 as the IR's operator library; LLP 0329.013's time relation,
capability decomposition, and SQLite-backed client store; rules index-served
with reported cost rather than refused (D-F); explicit maintenance with
compiler suggestions (D-I); TS syntax first with a SQL front end measured, not
assumed (D-H); a TypeScript client evaluator behind a corpus and the hydration
replay check until a Rust core reaches every arm without costing the dev loop
(D-A).

What is good about this: it is the only design on the table that satisfies
LLP 0329.004's test *and* preserves the authoring prior the benchmark measured
*and* keeps every guarantee the panel could name — shape, bound, closure,
noninterference, prediction closure — with year-two properties (IVM,
single-statement lowering) deferred rather than forfeited. It reuses what the
corpus actually built well: the Rust+SQLite authority, the mutation IR and its
client evaluator (LLP 0133/0155), the DK-3 matcher discipline, the diagnostic
honesty, and the benchmark loop. The client becomes what DK-9 said it should
be, reached by refusing ad-hoc completeness rather than proving around holes.

What is bad about it, honestly: it is a language and a compiler, and the
corpus has measured what languages cost it to specify; the evaluator twin is a
standing conformance obligation; the `partial` state will surface in the
offline milestone where an agent expects a list, and the diagnostic has to be
excellent or the agent widens `take` until refused; maintained aggregates
under viewer-varying rules and hot-key contention on SQLite are unmeasured;
and none of it touches the dev loop, which every family and both documents
name as the co-equal cause of the Clique losses.

## 8. The spike

One week, one Rust binary, one TypeScript package, no product surface. Merged
from the three round-3 checklists, LLP 0329.012's four-step spike, and
LLP 0329.013 §6. Any box that fails by needing a per-operation special case
falsifies the design; do not rebuild on it.

**Day 0 — paper Clique (LLP 0329.013 §6 step 1).** Write the complete M1–M8
model — every table, rule, query, mutation, maintained aggregate, channel —
in the TS subset, with no implementation. Record every shape that needs
contortion or refusal. *Fail:* any read or write outside the class of §2.3.

**Days 1–2 — IR, certificate, interpreter.** IR on two pages (≤ ~30 node
kinds) with the value model pinned; TS-subset lowering (`if`, `for` over
typed cardinality, `Map`/`Set`, inlined helpers, typed reads); certificate
emitter; Rust interpreter inside a SQLite transaction with rules inlined and
hoisted; trace recorder (ranges, stop reason, absences, N+1 halo); trace `⊆`
certificate assertion. *Pass:* `thread`, `send`, `inbox` (capped and
maintained), `boardView`, `boardSummary` as `maintain.count`, `ledger` as
`maintain.sum`, `ancestors`, `startDm`, and a mention scan all lower;
`expenses take 500 × shares` is refused at `G` with the `maintain` rewrite; a
helper wrapping `scan` still yields the bound; `body.split` in a loop yields
`maxSteps` from the column max and is refused when the column has none;
measured examined ≤ certificate on a 10k-message table; membership hoists to
one probe; a rule over a hidden table emits a verdict and never a row or an
extent.

> **Result, Days 1–2 (2026-08-29, lane S1, codex gpt-5.6-sol @ xhigh, ~2 h).**
> `spikes/bounded-programs/` — a standalone Rust crate (IR + value model in an
> 89-line `SPEC.md`, static certificate, one interpreter inside a SQLite
> transaction, authoritative trace + closure receipt, a second in-process
> client store built only from receipts) — **passes 7/7 tests**, re-run
> independently by the orchestrator (12.2 s). Every named program lowers,

## Confidence

- **High:** capability-by-authoring-form is the root defect (LLP 0329 F1),
  and one language with row rules as the only authorization removes the P18
  incentive. Four attestations, three families, two single-model documents.
- **High:** the shared fact of §1 — exact replay while
  `Reads ⊆ Coverage`, `partial` otherwise; the prediction closure is the one
  place static analysis beats tracing, and it is derivable over bounded
  programs for shape-keyed reads. Constructive on both sides.
- **High:** real `if`/`for` over typed-cardinality collections threaten no
  year-one guarantee given the three rules of §2 point 3; refusing them is a
  spelling artifact of build-time builders. Two panelists changed position
  on this under argument; LLP 0129's 12/13 mutation-corpus figure
  corroborates the class.
- **Medium-high:** the fifteen-shape gauntlet needs no second query class —
  established on paper by two families; the spike's Day 0 is the test.
- **Medium:** rule affordability under the index-served regime (D-F), the
  two-hop rule in particular; maintained aggregates and per-viewer fan-out on
  SQLite (LLP 0120's named, still unmeasured expectation); the client staying
  an order of magnitude below 212k lines (every past client started small —
  the discipline is "no holes to implement," not a line budget).
- **Medium-low:** the client evaluator choice (D-A) — a TS twin is a standing
  conformance obligation; the hydration replay check is the mitigation, not a
  proof.
- **Low:** spelling (TS syntax vs. SQL text) on agent time-to-green — a
  bakeoff, not a call; and that the language alone moves the Clique
  scoreboard, because Diary 0114's losses were lifecycle and media
  (LLP 0329.000 F-B).

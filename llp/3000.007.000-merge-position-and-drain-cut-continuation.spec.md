## Summary

A merge (`merge t … :` with lanes, and every single-scope `matching` page, which is a merge of word lanes) pages by
one cursor: a position in the merged order that every lane resumes after. Three things are wrong or missing in the
shipped engine, and this document specifies the fix for all three as one change to one function family:

1. **The position is not unique** (§1, D0.1 — a shipped P1). Two rows with equal ordering values fall on either side
   of an exclusive resume and one is skipped, silently, with `complete: true` — across lanes over a unique index that
   does not end in `id`, and *inside one lane* over a non-unique one, which a merge admits because its cursor never
   reaches `E_CURSOR_INDEX`. Fix: the merge position carries the stored row id when the suffix does not already end
   in it. No author changes a schema; paved tokens keep their bytes.
2. **`next` is decided when the page is recorded, not when the answer is built** (§1, D0.2 — a shipped P3), so §20.3's
   rule "the non-uniform class withholds `next`" holds in one statement order only. Fix: the traversal records a
   *candidate*; the outcome publishes it, for merge owners only.
3. **A cut while *draining* a lazy merge is a dead end, and need not be** (§2–§3). With every lane's head known, the
   rows already emitted are a true prefix of the merged order, provided the loop stops at the first head it cannot
   establish. Such a page now answers `complete: false, cut: 'meter'` **with** `next`. A cut while *opening* stays
   terminal.

Not delivered: ruling 26's fourth fixed point, the React hook discharging a followed cut page (§5; open as Q8). Not
here at all: the windowed continuation for merges wider than one read, which the author shelved (LLP 3000.007
§20.22.5 item 3).

The review artifacts call this design **Part D** (Part O was the windowed continuation, Part S the hook change) and
cite its items as D0.1, D2.4, D5.1, C2b and so on. Those labels are kept in the headings and text below so the
reviews stay legible: **D*n*.*m*** is item *m* of the section whose heading ends "(D*n*)". Line numbers are against
`origin/main` `70a663c22`; behaviour was measured on the published `snapback4-darwin-arm64@0.2.27`.

## 1. Two defects of the shipped engine that the change depends on (D0)

Tickets: [the merge-cursor skip, P1](../issues/20260917-snapback4-merge-cursor-skips-rows-with-equal-ordering-key.md) and
[`next` after a later non-uniform read, P3](../issues/20260917-snapback4-merge-next-survives-a-later-non-uniform-read.md);
probes under `benchmarks/runs/2026-09-17-snapback4-bounds-probes/`.

1. **A merged position is not unique, and a merge cursor never reaches
   `E_CURSOR_INDEX`.** A merge's position is the index suffix after the lane
   prefix; resumption is exclusive on it (`rows.rs:103–110`); `MergeHead` breaks
   ties by lane index (`read.rs:31–40`); merge lanes are bound with `cursor: None`
   and the shared cursor attached afterwards (`bind/program.rs:547–551`), so the
   check at `:274–277` never runs for them. On 0.2.27: over
   `unique byRoomAt: roomId, at` a walk returns 3 of 4 rows; over the non-unique
   `by byRoomAt: roomId, at`, which a direct page refuses, it returns 2 of 3
   *inside one lane*; every page `complete: true`.
   **Fix: the engine breaks the tie.** Every stored index key ends with the row id
   — plain, unique and per-token `words(..)` keys alike, on SQLite, Postgres and
   the device's memory store (both reviewers verified: `rows.rs:124–160`,
   `store.rs:405–423`, `postgres/store.rs:286–303`, `device/src/memory.rs:183`);
   uniqueness is checked separately on the components without it
   (`write.rs:315–320`). The merged position becomes the suffix values **plus the
   row id when the suffix does not already end in `id`** — append-if-missing, so
   today's paved `…, at, id` tokens stay valid and only the already-wrong tokens
   are refused once. It is **the merge position only** — one tuple used by the
   eager path's sort keys (`read.rs:766–790`), `MergeHead`/`key_of` (`:822`,
   `:31–39`), `Bounds.after` on every lane, the recorded candidate, and the merge
   owner's encode/decode descriptor (merge lanes already reach `bounds()` with
   `scan.merge_site` set, `:547–552`, `:834`; that is the branch that decodes the
   longer descriptor). **A direct page's `cursor_suffix` does not change** (Grok,
   round 4): a direct page over a unique index without `id` is legal
   (`bind/program.rs:274–277`) and its token must keep its length. Eager
   cross-table translation (`read.rs:749–757`) keeps the encoded suffix; both
   tables already share columns (`bind/program.rs:528`). Nullable suffix values and both directions are covered by
   the same encoding (`key.rs`). *Fallback only if a store could not seek on it
   (none found):* refuse such a merge at `check`, with a rewrite that adds a
   second plain index ending in `id` — never one that appends `id` to a unique
   index, which would stop enforcing the declared uniqueness.
2. **A merge's `next` survives a non-uniform read that runs later in the query.**
   `merge_can_continue()` reads `non_uniform` when the page is recorded
   (`read.rs:796–802`); nothing re-checks (`read.rs:687–695`, `mod.rs:715–725`).
   Fixed by D2.4.

## 2. The diagnosis (D1; confirmed by both families, three times)

`merge_lazy` (`read.rs:807–880`) opens one head per lane, then drains a heap. A cut
while **opening** leaves unknown heads: no position is safe. A cut while
**draining**, every head known, leaves the emitted rows a true prefix of the merged
order, provided the loop stops at the first head it cannot establish. Today the
refill is skipped once `capped_at` is set (`:863`) but the loop keeps popping;
`merge_can_continue()` does not ask which phase was cut; the token is gated on a
`has_more` (`:875`) that a short page never sets (`:856–857`); and
`done = rows.len() < batch` (`:843`, `:865`) is false information when the short
read was itself cut. The eager path (`:710–793`) is all opening.

## 3. The change (D2)

In `merge_lazy`, for the uniform class, with positions per D0.1:

1. **`opened_all`**: the opening loop ended with the meter uncut after the last
   lane's head read, lookahead included.
2. A lane is **`done`** only if a read returned short *and* the meter was uncut
   when it returned.
3. **After every heap pop — emitted or dropped as a duplicate `id`** — the popped
   lane's next head is established before any further pop; if that needs a refill
   and the meter is cut before or during it, set `drain_cut` and **stop**. This is
   the *drain* rule and applies only after an uncut opening (`opened_all`): after
   an opening cut the loop keeps popping the heads it already holds and skips
   refills, exactly as before, and no candidate can be minted (both arms of item 4
   are false). *(Qualified 2026-09-17 after the implementation review: the first
   implementation applied the stop after an opening cut too, shortening a two-lane
   page from 100 rows to 1; C2 stands.)*
4. **The owning traversal records a continuation *candidate*; the outcome decides
   whether to publish it.**
   - *At record time, from the traversal's own facts,* a candidate exists iff

     ```
     (has_more ∧ capped_at.is_none() ∧ word_cut_at.is_none())
       ∨ (opened_all ∧ drain_cut ∧ emitted ≥ 1 ∧ word_cut_at.is_none())
     ```

     The uncut conjunct on the first arm is load-bearing, not a gloss (Grok,
     round 4): with eight lanes or fewer the last lane's opening read can be cut
     with no head while its siblings hold full buffers; the drain then fills the
     page from those buffers, sets `has_more`, never refills and never sets
     `drain_cut` — and the unopened lane may hold a row that belongs inside that
     page. Today `merge_can_continue()` withholds because `capped_at` is set; D
     keeps exactly that.
     Nothing later deletes a candidate: a result shape that exhausts the meter
     *after* an uncut full page does not strand the merge's remaining rows (GPT,
     round 3). No candidate is stored merely because a row was emitted (Grok,
     round 3): loss accounting would otherwise count every auxiliary merge as lost.
     **The word cut is the runner-wide `word_cut_at` at record time, as today.**
     That is conservative in one direction only: an overflow earlier in the query
     withholds a candidate that was in fact safe, exactly as it does now; it never
     publishes an unsafe one, because an owner that overflowed finds the field set
     whoever set it first. GPT's round 4 proposed a traversal-local overflow fact
     to recover the safe-but-withheld case; Grok's round 4 proposed today's field.
     The field is adopted: it is the smaller change and it is the safe one. What
     must **not** be built is the thing GPT's scenario warns against — inferring
     "no own cut" from the global field being unchanged across the traversal.
   - *At outcome build, for a page owner that is a merge site only:* publish the
     candidate as `next` unless the whole answer is the non-uniform class on the
     server (a device as today). That enforces §20.3 in both statement orders
     (D0.2). A **direct** page keeps `scan_prepared`'s contract untouched (§5.6,
     `read.rs:662–668`).
   - The branch goes in the three builders that can carry `next` —
     `Engine::query` (`mod.rs:714–731`), `local::query` (`local.rs:34–45`), the
     Postgres query path (`postgres/mod.rs:143–158`); the two native builders have
     `next: None` and need nothing. `page_position_lost()`,
     `discarded_page_loss` and `record_page` (`read.rs:674–684`) keep reading the
     raw candidate, so §19.2's rules are unchanged.
5. **Wire.** A drain-cut page is what any cut read is today — `complete: false,
   cut: 'meter'` (a server's non-uniform answer `cut: null`, unchanged) — now with
   a v1 `next`. **`cut`'s meaning does not change, and `usePage` does not
   change.**

**Correctness.** With D0.1 positions are unique across the union. Every emission
before the stop happened with every lane's head known, so the emitted rows are a
prefix of the merged order. Let K be the last emitted position: the lane popped
last has its unread rows after its popped head, hence after K (or at K only for
the same row id, which de-duplication drops); every other lane's buffered head
was not the maximum at the last pop, hence follows K. Resuming every lane `after
K` yields exactly the unemitted rows. Expiry, further search terms and a lane
`filter` remove candidates inside `scan_prepared` before a row becomes a head
(`read.rs:585–635`); a starved read inside a lane `filter` rejects the candidate
and the cut stops the walk before it is emitted, so the continuation sees it
again. The step meter aborts rather than caps (`meter.rs:166–174`): no page, no
cursor.

A single-scope `matching` page is internally a merge of word lanes
(`scan_words` → `merge_with_words`) and records its page as one; D applies to it
as to any merge owner.

## 4. What D does not do (D3)

- **Every continuation re-opens every lane.** If opening does not fit one read the
  continuation is an opening cut: no `next`. D's scope is the merge whose lanes
  open within one read and whose *drain* is expensive.
- A word cut and an opening cut stay terminal. The eager path — cross-table lanes,
  lanes with `setup`, so the installed Following recipe — is unchanged; its card's
  "there is no cut continuation" stays true.
- No windows (shelved), no new token format beyond D0.1's position, no fill, no
  client de-duplication. The two-cursor card and `held` are not touched here.

## 5. Descoped: the hook's discharge, and the `cut` invariant it needs (D4)

Revision 3 proposed that `usePage` stop counting a followed cut page toward its
aggregate `complete` (ruling 26's fourth fixed point), made sound by narrowing
`cut` to "non-null only when the continuation covers everything the answer is
missing". Three rounds each found a contribution the invariant missed, and the
last were not about merges at all: a live aggregate emitted partially computed
(`read.rs:612–621`); a read that stops on an already-set cap without failing a
charge of its own (`:606–608`); a nested page's word cut under a direct owner.
Closing it means a complete accounting of incompleteness across every read path —
a project of its own, and one that touches direct pages the rest of D never goes
near.

So **D changes nothing in `react.tsx`**. A followed drain-cut page holds the
hook's aggregate `complete` false, exactly as a followed cut *filtered* page has
since wave 3. That is conservative and true; what it costs is a "may be
incomplete" label after a walk that did fetch everything. With matching lanes lazy
(§20.22.1 item 2) a drain cut should be rare. The discharge is recorded as an open
design question (Q8), not a promise.

## 6. Acceptance (D5)

Server and both device stores. **Positives fail with D disabled; controls pass
before and after and are labelled.** Every positive states its predicate, seed
order and budget arithmetic in the test.

Positive:

1. **Drain cut, pure filter.** Twelve lanes eligible for the lazy path over a plain
   index ending in `id`; a merge `where` whose first match in each lane is the
   100th examined row and every 100th after it (a filtered one-head read stops at
   its first match and takes no lookahead, `read.rs:625–642`); `last 100`; default
   ceilings. Opening costs 1,200 examined; 69 refills bring the ledger to 8,100;
   the 70th fails at 8,193. Page 1 has **70 rows**, `complete: false, cut:
   'meter'`, and **has `next`**; following it returns every match exactly once in
   merge order and ends with no `next`.
2. **Drain cut, further search term** (the unfiltered path, whose one-head read
   examines through its *second* match): a single-scope `matching` page over
   exactly **nine disjoint** matching word lanes — more than eight, far from the
   33-word overflow — with the second term present in every 100th row and enough
   rows in every lane. Opening costs 9 × 200 = 1,800; 31 refills cost 6,200; the
   32nd cuts at 8,193 during its lookahead, after **32 emissions**. Word discovery
   costs ten index entries. Same assertions.
3. **The duplicate pop.** More than eight overlapping word lanes (messages
   carrying nine or more words with the searched prefix), arranged so the cut
   lands on the refill of a lane whose popped head was a duplicate: the row after
   it is returned by the continuation.
4. A drain cut landing on a refill's lookahead: `next` present. A short read under
   a cut meter never marks its lane done.
5. *(Withdrawn as a positive; see C2b.)* Revision 4 claimed a filtered merge of
   eight lanes or fewer can drain-cut. It cannot: the opening batch asks
   `scan_prepared` for `take + 1`, so the filter's early exit stops after
   `take + 1` matches (`read.rs:840–842, :642`), and a lane is popped at most once
   per distinct `id` before the page ends — no lane can exhaust its buffer first
   (GPT, round 4: a lane's (N+1)th row, popped unique, is at least the (N+1)th
   distinct id and ends the page before any refill; popped as a duplicate, its
   first emission from another lane already ended it). Grok's rounds 3 and 4 hold
   the opposite and name `crates/snapback4-lang/tests/feed_forms.rs:87` — a
   same-table filtered two-lane lazy merge asserting every capped page has no
   `next` — as a test that would go red. **The lane settles this by execution:**
   that test is expected to stay green (its cuts are opening cuts); if it goes red
   the analysis above is wrong, and the lane stops and reports rather than editing
   the assertion.
6. **D0.1.** `unique byRoomAt: roomId, at`, two lanes: all four rows. The
   non-unique `by byRoomAt: roomId, at` with two rows at one `at` in one lane: all
   three. Run each configuration on the path that actually admits it — the lazy
   gate still excludes a non-unique index without `id` and a nullable unique one
   (`rules.rs:90–94`), and D does not widen it — eager and lazy, ≤ 8 and > 8
   lanes, ascending and descending, a nullable suffix column. A merge whose suffix
   already ends in `id`: its token bytes are unchanged. A v1 token minted before
   the change for a suffix *without* `id` is refused `CursorError::Descriptor`,
   never reinterpreted.
7. **D0.2.** A uniform merge and a non-uniform read in one query, both statement
   orders, both merge paths, two stores differing only in rows hidden from the
   viewer. Server: the merge's `next` absent and `cut` null in all four. A device
   keeps its precise `cut` and withholds the merge's `next` exactly when it sets
   `non_uniform`.
8. **Raw loss accounting for the new candidate.** A *non-owner* merge page that
   drain-cuts makes the answer `page_position_lost` (an ordinary candidate already
   does today; the new one must too).

Controls:

- C1. A cut during opening returns no `next`
  (`crates/snapback4-sim/tests/common/merge_cursor.rs:54–64` stays as it is).
- C2. Lazy merges of eight lanes or fewer over a position that **already ends in
  `id`**, with unchanged query classification: every answer and token unchanged.
  (A two-lane merge over `unique (roomId, at)` is *not* in this control: D0.1
  deliberately changes its order and descriptor.)
- C2b. Filtered merges of eight lanes or fewer, and any opening cut: no drain
  refill is reachable, no candidate is minted.
- C2c. **Later work does not strand a merge** (it passes today and must keep
  passing — revision 3 would have broken it): a uniform merge fills its page
  uncut with `has_more`, its result shape then exhausts the meter; `complete:
  false`, the merge's `next` still present, eager and lazy.
- C2d. An auxiliary search that overflows 32 words *before* an uncut owner page:
  the owner's candidate is withheld, as today (conservative, documented). Its twin
  is a positive safety test: the owner overflows too, with its omitted 33rd lane
  holding a row ahead of the last emitted key — no candidate is published.
- C3. A non-uniform **direct** filtered page still carries its §5.6 `next`.
- C4. The eager Following recipe: `following_removal.rs:150–160`'s
  `unsafe_cursors` stays empty; `init/following.ts:36`'s comment stays true.
- C5. `usePage`: a followed cut page still holds the aggregate incomplete;
  `react.test.mjs:389–402` is unchanged.
- C6. Pinned meter counts on the cards are unchanged (D charges nothing new).

## Summary

A derived fact in a Snapback 4 application should be declared and kept by the
engine. Today the application often maintains it by hand, and the rules then
have to verify it.

The engine keeps exactly one derivation shape: a count or sum grouped by the
source's own columns (LLP 3000.012 §2.4). Everything else is application code:
the Following feed, rankings, attribution copies. Those are the charges in the
weakest categories (LLP 3000.012 §2.2).

This RFC proposes one rule, three forms and one substrate:

- **The admission rule.** The engine writes a derivation when two things hold.
  First, the compiler can bound its per-write work from facts the schema
  already states: immutable columns, declared caps and the meter. Second, its
  output is ordinary rows with a declared audience. This keeps §21.2's reason,
  bounded work in the writing transaction. It narrows §21.7's exclusion of
  join views: joins become admissible through immutable references, and
  fan-out through capped edges.
- **F1, maintained columns.** LLP 3000.007.001 as accepted, unblocked by a
  ruling on MC1's gate 5.
- **F2, group keys through an immutable reference.** For example,
  `count likes by postId.authorId`.
- **F3, timelines.** A per-reader merge, declared once. The engine
  materializes it with the push/pull hybrid that the feeds recipe measured.
- **One substrate.** F1–F3 and today's maintains share adoption, rebuild,
  activation and prediction policy. A derived form defines its delta, its
  bound, its rebuild and its audience, and nothing else.

In Clique this deletes:

- the 258-line feed recipe;
- the three copied columns on `likes` and the three-clause rule that verifies
  them;
- `posts.replyAuthor` and the backfill job;
- the ranking's copied time and its zero-like fill.

It adds no query planner, no general view maintenance, no ceiling and no
second language.

## 1. The admission rule

LLP 3000.007 §21.3 P5 names three writer classes:

- principals, through rules;
- the engine, through a declaration;
- the system, through a job.

A fact computed from other rows belongs to the engine class when all four of
these hold:

1. **It is determined by committed rows**, without `now`, `viewer` or a helper,
   as maintain predicates are today.
2. **Its work per source write is bounded by the schema.**
   - A reference hop resolves by one point read. It is admitted only when every
     column it reads through is `immutable`.
   - A fan-out is admitted only over an edge table with a declared cap per
     reader, and the engine's own policy bounds each write's copies (§3.3).
   - `check --cost` prints the bound, and the meter enforces it. A write past
     the bound aborts with `E_BOUND`, as any mutation does.
3. **Its output is ordinary rows** (or, for F1, a field) **with a declared
   read audience.** The partition log, horizons, the device, `why`, `data` and
   `sql` then see rows they already handle.
4. **Its rebuild is a derived rebuild**, as a maintain's is today: declared,
   adopted with the generation, and reported by `doctor`.

No principal can write the output; the maintain-target refusal applies
unchanged. A derivation that fails any of the four conditions stays in the
principal or system class. A diagnostic then says which condition failed.

This is narrower than view maintenance: no arbitrary joins, no aggregation
over a fan-out, no planner and no refresh. It is wider than §21.7, because a
join through an immutable reference and a capped fan-out both meet §21.2's
condition.

## 3. The forms

Spellings are illustrative. None of the blocks below is a compiling example.

### 3.1 F1: maintained columns

F1 is LLP 3000.007.001, unchanged. Its value here is ranking. With `likeCount`
on the post and ruling 29's hour index, Trending becomes one merge over 25 hour
lanes (§21.3 P3):

- zero-like posts are included;
- ties break by post time;
- there is no copied time and no fill.

Its cost is recorded in LLP 3000.007.002 §2: each contribution re-sends the
parent row. On a 4,000-byte parent that is 4,323 wire bytes per like, against
329 for a separate synced maintain. F2 keeps aggregates in their own rows. It
is the better form for a hot parent's counter until the stream coalesces or
patches fields.

### 3.2 F2: group keys through an immutable reference

```text
maintain receivedLikes: count likes by postId.authorId
  read <- allow
maintain dailyLikes: count likes by postId.authorId, day(.at)
  read <- allow
maintain receivedReplies: count posts where .parentId != null by parentId.authorId
  read <- allow
```

- **Where a path may appear.** A path `ref.col` may appear in a group key or in
  a maintain predicate when `ref` is a reference column of the source and `col`
  is `immutable` on its table. The first scope is one hop. The key functions
  apply to a path as they do to a column: `hour(postId.at)`.
- **Resolving the key.** The engine resolves the key when the row contributes,
  with one point read inside the writing transaction. It records the resolved
  key in the maintain's internal contribution index, the mechanism expiring
  maintains already use. A later delete, expiry or update then subtracts from
  the group it added to, even after the referenced row is gone.
- **An unresolved reference.** A contribution whose reference does not resolve
  refuses the source write, with a code that names the maintain. Every Clique
  builder's rule already required the referenced post
  (`exists posts[id = .postId, …]`). What changes is that the client no longer
  supplies the copy that the rule verified.
- **A null reference.** An optional reference that is null contributes to the
  null group, as a null column does today.
- **Rules.** Source rules lose their verification clauses. Attribution cannot
  be forged, because no program supplies it.
- **On a device.** The key resolves from the held target row. An unheld target
  makes the aggregate's base unknown, which is an existing class.
- **Disclosure.** A group key reveals the target's column to the aggregate's
  audience, as the hand copy does today. `N_MAINTAIN_DELTA`'s analysis
  includes the target's read rule.
- **Rebuild and cost.** A rebuild reads each source row's target once.
  `check --cost` prints one point read per contribution.

Without F1, Trending under F2 is
`count likes where postId.parentId = null by hour(postId.at), postId.at, postId`.
The copies go; the zero-like fill stays.

### 3.3 F3: timelines

```text
maintain following(reader): merge posts by byAuthorTime where .parentId = null:
  [reader]
  [f.followedId] for f in follows[reader] by byFollower

query home(c: cursor ?):
  return following[viewer] after c last 20 postCard
```

- **The declaration.** A timeline is declared by the merge a builder would
  write for a read-time feed. That merge needs three things:
  - a source index whose leading column is the lane key;
  - a reader parameter;
  - lanes drawn from an edge table (`follows`) that declares
    `cap N by <reader column>`.

  A second source with the same ordering columns, such as reposts, is a second
  lane family, as a cross-table merge is today (§20.3).
- **How the engine keeps it.** The engine uses the hybrid the recipe measured
  (§20.3).
  - A lane key whose in-degree is at most T is pushed: its new rows are copied
    into each reader's timeline in the writing transaction.
  - Above T, the lane is pulled: it is read from the source index when the
    timeline is read.

  Three values are engine policy: T, the per-reader pull limit and the
  follow-time backfill. `check --cost` prints them with their worst-case writes
  and reads. Today's values are the recipe's: T = 50, 32 pulls, an 865-event
  backfill and a 107-write promotion.
- **Follow, unfollow and deletion.** The engine performs follow, unfollow,
  promotion and source deletion as the recipe's helpers do now, including
  removing pushed copies when a source row is deleted. `feedRegister`,
  `feedDeliver`, the removed-events table and its purge job all leave the
  application.
- **Sync.** The timeline's rows have the reader as their audience and sync as
  the recipe's private inbox does, so the partition log needs nothing new.
  Pulled lanes are read under the source's own read rule.
- **First scope: public sources.** Pushing a row into a reader's partition
  asserts that the reader may read it. For a public source, the rule makes that
  true. For a private source, the engine would have to recheck it on every
  membership change. That is partition inversion, the subject of the first of
  LLP 3000 decision 4's two blind reviews. Private-source timelines wait for
  that review.
- **The first implementation is a relocation.**
  - The engine expands the declaration into the recipe's tables, now
    engine-written, with the recipe's caps and algorithm.
  - It runs the recipe's existing tests against that expansion: the simulator's
    `following_candidates_at_scale`, the device tests on both stores, and the
    220-post lifecycle case.
  - The declaration becomes the surface first. The serving design can change
    later without changing any application, for example to the keys-only merge
    that LLP 3000.011.002.000 deferred.

### 3.4 Not proposed

- **Aggregates over a fan-out**, such as a count per reader over a timeline:
  the delta is unbounded.
- **Arbitrary join views, multi-hop paths, and paths through mutable
  columns.** A key that can move needs a subtract and an add for every
  referencing row.
- **A "latest row per group" column** such as `conversations.lastAt`. The
  ordered read `messages[conversationId] last 1 by byChatTime` already answers
  it.
- **Symmetric audiences for pair rows** such as `dmPairs`. That is a
  union-audience question (`snapback4/NOT-DOING.md`), outside this RFC.

## 4. One substrate, and the prerequisite

Today's derived writes go through `apply_aggregate`
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/maintains.rs`) and
`row_changed(…, Origin::Maintained)`. The partition log, horizons and the
device treat their output as rows. This RFC generalizes that path instead of
adding one beside it:

- **A derivation descriptor in the catalog** records the derivation's sources,
  delta, per-write bound, rebuild and audience. `check --cost` and
  `check --residency` print from it, adoption rebuilds by it, and `doctor`
  reports it.
- **Each form uses the path.** F2 reuses the maintain path with a key
  resolver. F1 already routes through a private maintain
  (LLP 3000.007.001, revision 3). F3's copies are engine writes to
  engine-owned tables.
- **The architectural test.** F2 and F3 add no new adoption, activation or
  prediction mechanism. If they cannot manage that, that is the finding
  LLP 3000.012 §3 names as the evidence for restructuring the core.

**The prerequisite is MC1's gate 5.** Any form that adds bytes to existing rows
(F1), or adds rows to partitions (F3's backfill), meets the page ceiling that
MC1 met. The gate needs a ruling, one of two ways:

- accept MC0's adopt-before-shrink recovery as meeting the gate; or
- commission the request-side generation check before materialization that MC0
  deferred.

Either way, when a new generation grows a page past the ceiling, the answer
should be the generation, not `E_BOUND`.

  copied attribution fields, and no ranking fill;

## 8. Risks

- **The engine owns more code.** The recipe's logic moves to the core owner
  (LLP 3000 decision 4), and gains blind review where it touches delivery.
  That is the point of this RFC, and it is also the cost.
- **Timelines are shaped by one domain.** Following, activity feeds and
  notification inboxes share the shape, and every Astra run rebuilt Following
  differently (§20.3). A second application, Workroom or Interview, should
  confirm the form before it widens.
- **Hot parents under F1** (§3.1). Measure bytes per contribution before
  choosing F1 over F2 for a counter.
- **Rebuild time.** MC1 measured 51 s to activate 100,000 parents over
  1,000,000 source rows. F2's and F3's rebuilds at scale are atomic and not
  resumable until that changes.
- **The score may not move.** Quarry's learning cost is charged in every run
  (LLP 3000.012 §2.2). The counters are the claim, not the score.

## 9. Alternatives

| Alternative | Disposition |
|---|---|
| General view maintenance; arbitrary join views | Rejected, for §21.2's reason: unbounded deltas and a planner |
| Generated recipe and bridges (LLP 3000.011.002.000) | Built. It removed 8 of 36 wiring lines, but the application still owns the tables and the numbers |
| Merge-on-read Following (§21.3 P1) | Measured at 2.5–3.1× metered and 7.3–9.0× bare at 500/5 follows, against the 3× check. It remains compatible with F3 as its all-pull strategy if keys-only heads get under the bar |
| Maintained columns alone (W14c) | Addresses rankings; leaves the feed and the attribution copies |
| Cards and recipes, as now | Sixteen runs of notes say otherwise (LLP 3000.012 §2.2) |

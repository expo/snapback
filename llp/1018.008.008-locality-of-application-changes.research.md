## Problem

A small application change can affect queries and offline evidence well beyond
the feature being edited. Snapback preserves data and explains many refusals,
but builders can still have to reshape unrelated-looking work to add an
optional field or make another ordinary change.

The desired property is locality: consequences should track the semantics and
resources the change actually affects, and necessary wider consequences should
be clear before the builder starts a correction loop. Locality does not imply
that every source edit is cheap, that bounds can be ignored, or that old
authorization survives a changed policy.

## Evidence and current strengths

The [0.0.21 day-two diary](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-10-clique-ri-0.0.21/day2-astra-diary.md)
records adding `editedAt: time ?` to messages. An extra 44 bytes of certified
row width pushed `messages.searchAll` to 4,199,688 bytes against its 4,194,304-byte
closure ceiling. The builder reduced its page from 20 to 19, preserving 100
conversation lanes and opaque cursor reachability. The old generation kept
serving during refusal, and the captured messages were preserved. This is a
real certification consequence, not established underpricing, data loss, or
proof that the extra field was unnecessary to the existing computation.

[LLP 1020 Part A](./1020-day-two-evolution-and-legible-ceilings.rfc.md#implementation-record)
already provides additive widening, checked narrowing, maintain projection
ownership, `check --cost`, and corrected pricing. Its cold day-two runs did
not justify commissioning Part B's `fill` form. Those improvements are the
baseline; their historical motivating failures are not current defects.

Separately, [Schema::rule_hash](../snapback2/crates/snapback2-core/src/schema.rs)
binds a rule to the complete schema. [LLP 1018.002](./1018.002-dependency-scoped-invalidation.rfc.md#7-follow-up-after-the-elegance-assessment)
investigated narrower binding but did not establish a useful fix for the
observed optional-field addition to the subject table. Generation expectations,
dynamic row digests, certification, and policy identity are separate possible
causes of a queued-write conflict. A smaller hash alone proves no compatibility.

Confidence is high in the reported closure refusal and current broad binding.
Whether practical edits can retain more evidence or use less certified space
without larger conceptual/runtime costs remains unproved.

## Desired outcomes

1. Routine additive changes preserve existing values and require little
   unrelated application work, within honest execution and storage bounds.
2. A builder can see which operation, fields, and repeated terms make a change
   expensive, and distinguish a conservative bound from measured work.
3. Suggested repairs are executable in the actual predecessor/successor
   context and preserve stated pagination, authorization, and data semantics.
4. Live and offline clients transition coherently through permitted changes;
   necessary resets or conflicts name the reason and preserve recoverable work.
5. A pure implementation refactor has no semantic-generation effect when its
   executable meaning is unchanged; existing successful examples remain controls.

A future proposal should measure edits and correction loops across the whole
application, plus check latency and affected resource/evidence costs. Improving
one example by choosing a smaller product population or silently discarding
queued work would not establish locality.

## Constraints and unresolved questions

[LLP 1001 §§3–4](./1001-query-engine.spec.md#3-read-and-write-states-no-silent-states),
[LLP 1012 §7](./1012-local-first-v1.spec.md#7-writes-offline), and
[LLP 1020](./1020-day-two-evolution-and-legible-ceilings.rfc.md) remain in force.
Certificates must bound every admitted execution. Hidden rows confer no
absence authority; a group cap is not visibility evidence. Queued intent bytes
and stable terminal lookup keep their current meaning across evolution.

Future solution work should answer:

- Which surprising costs are required by the actual query, and which are
  removable over-retention or overpricing? Can the compiler explain the distinction?
- Can a repair preserve the same reachable data instead of merely making a
  refusal disappear by narrowing the product?
- Which dependencies actually change in a benign edit? Would narrower evidence
  identity save a real application from a conflict after all other checks remain?
- How do authority-changing controls, cycles, account epochs, and active
  successor transitions constrain any proposed reuse?
- Does each optimization reduce total complexity and correction work enough
  to justify its check-time, runtime, and maintenance cost?

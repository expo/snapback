## Context

Goal: Snapback 4's *default* Postgres reads must beat the Convex advocate on S1 W2/W3 without G6's clock assumptions (G6D: `snapback4/reports/20261027-g6-default/REPORT.md`; LLP 3000.009.001.000.002 §4.2 "G6 default-on proposal"). G5-RI coalesces G5's per-read revision validation ReadIndex-style: a read enrolls in an open batch; enrollment closes; one fresh validation statement (new snapshot started after the close) certifies owner authority and exact revision/identity equality for every enrolled cached entry; parked readers do not hold execution workers, reader loans or publication permits; collection 200 µs, validation depth 2, fallback to a normal miss after 8 ms keeping the original deadline. ## Questions

1. **Linearizability without clocks.** Is every RI-served read linearizable — specifically, can any read return a value older than a write whose acknowledgement (by this owner, a successor owner, or any grant-unaware writer the G5 contract admits) completed before the read arrived? Check: the snapshot really starts after enrollment closes for every enrolled reader (including readers that enroll while a previous validation is in flight at depth 2); what exactly the validation statement proves (authority/token/fence, incarnation/generation, code identity, revision, head) and whether it equals what a per-read G5 validation proves; RYW tokens; cache fills racing with validation; ownership loss/takeover; uncertainty latches; unknown COMMIT; deadline/fallback paths; cancellation/disconnect while parked.
2. **Liveness and resources.** Can parked readers leak, starve, wedge on ownership loss, exceed the 30-reader/32-total connection cap, or turn into unbounded memory? Is the 8 ms fallback correct under overload?

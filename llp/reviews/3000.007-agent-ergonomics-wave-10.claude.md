# Blind review — Snapback 4 wave 10 (W10a–W10c), `263495ceb` against `2a791fe17`

## Overall assessment

Wave 10 is a well-executed documentation wave with one behavioural change that was not sized against the engine's own write meter. Most of the ranked items are genuinely closed: the README's "no other documentation" claim is gone and its replacement is accurate about the three-file split; the `feedRepost(eventId:)` rename is consistent across recipe, wired example, generated `following.ts` and README, with a device test on both stores; the `matching ''` statement is true and its two-query directory shape is compiled and paged by a simulator test; the service token now leaves stderr and is proven redacted in the worker echo; the cascade rule is narrow and sound where it is applied. The lead's merge of `report_diagnostics` is the right ordering and the reasoning in the comment at `snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/verbs.rs:75-77` holds.

Three things stop it. First, W10b raises the Following recipe's follow backfill from 200 to `min(authors, 1000)` without re-measuring the write meter: at the recipe's own documented envelope — an author at the declared 1,000-event per-actor cap crossing the declared 50-follower promotion threshold — a follow now writes ≈1,106 rows against the unchanged 1,024-row `rows_written` ceiling and refuses `E_BOUND`. The lane's own measurement (1,001 written) is 23 rows from the ceiling and it never adds the promotion path. No test reaches it. Second, the governing spec was never amended: LLP 3000.007 §20.3 still states "the backfill stops after the newest 200 events" (`llp/3000.007-relaxed-quarry-and-explicit-residency.rfc.md:2517`), there is no §20.19 for wave 10 at all (`git diff -- llp/` is empty), and the one new `@ref` that would have carried the cascade rule points at an unrelated section. The brief's instruction that "it is only docs" is not a mitigation is load-bearing here: F4, F5, F6 and F8 below are all documentation, and each has a concrete wrong action attached.

## Findings

### F1 — High. The new 1,000-event backfill breaks the mutation write ceiling on the recipe's own documented envelope

**File:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/recipe.rs:20` (`fn backfill(self) -> u32 { self.authors.min(1000) }`) and `:72`; the rewritten loop bound at `snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:254`.

**Scenario.** Author `A` is at the recipe's declared per-actor cap of 1,000 source events (`feeds.md:149`, `cap 1000 by actorId`), and has 50 followers. Reader `R` — the 51st — follows. `addFollow` (`feeds.md:237-260`) then, in one mutation:

- `insert follows` (recipe edge) — 1
- `update authors[authorId] { shared: true }` — 1
- `tryPull(authorId, authorId)` → `insert pulls` + `upsert pullCounts` (`feeds.md:233-234`) — 2
- `for f in follows[authorId] first 1000 by byFollowed: tryPull(f.followerId, authorId)` — 51 readers × 2 — 102
- the backfill loop: `a.shared` was read **before** the promotion update, and every existing event still carries `shared: false`, so all 1,000 are copied — 1,000

That is **1,106 rows written**, plus the app's own `insert follows` in the documented one-mutation bridge (`feeds.md:636-638`) = **1,107**, against `Ceilings::default().rows_written = 1_024` (`snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/meter.rs:23`). The follow refuses `E_BOUND` atomically. Even on the most conservative accounting (1 write per `tryPull`, promotion touching only unrouted followers) the total is ≥ 1,054, still over. At the previous bound of 200 the same follow wrote ≈306 and always succeeded. The crossover is roughly 918 backfilled events, well inside the declared 1,000-event envelope — and `--authors 1008`, the calibration's own install, selects backfill 1,000.

The lane's own measurement is the corroboration: `JOURNAL.md:23634-23635` records "backfill 1,000 measured 1,005 examined / 9,006 index entries / **1,001 written**" — 23 rows of headroom before any promotion, any app write, or any pull route.

A second consequence of the same change: `feed` declares `cap 1000 by readerId, actorId` (`feeds.md:183`). One follow of a 1,000-event author now fills that group exactly. If `A` deletes a post and creates a replacement before `purgeRemovedEvery` (every 1m, one tombstone per chunk, `feeds.md:338-343`) removes `R`'s copy, the delivery `copy(p, R, false)` exceeds the group cap and **`A`'s `createPost` refuses for every follower**, not just `R`. At backfill 200 the group held ≈200 and the post succeeded.

**Tests do not reach it.** `crates/snapback4/tests/init.rs:503-549` seeds 1,000 events and exactly **one** follower, so it measures 1,001 writes and passes. `crates/snapback4-sim/tests/following/mod.rs:77` exercises promotion at 220 events (326 writes) — which is why the wave only had to renumber its expectations from 201/202 to 221/222.

**Smallest fix.** Bound the installed backfill by the write ceiling rather than by `--authors`: `self.authors.min(1000).min(896)` (or a named `BACKFILL_MAX` derived from `Ceilings::default().rows_written` minus the promotion allowance), and add an engine assertion to `tests/init.rs:503` that follows a 1,000-event author as the 51st follower and asserts `WriteState::Sent`.

### F4 — Medium. The new `N_NO_HORIZON` sentence inverts the suppression rule it sits beside, and the test that "proves" it demonstrates the opposite

**File:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide.rs:705-706`, `snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/codes.rs:133`, `snapback4/packages/snapback4/README.md:1086-1089`.

Verbatim: `Silence means "no reader in this program forced the check", not "this table is bounded".` The sentence immediately after it, in the same paragraph, is the actual rule: "Silent when whole-partition reads or insert proofs of eligible mutations depend on that absence of a horizon." The implementation agrees with the second sentence: `bind/mod.rs:198` skips the note when `residency::needs_whole_partition(schema, name)` — i.e. **a reader is exactly what causes silence.**

The lane's own test demonstrates the inversion and then asserts only that the sentence is present (`crates/snapback4-lang/tests/residency_notes.rs:14-25`): `without_reader` (bare `sync public` table) → **1** `N_NO_HORIZON`; `with_reader` (add `mutation inspect(id: profiles): return profiles[id]`, eligible) → **0**. Note fires when there is no reader; silence means there *is* one.

**Scenario.** A builder has `likes` as uncapped `sync public` and an eligible `social.like` whose insert proof reads that whole partition — the exact schema of `friction-review.md:126` (V4). `check` is silent. They read the new sentence, conclude that no reader in their program depends on the table's horizon-freedom, and add `sync public last 500 by byLikeTime` to bound cold start. `social.like` immediately stops predicting. The note's own protective branch — "a horizon would cost these mutations their prediction: …; keep `sync public`" (`bind/mod.rs:218-220`) — is unreachable in this case precisely because the note is silent, so the builder never sees the warning the sentence talked them past.

**Smallest fix.** Replace the sentence with the direction that is true: `Silence can mean a reader in this program depends on the absent horizon, not that the table is bounded.` Update the assertion string in `residency_notes.rs:16` and the two copies in `codes.rs:133` / `README.md:1087`.

### F5 — Medium. The new `N_NO_CURSOR` cap remedy proposes a whole-table cap on an unscoped page, and proposes shrinking an existing cap

**File:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/mod.rs:238-245`.

Two cases the branch does not distinguish.

**(a) Unscoped page → whole-table cap.** For `query all(): return messages[] first 10 by byId`, `page.prefix` is empty, so `pinned` is empty, `by` is `""`, and `limit = 10`. The rewrite reads:

> `… ; or declare `cap 10` on table messages if that is the intended group limit; other metered work can still cut the read`

There is no group; `cap 10` is a global constraint that refuses the 11th row ever inserted into `messages`. `group_bound` treats an empty `by` as matching any pinned set (`snapback4-core/src/schema.rs:155-157`), so the note does fall silent — the app now refuses writes at 10 rows. The card repeats the advice as paved: "An unscoped plain page can use `cap 50` on the whole table instead" (`guide/reads.rs:174`). This shape is produced by the wave's own fixture at `tests/check.rs:110` and never asserted.

**(b) Already-capped table → shrink the cap.** `tests/residency_notes.rs:410-419` builds a table that declares `cap {cap} by owner` and fires the note for `cap 9000` and `cap 8193`. In **both** firing cases the test asserts the rewrite says ``or declare `cap 8192 by owner` `` — a cap smaller than the one already declared. A builder following it edits `cap 9000 by owner` down to `cap 8192 by owner`; every group between 8,192 and 9,000 rows now refuses its next write atomically, and the card is explicit that this is real ("A cap refuses writes beyond its limit: it is a data constraint, not a hint", `guide/reads.rs:175`). The rewrite never says an existing cap must be reduced.

**Smallest fix.** Emit the cap alternative only when `!pinned.is_empty()` **and** `schema.group_bound(&page.table, &pinned).is_none()`; otherwise use the existing "still needs continuation" branch at `bind/mod.rs:243`. Delete the `guide/reads.rs:174` sentence and the `residency_notes.rs:417` assertion with it.

### F7 — Medium. Sizing the backfill from `--authors` silently reduces delivered history below the previous fixed 200

**File:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/recipe.rs:20`.

`--authors` is the **global author-count cap** (`recipe.rs:44-48`, `summary()` at `:44`); the backfill is a **per-follow event count**. Coupling them means every install with `--authors < 200` now delivers *less* history than before the wave.

**Scenario.** A builder installs a 50-person team app with `--authors 50 --edges 1000` (a combination the wave's own test installs, `tests/init.rs:505`). Before wave 10, every follow backfilled the newest 200 events. After it, each follow backfills 50. Nothing in `check` says so, and the card states the formula (`feeds.md:45-47`) without noting that it is a reduction for small author counts. The card also never states what the journal records — that a smaller backfill does not reduce metered cost, because the source page is still `last 1000` ("1,005 examined" for backfill 50, `JOURNAL.md:23634-23635`) — so a builder who shrinks `--authors` to reduce follow cost gets less history for the same meter.

**Smallest fix.** `fn backfill(self) -> u32 { self.authors.clamp(200, 1000) }`, or introduce `--backfill N` and leave `--authors` measuring authors; add the "fewer copies do not imply fewer examined rows" sentence from `JOURNAL.md:23636` to `feeds.md:47`.

### F8 — Medium. The one printed residency pointer is not a runnable command, and it prints at the moment a builder can least act on it

**File:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/verbs/init.rs:125`.

Printed verbatim: `cold rows: snapback4 test --residency --fixture fixture --as <principal>`.

**Scenario A.** A builder copies the line into `sh`/`zsh`. `<principal>` is a redirect operator; the shell fails with `no such file or directory: principal` before the CLI runs. Nothing on the printed line says the two tokens are placeholders — that explanation lives only in `README.md:1514-1516` and `feeds.md:40-42`, which is where the pointer was supposed to stop being.

**Scenario B.** The builder quotes the argument and substitutes a principal but leaves `--fixture fixture`. `verbs.rs:744` is `for entry in std::fs::read_dir(fixture).into_iter().flatten().flatten()` — a missing directory yields no error. The command prints `test: loaded 0 rows from fixture in 0.0 s` and then a residency **measurement** of an empty store, labelled "cold rows", with exit status 0. The builder records a zero-row acquisition figure as a measurement.

**Scenario C (timing).** The pointer prints at `init --recipe feeds`, and `tests/init.rs:551-573` asserts it never prints again — not from `check`, not from a repeated install. At install time there is no fixture and no data. The friction review's own evidence is that a pointer printed at the wrong moment goes unfollowed for four consecutive lanes (`friction-review.md:668`); this one is printed at the single moment with the least actionability and then never repeated.

**Smallest fix.** Print `cold rows, once you have a fixture: snapback4 test --residency --fixture <dir> --as <principal>` (angle brackets on both, so neither reads as runnable), and make `verbs.rs:744` refuse a missing `--fixture` directory with a non-zero exit instead of loading zero rows.

### F9 — Medium. No test pins the merge's ordering, and three concrete cases are uncovered

**Files:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4/tests/check.rs:13`, `:35`, `:108`, `:164`; `snapback4/crates/snapback4/tests/fix.rs:133`.

The W10a tests produce only bind-time diagnostics (`return absentA`, `return 1 + true`, `insert …` in a query), so no cascade exists and the ordering is never exercised. The W10c tests do produce cascades but assert nothing that distinguishes the orderings: `tests/check.rs:24` counts `" — E_QUARRY_SYNTAX:"` occurrences, and an excerpt's first line is `E_QUARRY_SYNTAX: …` with no `" — "`, so **`w10c_check_hides_only_the_redundant_operation_cascade` passes unchanged if the lead had counted repeats before suppressing.** The same is true of `tests/check.rs:35` and of the updated `fix.rs:136-137`.

Concretely missing:

1. A `check` in which a cascade is hidden **and** a repeat threshold is computed — e.g. one file with one real `E_QUARRY_SYNTAX` plus its empty-body cascade, asserting that **no** excerpt prints (the merge decision), and a second with two real syntax errors plus two cascades, asserting that one excerpt still prints.
2. The `E_BOUND` case in **F1**: a follow of a 1,000-event author as the 51st follower.

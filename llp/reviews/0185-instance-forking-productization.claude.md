## MATERIAL findings

1. **The cross-cutting finite-TTL constraint is not self-enforcing, and no phase makes it so — while Phase 8 applies the stricter rule to hosted only.**
   "Authority and boundaries" states as an invariant on *every* phase: "Every product-created fork has a finite TTL. Renewal may move the expiry but may not create an unbounded fork." In the implemented substrate the TTL is recorded metadata plus an entirely externally-driven sweep. `ForkCommand::Reap` (`/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/src/cli.rs:1290-1299`) declares `#[arg(long)] now_ms: u64` with **no default**, and `instance_fork::expired_managed` has exactly one non-test caller (`/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/src/cli.rs:2548`). There is no ambient reaper, no boot-time sweep, and no scheduled cleanup anywhere in `src/`. A fork whose expiry passes keeps running and keeps its bytes until a human or script invokes `snapback fork reap --now-ms <clock>`.
   The program then hands fork creation to exactly the actors least likely to run that sweep: Phase 2 items 3 and 5 (fork-per-agent wrapper, parallel test-runner integration), and Phase 7 items 1-3 (agent control-plane creation under policy). Phase 2 item 6's exit language — "leave either recoverable finite-TTL forks or fully cleaned state" — treats "finite-TTL fork" as a self-limiting outcome, which it currently is not.
   The Plan itself demonstrates it knows this is required: **Phase 8 item 5** mandates "finite leases, quotas, cost estimates, budgets, billing attribution, and **automatic deletion before enabling agent creation**." The identical hazard is enabled locally in Phases 2 and 7 with no automatic-deletion requirement. Either the invariant needs an enforcement item on the local path (ambient/on-boot/on-create reaping, or a create-time refusal when expired forks exist) before Phase 2, or the constraint should be honestly restated as "every fork carries a finite recorded expiry, enforced by an externally invoked sweep," and the Phase 2/7 exits reworded to stop leaning on it.

2. **Phase 2's exit criterion asserts properties whose evidence is Phase 3's job — the phases are inverted relative to their own gates.**
   Phase 2's exit reads: "parallel consumers cannot trample data, ports, credentials, or cleanup ownership." Every clause of that is Phase 3 work:
   - *data* — Phase 3 item 1 ("Prove that writes committed to the parent after the fork point never appear in the existing fork"), which is also the one baseline path with no automated coverage (the readiness-descriptor live-parent source in `source_from_path`, `/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/src/cli.rs:2061`, is exercised only by the manual 2026-08-03 dogfood; `tests/instance_fork_cli_v1.rs` only drives `--from <db>`);
   - *credentials* — Phase 3 item 2 (the parent/fork credential matrix);
   - *cleanup ownership* — Phase 3 item 5 ("create/drop/reap races, duplicate cleanup, ... partial cleanup").
   Phase 2 therefore cannot close on its own evidence, and the "critical path is ... Phase 2 → Phases 3 and 4" ordering means the integrations that most need the isolation guarantee are built and put into routine use (Phase 2's exit demands "at least one real demo or acceptance program uses the harness and worktree path repeatedly") before the guarantee is proved.
   The same inversion applies to resources: Phase 2 item 5 explicitly targets "parallel suites sharing an expensive prepared parent," and Phase 3 items 7-8 are where fork-count, total-byte, per-fork-byte, and diff-work limits and the disk-pressure/"excessive fork counts" corpus arrive. Recommend either moving Phase 3 items 1, 2, 5, 7 ahead of Phase 2, or splitting Phase 2 into a single-consumer slice (harness fork-instead-of-seed, one worktree) that can honestly precede Phase 3, with the parallel/agent-fleet items gated behind it.

**Why NOT READY.** The findings cluster into three fixable groups rather than a fundamental redesign. (i) *Ordering*: MATERIAL 2 and the Phase 5 leg of MATERIAL 4 are prerequisites appearing after their consumers; both are addressable by moving items, not by re-planning. These are the two I would fix first, because both are load-bearing on the "resource-bounded" and "honest published capability" claims in the Summary. (iii) *Ownership*: MATERIAL 3 and MATERIAL 5 are the Plan describing its neighbors and its own starting point slightly wrong in ways that let Phases 0, 1, 4, and 7 drift into contract work they do not own.

To the brief's decision-quality question about Phase 3-before-Phase 2: yes, at least partially — but I would not simply swap the phases. The genuinely valuable early slice in Phase 2 is item 1 (harness fork-instead-of-seed with one parent fixture), which is single-consumer, closes the named guide-gap ticket, and does not depend on any parallelism guarantee. Items 3, 5, and 6 — agent fleets, parallel suites, interruption behavior — are the ones that consume Phase 3's isolation, credential, cleanup-race, and resource evidence, and they are also the ones that make the missing ambient reaper bite. Splitting Phase 2 on that seam would resolve MATERIAL 2 and materially reduce the exposure in MATERIAL 1 without lengthening the critical path.

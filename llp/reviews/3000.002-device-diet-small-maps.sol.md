## Overall assessment

The `SmallMap` implementation preserves `BTreeMap`’s relevant ordering, replacement, serde, entry, comparison, hashing, and copy-on-write semantics. I found no wire-, durable-format-, or byte-order regression.

However, three conversions violate the “small/build-once” premise. They introduce unmetered quadratic work in remotely influenced or row-proportional paths. Mandatory S2 browser evidence and the planned size-budget ratchet are also absent. This is not ready to land as-is.

## Findings

### High — Durable device-history groups became quadratic

**Location:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/history.rs:10`, `:28-46`; `snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/snapshot.rs:36-63`

**Scenario:** A viewer joins and leaves thousands of distinct groups over time. The persisted inner `group → membership` map grows without a meter ceiling. A later sync page can introduce up to 4,000 group updates in arbitrary order. Each inner insertion may shift most of the vector, producing O(E·G + E²) work where baseline was O(E log G). Reopening and snapshot reconstruction repeat work against the durable history.

This is a new remotely influenced device availability problem.

**Smallest fix:** Keep the outer table map as `SmallMap`, but restore the inner group map to `BTreeMap<String, Membership>`. Add a randomized/reverse-order thousands-of-groups scale regression.

### Medium — Effect-presence dependencies became quadratic below existing limits

**Location:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/presence.rs:6`, `:35-56`, `:93-99`

**Scenario:** An admitted effect loops over 8,192 rows. Each row reads `connected[principal]` for a distinct, shuffled principal and pushes into the same target. The target dependency map grows by one key per row. Vector insertion performs O(n) shifts, making the loop O(n²). The associated heap remains comfortably below 16 MiB, while interpreter steps count expressions rather than shifted entries, so existing meters do not constrain this work.

Baseline used logarithmic B-tree insertion.

**Smallest fix:** Restore `Dependencies` to `BTreeMap<String, Option<i64>>`; the outer, small variable/guard maps can remain `SmallMap`. Add an 8,192-distinct-principals shuffled-order benchmark or an instrumented complexity test.

### Medium — Filtered scans now shift the entire environment twice per row

**Location:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:46`; `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:625-630`

**Scenario:** A program with hundreds of live bindings performs a filtered scan over 8,192 visible rows. For every row, `"$filter"` is inserted and removed. Because it sorts before normal identifier names, both operations shift essentially the complete environment vector. This creates O(rows × bindings) container work that the interpreter meter does not count; baseline B-tree operations were logarithmic.

**Smallest fix:** Keep the runner environment as a `BTreeMap`, or store the transient filter binding in a dedicated slot outside the ordered environment. Add a wide-environment filtered-scan benchmark.

### Low — Two regression tests do not prove the representation transition they imply

**Location:** `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/tests/smallmap.rs:130-142`; `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/tests/ordered_identity.rs:29-35`

**Scenario 1:** Reverting `ObjectFields` to `BTreeMap` leaves `row_images_keep_sharing_order_and_meter_charges` passing, because sharing, order, and charge equality were already baseline behavior.

**Fix:** Add a compile-time assertion that `ObjectFields` dereferences to `SmallMap<String, Value>` while retaining the behavioral checks.

**Scenario 2:** The context-argument identity pin constructs a `SmallMap` directly. A context-argument serialization difference between the old `BTreeMap` representation and `SmallMap` would not be detected by that pin, despite the evidence describing it as baseline-produced.

**Fix:** Serialize/hash the same corpus through both `BTreeMap` and `SmallMap`, assert byte identity, and pin the baseline `BTreeMap` result. The remaining fixed identity corpus is valid: it intentionally should pass at baseline because the requirement is no observable change.

## Explicit cross-cutting answers

**Did the change introduce a new problem?** Yes. It introduces quadratic, unmetered work for durable device-history groups, accumulated effect dependencies, and filtered scans with wide environments. I found no new byte, ordering, COW, heap-charge, persisted-key, or public-TypeScript semantic regression.

**Is there a regression test that would also pass without the change it claims to test?** Yes. `row_images_keep_sharing_order_and_meter_charges` passes if `ObjectFields` stays a `BTreeMap`; it needs a representation assertion. The context-argument portion of `ordered_identity` does not compare against baseline `BTreeMap` bytes; it needs an explicit cross-representation byte/hash assertion.

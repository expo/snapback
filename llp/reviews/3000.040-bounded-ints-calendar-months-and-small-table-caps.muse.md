# Blind review: LLP 3000.040 (bounded ints, calendar months, small-table caps)

I checked every cited claim against the tree. Section numbers below are the RFC's. Code refs are `snapback4/`-relative.

## What is sound (no change needed)

- **(A) `IntRange` as a new variant** (§1.3). Agree versus both alternatives: changing `Int` would break every stored `"Int"` byte; a `Constraint` can't cover list items or arguments and would misreport `E_CONSTRAINT`. The new-tag-fails-closed argument matches the `ListRange` precedent (LB, 3000.007 §5.4), and `is_int()`/`unbounded()`/optional/list composition is the right shape.
- **`E_INPUT`, not `E_CONSTRAINT`, for out-of-range values** (§1.4). Sound: it matches text's maximum, and unlike caps/uniques there is no hidden-row collision to mask, so `E_INPUT` leaks nothing.
- **`/` and `%` floor** (§2.2's parenthetical). Verified: `/` lowers to `Divmod` ([expr.rs:497-503](…)) which uses `div_euclid`/`rem_euclid` (runner.rs:1134). So `month(t)/12+1970 = year(t)` really does hold before 1970. The ordinal choice (§2.2) is right for the same reason `day(t)` is: `202612 + 1` is not January, `month(now) - 1` is last month. 681 = 2026-10 checks out.
- **Zero-period fails closed today** (§2.4's load claim). Verified: `engine/mod.rs:594` refuses `every_ms == 0` at admission, so an old engine refuses a calendar schedule rather than reaching `Schedule::boundary`'s `div_euclid(0)` panic. Devices don't run schedules.
- **(C) current-behaviour description** (§3.1). Verified accurate: `parse_cap`'s empty-`by` arm (parser.rs:529-… with the §20.3 `@ref`), the engine's empty-group count, the adoption constraint walk (store/successor.rs:138), device whole-group prediction ("only full public sync covers it", write_target.rs:153-157; prediction guide), and the broken refusal text (`"…per group by "` with an empty join, write.rs:567).
- **Refusing days 29–31** (§2.4) and **deferring `first n` over bounded args** (§1.7). Both right; clamping would reintroduce exactly the drift this feature removes.
- **Branded-TS refusal** (§1.5) and **Lean-out-of-fragment for int bounds** (§1.8). Both correctly reasoned; the `Bounds:` extension point exists (emit.rs:76-78).

## Findings

**1. High — §1.3/§1.4: the design omits the one enforcement site that matters, `fits()`.** `validate.rs:fits` has no `IntRange` arm: `(Int|Time|…, Int)` matches, `(IntRange, Int)` falls to `_ => false`. Two consequences: a top-level bounded column would refuse *every* int including in-range ones, and the RFC's own headline example `scores: [int 0..100] <=8` refuses *all* writes, because list-item recursion goes through the same `fits`. `is_int()` (ordering/keys/budgets) does not cover this. *Fix:* specify the arm `(IntRange{min,max}, Int(v)) => i53 ∋ v ∧ min ≤ v ≤ max` in `fits` (which then covers args via `arg_as_column`, list items, upserts, defaults-filled images, fixtures, restores — all `validate_row` callers), plus a regression with `[50]` accepted and `[101]` refused `E_INPUT`.

**2. High — §1.2/§1.6: `int <=N` bakes a cross-type contradiction into the end-game language.** Text's `<=N` means `0..N`; the RFC's `int <=N` means `-∞..N`. Concrete: an author copying `note: text <=200` writes `delta: int <=200` expecting 0..200 and silently stores `-5000`. A one-line guide sentence does not fix a permanent spelling trap, and it violates the RFC's own one-spelling house rule (§1.2). *Fix:* drop `int <=N`; spell at-most as `int ..N`, with `<=N` refused by the existing targeted diagnostic (same treatment as `>=`/`<`/`>`).

**3. High — §1.3/§1.4: silent on bounded maintain/`sum` targets.** §1.3 exempts sequences, delivery positions and calendar keys from bounds but never says whether a bounded column may be a maintain target or `sum` destination. Concrete: `total: int 0..` as a `sum` target exceeds its bound through ordinary engine writes; `validate_maintained_cache` → `validate_image` would then refuse engine-derived data with caller-blaming `E_INPUT` and break activation. *Fix:* binder-refuse bounded columns as maintain/`sum` targets (one line in the maintain binding, mirroring the sequence rule), or specify exempt-and-saturate semantics — the former.

**4. High — §2.2 + §6: deferring time zones contradicts the end-game billing story.** The RFC's own Tokyo example proves a fixed offset cannot express a JST month start (15:00 UTC, previous day). Monthly billing is a local-time concept; UTC-month invoice keys misattribute ~9h of JST transactions every month. Shipping §6's billing recipe on UTC months while "waiting for a time-zone design" bakes keys a later zone design must migrate. *Fix:* either design zones now, or fence the whole feature's scope to UTC explicitly — builtins, keys, schedules, and the recipe retitled "UTC monthly billing", with local-time billing marked as requiring zones.

**5. Medium — §2.4/§6: the billing recipe is missing its two load-bearing details.** (a) Lookback bound: "bills every unbilled month up to the period's month" from subscription start is an unbounded sweep that will cross the examined/step ceilings for old subscriptions after a long outage. (b) Idempotent write: retry-after-mid-sweep double-bills unless the invoice write is `insert … if absent`/upsert on the `(subscription, month)` unique key — bare `insert` turns a retry into a stuck `E_CONFLICT` loop, bare re-insert into a double bill. *Fix:* name the idempotent write and a bounded lookback (or chunked cursoring), and add the outage test the plan lacks: two-month outage bills both months exactly once; crash mid-sweep then retry bills nothing twice.

**6. Medium — §2.2: two-implementation risk needs pinned vectors, not just prose.** Rust civil code and Lean civil code will both be written "from the documents". Prose diverges on exactly the edges that matter: 1969-12-01T00:00Z (month −1 boundary), leap days including 2000-02-29 vs 1900 (no leap day — proleptic Gregorian must be stated), year 0/negative years, and `monthStart`/`yearStart` at the i53 time edges (±2^53−1 ms ≈ ±285,000 years; ordinals fit easily, the *results* don't). *Fix:* hardcode a shared vector table (engine tests + Lean examples + oracle seeds) covering those points, including `monthStart(month(t)) ≤ t` for t < 0.

**7. Medium — §2.4: the `every N months` anchor is unstated.** Divisible-ordinal anchoring means `every 2 months` fires Jan/Mar/May… (ordinal 0 = 1970-01), regardless of when the schedule is created. A builder creating a bimonthly schedule in June gets July, not August. This mirrors fixed periods, so it's defensible — but the anchor and the next-boundary rule must be in the spec, or server/dev-clock/next-boundary displays will be re-debated. *Fix:* one sentence: epoch-anchored, fires months with `ordinal % N == 0`.

**8. Medium — §4: the three capability names do not exist.** `capabilities.rs` has no `int-range`, `calendar-month`, or `calendar-schedule` entries. Old binaries do fail closed via unknown serde tags / the `every_ms == 0` admission check — but with un-named decode errors, not the promised `E_SCHEMA_CAPABILITY`, and §4 presents the table as settled rather than to-be-added. *Fix:* mark the table as work (§5 tests must pin it), and add a fence test: old binary opens an `IntRange` schema and a monthly schedule → named refusal, never a panic or silent accept; older device refuses a program with the new `Builtin` tags whole.

**9. Medium — §3.2: four whole-table-cap behaviours are missing.** (a) *Index prerequisite:* empty-`by` caps still require a plain index (schema.rs:1032, write.rs:519 — `starts_with([])` needs *some* plain index). A lookup table with only `byId` may not be able to declare `cap N` at all; the RFC never mentions it. (b) *Private tables:* capped private inserts never predict ("a private viewer partition cannot count hidden rows") and server enforcement masks as `E_RULE` with hidden occupants — §3.2's Devices paragraph covers only the public case. (c) *Expiring tables:* device writes queue on server physical occupancy (write.rs:517) — unstated. (d) *Raising a cap* cannot fail yet classifies Narrowing and forces a full count (successor.rs:159 classifies every constraint change Narrowing); it should be Additive. *Fix:* document (a)–(c) in one paragraph each; reclassify pure cap-raises.

**10. Medium — §5: five additions to an otherwise good plan.** The unit/parity/oracle structure is right, but it misses: (a) the §8 fence tests; (b) the §5 billing end-to-end; (c) negative controls for the new oracles (plant a month-off-by-one and a bound-off-by-one; both oracles must catch them, as slice A did); (d) directed calendar vectors — seeds 0–2,999 will likely never draw 1900-02, 1969-12, or i53-edge times; (e) IntRange-in-`sum`/sequence/maintain-target refusal tests and a device-queued-write-tightens-bound adoption test. *Fix:* add all five; (d) is covered by the §6 vector table.

**11. Low — §1.5/§1.6, two small corrections.** (a) The `Rows` JSDoc claim is unverified (I confirmed the `Bounds:` args clause; I did not confirm `Rows` emission) — verify before landing. (b) List-item bound evolution is unmentioned: `[int]<=8` → `[int 1..5]<=8` correctly goes Narrowing via the item-inequality in `column_change`'s `list()` helper, but so does the widening direction (safe, wasteful — forces a walk); state that this over-classification is accepted. Also note `column_change` currently maps all `Int↔IntRange` changes to Destructive (successor.rs:183-208 has no arm) — §1.6's table is the fix spec, fine, but it must name that function.

## Verdict: NOT READY

The ordinal design, the `IntRange`-as-type choice, `E_INPUT`, the (C) behaviour account, and the refusal of 29–31/clamping are all sound and should survive revision. But findings 1–4 block: as written, the headline list example refuses all valid writes (missing `fits` arm), one spelling permanently means two things (`<=N`), bounded `sum` targets are unspecified, and UTC-month billing ships while the time-zone design it needs is deferred. Fix 1–4, answer with the vectors (6), fences (8), recipe details (5), anchor (7), and cap paragraphs (9), extend the plan (10), and it will be ready for a second round.

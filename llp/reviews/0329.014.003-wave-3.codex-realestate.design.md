### 1. Product contract

The product is a public real-estate catalog for anonymous visitors and registered shoppers, with operating surfaces for agents, brokerage administrators, feed operators, data stewards, and privacy staff.

1. Public users can search millions of sale and rental listings by map geometry, price, beds, baths, property type, status, open-house window, estimated value, and declared facets.

2. Search cards, photos, map pins, facets, counts, and ranks contain only public information; approximate counts are labeled and never determine alert eligibility.

3. Search pagination is stable against a catalog snapshot, so concurrent listing changes do not duplicate or skip results between pages.

4. Physical properties, market listing episodes, and MLS source records have separate identities: one property can be relisted, and several feeds can describe one active episode.

5. Deduplication produces one public card for a mirrored listing while preserving stable URLs, source provenance, favorites, prior alerts, and reversible merge/split history.

6. Listing pages show immutable price, status, photo, and open-house history, distinguishing when a fact allegedly took effect from when the product learned or corrected it.

7. Estimated values are separate from list and sale prices and show valuation date, model generation, range, confidence, and a “not an appraisal” qualification.

8. A saved search is a durable server-side standing query that remains active while every user device is offline.

9. Saving or editing a search creates an immutable query generation and baselines existing matches at a catalog cut; existing inventory does not create alerts unless the user explicitly requests a baseline digest.

10. A “new match” occurs when the prior public state did not match the active query generation and the new state does, including publication, back-on-market, facet changes, estimate changes, or an open house entering a relative time window.

11. A matching listing’s list-price change creates a price alert according to the saved policy—any change, drop-only, and an optional per-transition amount or percentage threshold—while estimate changes are never labeled price changes.

12. One catalog transition creates at most one user notification per listing and reason family, even when several of that user’s searches match; the alert identifies every contributing search.

13. The in-product inbox is authoritative and deduplicated; email, push, and CRM delivery are retryable external effects and may be delayed, with provider idempotency used where available.

14. Under ordinary load, accepted catalog changes become searchable within two minutes at p99 and qualifying inbox alerts appear within five minutes at p99; backlog is displayed and delayed rather than silently dropped.

15. Agent edits, representation changes, and open-house changes require current, listing-scoped authority and use expected versions so concurrent work never overwrites silently.

16. A contact-agent form names the intended brokerage and agent before consent, binds consent to that routing snapshot, encrypts its PII, and does not silently transfer old leads when representation changes.

17. Favorites and comparison sets are private and available offline: queued personal changes synchronize later, cached listing data shows its freshness, and online-only actions are never presented as committed while offline.

18. Multi-device saved-search edits use whole-generation conflict detection; concurrent favorite operations merge causally, while capacity-sensitive comparison edits return explicit conflicts.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **A standing-query match is a temporal transition, not a current boolean.**

   - **Wrong version:** Store query JSON, periodically rerun it over current listings, and diff the latest result IDs.
   - **Why wrong:** It races activation and edits, misses intermediate changes, duplicates replays, and cannot detect predicates whose truth changes only because time advances.
   - **Exposing observation:** A listing moves from $600,000 to $550,000 and back to $600,000 between polls while the user edits their maximum from $575,000 to $525,000; only the query generation active at each catalog transition may govern, and replay must not duplicate anything.

2. **Property, offering, and feed-record identity are different.**

   - **Wrong version:** Deduplicate by normalized address or MLS number and update one lifetime listing row.
   - **Why wrong:** Condo units share addresses, identifiers can be reused, relistings are new market episodes, and syndicated feeds mirror the same episode.
   - **Exposing observation:** Two feeds for unit 4A should converge, unit 4B must remain separate, and splitting an erroneous merge after users have favorited or contacted 4A must not move those records to 4B.

3. **Listing history has both business time and knowledge time.**

   - **Wrong version:** Overwrite current fields and order history solely by a source’s `modified_at`.
   - **Why wrong:** Late and backdated corrections make the product claim it knew facts earlier, can roll current state backward, and can manufacture price alerts.
   - **Exposing observation:** On June 10 a feed corrects June 1’s price from $800,000 to $780,000 while the current June 8 price is $760,000; history changes, but current public truth and alerts do not.

4. **Current professional role is neither historical authority nor consent.**

   - **Wrong version:** Let the current listing agent see every historical lead, or let any former recipient retain access forever.
   - **Why wrong:** Reassignment exposes consumers to strangers, departed agents retain brokerage PII, and even counts or different denials reveal lead existence.
   - **Exposing observation:** A lead is submitted at 11:59, the agent leaves at noon, and the listing transfers at 12:01; neither the departed agent nor the new brokerage may gain post-transfer access merely from those role facts.

5. **Feed absence is evidence only after a fenced complete batch.**

   - **Wrong version:** Upsert rows as they arrive and withdraw every previously known record absent from the processed prefix.
   - **Why wrong:** A truncated snapshot or overlapping newer delta can remove millions of valid listings and later create a false back-on-market alert storm.
   - **Exposing observation:** A manifest promises one million rows but only 700,000 arrive; no absent listing may be withdrawn, and a newer delta must defeat later reconciliation from the older snapshot.

6. **Offline “last write wins” has no trustworthy last.**

   - **Wrong version:** Resolve every device conflict using device timestamps or field-wise merge.
   - **Why wrong:** Clocks drift, a stale search edit can combine incompatible predicate and delivery fields, and a favorite removal can erase an unseen concurrent add.
   - **Exposing observation:** An offline phone removes a favorite while a tablet concurrently adds it, two devices add a seventh comparison item, and two devices edit one search as a listing publishes; every object needs its declared merge or conflict rule.

### 5. The hard operations

**1. Search and read the public catalog**

- **Inputs:** Geometry, facets, sort, page size, and a signed cursor containing filter hash and index generation.
- **Authorization:** Public projection only; protected location, private remarks, embargoed records, and removed listings are excluded before counts and ranking.
- **Steps:** (1) Normalize the predicate; (2) pin an index generation; (3) retrieve spatial/facet candidates; (4) resolve aliases and deduplicate canonical episodes; (5) apply a strongly consistent visibility-denial set and exact public predicate; (6) hydrate cards or immutable history; (7) return an opaque continuation cursor.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** The read uses one retained index generation, while indexing, photos, counts, and estimates may lag within their SLA; the UI may retain old pins as “updating” but invents no results, and an expired cursor requires refresh rather than silently switching generations.

**2. Create, edit, pause, or delete a saved search**

- **Inputs:** Normalized predicate, alert policy, channels, time zone, client operation ID, and expected current revision.
- **Authorization:** Authenticated owner with verified destinations and only supported public predicates.
- **Steps:** (1) Deduplicate the client operation and check the expected revision; (2) compile conservative routing tokens; (3) append a preparing revision; (4) install routes; (5) establish per-partition barrier offsets across old and new geography; (6) baseline inventory at those barriers without alerts; (7) activate the new revision and retire the old one at the cut; (8) replay every later change.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Revision/idempotency/outbox creation and final activation are atomic state transitions, while route installation is recoverable and eventual; the UI shows “alerts activating,” and stale edits return the current complete revision rather than field-merging.

**3. Evaluate catalog or clock transitions against standing queries**

- **Inputs:** An immutable catalog change with old/new snapshots and partition offsets, or a predicate-boundary event with before/after evaluation instants.
- **Authorization:** System evaluator only.
- **Steps:** (1) Form routing tokens from both old and new facts; (2) retrieve a provable superset of candidate revisions; (3) select the revision active at the event offset; (4) evaluate its exact predicate on both states; (5) classify false→true as `NEW_MATCH`, true→true list-price change as `PRICE_CHANGED`, and all other transitions as non-notifying; (6) attach a price delta to one new-match alert when both occur; (7) atomically insert the evaluation receipt, transition, coalesced notification intent, and inbox item.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Each `(revision,event)` is atomic and unique, but million-query fan-out is intentionally asynchronous; no optimistic UI exists, retries are idempotent, and lag never authorizes candidate caps or sampling.

**4. Dispatch immediate alerts and scheduled digests**

- **Inputs:** Due intents, current channel preferences, quiet hours, cadence, and delivery-attempt identity.
- **Authorization:** System dispatcher may reveal only fields needed by the selected destination.
- **Steps:** (1) Claim intents with a fenced lease; (2) suppress external delivery if the contributing search was retired before dispatch or the channel is disabled; (3) deterministically group overlapping searches and repeated listing changes; (4) render the immutable triggering facts plus a current-state warning; (5) persist an attempt with a provider idempotency key; (6) call the provider; (7) record acknowledgment, retry, or permanent failure.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Leasing and attempt recording are atomic, but the provider call is not; the inbox can show delivery status, and competing workers or ambiguous timeouts cannot duplicate the logical alert even if a non-idempotent provider duplicates an external message.

**5. Stage and promote an MLS bulk import**

- **Inputs:** Authenticated source, signed manifest, batch ID, snapshot/delta mode, source watermark, object reference, declared count, checksum, and terminal marker.
- **Authorization:** Feed credential scoped to that source namespace.
- **Steps:** (1) Create the idempotent batch; (2) stream immutable rows into staging; (3) validate bytes, schema, count, checksum, and watermark; (4) for each valid row, serialize on its source key, reject stale source order, append a source version, resolve or propose a source binding, reconcile field provenance, and publish a public version only when semantic state changes; (5) checkpoint every shard; (6) after a complete full snapshot, reconcile absent records only after the source’s grace rule and only if no newer delta observed them; (7) quarantine ambiguous dedup and invalid rows.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Each source-row promotion and resulting canonical change is atomic, never the whole batch; media, geocoding, indexing, and alerts are eventual, operators see exact progress, and generation fencing makes newer deltas defeat stale snapshot reconciliation.

**6. Merge or split canonical listing identities**

- **Inputs:** Candidate roots, evidence, expected identity-graph revision, source bindings to move, and proposed survivor or split allocation.
- **Authorization:** A trusted deterministic rule for strong evidence or a data steward; brokers cannot merge competitors’ records unilaterally.
- **Steps:** (1) Lock affected roots in stable order; (2) revalidate unit, property, offering type, temporal overlap, and source evidence; (3) append a reversible identity decision; (4) update time-bounded bindings and aliases without deleting versions; (5) create corrected public snapshots; (6) mark identity-only changes non-alerting; (7) preserve lead routing snapshots and resolve favorites by source affinity, leaving ambiguous favorites unresolved; (8) enqueue redirects and reindexing.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** The bounded identity graph, aliases, decision, and new catalog changes are atomic, while bulk index cleanup is eventual; public pages redirect or show a correction notice, and a changed graph revision forces recomputation.

**7. Publish, amend, or correct a listing**

- **Inputs:** Listing ID, authorized field patch or source assertion, expected public version, effective time, correction target/reason, media IDs, and idempotency key.
- **Authorization:** Current listing-scoped permission for agent-owned fields or the authenticated source for source-owned fields; backdated compliance correction requires elevated review.
- **Steps:** (1) Resolve aliases; (2) recheck authority and expected version in the write transaction; (3) validate price, status transition, address policy, media rights, and field ownership; (4) append the assertion and provenance; (5) recompute effective history and current canonical state; (6) if current public facts changed, append a public snapshot, catalog change, and index/evaluator outbox; otherwise append only the correction audit.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Assertion, current pointer, history fact, catalog change, and outbox are atomic; search and media publication are eventual, the agent sees a pending draft, and conflicting edits return a field diff with no automatic merge of price, status, or provenance.

**8. Grant, expire, or revoke professional authority**

- **Inputs:** Subject, brokerage, listing scope, permissions, effective interval, license evidence, expected roster revision, and reason.
- **Authorization:** Brokerage owner or compliance administrator who cannot grant beyond their own scope or self-grant owner/privacy power.
- **Steps:** (1) Verify license and brokerage evidence; (2) validate delegator authority; (3) append grant or revocation events; (4) rebuild the affected term; (5) increment the authorization epoch; (6) fence listing writes, lead capabilities, exports, and calendar jobs; (7) schedule expiry and directory updates.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Authority event, term, audit, and epoch change atomically and take effect immediately, while external verification and public directories lag; the UI shows pending verification, and stale roster revisions fail explicitly.

**9. Create, reschedule, or cancel an open house**

- **Inputs:** Listing and schedule versions, local start/end, IANA zone, explicit offset for an ambiguous civil time, host, venue, and operation ID.
- **Authorization:** Current `manage_open_house` authority for the listing and a valid host.
- **Steps:** (1) Verify current listing eligibility and authority; (2) reject nonexistent daylight-saving times and require a choice for repeated times; (3) enforce no overlapping active event for the same listing and venue; (4) append a schedule version, with reschedule represented as successor rather than overwrite; (5) create a new public listing snapshot and catalog change; (6) install generation-fenced boundary events for relative-time predicates; (7) enqueue external calendar synchronization.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Schedule version, exclusion check, catalog change, boundaries, and outbox are atomic; search, alerts, and calendars are eventual, the UI says “pending sync,” and expected versions ensure cancellation versus rescheduling has one winner.

**10. Submit a contact-agent lead**

- **Inputs:** Listing snapshot, short-lived signed recipient-disclosure token, contact fields, message, consent version, separate marketing choice, abuse token, and idempotency key.
- **Authorization:** Public submission for a currently contactable listing after rate and abuse checks.
- **Steps:** (1) Resolve the listing and recompute active recipients; (2) if they differ from the disclosure token, return the new disclosure for reconfirmation without creating a lead; (3) validate and abuse-scan input; (4) encrypt PII with a per-lead key; (5) atomically create the protected lead, consent, brokerage/agent recipient snapshot, initial assignment, audit event, idempotency record, and delivery outboxes; (6) return a non-PII receipt.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** All internal facts and delivery intents are atomic, but inbox, email, and CRM delivery are eventual; submission is not optimistic, and a concurrent representation change either invalidates consent before commit or occurs afterward without widening recipients.

**11. View, assign, transfer, count, or export leads**

- **Inputs:** Lead or authorized lead query, actor, purpose, requested fields/action, and expected assignment revision.
- **Authorization:** Current brokerage authority plus an active lead assignment or privacy scope; a new listing agent does not inherit historical leads.
- **Steps:** (1) Perform a uniform existence-and-authorization check; (2) verify recipient brokerage, assignment interval, field grant, retention state, purpose, and authorization epoch; (3) for viewing, append disclosure audit and issue a one-use field-scoped decryption capability; (4) for same-brokerage reassignment, append a new assignment interval; (5) require renewed authority or consent for cross-brokerage transfer; (6) create a field-minimized, idempotent CRM outbox for export.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Authorization, disclosure audit, capability, assignment, and export intent are atomic as applicable, while CRM delivery is eventual; nothing is optimistic, and revocation serializes through the authorization epoch and invalidates outstanding capabilities.

**12. Synchronize favorites and comparisons**

- **Inputs:** Device ID and sequence, causal frontier, operation ID, listing ID, observed favorite-add tags, collection base revision, comparison position, and baseline snapshot.
- **Authorization:** Collection owner only.
- **Steps:** (1) Deduplicate the device operation; (2) resolve listing aliases without crossing an ambiguous split; (3) represent favorite add as a unique tag and remove as tombstones for observed tags; (4) derive membership from surviving tags; (5) apply comparison additions, removals, and moves using collection compare-and-swap; (6) enforce a six-item comparison limit; (7) retain withdrawn items as unavailable rather than silently deleting them; (8) return canonical state and conflicts.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Each personal operation and projection update is atomic, device propagation is eventual, the originating device updates optimistically, concurrent favorite add wins over an unobserving remove, and stale capacity-sensitive comparison edits are rejected individually.

**13. Compute and publish estimated values in bulk**

- **Inputs:** Approved model version, immutable feature snapshots, valuation date, region, and expected published generation.
- **Authorization:** Valuation pipeline service, with model approval separate from execution authority.
- **Steps:** (1) Create a staged run with full lineage; (2) batch-score properties into immutable estimate rows; (3) record exclusion reasons; (4) validate coverage, drift, range, and suppression rules; (5) complete the declared regional manifest; (6) atomically switch the region’s published-generation pointer and append an ordered batch catalog marker; (7) deterministically expand changes for public listings before standing-query consumers pass the marker; (8) retain the prior generation for rollback.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** A regional pointer and batch marker are atomic, while scoring, indexing, and standing-query expansion are bulk background work; no optimistic value is shown, and generation compare-and-swap prevents an older run from replacing a newer one.

**14. Expire and destroy lead PII**

- **Inputs:** Leads past `retention_until`, legal-hold state, privacy-policy version, and worker checkpoint.
- **Authorization:** Fenced privacy worker or recorded compliance action.
- **Steps:** (1) Select due leads without exposing them outside the protected service; (2) recheck legal holds and active delivery work; (3) serialize against disclosure capability issuance; (4) revoke the per-lead key, mark PII destroyed, invalidate capabilities, and append a nonreconstructive destruction record; (5) cancel internal PII-bearing outboxes; (6) enqueue deletion requests to integrated external recipients; (7) retain only pseudonymous operational and audit facts.
- **Atomic/eventual/UI/conflict:** Key revocation, lead state, capability invalidation, and internal cancellation are atomic, while external deletion acknowledgments are eventual; agents see a protected tombstone, and whichever of disclosure or destruction serializes first determines whether that final disclosure occurred.

### 6. Scale and cost

These are design-load targets for a successful North American deployment, not claims about an existing market.

| Dimension | Design target |
|---|---:|
| Physical properties/units | 150 million |
| Retained listing episodes | 100 million |
| Concurrently public/searchable listings | 8 million |
| Raw source versions | 3–8 billion |
| Public listing/search snapshots | 1–3 billion |
| Current photo references | 300–700 million |
| Registered users / daily active users | 40 million / 5 million |
| Saved searches | 60 million; 35 million alert-enabled |
| Logical current search–listing matches | 5–10 billion |
| Live favorites | 0.5–1 billion |
| Agents / brokerages | 2.5 million / 250,000 |
| Leads | 200,000–500,000/day; 100–300 million retained |
| Future / historical open houses | 500,000 / 50–100 million |
| Values per estimate generation | Approximately 150 million |

Public search may average 20,000–30,000 queries/second and peak near 200,000. Feeds may deliver 20–100 million snapshot rows daily, with 20,000–50,000 rows/second during catch-up. Canonical public changes may reach 1–3 million daily.

A listing change may route to hundreds of searches at median, around 10,000 at p95, 100,000 at p99, and 1–3 million in an extreme metro or broad-price case. That implies roughly 1–5 billion exact predicate evaluations and 20–80 million logical alert facts per day, reduced to perhaps 5–15 million external messages by coalescing and digests.

The dominant costs are reverse-query fan-out, geo/facet indexing, billions of logical match relationships, dedup recanonicalization, immutable history, photo storage and delivery, estimate-generation activation, PII audits/exports, and source-backlog recovery. None may control cost by silently truncating candidate searches or sampling alerts.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Durable reverse standing queries.** The backend must index predicates as data, establish no-gap activation cuts, route each old/new catalog transition to a complete candidate superset, execute the exact public-search predicate, synthesize clock boundaries, and persist idempotent high-fan-out results. A legitimate concession is a fixed predicate grammar, declared latency, and digests for broad searches; it becomes a cheat when broad queries are capped, sampled, evaluated with different semantics, or reduced to polling current rows.

2. **Massive public geo/facet search with strong visibility revocation.** Search needs spatial and faceted indexing, retained snapshot cursors, exact post-filtering, alias resolution, and immediate suppression of newly protected records despite index lag. Approximate labeled counts and minute-scale freshness are legitimate; it is a cheat if approximate results decide alerts or hidden inventory leaks during reindexing.

3. **Bitemporal, reversible canonicalization over bulk sources.** The backend must retain raw source order, business time, knowledge time, field provenance, complete-snapshot barriers, reversible merges/splits, and stable identity lineage while publishing incremental public state. Manual quarantine and hours-long handling of ambiguous records are legitimate; destructive merging, arrival-order overwrite, or absence processing from an incomplete snapshot is a cheat.

4. **Temporal authority and protected derived outputs.** Authorization must govern writes, counts, ranks, searches, exports, decryptions, and external disclosures using current resource-scoped intervals while retaining historical attribution. Routing every lead solely to the disclosed brokerage is a legitimate simplification; granting historical PII through a current role or returning plausible visibility-filtered counts is a cheat.

5. **Ordered database facts plus non-atomic external and bulk effects.** Listing changes, history, evaluator work, inbox facts, and outboxes must share a durable commit boundary, while email, CRM, search indexing, valuation expansion, and deletion acknowledgments remain retryable. An authoritative inbox with at-least-once external attempts and region-sized valuation activation is legitimate; claiming external exactly-once delivery, notifying before the public revision is readable, or advancing past an unexpanded batch marker is a cheat.

### 8. Confidence

The source/canonical identity split, immutable versions, bitemporal history, staged snapshot imports, transactional outboxes, geospatial search indices, time-bounded authority, encrypted PII, disclosure audits, retention controls, versioned model outputs, and optimistic concurrency are standard practice.

The exact scale targets, five-minute alert objective, query-generation cutover semantics, clock-boundary mechanism, per-transition price threshold, coalescing identity, add-wins favorites, six-item comparison limit, brokerage-versus-agent lead policy, and estimate-driven new-match behavior are deliberate product choices for this exercise. They require product validation, feed-contract review, and jurisdiction-specific privacy and real-estate counsel before production use.

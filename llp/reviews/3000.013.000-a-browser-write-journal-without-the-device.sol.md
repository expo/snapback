## Verbatim review

1. **MATERIAL — §4.1–4.2: capture keys allow old-session cleanup to delete a new session’s click.**  
   Checked `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:269`, `snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/app.ts:383`, and `admission-authority.ts:43`. Server IDs are scoped by principal, so two accounts may legitimately use the same ID. Tab A captures `(url, id)` under account A; account B replaces the session and captures the same ID, overwriting that key. A’s locked authority check then fails and removes or cancels **B’s record**. If B navigates before its IndexedDB admission, its click is lost. Include principal, authority source, and a capture incarnation in record identity; cleanup must target the exact captured record.

2. **MATERIAL — §4.4: a different authority source does not prove that the capturing session ended.**  
   Checked `auth.ts:102`, `session-authority.ts:85`, and `admission-authority.ts:34`. Custom mirror adapters with distinct authority namespaces are supported. A record captured under namespace A can remain authorized there after its runtime closes. A scope using namespace B scans the same backend records; `sameAdmissionAuthority` returns false because the sources differ, and the proposed recovery cancels A’s valid click. Partition recovery by authority source. An unsupported or foreign source must remain untouched, rather than being classified as an ended session.

3. **MATERIAL — §4.2 and §5: marker collection trusts a localStorage absence that may be stale.**  
   Checked `session-authority.ts:50`, `app.ts:415`, and the governing LLP 3000.013 §13.3. The design explicitly acknowledges that Web Locks do not make Chromium’s renderer-local storage view authoritative. A captures K and commits P/M; removal fails. Another tab with a stale view sees `getItem(K) === null` and collects M. After settlement and receipt eviction, that tab reloads, sees the surviving K, and admits it again. A Web Lock cannot establish the required absence proof. Retain markers until cleanup has trustworthy evidence tied to that capture; a peer’s `getItem() === null` alone is insufficient.

4. **MATERIAL — §4.2 and §5: the reused live admission body does not check transfer markers.**  
   Checked `app-online.ts:187`, `app-online.ts:195`, and `app-journal.ts:44`. The existing body checks pending intents, receipts, and retired IDs, then pushes pending; it has no marker branch. Let K survive failed removal, settle its intent, and evict its ordinary receipt. A fresh runtime retries that ID before recovery removes K. Even with M retained, the specified live admission body pushes another pending entry. Server deduplication prevents another execution, but client admission is repeated. Both live admission and recovery must consult M, including when only the marker remains, and define the resulting lookup/refusal behavior.

5. **MATERIAL — §4.4–4.5: the recovery filter excludes this runtime’s failed captures.**  
   Checked `app-online.ts:200`, `app-online.ts:310`, and `writes.ts:71`. After an admission commit fails, K remains, but its runtime continues holding the lifetime lock. Recovery requires the record’s runtime to be **not live**, so the promised recovery before this runtime’s next admission skips K indefinitely. A same-ID retry also follows `MutationIds`’ recovery branch, rather than a fresh admission, and can return `E_WRITE_RECEIPT_EXPIRED` when no durable intent exists (`writes.ts:78–82`). Admit this runtime’s records when they are no longer in flight, while retaining the liveness exclusion for other runtimes; invoke that recovery before same-ID lookup as well.

6. **MATERIAL — §2 G2/G6, §4.4, and §5: retirement saturation explicitly drops a captured click.**  
   Checked `app-journal.ts:63`, `app-journal.ts:70`, and `app-online.ts:195`. Recovery says “retirement ids are saturated: cancel, report.” A click can be captured, lose its page before admission, then encounter saturation on recovery. Its session remains valid, but recovery cancels it without admission or a live caller receiving refusal. This contradicts G2’s sole cancellation exception and G6’s stated custody rule. Keep the record as visibly blocked custody, or specify a durable, queryable recovery refusal and revise the guarantees accordingly. A diagnostic report alone does not satisfy the present contract.

7. **MATERIAL — §4.4: recovery starts too late for the claimed startup ordering.**  
   Checked `app.ts:266`, `app.ts:677`, `app-online.ts:44`, and `app-storage.ts:35`. `publish("signed-in")` invokes listeners synchronously **before** `custody.start()`. A listener can submit a new click and queue its journal transaction first. The subsequently queued recovery lock therefore cannot make that admission wait; the new pending entry can precede older captured intent. The additional dispatch barrier could protect transport order, but the RFC does not establish an admission barrier before publication. Install recovery gating during custody construction, before exposing the signed-in client, while starting transport only after session commit.

8. **MATERIAL — §5: a backup that loses server receipts is not necessarily detected as `E_STORE_RESTORE`.**  
   Checked `client.ts:220`, `client.ts:470`, `app-journal.ts:9`, and `snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:1094`. Restore detection compares the server head against this runtime’s in-memory floor; the online journal persists no head floor. Execute an ID, retain uncertain client custody, close the runtime, and restore an older backup with the same store ID but without its receipt. A reopened client starts with floor zero, accepts the restored store, and resends. The server finds no receipt and executes again. Require a changed store incarnation on restore, add sufficient durable restore fencing, or explicitly exclude undetected same-identity restores from G3. The existing refusal does not cover this interleaving.

9. **MINOR — §2 G3 versus §5: journal damage already invalidates the unconditional client admission guarantee.**  
   Checked `app-journal.ts:109`. Preserving a damaged journal and replacing it with an empty one discards its markers. A surviving K can consequently be admitted again, exactly as §5 acknowledges. State the intact-journal assumption in G3 itself. Server deduplication can preserve execution safety while the local “admitted at most once” claim no longer holds.

What I found sound:

- The proposed journal → session lock order has no reverse acquisition in the existing paths checked. `onlineCustody` receives the raw storage wrapper (`app.ts:610`), avoiding re-entry through scoped persistence.
- A fresh IndexedDB authority check held through admission commit correctly fences logout and account replacement; same-epoch credential rotation remains admissible.
- Server store validation precedes receipt lookup (`serve.rs:1183`), and mutation effects and receipts commit atomically (`engine/mod.rs:1214`). Replays omit historical values (`engine/receipt.rs:30`).
- Schema-based selection and explicit `writes: "online"` are compatible with server execution. A later sync-schema change does not inherently compromise write custody; malformed or unavailable schema responses must remain an unknown decision.
- Synchronous capture before the first await, with synchronous `E_ADMISSION` refusal on capture failure, is the right mechanism for the stated navigation guarantee.

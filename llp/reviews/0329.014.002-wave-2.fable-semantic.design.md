# Design: "Marginalia" — semantic notes for people and small teams

Archetype: semantic notes / recommendations. Axis under test: reads over indexes that are not range-decomposable (vector kNN, ranked full-text), approximate and implementation-dependent results, server-only computation, and what an offline client can honestly show.

Design stance: the traps come first. This archetype's analogue of active-versus-historical membership is that **every search surface is a lagging, approximate, visibility-scoped view of content that has moved on**, and a naive app presents that view as the content itself.

### 1. Product contract

Marginalia is a notes app for individuals and teams of 5–200 people. A note is a titled sequence of paragraphs ("blocks") owned by one person. Notes can be shared with named people, with a whole team, or by link. The app's reason to exist is that it *finds* notes for you: keyword search, "related notes," suggestions while you type, daily resurfacing, and answers to questions over what you can read.

Users: note authors (everyone), team admins (membership, AI policy, spend), and the system's background workers (indexing, digests, alerts).

Promises, user-visible:

- **P1. Your notes are yours.** Only you can read a note until you share it. Nobody — teammate, admin, or the app's own suggestions — learns a note exists until you share it.
- **P2. Sharing is explicit and reversible.** Share with a person (viewer/editor), a team (all *current* members), or an expiring link (viewer). Un-sharing takes effect immediately online; a device holding a copy purges it at its next sync, and we say so.
- **P3. Keyword search is complete and current.** Every note you can read that contains your words is findable the instant it is saved. Results are ranked; snippets come from the note as it is now.
- **P4. Related notes on every note.** Up to 10 notes you can read that the system considers similar. "Up to" is literal: similarity is approximate, and we never show a count or a score.
- **P5. Freshness is labeled.** A new or edited note shows "indexing" until semantic features reflect it (seconds, at most a minute or two). A related list built from an earlier version says "based on an earlier version."
- **P6. Suggestions while you write.** The paragraph you are typing pulls in related notes, live. That draft text is sent to our model provider; teams can turn this off.
- **P7. Ask your notes.** Answers cite the exact passages used, with each note's version; you can open each citation. An answer never draws on a note you cannot read.
- **P8. Summaries** of a note or a selection, with the same citations.
- **P9. AI artifacts are shareable only when the recipient can read everything they were built from.** Otherwise the share is refused and tells you which sources block it.
- **P10. AI can be turned off** per team and per note. Such notes never go to the provider; they are excluded from semantic features and the UI says "excluded by policy," never "no results."
- **P11. Spend is capped** per team. Past the cap, answers refuse with a reason; search, related notes, and existing artifacts keep working.
- **P12. Offline reading and editing** of your own notes, pinned notes, and notes opened in the last 30 days (up to 5,000 per device). Edits sync on reconnect.
- **P13. Concurrent edits never lose text.** Different paragraphs merge silently; the same paragraph edited in two places produces a visible conflict holding both versions, which either editor resolves. (No character-level merging: we say so.)
- **P14. Offline search says what it searched:** "searched 1,240 notes on this device." Offline related notes are computed over the device's notes, labeled as such, and may differ from online results.
- **P15. Multi-device:** the same account on phone and laptop sees the other's saved edits within a few seconds when online; no duplicated paragraphs after both sync.
- **P16. Daily resurfacing:** up to five notes you haven't opened in two weeks, related to what you worked on this week.
- **P17. Saved searches** (keyword or semantic) alert on genuinely new matches — a new or newly shared note — never because the index reshuffled.
- **P18. Everything AI-generated shows what it was based on**, and which version of each source.

Users expect: two people can edit one note without overwriting each other; offline, what they cached is there, what they wrote is not lost, and "search" is honest about its scope; across devices, convergence without duplicates.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: [S] secrecy, [T] time, [M] money, [O] other people's data.

1. [S][T][O] A user can read a note only if they own it, hold an unexpired and unrevoked direct grant, or are a *current* member of a team the note is shared with or lives in.
2. [S] Anything derived from a note's content — chunks, vectors, search entries, snippets, related-list entries, summaries, answers, autocomplete terms — is shown only to someone who can read the note at that moment. One stated exception: an AI artifact already delivered to its requester stays theirs.
3. [S] No result reveals a hidden note's existence: not by count, rank gap, score, snippet, refusal text, page shortfall, or timing distinguishable from "no such note."
4. [T] A result is always presented against the note's current version; if it was matched or summarized at an earlier version, it is labeled with that version — never shown with a highlight for text that is no longer there.
5. [T] Vectors compared in one query all come from the same embedding model.
6. [T] The vector recorded as current for a note never comes from an older version than the one already recorded; a slow job for an old version loses.
7. [S][T] A deleted or un-shared note stops contributing to any new result within the stated freshness bound, and nothing new is derived from it.
8. [O] Two devices editing the same note both keep their edits; same-block edits produce a conflict, never silent loss.
9. Semantic results are "up to k"; a paginated result set is a snapshot — page 2 belongs to page 1's snapshot, with no duplicates or gaps.
10. [T] Offline, the client shows only what it computed from what it holds, labeled with scope and version, and demotes server-computed derived content once a local edit invalidates it.
11. [S][M] Content covered by an AI opt-out never leaves the system to a provider — no vector, no summary, no answer, and no query embedding in that context.
12. [S] An answer is generated only over passages the requester can read at request time; a cached answer is reused only when the retrieved evidence (chunks and versions) and model are identical.
13. [S][O] An artifact shared beyond its requester is readable only by people who can read every one of its sources — checked at share time and again at read time.
14. [T][S] A link grants access only while unexpired, unrevoked, and under its use cap; revoking a link revokes every grant created through it.
15. [O] Only the owner widens a note's sharing; team admins and grantees can only narrow it.
16. Background workers read content with system authority but write only derived data; they never change content, grants, or policy.
17. [M] A team's monthly AI spend never exceeds its cap; the cap is enforced by reservation, so two concurrent requests cannot both squeeze under it.
18. Every artifact records the exact (note, version, chunk) set it was built from.
19. [S] Recommendations and alerts never name a note the user cannot currently read, and never fire because approximate results reshuffled.

### 3. The traps, ranked

**Trap 1 — Top-k over a domain wider than the viewer's, filtered afterwards** (visibility, rank, refusal).
*Wrong version:* ask the vector index for the 10 nearest notes, then drop those the caller can't read. Same for ranked keyword search over one global index.
*Why wrong:* a member of a 1,000-note team whose colleague keeps 5,000 private notes on the same topic gets 10 nearest neighbors that are all private, filtered to nothing. The feature is broken for her, and the *shape* of the breakage leaks: keyword search finds plenty of team notes about "quarterly planning," but "related" is empty, which says similar hidden notes exist. "3 of 12 related" leaks more; padding with worse matches lies; caching the unfiltered list puts hidden IDs in a row addressed by her note. Overfetching (k×20, then filter) only moves the cliff.
*Exposing observation:* two users open the same shared note; one sees 10 related notes, the other 0. A user whose visible set is 3% of the corpus gets a different *number* of results per query, correlated with how much hidden content sits near the query.
*Design response:* indexes are partitioned by visibility (personal, team) so the candidate domain *is* the viewer's domain; a small residual filter handles expiry and deletion with a top-up loop when it removes anything; no counts, no scores; small domains are searched exactly.

**Trap 2 — The index is a lagging, versioned view presented as the present** (temporal + concurrency).
*Wrong version:* on save, fire an embedding job that reads "the note" when it runs and writes its vector unconditionally; show related notes as if about the current text; let jobs for versions 7 and 8 race.
*Why wrong:* the v7 job finishes after v8's and becomes the current vector until the next edit — a permanent stale state with no error. A job running after a delete writes a vector for a dead note, which surfaces as a ghost in other notes' related lists. A job queued before a team turns AI off sends content after the policy changed. A related list for a note the user just rewrote describes a note that no longer exists, unlabeled.
*Exposing observation:* edit a note three times within five seconds and watch which vector wins. Delete a note while its job is queued, open a similar note, see the ghost. Turn AI off and read the provider's request log.
*Design response:* jobs carry (note, target_version, model); they read the version snapshot, not "the note"; the write is fenced on version, deletion, and policy inside one transaction; the read side labels "based on version N."

**Trap 3 — Derived artifacts are copies of their sources with a different ACL** (secrecy via derivation).
*Wrong version:* an answer is a row owned by its requester and shareable like a note; the retrieval pipeline runs as a service account over the whole corpus and filters "before sending to the model" (ranking already consumed hidden passages) or after (the model saw them); answers are cached by question text.
*Why wrong:* Alice's answer to "what did we decide about pricing?" quotes her private note; she shares the answer with the team; Carol reads the content of a note she cannot open. Bob asks the same question and receives Alice's cached answer, built on Alice's private evidence.
*Exposing observation:* share an answer with a teammate who lacks one source. Ask the same question from two accounts with different visibility and compare.
*Design response:* retrieval runs in the caller's visibility domain; artifacts carry (note, version, chunk) provenance; sharing checks every recipient against every source; wider-than-requester visibility is re-checked at read time; reuse is keyed by the evidence set, not the question.

**Trap 4 — Two devices compute two different functions** (merge + offline honesty).
*Wrong version:* offline = queue the mutation, cache the query result. Last-writer-wins on the note body. Cached related lists and summaries shown unchanged after an offline edit. On reconnect, device-computed results silently replaced by server results.
*Why wrong:* phone and laptop each append a paragraph offline; one vanishes. The summary describes text the user deleted an hour ago, presented as *the* summary. Offline "related" was exact cosine over 800 cached notes; online it is approximate over 40,000 — different lists, so the user concludes the app is flaky. A device that lost access while offline keeps the note until sync, and a protocol that pushes changes but never revocations keeps it forever.
*Exposing observation:* two offline devices, one note, one paragraph each, sync. Edit offline, open the summary. Compare the related list before and after reconnect.
*Design response:* block-level merge with conflict blocks; every cached derived item carries (version, scope, generation) and is demoted, not hidden, on local edit; the server keeps a per-device manifest and sends targeted purges; offline results are labeled with their scope.

**Trap 5 — A read that calls out** (authority + money + secrecy on the query path).
*Wrong version:* "suggest while writing" and "ask" are treated as plain reads — no policy check, no cost attribution, no rate limit, no model pin — because they write no rows.
*Why wrong:* the unsaved paragraph leaves the trust boundary on every keystroke; a team that disabled AI still pays for and leaks query embeddings; two concurrent questions both pass the cap check and overshoot it; a query embedded with a model different from the index's returns garbage silently during a migration.
*Exposing observation:* disable AI for a team and type in a team note with the provider log open. Fire 50 questions at once at a team $0.10 below its cap.
*Design response:* query-path external calls check policy in the caller's context, reserve spend transactionally, pin the model, and are rate-limited per user.

**Trap 6 — Statistics and suggestions are aggregates over hidden rows** (visibility through numbers).
*Wrong version:* term-rarity statistics computed over the whole deployment; match percentages displayed; query autocomplete built from all indexed terms; saved-search alerts diffing on scores.
*Why wrong:* "Project Nightfall" autocompletes for someone who can read no note containing it. A displayed score reveals how common a term is in notes the viewer cannot see. An alert fires because the approximate index returned a different tenth neighbor.
*Exposing observation:* type a codename from a private note into another user's search box. Watch alerts on a corpus nobody edited.
*Design response:* statistics per visibility partition; scores never shown; autocomplete over the viewer's domain only; alerts diff on note identity with a stability window and a "new or newly visible" predicate.

### 4. Data model

Types: `id` opaque unique string; `ts` millisecond timestamp; `vec` float array of the model's `dims`; `txt` text. **Derived** tables are rebuildable from others; **append-only** rows never change; **time-bounded** values carry validity intervals.

**users** (user_id pk, email txt unique, display_name txt, ai_allowed bool default true, created_at ts)

**teams** (team_id pk, name txt, ai_allowed bool, monthly_cap_cents int default 5000, created_at ts)

**team_members** (team_id, user_id, role enum owner|admin|member, joined_at ts, left_at ts nullable) — pk (team_id, user_id, joined_at). **Time-bounded**: current iff left_at is null; rejoining appends a new row. An interval, not a role enum: a role alone cannot say "was a member when."

**notes** (note_id pk, owner_id → users, space enum personal|team, team_id nullable, title txt ≤ 200, version int, ai_opt_out bool, created_at, updated_at, deleted_at nullable, content_digest txt derived). Tombstones live 30 days for undelete, then hard-delete cascades to every derived row.

**note_blocks** (note_id, block_id, seq txt — fractional ordering key, text txt ≤ 20,000 chars, block_version int, updated_by, updated_at, deleted bool, conflict_of block_id nullable) — pk (note_id, block_id). A *conflict block* has conflict_of set and holds the losing variant immediately after the winner.

**note_versions** (note_id, version, author_id, device_id, created_at, text_snapshot txt, snapshot_digest txt) — **append-only**; snapshots retained ≥ 90 days and while any embedding or artifact references the version.

**note_grants** (grant_id pk, note_id, principal_kind enum user|team, principal_id, role enum viewer|editor, granted_by, granted_at, expires_at nullable, revoked_at nullable, via_link_id nullable) — **time-bounded**: live iff revoked_at null ∧ (expires_at null ∨ expires_at > now).

**share_links** (link_id pk, note_id, token_hash txt, role = viewer, created_by, created_at, expires_at ≤ +90 days, revoked_at nullable, max_uses int nullable, use_count int)

**embedding_models** (model_id pk, provider txt, dims int, status enum migrating_in|active|retired, activated_at, retired_at) — **time-bounded**; exactly one `active` at any instant.

**embedding_jobs** (job_id pk, note_id, target_version int, model_id, state enum queued|running|done|superseded|failed, attempts int, enqueued_at, started_at, finished_at, reason txt) — the outbox to the provider; **derived** from notes and policy.

**note_chunks** (chunk_id pk, note_id, note_version, model_id, chunk_index int, text txt, text_digest txt, token_count int) — **derived**; replaced as a set per (note, model). Chunks are ≤ 400 tokens with 40-token overlap.

**chunk_vectors** (chunk_id, model_id, vector vec) — **derived**; unique (chunk_id, model_id).

**note_vectors** (note_id, model_id, vector vec — normalized mean of the chunk vectors, note_version int) — **derived**; pk (note_id, model_id). `note_version` is the *embedded version*.

**index_partitions** (partition_id pk, kind enum personal|team, owner_ref id, generation int monotonic, size int) — **derived**. One per user, one per team.

**partition_members** (partition_id, note_id, reason enum owner|space|team_grant, added_at, removed_at nullable) — **derived**, **time-bounded**. Each partition owns three opaque indexes — note-level vector, chunk-level vector, ranked full-text — covering exactly the rows with removed_at null, with ranking statistics computed per partition. Physical removal by compaction ≤ 24 h after removed_at. Indexes are not tables: they answer only "top-k among members by similarity/relevance."

**note_opens** (user_id, note_id, last_opened_at, open_count) — per-reader state; one row per (reader, note).

**result_snapshots** (snapshot_id pk, user_id, kind enum keyword|semantic, query_digest, model_id nullable, created_at, expires_at = +10 min, ordered_ids list of (note_id, version_at_search)) — **time-bounded**; ≤ 500 entries.

**related_cache** (user_id, note_id, model_id, generation_key txt, computed_at, neighbor_ids list, expires_at = +60 s) — **derived**, per viewer.

**ai_artifacts** (artifact_id pk, kind enum answer|summary|digest, requested_by, team_id nullable, llm_model txt, question txt nullable, status enum pending|done|failed, text txt, sources list of (note_id, note_version, chunk_id), evidence_digest txt, reused_from artifact_id nullable, cost_cents int, created_at, finished_at) — immutable once `done`.

**artifact_grants** (artifact_id, principal_kind, principal_id, granted_at, revoked_at nullable)

**spend_ledger** (entry_id pk, team_id, user_id, kind enum reservation|charge|release, cents int, artifact_id nullable, created_at) — **append-only** [M]. Month spend = charges + open reservations.

**saved_searches** (search_id pk, user_id, query txt, mode enum keyword|semantic, query_vector vec nullable, notify bool, last_run_at, recent_result_sets list of ≤ 3 id-sets)

**recommendations** (user_id, note_id, reason txt, generated_at, expires_at = +48 h, dismissed_at nullable) — **time-bounded**.

**devices** (device_id pk, user_id, last_sync_at, sync_cursor)

**device_manifest** (device_id, note_id, held_version int, has_vector bool) — the server's record of what each device holds; **derived** from sync.

**device_purges** (device_id, note_id, enqueued_at, delivered_at nullable) — outbox to devices.

Client-side, per device (not server tables): held notes and blocks; held `note_vectors` for one model_id; a local keyword index the client builds over held text (its own tokenizer, statistics over the held set only); cached related lists and artifacts with their stamps; `pending_ops` (note_id, block_id, base_block_version, op, text, client_ts).

Derived predicates, stated once:
- `current(u, t)` = ∃ team_members row (t, u) with left_at null.
- `canRead(u, n)` = n.deleted_at null ∧ (n.owner_id = u ∨ live user-grant to u ∨ live team-grant to some t with current(u, t) ∨ (n.space = team ∧ current(u, n.team_id))).
- `canEdit(u, n)` = owner ∨ live editor grant ∨ (n.space = team ∧ current(u, n.team_id)).
- `aiAllowed(n)` = owner.ai_allowed ∧ ¬n.ai_opt_out ∧ (n.space = team ⇒ team.ai_allowed).
- `viewerPartitions(u)` = personal(u) ∪ { team(t) : current(u, t) }.
- `directSet(u)` = { n : live user-grant to u }, searched by brute force, capped at 5,000.
- `indexState(n, m)` = `current` if note_vectors(n, m).note_version = n.version; `stale` if a vector exists; `unindexed` otherwise; `excluded` if ¬aiAllowed(n).

Placement rule: a note is a member of its owner's personal partition (reason owner), of its team's partition if space = team (reason space), and of each partition of a team holding a live team-grant (reason team_grant). Direct user grants create no placement; grantees reach those notes through directSet. **Grants therefore rewrite indexes**, which is where a row-oriented backend first strains.

### 5. The hard operations

**O1 — saveEdit(note_id, ops[], device_id)** — write.
Inputs: ops, each `insert(block_id, after_block_id, text)`, `update(block_id, base_block_version, text)`, `delete(block_id, base_block_version)`, or `move(block_id, after_block_id)`; client_ts.
Authorization: canEdit(caller, note).
Steps, one transaction:
1. Load note (refuse NOTE_GONE if deleted) and blocks; check canEdit.
2. Per op, in order. `update`: if block_version = base → set text, block_version+1; else if text identical → no-op; else create a conflict block (new block_id, seq just after, text = incoming, conflict_of = block_id) and leave the winner. `delete`: version matches → deleted = true; else conflict block carrying the "deleted elsewhere" variant. `insert`: seq strictly between neighbors; block_version = 1. `move`: reassign seq only.
3. note.version += 1; updated_at = now; recompute content_digest; append note_versions with the full text snapshot.
4. Rebuild the full-text document in every partition where partition_members(note).removed_at is null — same transaction.
5. If aiAllowed(note): for each model with status ∈ {active, migrating_in}: mark queued jobs for (note, model) superseded; insert embedding_jobs(note, note.version, model, queued). Else delete any vectors that exist (opt-out just enabled).
6. Return note.version, per-op outcomes, conflict block ids.
Atomic: 1–6. Eventually consistent: vectors, related lists, other devices' copies. Optimistic UI: apply locally at once, show "saving"; render conflict blocks as a two-variant paragraph with keep-mine / keep-theirs / keep-both. Concurrent conflict: different blocks → both apply; same block → the later-arriving op becomes the conflict block, visible to both editors within seconds.

**O2 — syncDevice(device_id, batches[], cursor)** — write + read; the merge.
Inputs: per-note batches of pending ops with base block versions; the device's cursor.
Authorization: device.user_id = caller; per note, canEdit at merge time.
Steps:
1. Per batch: if ¬canEdit or deleted → outcome REJECTED_ACCESS / REJECTED_DELETED; else run O1 as a per-note transaction; record outcomes.
2. Pull, per manifest row: server version > held → send current blocks; ¬canRead or deleted → send purge and delete the manifest row.
3. Deliver pending device_purges (mark delivered).
4. For held notes whose held_version equals the embedded version and aiAllowed → send note_vectors(active model); set has_vector.
5. Add notes newly in the offline set (own, pinned, opened ≤ 30 days; cap 5,000) to the manifest and send them.
6. Advance cursor; return outcomes.
Atomic: each batch individually; the pull is per-note consistent, not globally. Eventually consistent: the device converges after the pull; a second sync may be needed if edits landed mid-pull. Optimistic: none. Conflict: REJECTED batches are kept on the device as an *unsyncable copy* the user can copy out — never dropped; a purge arriving for a note with pending local edits also becomes an unsyncable copy (the device already had the content; stop syncing it rather than pretend it never existed).

**O3 — embedNote** — background worker; external system.
Inputs: one embedding_jobs row.
Authorization: system worker; reads note_versions snapshots; writes only derived tables (invariant 16).
Steps:
1. Claim: atomically queued → running where state = queued; attempts+1. If a queued or running job for (note, model) has a higher target_version → superseded, stop.
2. Load note; if deleted_at set or ¬aiAllowed → superseded(reason), stop, no external call.
3. Load text_snapshot(target_version); chunk. For each chunk whose text_digest matches an existing chunk_vectors row for this model → reuse; call the provider only for the rest (edit bursts cost one call).
4. Provider call, 30 s timeout; on failure retry with backoff ≤ 5 attempts, then `failed` — the UI keeps "indexing…", an operator alarm fires, the note stays keyword-searchable.
5. Commit, one transaction, re-checking: deleted_at null; aiAllowed unchanged; note_vectors(note, model).note_version < target_version (the fence). Any failure → discard, superseded. Else replace note_chunks and chunk_vectors for (note, model); write note_vectors; for each live partition_members row, upsert the note-level and chunk-level index entries and bump partition.generation; job done.
Atomic: 1 and 5. Eventually consistent: everything between. Concurrent conflict: the v7/v8 race resolves at the fence; deletion during the call resolves at the re-check; two workers cannot both claim.

**O4 — keywordSearch(query, page_token?)** — read.
Inputs: query ≤ 200 chars; optional snapshot token; page size 20.
Authorization: any signed-in user; domain = viewerPartitions(caller) ∪ directSet(caller).
Steps:
1. With page_token: load result_snapshots(user = caller, unexpired; else SNAPSHOT_EXPIRED and the client restarts). Take the next 20 ids. Drop any that fail canRead now or are deleted (the page may be short; "n results no longer available" distinguishes nothing, because the viewer already saw them). If note.version > version_at_search and the current text lacks every query term → keep, labeled "matched before an edit." Snippets from current text. Return.
2. Otherwise tokenize (lowercase, stem). Per partition in viewerPartitions: top-100 by that partition's ranking statistics. For directSet: brute-force score against the caller's personal-partition statistics.
3. Merge by per-partition-normalized score (score ÷ that partition's top score); keep ≤ 500; residual canRead.
4. Insert result_snapshots (ids with versions, +10 min); return page 1 with snippets and "showing 20 of up to 500" — never a total.
Atomic: the snapshot insert. Eventually consistent: nothing — indexing is transactional, so a save is searchable at once; if replicas serve reads, route the saver's searches to the primary for 5 s. Conflict: notes shared or un-shared between pages are handled at page time.

**O5 — relatedNotes(note_id, k = 10)** — read.
Authorization: canRead(caller, note).
Steps:
1. m = active model. If ¬aiAllowed(note) → {status: excluded}. Load note_vectors(note, m); none → {status: indexing, items: []}; note_version < note.version → status = earlier_version (still proceed with the old vector).
2. Cache key = (caller, note, m, concatenated generation of each viewerPartition, digest of directSet). Unexpired related_cache → return its ids after residual canRead.
3. Per partition p in viewerPartitions: if p.size ≤ 20,000 → exact cosine top-3k over live members; else approximate search (graph index, ef = 200) top-3k with removed_at-null filtering inside the walk. directSet: exact cosine over ≤ 5,000 vectors.
4. Merge by cosine; drop note_id itself; residual canRead ∧ deleted_at null ∧ aiAllowed; keep score ≥ 0.35. If fewer than k survive and some partition returned a full 3k → request its next 3k (top-up, ≤ 3 rounds).
5. Return ≤ k ids with titles, no scores, plus status; write related_cache (60 s).
Atomic: none. Eventually consistent: reflects vectors up to job latency old; approximate in large partitions (P4). Optimistic UI: show the previous list dimmed; replace on arrival; if the user edited since, show "based on version N" until indexState = current. Conflict: a neighbor edited concurrently may no longer be related — accepted.

**O6 — suggestWhileWriting(draft_text, note_id?)** — read with an external call.
Inputs: ≤ 2,000 chars of the paragraph being typed; note_id for self-exclusion. Client debounces 1.5 s and cancels superseded requests.
Authorization: signed in; canRead(note) if given; context team's ai_allowed ∧ user.ai_allowed, else AI_DISABLED ("suggestions are off for this team"); 20/min/user.
Steps: 1. Pin m = active model. 2. Embed the draft via the provider (3 s timeout → {status: unavailable}); never persist the draft. 3. Run O5 steps 3–4 with the draft vector, k = 5. 4. Append spend_ledger charge (≈ 0.001 ¢) to the team. 5. Return, labeled `live`.
Atomic: nothing. Optimistic: the client keeps the previous suggestions until new ones arrive. Conflict: none; a model cutover mid-request is harmless because m was pinned.

**O7 — askQuestion(question, scope)** — read, then a write; external.
Inputs: question ≤ 1,000 chars; scope ∈ {all, note_ids[], team_id}.
Authorization: caller; every scoped note canRead; team scope requires current membership; team.ai_allowed ∧ user.ai_allowed; 200 questions/user/day.
Steps:
1. Transaction: month_spend(team) + 3 ¢ estimate ≤ cap, else CAP_EXCEEDED with cap and current spend; insert spend_ledger(reservation, 3 ¢); insert ai_artifacts(pending, requested_by, question). Return artifact_id.
2. Embed the question (pinned m).
3. Retrieve: chunk-level search over viewerPartitions ∩ scope (same exact/approximate rule), top-40 chunks; residual canRead ∧ aiAllowed; keep ≤ 12 chunks with cosine ≥ 0.30.
4. evidence_digest = hash(sorted chunk_ids, m, llm_model). If a `done` artifact exists for (normalized question, evidence_digest) → copy its text with reused_from; release the reservation; done. Safe because identical evidence means identical readable passages.
5. Call the language model with question + chunks (60 s timeout, 2 retries). Success → one transaction: text, sources = [(note_id, note_version, chunk_id)], status done, ledger charge(actual) + release(reservation). Failure → failed, release.
Atomic: 1; 5's commit. Eventually consistent: the answer, seconds. Optimistic UI: "thinking…" against the artifact_id; on failure "couldn't answer — nothing charged." Concurrent conflict: two questions racing the cap are serialized by the reservation (invariant 17). A source un-shared between step 3 and reading: the answer stands for the requester (invariant 2's exception); its citation renders "no longer accessible."

**O8 — shareArtifact(artifact_id, principal)** — write; authority + secrecy.
Authorization: artifact.requested_by = caller.
Steps, one transaction: 1. Load the sources' note_ids. 2. Recipients = {user} or current members of the team. 3. For each recipient r and source n: require canRead(r, n); any failure → refuse ARTIFACT_SOURCES_NOT_SHARED listing the blocking sources (the sharer can read them, so nothing leaks). 4. Insert artifact_grants.
Read rule (`readArtifact`): viewer = requester → always; otherwise require a live artifact_grant and canRead(viewer, n) for every source *now*; on failure show "this answer references notes you no longer have access to" with the text hidden.
Atomic: 1–4. Conflict: a member who joins after the share is decided by the read rule; a source un-shared later hides the artifact for everyone but the requester.

**O9 — shareNote / revokeShare / links** — write; authority.
Inputs: note_id, principal (user or team), role, expires_at ≤ +90 days; or grant_id; or link operations.
Authorization: create grant or link: owner only. Revoke: owner, the grantee themselves, or an admin of the team named in a team grant.
shareNote, one transaction: 1. Insert note_grants. 2. If the principal is a team: insert partition_members(team partition, note, team_grant); copy the full-text document; copy the note-level and chunk-level index entries for the active model. If the vector index cannot join the transaction, mark the grant index_state = placing and run a fenced placement job; until it completes, team members find the note by keyword but not semantically, and the sharing panel shows "indexing for team." 3. Bump partition.generation.
revokeShare, one transaction: 1. revoked_at = now. 2. Team grant → partition_members.removed_at = now (the in-walk filter hides it; compaction removes it ≤ 24 h). 3. For each affected user u (the grantee, or each current member, ≤ 200): if ¬canRead(u, note) after revocation → insert device_purges for each device of u holding the note; delete u's related_cache rows for the note.
Links: createLink → share_links row, token shown once. openLink(token), one transaction: find by hash; require revoked_at null ∧ expires_at > now ∧ (max_uses null ∨ use_count < max_uses); if the caller has no live grant → insert note_grants(user = caller, viewer, via_link_id); use_count+1. revokeLink: set revoked_at and revoke every grant with via_link_id = link, fanning out purges as above.
Atomic: as stated per operation. Eventually consistent: device purges; physical index removal. Optimistic UI: the new grant appears immediately, with a placement-pending marker if applicable. Conflict: open-link vs revoke-link → the transaction sees revoked_at; two opens at the last use → one wins.

**O10 — deleteNote(note_id) / undelete** — write.
Authorization: owner; team admins for team-space notes.
Steps, one transaction: 1. deleted_at = now. 2. partition_members.removed_at = now for every row; delete full-text documents. 3. Mark queued embedding_jobs superseded. 4. Insert device_purges for every manifest row holding the note. 5. Artifacts citing the note keep their text; the citation renders "deleted."
Undelete within 30 days reverses 1–2 and enqueues a fresh embedding job if indexState was stale. After 30 days a sweeper hard-deletes every dependent row.
Atomic: 1–4. Eventually consistent: physical index removal (the residual filter covers the gap); device purge. Optimistic UI: the note disappears locally at once; a pending local edit becomes an unsyncable copy at sync. Conflict: an in-flight embed job's fence sees deleted_at and discards; a concurrent editor gets NOTE_GONE and keeps the edit locally.

**O11 — nightlyResurface** — scheduled, per user at 06:00 local.
Authorization: system worker computing *as each user*: viewerPartitions(u), canRead(u, ·).
Steps, per user active in the last 14 days: 1. Activity vector = normalized mean of note_vectors(active model) over notes the user opened or edited in the last 7 days (≤ 50, skipping ¬aiAllowed); none → skip. 2. Run O5 steps 3–4 with that vector, k = 5, additionally excluding notes with last_opened_at > now − 14 d, notes recommended in the last 30 days, and the user's own notes edited in the last 7 days. 3. Insert recommendations(+48 h); idempotent on (user, date).
At read, drop any recommendation failing canRead now.
Atomic: per-user insert. Conflict: a note un-shared before morning is dropped at read; a list shorter than five is the honest outcome.

**O12 — migrateEmbeddingModel(new_model)** — bulk.
Authorization: operator.
Steps: 1. Insert embedding_models(new, migrating_in); from now O1 enqueues jobs for both models while O5/O6/O7 keep using `active`. 2. Backfill: one job per live aiAllowed note for the new model, oldest-updated first, throttled to 500/s (8M notes ≈ 4.5 h); a live edit's job supersedes the backfill job by O3's claim rule. 3. Per-partition indexes for the new model are built as vectors land (O3 step 5 is per model). 4. Cutover, one transaction, when coverage (notes with a current-version vector for the new model) ≥ 99.5% of live aiAllowed notes and every partition's new-model index is ready: old → retired, new → active. In-flight reads keep the model they pinned; related_cache and result_snapshots are keyed by model and expire naturally. 5. The uncovered ≤ 0.5% show "indexing" until their job completes. 6. Sync ships new-model vectors; a device compares only same-model vectors, so a half-migrated device computes offline related over the subset it holds and labels the count. 7. Delete old-model rows and indexes after 7 days.
Money: embedding spend doubles during migration. Conflict: fencing covers concurrent edits; cutover during a placement job is safe because placement is per model.

**O13 — evaluateSavedSearches** — scheduled every 15 min; percolation form preferred at scale.
Authorization: system worker computing *as the owner* of each saved search.
Steps, per saved search with notify: 1. Run O4's or O5's core with k = 50 as the owner, now. 2. new = ids ∉ union(recent_result_sets) ∧ (note.updated_at > last_run_at − 24 h ∨ a grant or placement making the note visible to the owner was created after last_run_at − 24 h). 3. Notify for `new`; push the id-set into recent_result_sets (keep 3); last_run_at = now.
Event-driven form: on each placement into partition p (O3 step 5, O9 step 2), compare the note's vector against the query_vector of every semantic saved search whose owner has p in viewerPartitions (≤ 50,000 vectors, exact), then apply canRead(owner, note) and the same stability rule.
Atomic: per-search state update. Eventually consistent: alerts within 15 min. Conflict: a note that appears only because the approximate index reshuffled fails the "updated or newly visible" predicate — no phantom alert (invariant 19).

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment: 20,000 users, 2,000 teams, 8M live notes (median 350 words), 12M chunks. Vectors at 768 float32: 37 GB chunk-level plus 25 GB note-level; int8 quantization quarters that. Partitions: 20,000 personal (median 400 notes, p99 20,000) and 2,000 team (median 3,000, p99 400,000). Exact search covers ~95% of partitions; approximate search is needed only for the ~100 largest team partitions, which hold ~60% of all vectors.

Rates at peak: 50 saves/s → 50 transactional full-text updates/s and ~30 embedding jobs/s after coalescing; provider latency p50 300 ms, p95 4 s → semantic freshness p95 under 10 s, worst case bounded by queue depth (alarm at 5 min). Keyword search 200 qps; related notes 300 qps (every note open); while-you-write 500 qps (debounced); questions 20,000/day; note_opens upserts 300/s; sync 100/s with a median pull of 3 notes.

Money: embeddings ≈ 3.6B tokens/year, $70–700 depending on provider; answers ≈ $0.01 each → ~$200/day; a model migration re-embeds 12M chunks for $70–700 and 4–7 hours of throughput.

Expensive at these sizes:
- Related notes in the p99 team partition: approximate search over 400,000 vectors ≈ 5–10 ms; the top-up loop under heavy residual filtering (a team that just revoked thousands of grants before compaction) triples it.
- Revoking a team-wide grant fans out to ≤ 200 canRead re-evaluations and ≤ 400 device purges — cheap per event; a user leaving a 400,000-note team purges up to 5,000 manifest rows per device.
- Team placement on share: a bulk "share 2,000 notes with the team" is ~3,000 chunk-index inserts plus a generation bump per note — throttle to 200 notes/s.
- Saved searches: 50,000 × every 15 min = 55 qps of background search; the percolation form reduces it to one reverse comparison per placement.
- Nightly resurface: 20,000 filtered searches ≈ minutes; nightly compaction rebuilds the large partitions' graphs ≈ 1–2 CPU-hours.
- Device fill: 5,000 × 3 KB vectors = 15 MB on first sync, incremental after.
- note_versions: 50/s × ~2 KB ≈ 8 GB/day; prune snapshots to digests at 90 days unless referenced.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

**U1 — Top-k under the caller's visibility, with snapshot pagination and no leakage through shortfall.** Nearest-neighbor and ranked text are not range scans: there is no key to seek to for "the next most similar," and the top-k of a filtered domain is not the top-k of the whole domain filtered. The backend must either evaluate the caller's visibility predicate *inside* the index walk or organize indexes so the caller's domain is a union of whole indexes (this design's partitions), then k-way-merge with a residual filter and a top-up loop, and hold a per-caller result snapshot so page 2 is consistent with page 1. *Concession:* overfetch k×20 from one shared index, post-filter, show what survives with "up to k" wording and no counts, and search exactly by brute force for small domains. *Cheat boundary:* the moment the shortfall is not surfaced — a padded list, a displayed count, an unfiltered cached list — or the moment the same query returns 0 for a narrow viewer and 10 for a broad one and the product calls both "related notes" without saying why.

**U2 — Derived data with an external call in the middle, version-fenced and out-of-order-tolerant.** A vector is a function of (content version, model), produced by a call that takes seconds, fails, and completes out of order. The backend must let a background actor with system read authority write derived rows under a fence — only if the note still exists, policy still allows, and no newer version is recorded — and let readers see *which* version a derived row reflects. *Concession:* accept staleness and label "indexing…"; re-embed on a timer rather than per edit. *Cheat boundary:* an unconditional write (a stale vector silently becomes current), a job that reads the note as it is *now* while labeling it with the triggering version, or a related list shown without its "based on version N" stamp.

**U3 — Ranking statistics and suggestions are aggregates over hidden rows.** Term rarity, autocomplete vocabularies, and "similar notes exist" counters are computed over a corpus; if that corpus is wider than the viewer's domain, the numbers leak. Ordinary backends build one index with one set of statistics. The backend must compute statistics per visibility domain, never expose scores, and build autocomplete from the viewer's domain only. *Concession:* statistics per tenant (team plus personal) rather than per viewer, accepting that a score reflects teammates' notes the viewer *can* read; scores hidden. *Cheat boundary:* statistics spanning tenants who cannot see each other's notes, or any displayed number derived from them.

**U4 — Artifacts whose ACL is a function of other rows' ACLs at two different times.** An answer is readable by its requester because they could read its sources *then*; by anyone else only if they can read all sources *now*; and it may be reused only when the evidence set is identical. Row-level access rules do not express "the intersection of these N rows' visibility for this viewer," nor a cache keyed by evidence identity. *Concession:* artifacts are private to their requester and never shareable — the simplest honest product. *Cheat boundary:* a share that checks only the artifact row's owner; a cache keyed by question text; "filtering before the model" when ranking already used hidden passages.

**U5 — An offline client that computes a different function from the server, and says so.** The client can search its cache with its own tokenizer and compute exact similarity over ≤ 5,000 held vectors; the server ranks approximately over the viewer's whole domain. The client must stamp every cached derived thing with (version, scope, generation), demote rather than hide it after a local edit, merge at block granularity with conflict rows, and receive *targeted* purges from a server that knows what the device holds. Ordinary sync — queued mutations plus cached query results — provides none of that. *Concession:* no semantic features offline (keyword over cache only, labeled), last-writer-wins with a whole-note conflict copy. *Cheat boundary:* presenting cached server results as current after a local edit; silently swapping device-computed results for server results on reconnect; a protocol that pushes changes but never revocations; whole-note last-writer-wins without a conflict copy.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice: per-tenant or partitioned vector indexes with residual filtering; transactional full-text indexing; the outbox pattern with version-fenced asynchronous indexing; block-level merge with conflict copies; citation-carrying retrieval-augmented answers; scroll-style result snapshots for pagination; percolation for standing queries; two-model coexistence during an embedding migration. §6's numbers are plausible mid-market figures, not measurements.

Invented or sharpened for this exercise: (i) the placement-follows-grant rule, which makes access changes rewrite indexes transactionally; (ii) the "delivered artifacts are not retracted; wider audiences are re-checked at read time" policy; (iii) the exact-below-20,000 / approximate-above rule as a product-level honesty statement rather than a tuning detail; (iv) the per-device manifest with targeted purges and demote-not-hide stamps; (v) evidence-digest answer reuse; (vi) the reservation-based spend cap; (vii) the alert stability window with a "new or newly visible" predicate.

Least confident: whether cross-partition score normalization ranks acceptably; whether block-level merge is tolerable for online co-editing — real products use CRDT or OT, and this design deliberately does not, stating so in P13; whether the 0.35 cosine floor, the 0.30 retrieval floor, and the 99.5% cutover threshold are right (placeholders to tune); whether shipping vectors to devices earns its 15 MB. None of these softens the axis: the §3 traps hold whichever side is chosen.

### 1. Product contract

This is a multi-tenant calendar service for employees, external guests, assistants, calendar owners, organization administrators, and managers of rooms or equipment. An external guest is a first-class invitation recipient even without an account.

1. Users can create one-time or recurring timed events and all-day date spans.
2. Recurring timed events remain at their stated local wall time in a named IANA time zone across DST changes.
3. Nonexistent and repeated local times follow disclosed deterministic rules: shift forward by the DST gap and choose the earlier offset during an overlap.
4. Users can edit or cancel one occurrence, all unstarted occurrences, or a selected occurrence and those following it without silently losing exceptions or responses.
5. Started occurrences retain their historical instant and audit record even after later rule or time-zone-data changes.
6. Organizers can invite internal users, groups, external addresses, rooms, and resources without learning whether an address has an internal account unless directory policy already reveals that fact.
7. Accountless guests can view their invitation, RSVP, and propose a new time through a revocable invitation link.
8. An RSVP can apply to a series or one occurrence, and a stale RSVP never silently applies to a materially rescheduled invitation.
9. A proposed time does not move the event or reserve a room until the organizer accepts it.
10. Free/busy lookup reveals merged availability intervals, including occupancy caused by hidden events, but not titles, event identifiers, multiplicity, organizers, or attendees.
11. Shared calendars support three detail levels: free/busy only, titles and times, or full ordinary details; private events can cap calendar-level readers at free/busy.
12. Assistants can act under explicit, time-bounded scopes such as editing events, inviting guests, responding on behalf of someone, or viewing private details, with every action attributed to both actor and owner.
13. Concurrent room requests cannot both confirm an exclusive resource for overlapping intervals; recurring requests receive a visible result for every requested occurrence.
14. A recurring event may outlive its room reservation, but room requests are limited to 730 occurrences or two years and the UI states exactly where the reservation ends.
15. Working hours are effective-dated local schedules, while out-of-office periods can optionally decline new invitations or reconcile both new and existing invitations.
16. Reminder handoff never begins before its due instant and, under normal load, 99.9% of in-app, mail, or push handoffs begin within ten seconds afterward; external-provider display time is not guaranteed.
17. The organizer sees a committed edit immediately, while participant indexes, resource decisions, and deliveries may show “updating” or “pending” for up to five seconds under normal load.
18. Concurrent edits merge only when they change disjoint semantic fields; conflicting edits are surfaced rather than resolved by whole-row last-write-wins.
19. Multiple devices converge through revisioned change streams, with permission downgrades producing redactions or tombstones at the next synchronization.
20. Offline edits, invitations, and RSVPs may be queued optimistically, but they are reauthorized and revision-checked when uploaded and can return a user-visible conflict.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[TIME]** A recurring event promised for 09:00 in a named zone occurs at 09:00 there after ordinary DST changes.
2. **[TIME]** Every nonexistent or repeated wall time resolves by its stored deterministic policy and records the resolution used.
3. **[TIME]** Once an occurrence starts, later schedule or time-zone-database changes never alter its recorded start or end instant.
4. **[TIME]** Moving an occurrence never changes the original occurrence reference to which its cancellation, response, and reminder state attach.
5. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A recurrence edit never silently discards or retargets an occurrence-specific response, exception, proposal, room claim, or external update.
6. Exactly one active result exists for a recurrence key: its generated occurrence or its latest exception, never both.
7. **[TIME]** An all-day event remains the same civil date span for every viewer and is never stored as midnight UTC.
8. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Free/busy is computed from every authoritative blocking occurrence, including rows the requester cannot otherwise read.
9. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Free/busy returns only clipped, merged occupancy intervals and never exposes source identifiers, counts, titles, owners, attendees, or reasons.
10. **[SECRECY]** A missing subject, an undiscoverable subject, and a subject denying free/busy access have the same `unknown` response envelope, and `unknown` never means free.
11. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Filters, ordering, counts, and pagination may use only fields included in the caller’s projection.
12. **[SECRECY]** A private event is capped at free/busy for calendar-level readers unless the reader is an invited participant or holds explicit private-detail authority.
13. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Two active claims for the same exclusive resource never overlap.
14. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A resource conflict discloses only that the requested interval is unavailable.
15. **[SECRECY, TIME]** Verifying an address today never grants an account access to historical invitations sent to a previous owner of that address.
16. **[SECRECY]** An accountless invitation capability authorizes only that participation’s visible event projection, RSVP, and proposal operations.
17. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** An RSVP applies only to the recipient’s participation and an event revision the recipient actually viewed.
18. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A proposed time remains inert until an authorized organizer accepts it against the current event revision.
19. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Every delegated action checks current authority at commit and records both the human actor and the effective calendar owner.
20. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Creating an event atomically records its canonical schedule, participation edges, resource requests, audit entry, and durable fan-out intents.
21. **[TIME, SECRECY]** A stale reminder or notification generation can neither fire after cancellation nor render details no longer visible to its recipient.
22. Offline writes are semantic commands with observed revisions and idempotency keys, never blind replacements of current rows.
23. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Once an out-of-office policy reports reconciliation complete, every covered overlapping invitation is declined or recorded as an explicit conflict.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Recurrence identity is confused with an instant.**  
   **Wrong version:** Store a weekly meeting as “add 604,800 seconds” and key exceptions by the resulting UTC start.  
   **Why wrong:** Local time shifts at DST, governments revise time-zone rules, and a moved occurrence no longer has its original start. Subsequent cancellations or series edits then miss, duplicate, or retarget exceptions.  
   **Exposing observation:** Create a 09:00 New York series spanning March, move one Tuesday to Thursday, change later occurrences to 10:00, then cancel the original Tuesday; the series must remain at 09:00 before the edit and cancel exactly the moved occurrence.

2. **Visibility filtering is applied before availability computation.**  
   **Wrong version:** Compute free/busy from events the requester may read, or redact forbidden fields only after filtering and counting.  
   **Why wrong:** Private events disappear and produce confidently wrong “free” answers, while predicates, counts, pagination, refusal differences, and latency reveal hidden columns.  
   **Exposing observation:** Add two overlapping private events from 10:00–11:00; a free/busy reader must see one merged busy interval, not zero intervals or two distinguishable rows, and a titles-only reader must not test whether a hidden description contains “oncology.”

3. **Resource allocation is a snapshot check followed by an insert.**  
   **Wrong version:** Read a room’s current reservations, see no conflict, and then create the booking without serializable range protection.  
   **Why wrong:** Two organizers can both observe availability and both commit, while a recurring request can hide one conflicting occurrence among hundreds.  
   **Exposing observation:** Submit simultaneous requests for the same room and interval from two organizations, then request a 52-week series with only week 37 occupied; at most one concurrent request confirms, and week 37 receives an explicit unavailable result.

4. **An email address is treated as permanent identity.**  
   **Wrong version:** Attach every invitation ever sent to `alex@example.com` to whichever account verifies that address today.  
   **Why wrong:** Aliases, contractors, shared mailboxes, and recycled corporate addresses change owners over time.  
   **Exposing observation:** Reassign an address after an employee leaves; the new owner may receive future invitations but must not discover the predecessor’s confidential historical meetings without the original invitation capability.

5. **Actor, owner, and offline authority are collapsed.**  
   **Wrong version:** Record an assistant’s edit as the executive’s action, cache delegation indefinitely, or accept an offline patch because it was authored before revocation.  
   **Why wrong:** Authorization is a fact at commit time, while attribution is historical; whole-row merging can also erase a newer edit from another device.  
   **Exposing observation:** Revoke an assistant while their laptop is offline, edit the same occurrence on the owner’s phone, and reconnect the laptop; its command must be rejected for current authority rather than overwriting the owner’s edit.

6. **Reminders are fixed payloads attached to old times.**  
   **Wrong version:** Enqueue a title-bearing job once, scan periodically, and send it without rechecking the event, permission, or time-zone generation.  
   **Why wrong:** An event may move or cancel, a grant may downgrade, and a time-zone update may change the due instant after the job was created.  
   **Exposing observation:** Queue a reminder, move the meeting, downgrade the recipient to free/busy, and install a time-zone-rule update; the old job must be fenced, and the replacement must fire only at the new due instant with the newly allowed projection.

### 4. Data model

All identifiers are opaque 128-bit values, instants are UTC with microsecond precision, civil dates and times are distinct types, and time ranges are half-open `[start,end)`.

- `principal(principal_id PK, kind internal_user|external_contact|group|room|resource|service, organization_id?, account_id?, display_name, state active|disabled|merged)`.
- `principal_address(address_id PK, principal_id FK, normalized_address, delivery_address, valid_from, valid_until?, verified_at?)` is versioned and time-bounded; normalization lowercases the domain but makes no provider-specific dot or plus assumptions.
- `organization_membership(organization_id, principal_id, roles, valid_from, valid_until?)` and `group_membership(group_id, principal_id, valid_from, valid_until?)` are versioned because membership-derived authority is temporal.
- `calendar(calendar_id PK, owner_principal_id FK, organization_id?, name, default_zone, visibility_epoch, change_sequence, deleted_at?)`.
- `calendar_grant(grant_id, version, calendar_id, grantee_principal_id, detail_level free_busy|titles|full, capabilities, view_private, valid_from, valid_until?, granted_by)` is append-only by version.
- `delegate_grant(grant_id, version, owner_principal_id, delegate_principal_id, calendar_id?, scopes edit_events|invite|respond_as|manage_sharing|view_private, valid_from, valid_until?)` is append-only and time-bounded; only the owner can grant delegation authority.
- `working_hours_revision(principal_id, revision, effective_start_date, effective_end_date?, timezone_id, weekly_windows map<weekday,list<local_range>>, date_overrides map<date,closed|list<local_range>>)` is append-only with non-overlapping effective dates.
- `event_series(series_id PK, external_uid UNIQUE, calendar_id, organizer_principal_id, state, current_schedule_generation, current_content_revision, predecessor_series_id?, created_at)`.
- `schedule_segment(segment_id PK, series_id, generation, predecessor_segment_id?, key_lower_inclusive?, key_upper_exclusive?, timing_kind timed|all_day, start_local_datetime?, start_date?, timezone_id?, elapsed_duration_seconds?, date_span_days?, recurrence_rule?, explicit_additions, explicit_exclusions, created_at)` is immutable.

A timed recurrence key is its original civil datetime in the segment’s zone; an all-day key is its original date; the stable occurrence reference is `(series_id, segment_id, recurrence_key)`. A move changes actual time, not that reference.

A recurrence rule contains `frequency daily|weekly|monthly|yearly`, `interval 1..99`, optional `count` or inclusive local `until`, `by_weekday`, `by_monthday`, `by_month`, `by_set_position`, and `week_start`. Expansion proceeds by frequency bucket, applies the `by_*` filters, applies set positions, orders and deduplicates keys, stops at `count` or `until`, unions explicit additions, and finally removes explicit exclusions.

- `timezone_binding(segment_id, from_recurrence_key, tzdb_version, applied_at)` is append-only; future bindings may change resolved instants while past projections remain frozen.
- `event_content_revision(series_id, revision, effective_from_occurrence_ref, title, description, location, conference_data, privacy default|private, transparency free|busy, guest_list_visibility, created_by, created_at)` is append-only.
- `occurrence_exception_revision(series_id, segment_id, recurrence_key, revision, state modified|cancelled|detached, override_mask, override_values, base_schedule_generation, created_by, created_at)` is append-only.
- `occurrence_alias(old_occurrence_ref PK, new_occurrence_ref?, detached_event_id?, reason, created_at)` records explicit mappings across schedule edits and must be acyclic.
- `occurrence_projection(occurrence_ref, projection_generation, resolved_start?, resolved_end?, local_date_span?, resolved_offset_seconds?, dst_resolution exact|gap_shifted|overlap_earlier, content_revision, exception_revision?, tzdb_version, frozen_at?)` is derived and rebuildable until frozen.
- `participation(participation_id PK, series_id, recipient_principal_id?, delivery_address_id?, immutable_delivery_snapshot, role required|optional|resource, invited_revision, removed_at?)`.
- `invitation_capability(capability_id PK, participation_id, token_hash, generation, scopes, expires_at, revoked_at?)` stores no raw token.
- `invitation_claim(participation_id, account_principal_id, verified_address_id, capability_generation, claimed_at)` is append-only and claims only one invitation.
- `response_history(response_id PK, participation_id, occurrence_ref?, event_revision, response needs_action|accepted|tentative|declined, source manual|auto_ooo, actor_principal_id, effective_principal_id, idempotency_key, created_at)` is append-only; current series and occurrence responses are derived.
- `time_proposal(proposal_id PK, participation_id, occurrence_ref, base_event_revision, proposed_timing, message, state pending|accepted|declined|stale, decided_by?, resulting_revision?, created_at)` retains proposal history.
- `resource_profile(resource_principal_id PK, resource_calendar_id, policy, booking_unit_id, approval_mode)` represents each exclusive room or independently bookable unit separately.
- `resource_request(request_id PK, resource_id, series_id, occurrence_ref, event_generation, start, end, state pending|accepted|declined|obsolete, reason_code)` is unique by resource, occurrence, and generation.
- `resource_claim(claim_id PK, resource_id, occurrence_ref, event_generation, start, end, state active|released)` is partitioned by resource and enforces non-overlap among active claims.
- `busy_segment(subject_id, source_occurrence_ref, source_generation, start, end, state busy|tentative|out_of_office)` is a protected derived index whose raw rows are never directly returned.
- `reminder_rule(rule_id, recipient_id, series_id, channel, offset_seconds? or local_clock_spec?, valid_from, valid_until?)`.
- `notification_intent(intent_id PK, recipient_id, occurrence_ref, rule_id?, schedule_generation, channel, due_at, state, lease, attempt_count, delivery_key UNIQUE)` contains references, not title or description payloads.
- `delivery_attempt(attempt_id PK, intent_id, provider_idempotency_key, started_at, completed_at?, result)` and `change_outbox(outbox_id PK, aggregate_id, generation, kind, not_before, dedupe_key UNIQUE)` are append-only.
- `calendar_change(calendar_id, sequence, object_ref, revision, kind, visibility_epoch, committed_at)`, `client_action_receipt(principal_id, device_id, action_id, result_digest)`, and `authority_audit(actor, effective_principal, grant_version?, operation, object_ref, before_digest?, after_digest?, committed_at)` support synchronization, idempotency, and attribution.

The field projections are fixed:

| Relationship | Occurrence identity and time | Title/status | Description, location, conference | Guests, responses, audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free/busy | No stable identity; merged intervals only | No | No | No |
| Titles | Viewer-scoped handle and time | Yes, unless private | No | No |
| Full calendar reader | Yes | Yes | Yes, unless private | Only if separately allowed; never audit |
| Invited participant | Yes | Yes | Yes | Guest list only under the event’s guest-list rule |
| Owner or scoped delegate | Yes | Yes | Yes | Only fields covered by explicit scopes |

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Read or synchronize a calendar.**  
   **Inputs:** calendar, time range, cursor, requested filters and fields. **Authorization:** Resolve direct, group, participant, and delegated relationships at the requested snapshot. **Steps:** Derive the field projection first; reject predicates, ordering, or counts over omitted fields; expand authorized occurrences; downgrade private events; return visible rows plus merged busy blocks and a cursor bound to `visibility_epoch`. **Atomic:** Grant resolution and page snapshot. **Eventual:** Derived occurrence indexes may lag, with authoritative expansion as fallback. **Optimism:** Previously cached rows may remain visibly marked stale while offline. **Conflict:** Revocation invalidates the cursor and produces redactions or tombstones; already copied data cannot be clawed back.

2. **Look up free/busy.**  
   **Inputs:** up to 100 subjects, a range of at most 62 days, and requested granularity. **Authorization:** Check each subject’s availability policy without disclosing subject existence. **Steps:** Read all protected blocking segments; clip to the range; sort and union overlapping or adjacent intervals; overlay working-hours boundaries; return intervals and `as_of`, or the same `unknown` envelope for missing and unauthorized subjects. **Atomic:** One consistent availability snapshot per subject. **Eventual:** Cached projections may lag by five seconds, but recent writes are overlaid or expanded directly. **Optimism:** None. **Conflict:** Concurrent edits appear wholly before or after `as_of`, never as partially updated intervals.

3. **Create an event and invite participants.**  
   **Inputs:** calendar, content, timing, recurrence, participants, resources, reminders, and client action ID. **Authorization:** Require event-creation authority, invitation scope when acting for another person, and permission to request each resource. **Steps:** Canonicalize recurrence; resolve DST-adjusted first occurrence; privately resolve addresses to existing or new external principals; insert the series, initial segment, content revision, participations, resource requests, reminder rules, audit row, change row, and fan-out intents. **Atomic:** All canonical rows and outbox intents. **Eventual:** Recurrence projections, room decisions, participant indexes, and external delivery. **Optimism:** Show a local draft, then committed event with attendee and room states marked pending. **Conflict:** Duplicate action IDs return the original result; a reused event ID with different content is rejected.

4. **Edit or cancel an occurrence or series.**  
   **Inputs:** occurrence reference, scope `one|all_unstarted|this_and_following`, typed patch, observed revisions, and explicit mappings for ambiguous stateful occurrences. **Authorization:** Organizer, permitted editor, or current delegate. **Steps:** Lock schedule and exception heads; keep started occurrences frozen; for one occurrence append an exception retaining its key; for a structural edit append a schedule segment, close the predecessor, map stateful future occurrences by exact key or organizer-approved explicit mapping, detach or cancel unresolved instances, fence old resources and notifications, and append updates. **Atomic:** Schedule, aliases, exception migrations, generation changes, audit, and outbox. **Eventual:** Projections, deliveries, and new resource decisions. **Optimism:** Show a preview, but not a successful structural edit before commit. **Conflict:** Stale same-field or schedule edits return a three-way conflict; disjoint content fields may merge.

5. **RSVP.**  
   **Inputs:** participation, optional occurrence reference, response, viewed event revision, and action ID. **Authorization:** The recipient, an active invitation capability, or a delegate with `respond_as`. **Steps:** Load only that participation and its visible event projection; compare the viewed revision; append response history; update derived current response; enqueue organizer notification and busy-index change. **Atomic:** Response history, current-response pointer, audit, and outbox. **Eventual:** Organizer notification and availability projection. **Optimism:** Show “sending response” locally. **Conflict:** An identical retry is idempotent; a conflicting response or material reschedule after the viewed revision requires the guest to review again.

6. **Propose a new time.**  
   **Inputs:** participation, occurrence, proposed zoned start and duration, message, and viewed revision. **Authorization:** The invitee or their response delegate. **Steps:** Validate timing; append a pending proposal tied to the current occurrence and base revision; enqueue a notice visible only to proposer, organizer, and authorized delegates. **Atomic:** Proposal and outbox. **Eventual:** Delivery. **Optimism:** Show a pending proposal. **Conflict:** Multiple guests may propose concurrently; an event change marks incompatible proposals stale, and no proposal reserves resources.

7. **Accept a proposed time.**  
   **Inputs:** proposal, expected event revision, and whether confirmed resources must be retained. **Authorization:** Organizer or editing delegate. **Steps:** Lock proposal and event; require the proposal to remain pending and current; construct the occurrence edit; if resources must be retained, test and move their claims through the authoritative allocator; append the event revision; accept this proposal; mark competing proposals stale; return materially affected participants to `needs_action`; enqueue updates. **Atomic:** Event revision, proposal decisions, required resource moves, participation changes, and outbox. **Eventual:** Non-required room requests and delivery. **Optimism:** None beyond a preview. **Conflict:** One concurrent acceptance wins; a resource conflict leaves both event and proposal unchanged.

8. **Grant, downgrade, or revoke access.**  
   **Inputs:** calendar, subject, detail level or delegate scopes, effective interval, and observed grant version. **Authorization:** Calendar sharing requires owner or `manage_sharing`; creating delegates is owner-only. **Steps:** Validate non-overlapping periods; append a grant version; increment `visibility_epoch`; append audit and invalidation entries. **Atomic:** Grant, epoch, audit, and invalidation. **Eventual:** Cache removal and device tombstones. **Optimism:** None for revocation or privilege increases. **Conflict:** Compare-and-swap on the grant version decides concurrent changes; an action losing a revocation race is reauthorized and rejected.

9. **Allocate a room or resource.**  
   **Inputs:** resource request and event generation. **Authorization:** Current resource-booking policy and current event edit authority. **Steps:** On the resource’s linearizable partition, reload the request; mark obsolete generations obsolete; search active overlapping claims under serializable conflict detection; insert a claim and accept the request if clear, otherwise decline it as `unavailable`; publish the response. **Atomic:** Claim and request decision. **Eventual:** Calendar display and notification. **Optimism:** Show pending, never confirmed. **Conflict:** The exclusion rule lets exactly one overlapping request commit; losers learn no conflicting event details, and separate recurring occurrences decide independently.

10. **Set working hours or out-of-office.**  
    **Inputs:** effective-dated weekly windows or an out-of-office interval, decline mode, and optional message. **Authorization:** The principal or a `respond_as` delegate. **Steps:** Append the schedule revision; increment the principal’s availability generation; for out-of-office create its event and policy; scan covered invitations; append automatic declines; rescan invitations committed after the starting cursor; mark reconciliation complete only after a generation compare-and-swap. New invitation creation must read the same generation and apply an active policy. **Atomic:** Activation, generation, event, and reconciliation cursor transitions. **Eventual:** Bulk declines and notices. **Optimism:** Show “processing existing invitations.” **Conflict:** Concurrent invitations are handled by either their creation transaction or the closing reconciliation scan, never missed.

11. **Expand or reproject recurrence.**  
    **Inputs:** series, schedule generation, civil or instant range, and time-zone-data version. **Authorization:** System-only worker lease. **Steps:** Enumerate civil recurrence keys; apply additions and exclusions; enforce segment bounds; resolve gaps and overlaps; apply exceptions; separately include moved exceptions whose original keys lie outside the query range; write derived projections with source generation; compare-and-swap the completed frontier. **Atomic:** Each bounded chunk and frontier advancement. **Eventual:** The rolling 18-month projection. **Optimism:** None; reads may expand directly while projection lags. **Conflict:** Competing workers write deterministic keys, while a generation change makes every stale row invisible.

12. **Generate and dispatch reminders or invitations.**  
    **Inputs:** current occurrences, reminder rules, invitation changes, and due times. **Authorization:** System worker plus recipient authorization rechecked at dispatch. **Steps:** Generate intents through a 14-day horizon; partition them by UTC day and 4,096 shards; promote the next 15 minutes into a one-second timing wheel; lease due intents only when database time reaches `due_at`; reload event, participation, grant, and generation; render the current allowed projection; commit a delivery attempt; send with a stable provider idempotency key. **Atomic:** Intent lease and `committed_to_send` transition. **Eventual:** External provider delivery. **Optimism:** None. **Conflict:** An edit before `committed_to_send` fences the old intent; afterward an external send may be irretractable, and providers lacking idempotency can rarely duplicate after an ambiguous timeout.

13. **Apply offline mutations.**  
    **Inputs:** ordered semantic commands with device ID, action IDs, observed object revisions, and typed patches. **Authorization:** Recheck current authority for every command rather than trusting cached grants. **Steps:** Deduplicate action IDs; load changes since each observed revision; merge patches only when their semantic groups are disjoint; invoke the normal event, RSVP, proposal, or access operation; record each result receipt. **Atomic:** Each command independently, not the entire upload batch. **Eventual:** Its ordinary projections and fan-out. **Optimism:** Keep successful-looking local changes marked pending until acknowledged. **Conflict:** Cancellation defeats a stale edit, revocation defeats offline authority, and same-group changes produce a user-resolvable three-way conflict.

14. **Reconcile derived state periodically.**  
    **Inputs:** partition, generation manifests, and restartable cursor. **Authorization:** System-only lease. **Steps:** Every minute retry overdue resource requests and due notification leases; every 15 minutes recompute expected reminder key count-and-hash manifests for the next 72 hours; target affected zones after time-zone-data updates; weekly sweep every series partition for projection frontiers, stale claims, and missing intents; repair only through normal idempotent allocators and writers. **Atomic:** One repair chunk and cursor advancement. **Eventual:** Completion of the global sweep. **Optimism:** None. **Conflict:** Leases, deterministic keys, and generation checks make repeated or concurrent repairs harmless.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment could have 30 million monthly active users, 90 million internal and external principals, 120 million calendars, and 8 million rooms or equipment units. Retained state could reach 6 billion event, segment, and exception rows; 35 billion participation edges; 1.5 billion projected future occurrences; 300 million future resource claims; and 1–2 billion reminder intents across the rolling horizon.

Representative traffic is:

- 40 million new events per day and 250 million combined edits, RSVPs, exceptions, proposals, and permission changes per day.
- Roughly 3,000 writes per second on average and 30,000 per second at weekday peaks.
- Two billion calendar or free/busy reads per day, peaking above 200,000 per second.
- 120–300 million reminders per day, with quarter-hour boundaries producing 80,000–150,000 eligible sends per second.
- Median event fan-out of four participants, p99 near 100, and a hard maximum of 10,000.
- Free/busy requests for 100 people over 62 days that may union hundreds of thousands of protected intervals.

The dominant costs are participation-edge storage, recipient and busy indexes, future occurrence projections, durable notification intents, and external delivery. The most expensive interactive operations are large free/busy unions, structural recurrence edits with many stateful exceptions, and popular-room interval allocation. Large-event fan-out must store canonical content once and asynchronously derive recipient edges; a million-subscriber shared calendar must not copy every event a million times.

Completed delivery attempts can expire after 90 days, while authority audit and event history retain longer. Rolling projections and reminder horizons bound derivation work, but authoritative recurrence rules must remain queryable outside those horizons.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Civil recurrence with durable exception identity.** An ordinary row backend rarely provides recurrence expansion, named-zone rule versioning, explicit gap/overlap resolution, exception rebasing, and generation-fenced reprojection. An acceptable concession is a documented recurrence subset and bounded projection horizon. It is a cheat to repeat UTC intervals, key exceptions only by current instant, or silently omit instances beyond the cache.

2. **Protected computation over unreadable rows.** Free/busy must read all hidden occupancy while emitting a deliberately weaker result, and ordinary row filters, counts, ordering, errors, and timings must not become side channels. An acceptable concession is five-minute granularity and merged intervals. It is a cheat to filter by ordinary row visibility, return `unknown` as free, or materialize secret fields into recipient-facing caches.

3. **Serializable interval exclusion.** Arbitrary-time resource booking needs a range predicate whose read participates in conflict detection. An acceptable concession is fixed 15-minute slots enforced by unique slot rows, or an explicitly bounded recurring reservation. It is a cheat to retain arbitrary times while using snapshot “check then insert.”

4. **Billions of exact-time, policy-aware jobs.** Reminder and invitation fan-out needs durable timers, generation fencing, current authorization, send-time rendering, leases, and provider idempotency. An acceptable concession is a visible one-minute handoff window. It is a cheat to deliver early for load smoothing, scan all events periodically, embed stale private content in jobs, or claim exactly-once external mail without provider support.

5. **Time-bounded authority plus semantic offline merge.** External address ownership, organization membership, calendar grants, and delegation all change over time, while queued commands must retain attribution but use current authority. An acceptable concession is rejecting ambiguous offline merges and capping large events at 10,000 recipients. It is a cheat to equate email with permanent identity, trust cached delegation after revocation, or use whole-row last-write-wins.

### 8. Confidence

Civil-time recurrence, immutable recurrence references, IANA zones, invitation capabilities, append-only response history, field projections, merged free/busy, serializable resource exclusion, outboxes, leases, idempotency keys, optimistic revision checks, and generation-fenced projections are standard calendar or distributed-systems practices.

The exact recurrence subset, earlier-offset DST policy, explicit exception-mapping workflow, 62-day lookup limit, 730-occurrence room cap, 18-month projection horizon, ten-second reminder objective, visibility matrix, and out-of-office reconciliation protocol are concrete product choices made for this exercise. Confidence is high in the traps and required invariants, and medium in those thresholds and interaction choices, which would need validation against real usage and interoperability requirements.

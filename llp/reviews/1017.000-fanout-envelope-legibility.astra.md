## Review as received (2026-09-09)

## Overall assessment

The RFC identifies a credible diagnostic and documentation defect, and the proposed scope is appropriate. The supplied code confirms where the suggested `50` comes from, and the parent confirms that the whole-table continuation guidance is stale. D1–D3 are directionally sound, but the causal claims, architectural guidance, and acceptance criteria need revision before acceptance.

## Strengths

- **Concrete evidence of the misunderstanding (§§1–2).** The quoted final report explicitly treats 50 as a platform limit. The diagnostic code and shipped example explain how that inference could arise.
- **Preserves the structural policy (§2, “What is not wrong”; §5).** The RFC correctly distinguishes a schema cap from an execution bound and preserves the refusal, metering, and certificate budgets.
- **Repairs the surfaces cold builders actually encounter (§§3.1–3.2).** Replacing inaccessible LLP references with shipped guide topics is well justified. The parent’s §3.5 directly supports recommending the existing `chunk` admission.
- **Generally disciplined scope (§§3.4–5).** Adjacent issues are separated, and the explicit rejection of a predicted score of 82 is appropriate.

## Concerns

1. **Major — The causal conclusion exceeds the evidence (§§1–2, §3.2).**  
   “The difference was not capability but legibility” and the claim that the field run “proves” a sentence is load-bearing are too strong. Different models, reasoning settings, and architectural choices are confounded. Astra never attempted the refused write-time design, so “both builders hit the same wall” is also misleading. The evidence supports a plausible contributing mechanism and demonstrates an available architecture; it does not isolate the cause of the performance difference.

   **Resolution:** Separate observations from interpretation. Describe the diagnostic and guide as likely contributors to Grok’s documented misconception, and treat the next run as an intervention whose effect remains uncertain.

2. **Major — The score attribution is not auditable from the packet (Summary; §1).**  
   The 82-versus-30 comparison is between builders’ Snapback results; the 80-versus-67 comparison is between platforms for Grok. Those numbers alone do not establish that this issue explains “nearly the entire” overall platform gap. The scoring weights and Grok’s Convex category score are missing. The replay and timing claims also lack an inspectable run record in the RFC.

   **Resolution:** Supply the relevant category scores, weighting calculation, and compact evidence references. Identify which results were mechanically verified and which were builder or scorer reports. Remove the quantitative attribution if that record does not support it.

3. **Major — The alternatives need explicit semantic boundaries (§§3.1–3.2; §4).**  
   `merge`, `maintain`, and scheduled `chunk` jobs are not interchangeable replacements for arbitrary mutation-time fan-out. Here, merge bounds **followees per viewer**, whereas the refused mutation targets **followers per author**. Maintained aggregates support particular computations; chunk jobs spread work across commits and may change freshness and atomicity. The reported successful replay supports Astra’s particular composition, not the unrestricted claim that these shapes “cover” an uncapped population.

   The acceptance anti-goal is therefore problematic: no synchronous write-time fan-out to an uncapped follower set remains an accurate limitation. Also, D1’s `chunk`/`enqueue job` forward-pointer wording blurs the distinction between shipped and future capabilities.

   **Resolution:** Map each relevant requirement to a supported shape, its bounded dimension, and its timing guarantees. Explain the digest pattern’s cutoff and consistency assumptions. Preserve the explicit unsupported case, keep current instructions usable without J3, and distinguish deliberately bounded populations—where a cap can be appropriate—from populations the application must leave uncapped.

4. **Major — Numeric provenance and the absence-of-limits claim need tightening (§§3.1–3.3).**  
   D1 says a cap is something the author declares, but its example still does not explicitly say that **50 was taken from this loop’s bound**. Moreover, the supplied code uses `maximum().max(1)` before checking whether the bound is an argument, so “the loop’s literal bound” is not universally accurate. Existing-cap repairs have another provenance: the schema constraint.

   Separately, one certified 500-lane program disproves a universal 50-lane ceiling; it does not establish the broader statement that “there is no platform lane constant.”

   **Resolution:** Specify wording for literal-derived suggestions, argument-derived suggestions, and existing schema caps. Make the insert ordinal dynamic. Narrow the guide claim to “50 is this example’s chosen bound; the complete query must certify,” unless the broader absence of a fixed ceiling is independently established.

5. **Major — “Every rewrite … certifies” is stronger than the supplied implementation supports (§4).**  
   The no-cap branch returns before checking uniform visibility or an argument-valued bound. Adding the proposed cap can therefore expose another structural refusal. Adding a cap or increasing a loop bound can also encounter certificate budgets. Whole-table and merge guidance are architectural directions rather than directly pasteable replacements.

   **Resolution:** Distinguish executable edits, sufficient repairs under stated preconditions, and architectural guidance. Require successful certification for appropriate single-error fixtures, and specify useful subsequent diagnostics for cases with multiple failures. If unconditional certification is intended, the design must explain how complete repairs are constructed and validated.

6. **Major — The acceptance criteria are not sufficiently measurable (§4).**  
   A builder can never be guaranteed to “never conclude” something. Conversely, citing any unrelated refusal is too weak a success criterion: misunderstanding could persist while the stated condition passes. Checking revised message text alone does not establish that the guidance is correct or usable.

   **Resolution:** Define observable checks: accurate numeric provenance across representative bounds; resolution of every referenced topic from the published package; a complete, certified 500-lane example; an example that reports actual budget exhaustion; and clear explanation of the unsupported synchronous case. For the cold run, record the builder’s interpretation, architecture, replay attempt, and result separately. Retain the N=1 caveat.

7. **Minor — The optional ceiling calculation expands the scope (§3.3).**  
   Computing the “largest certifiable bound” is a new analysis feature. Knowing row sizes and budgets does not, by itself, specify how to establish that maximum for the complete program. “Up to N certifies here” could become another misleading authoritative number.

   **Resolution:** Defer it to a separate proposal, or define its assumptions, validation, and strictly site-specific meaning.

## Suggestions

- Lead the no-cap diagnostic with explicit provenance: “The suggested `cap 50 by followed` uses 50 from your loop’s `first 50`. It would impose a schema limit of 50 rows in each group.” Then explain pricing and supported alternatives.
- Make the new 500-lane example reproducible with its required schema and projection. The packet’s enriched-lane observation makes simply increasing the existing example’s bound insufficient.
- Include a compact diagnostic reason matrix covering numeric repairs, cursor/range cases, whole-table scans, and merge-union refusals. Identify the current shipped guide destination for each.

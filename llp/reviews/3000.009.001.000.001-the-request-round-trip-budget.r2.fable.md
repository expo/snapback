## Findings

**MAJOR 1. The single replay check now runs after serve decodes arguments and looks up the program, which breaks the replay-before-judgment contract.** Today the preflight lookup at `serve.rs:1177` returns a hit at line 1192 before the program lookup at line 1205 and the argument decode at line 1217. §3.1 deletes that lookup and the one at `engine/mod.rs:946`, leaving only the in-transaction check at `engine/mod.rs:963`, which serve reaches only from the executing section at line 1405. So a resent id whose operation was renamed gets 404 `E_OP` at line 1213, and one whose argument was narrowed gets `argument_refusal` at line 1221, both before any receipt is read. The contract the RFC breaks is stated at `engine/mod.rs:931–936` and in LLP 3000.022 §6 ("replaying a write id still returns its recorded outcome before any program lookup"). This is exactly a path still relying on a removed check. Fix: the receipt lookup inside the transaction must precede signature lookup and decoding, so decoding moves behind the miss (it is in-memory, so no flight cost), and the LLP 3000.022 §5 annotation at `serve.rs:1185` is rewritten. The same ordering must hold for `dev_doors::persona_argument` and `check_delivery`.

**MAJOR 2. The B2 schedules are reachable only with mechanism (a); mechanism (b) alone lands at B1 counts for reads and about 10–11 for the mutation.** Every B2 flight mixes argument-bearing commands (fence token, authority digest and clock, receipt key, tail parameters) with argument-free framing (BEGIN, SAVEPOINT, RELEASE, SET LOCAL, ROLLBACK). With the synchronous driver and extended-protocol pipelining excluded, a typed extended-protocol call is one exchange on its own, and PL/pgSQL and SQL functions cannot issue SAVEPOINT or RELEASE. Under (b) the tiny increment is ingress, BEGIN, `sb4_open`, savepoints, read, UPDATE, read, RELEASE, tail, RELEASE plus SET LOCALs, COMMIT: 11, or 10 if the tail moves inside the program savepoint. W1 under (b) is 6, the B1 count. So (b) answers "how does the fence raise and keep typed arguments" and (a) answers "how do several commands share a flight"; they are not alternatives for the same budget, and §4's 8 and 3 silently assume (a). The RFC should give the predicted count per mechanism, or define the hybrid. Two typed routes exist that the RFC half-notices: for reads, a k2-form composed typed statement with open question 3 answered gives W2 at 2 without literals; for writes, one typed `set_config` flight followed by `current_setting()` in SimpleQuery commands reaches 9 without a literal encoder. RT5 should qualify against stated per-mechanism counts, not against a mechanism-free 8.

**MAJOR 3. "Exactly one replay check" contradicts the committer's design for pre-executed members, and the RFC's final boundary for them omits receipts.** Committer §3.6 reads the receipt's absence at S and then "re-checks the receipt serially at admission into the batch"; §3.4 reruns a failed batch's members "in place, with the replay check". That serial re-check is the analogue of this RFC's in-transaction lookup and is the one that counts for a keyed pre-executed member. §3.1 defers pre-executed members to committer §3.6 for authority only, and RT3's "one replay check, inside the transaction" will be read as removing the serial re-check when K3 lands, reopening the two-copies race §3.6 names. State that for a pre-executed member the final replay check is the serial admission re-check inside the batch transaction, that a hit there is an abort returning the stored outcome, and that the S-time read is an early refusal that does not count.

**On the brief's other soundness questions, the in-place design holds.** The receipt INSERT and bind UPDATE (`engine/mod.rs:1082–1083`) precede COMMIT under the head lock, and the lookup is its own command after the head-lock command, so under (a) it takes a fresh snapshot and under a `VOLATILE` function each statement does too. A concurrent same-id writer waits on the lock and sees the committed receipt. Sound.

**MINOR**

- **Replay hits become fenced.** Today a hit is served from a read-only snapshot that takes no owner fence (`store.rs:106` applies only to writers). Under §3.1 a process that lost its lease refuses replays with the ownership error instead of answering from the durable receipt. Safe, but a behaviour change; say it, name its status code, and add the replay row to the parent's P1a per-path retry table.
- **Mis-citation of the engine.** §2's second and third replay checks cite `engine/postgres/mod.rs:300` and `:331`, which is `PostgresEngine`, not the path serve runs. RT-A attributes serve to `engine/mod.rs:1019` and `engine/committer.rs:278`; the checks RT3 edits are `engine/mod.rs:946` and `:963`.
- **§4 keyed rows are not schedule-derived.** "RT0 + 1" compares a measured baseline with a target. The prose already gives the schedule: lookup joins the second setup flight, receipt INSERT and UPDATE join the tail flight, so B2 is 8 under (a); B1 for the replay hit is 8, not "RT0". §2's keyed list also omits the two receipt-write statements (`semantic.rs:61` plus the bind UPDATE).
- **(a) bypasses the rule-1 bind chokepoint and adds a text decode path.** With SimpleQuery there is no Bind, so `Tx::query`'s pre-bind NUL guard never runs and rows return as text. The literal encoder becomes the new chokepoint and must reproduce the typed `E_INPUT`, transaction-poisoning semantics; the text decode must be difftested against the binary decode. §3.6 should say both.
- **§3.6's table cites smoke figures that S1b excludes from sizing.** The full-window C=32 results (k3 9,544/s, k2 6,969/s, k3p 381/s) make the same point and are the qualified evidence.
- **Carried capability omits scope.** Serve uses the route check's `SessionScope` to pick the delivery manifest (`serve.rs:1152`); either carry scope from ingress or state that the final check supplies it.

## Agreed by three seats (Fable, Grok, Muse)

**1. The Draft's §§5.1, 6.2 and 6.3 build a second replication protocol to solve a consistency problem the replica does not have.** Private candidates at a base cut, revision-qualified immutable row images and tombstones stored on the server, exact-cut replay to a frozen publication cut, a second "common applied cut" beside the watermark, candidate rebasing, and a certified group-discovery boundary all exist to bring a privately staged candidate forward to an exact cut. The published partition has never been an exact cut. Verified in the tree:

- `snapshot_page` (`snapback4-core/src/partition.rs`) reads every page in its own transaction long after the first page pinned the watermark; the log repairs the difference. The sync card states the same.
- `expand_joins` serves a join's group as its *current* readable rows at serve time and emits the group's floor marker on an ordinary stream page (`@ref LLP 3000.007#18.2 — ruling 36`); `deferred.rs:210` applies those markers straight into the published boundaries. A group is therefore already acquired live, at the device's current position, without staging.
- LLP 3000.001 Repair B records that pages cut events at the watermark and read images at head, that "rows a page touched running ahead of rows it did not" is inherent and converges, and that the only unsoundness was own-write doubling, closed by `images_at` and `committed_above` deferral.
- LLP 3000.001 (the paragraph after Repair B's integration record) explicitly parks a cross-row during-catch-up guarantee with the staged-sync proposal, "with its historical-image storage path reopened explicitly rather than adopted by implication." The Draft adopted it in one sentence of §6.2.
- The server's current-image rule is a standing disclosure decision (`@ref LLP 3000#7-decisions-during-execution — the server serves current readable images`). Exact-cut replay would ship superseded or revoked intermediate versions that today's protocol never sends (Muse 3, Grok 1).

Disposition: adopted. Revision 1 acquires a group as a join-expansion-shaped page served at the server's current head, applied directly to the live partition in its own transaction, under the one device watermark, carrying the same `images_at`/`committed_above` evidence as every ordinary page. No staging for cold acquisition, no replay images, no second cut, no rebasing, no discovery protocol. The staged whole-replacement path stays for re-acquisition.

**2. The unit of progress is wrong for a messaging app: the inbox and navigation come first, and they live in small horizonless tables the Draft never schedules.** `participants` syncs `by .personId` (principal partition, no horizon; `cap 50 by threadId`), `threads` one row per group with no horizon; the local read rule `exists participants[.conversation, viewer]` and the inbox's cross-group membership page both need those tables whole. Under the Draft's own §4.2 a phone could hold fifty conversations' recent slices and no offline inbox, failing the §1 scenario (Fable 2, Grok 2, Muse 4). Grok adds that "absence of a membership is the conversation list" and a message floor cannot certify it.

Disposition: adopted. Revision 1 makes the schedule two-tier: horizonless declared tables are acquired whole and first, published per page, each table recording a whole-table fact when its walk completes; horizoned tables follow group by group under the foreground hint. The contract table names when the inbox becomes offline-usable.

## Agreed by two seats, opposed by one

**Sub-horizon slicing.** Fable (3) says the declared horizon is already the bound (`last 500` is about one page) and floor extension, deepening and the island rule are machinery to drop until evidence; Muse (1) says a floor at partial `taken` is the same certificate as a floor at the horizon and comes free from `SnapshotCursor.after/taken`. Both are right about the mechanism. Disposition: the first increment's unit is the group at its declared horizon; a partial-`taken` floor with later deepening is named as the second-increment mechanism, gated on a measured need (time to a recent slice in every conversation on the heavy fixture).

## Split

**Whether revision-qualified exact-cut replay and the common cut are needed at all.** Astra: "the current stream's hydration of present-day row images cannot establish an exact historical cut," and the common cut "preserves coherent reads across published groups and commit boundaries." Fable, Grok and Muse: nothing needs a historical cut, and the coherence Astra defends is a guarantee the replica does not make today (Repair B) and no read in the target application claims. The orchestrator verified the three-seat side against the tree (above). Astra's claim is true as stated and does not establish that an exact cut is required. Disposition: the simplification stands; Astra's residual concern (cost and progress under sustained traffic) is carried into §10.2 as a measured acceptance item. Recorded here for the author.

## Single-seat, checked

- **Muse 2: one common live cut couples every slice to whole-account traffic and risks livelock.** Under the Draft's finite-P model, yes. Under revision 1 there is no second cut: the device's one watermark is the cut, the live stream is the catch-up, and a group acquisition page is applied at whatever position the device holds. The livelock disappears with the mechanism. Muse's remedy (independent per-group cuts) is not adopted: it would break the one-watermark fence in `snapback4/NOT-DOING.md` and is unnecessary once rebasing is gone.
- **Muse 5: offline sends stay gated on complete acquisition.** Verified: `require_acquired` (`snapback4-device/src/device.rs:386`) refuses any program touching a synced table until the whole-generation `ACQUIRED` marker is set, and the Draft's §7 kept that for writes. Held. Disposition: revision 1 makes per-program prediction admission the explicit second increment (a mutation predicts when every synced table it reads or proves against is whole-acquired or inside a published floor), and moves the acceptance anchor from full acquisition to time-to-foreground-usable, with full acquisition still measured for regression.
- **Fable: the atomic web publication of the staged set peaked at 307 MB on the heavy fixture.** Verified in the sync card's bounds paragraph (307.69 / 307.63 MB peak wasm heap, 1,000 memberships, 90,500 messages). Direct per-page application removes that staged set for cold acquisition; revision 1 states it as a measured hypothesis, not a result.
- **Grok: the shipped inbox is an ascending cross-group read with unknown coverage until the membership table is exhausted.** Consistent with the sync card ("Ascending reads and other indexes have unknown coverage"). Folded into the two-tier schedule.

## Deferred (for implementation)

- Whether out-of-order foreground groups are skipped by the later walk (a bounded skip set on the request) or re-served idempotently; the cost is one duplicate download per foreground group.
- Whether the server orders the remaining groups by newest horizon key (one indexed probe per group) or walks in group-key order; first increment walks, recency order is measured later.
- Holding versus dropping puts for a not-yet-acquired group arriving on the live stream; both are sound because the acquisition page re-serves current rows.
- The exact wire spelling of the acquisition request (walk cursor plus foreground hint) and the handshake capability name.

No second pass: the direction did not change, the two agreed changes are reductions, and the one split is recorded for the author. Revision 1 has not been reviewed by any seat.

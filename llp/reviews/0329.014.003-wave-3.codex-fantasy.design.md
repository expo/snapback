### 1. Product contract

This is a real-money daily fantasy football service. A contest covers a published slate of games. Each entry pays a fixed fee and contains exactly nine selections: one QB, two RBs, three WRs, one TE, one FLEX eligible at RB/WR/TE, and one D/ST, with no duplicate selectable and a total salary at or below $50,000. Some contests accept 500,000 entries and up to 150 entries per user.

A game’s authoritative start transition is its lineup lock. The published `lock_at` normally equals the official scheduled start; an official delay received before lock may move it later, but a locked game never silently reopens. Players in locked games remain fixed in their exact roster slots while players in unstarted games remain swappable.

The service promises:

1. Contest rules disclose entry fee, capacity, minimum entries, per-user cap, prize schedule, roster template, salary cap, scoring rules, slate, and entry deadline before purchase.
2. Salaries, eligibility, scoring rules, and prizes are frozen for a contest before entries open.
3. A purchase either creates every requested entry and charges the exact total once, or creates none and charges nothing.
4. A contest never accepts more active entries than its capacity or more entries from one user than its published per-user cap.
5. Every accepted lineup contains nine distinct, eligible selections and costs at most $50,000.
6. A selection and its roster slot lock together at that selection’s game start; other slots remain editable for late swap.
7. An official delay may move an open game’s lock, but no schedule correction or administrator action silently reopens a locked game.
8. An edit is won or lost according to its server commit order relative to lock, never according to a client clock or when an offline device says it was authored.
9. A retry of an edit that already committed returns its original result even if the retry arrives after lock.
10. Saved lineups synchronize across devices; non-overlapping stale edits may merge only when the resulting whole lineup remains legal, while overlapping edits produce an explicit conflict.
11. Users may draft while offline, but purchase and lineup changes are not authoritative until acknowledged online; a queued edit may be rejected after its game locks.
12. Multi-entry purchases of up to 150 complete lineups are all-or-nothing, with per-lineup validation errors returned before any charge.
13. Before lock, only an entry’s owner can see its selections; after each game locks, opponents may see selections from that game while unstarted selections remain hidden.
14. Live scores, ranks, player breakdowns, and pages of the leaderboard come from one labeled scoring generation rather than a mixture of partially updated rows.
15. Live scoring normally trails the external feed by no more than five seconds; after ten seconds the UI says scoring is delayed and shows its last trustworthy timestamp.
16. Equal fantasy scores receive equal competition rank, and tied entries split the prizes for all positions they occupy under a published cent-remainder rule.
17. Payouts are based on a fresh final recomputation from frozen lineups and certified statistics, not on the potentially lagging live leaderboard.
18. Every prize or refund is credited exactly once, including after worker crashes and retries, and responsible-gaming exclusions never block refunds, winnings, or withdrawals.
19. Daily, weekly, open-exposure, cooling-off, and self-exclusion controls are enforced across all devices and simultaneous requests without revealing them to other users.
20. If a contest lacks its published minimum entry count at the first game lock, it is voided and every entry fee is refunded.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[MONEY]** Every accepted paid entry has exactly one durable fee debit and every rejected request has none.
2. **[MONEY]** Active entries never exceed the contest capacity or the user’s published entry cap.
3. **[MONEY, TIME]** Wallet balance, contest capacity, per-user allowance, and responsible-gaming exposure are all secured before an entry becomes accepted.
4. Every accepted lineup has exactly nine distinct selections in legal slots with a salary total no greater than $50,000.
5. **[TIME]** An accepted lineup uses the salary, eligibility, scoring-rule, and contest-rule versions frozen for that contest.
6. **[TIME]** A lineup mutation may commit only while every changed slot’s old and new selections belong to games that are still open.
7. **[TIME]** Once a slot locks, neither its selection nor its roster-slot assignment changes.
8. **[TIME]** Once a game locks, ordinary schedule changes and administrators cannot reopen it.
9. **[TIME]** Server transaction order, rather than client time or feed-delivery time, decides whether an edit preceded lock.
10. A repeated request with the same user and idempotency key returns the same business result and creates no additional effect.
11. Every displayed entry score is the exact fixed-point sum of that entry’s nine selections under one lineup revision, scoring-rule version, and feed watermark.
12. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Every score, rank, tie count, and page returned together belongs to one published leaderboard generation.
13. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** An entry’s competition rank is one plus the number of entries with a strictly higher score, regardless of how ties are ordered for display.
14. **[MONEY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A tie occupying positions `r` through `r+k−1` divides the prizes assigned to all `k` positions among all `k` entries.
15. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA, TIME]** Another user’s selection remains undisclosed until that selection’s game locks.
16. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Counts, ranks, response shapes, timing, and refusals do not bypass the lineup-visibility rules.
17. Every external stat fact is deduplicated by provider identity and revision, and corrections supersede facts without erasing history.
18. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A delayed or incomplete feed is labeled stale rather than presented as current.
19. **[TIME, MONEY]** A contest cannot settle until every game is certified final, the correction window has closed, and every entry has been reconciled to the frozen final watermark.
20. **[MONEY]** Final entry-level payout allocations sum exactly to the published prize pool.
21. **[MONEY]** Wallet credits sum exactly to the sealed payout allocations, and no entry receives both a normal payout and a void refund.
22. **[MONEY]** Posted wallet transactions are append-only, balanced, and repaired only through compensating transactions.
23. **[MONEY, TIME]** Tightened limits and self-exclusion block new paid risk immediately, while limit increases take effect only after the required cooling-off interval.
24. **[MONEY, SECRECY]** A responsible-gaming enforcement outage fails closed for new paid entries but does not block account access, refunds, payouts, or withdrawals.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **A player score is an unbounded dependency, not a row update.**  
   **Wrong version:** Update the player, loop over current lineup rows containing that player, increment every entry total, and let rank queries observe the results as they arrive.  
   **Why wrong:** A popular player can occur in 300,000 entries in one contest and millions across contests. Retries can double-add a delta, corrections can arrive out of order, and a late swap can change membership while the loop is running. Even an exactly-once loop exposes a torn leaderboard during its minutes of work.  
   **Observation:** Two entries with identical lineups temporarily show different totals, bucket multiplicities no longer sum to contest size, or a stale worker adds points for both the removed and replacement player. The correction is absolute, version-guarded recomputation plus a publish barrier for each visible generation.

2. **Rank is a computed aggregate and a monetary boundary, not a stored ordinal.**  
   **Wrong version:** Store `rank` on each entry, sort 500,000 rows on every read, or update only entries whose scores changed.  
   **Why wrong:** One changed score can shift the rank of nearly every unchanged entry. Cursor pages split or duplicate as ordering moves, ties occupy multiple prize positions, and a correction near the payout cutoff can alter thousands of allocations. Updating only changed entries leaves unchanged ranks silently wrong.  
   **Observation:** An “around me” view disagrees with the top page, pagination returns one entry twice, competition ranks become `1,2,3` for equal scores, or a 100,000-entry tie pays more or less than the published pool. Rank must derive from a versioned ordered score distribution with entry multiplicities.

3. **Late swap is an interval fact over a whole constrained lineup.**  
   **Wrong version:** Give the contest one `locked` Boolean, trust the client’s save time, allow movement of an already-started player between eligible slots, or merge nine slots independently with last-write-wins.  
   **Why wrong:** Different games lock at different times, moving a locked player creates new flexibility, and two individually legal edits can combine into a duplicate-player or over-cap lineup. An offline device can otherwise restore a player removed on another device.  
   **Observation:** A phone edit and laptop edit race around the 1:00 p.m. lock and produce ten selections, $50,400 salary, or a started FLEX moved to WR. A lost pre-lock response retried after lock is wrongly rejected if idempotency is checked after current lock state.

4. **Contest admission is one decision over several independently hot resources.**  
   **Wrong version:** Read visible entries, wallet balance, and limit usage; then insert entries, debit money, and increment counters in separate steps.  
   **Why wrong:** Two devices can both pass stale checks, a contest can sell its last seat several times, and a 150-entry batch can partially charge. Counting only rows visible to the caller is particularly dangerous: it returns that user’s entries, not structural contest occupancy.  
   **Observation:** A 500,000-cap contest reaches 500,001 entries, a user with $10 remaining exposure accepts two concurrent $10 entries, or a retry buys another 150. Failure must bias toward temporary under-admission, never oversubscription, double charging, or regulatory overspend.

5. **Payout is a fenced, resumable bulk workflow under system authority.**  
   **Wrong version:** Let an administrator call a public mutation that loops through winners, credit wallets from the live rank table, and mark the contest paid when the loop returns.  
   **Why wrong:** Hundreds of thousands of durable credits cannot share one reliable transaction. Workers crash after a credit but before a checkpoint; live ranks may still be stale; and an unconstrained retry doubles money. Read elevation cannot supply write authority, finality, or a generation fence.  
   **Observation:** Killing a worker halfway leaves a contest marked paid with missing credits, or retrying credits the first half twice. The fix is a sealed allocation digest, entry-level instructions, unique ledger-effect keys, bounded batches, reconciliation, and a final status transition.

6. **Visibility filtering can turn global truth into a plausible lie.**  
   **Wrong version:** Apply the caller’s row visibility before computing contest fullness, ownership, score buckets, rank, or payout, and expose direct-entry refusals that distinguish hidden from nonexistent entries.  
   **Why wrong:** A user may see only their own pre-lock lineups, so a visibility-filtered count says a nearly full contest contains one entry and a visibility-filtered rank says the user is first. Response differences can also reveal a hidden entry or player before lock.  
   **Observation:** The lobby says 149,999 seats remain while admission says sold out, pre-lock “rank” is always one, or probing entry IDs reveals which opponents use a late-game player. Structural aggregates must use the full authorized contest domain and disclose only an explicitly public result.

### 4. Data model

Types are `uuid`, `string`, `boolean`, `integer`, `instant` in UTC, integer `cents`, integer `milli_points`, opaque transaction `snapshot_token`, and enumerations. Money and fantasy points never use floating point.

**Identity and compliance**

- `User(user_id uuid PK, display_name string, jurisdiction_id uuid, currency string, status enum, created_at instant)`.
- `Device(device_id uuid PK, user_id FK, last_seen_at instant, revoked_at instant?)`.
- `ResponsiblePolicyVersion(policy_id uuid PK, user_id FK, version integer, effective_from instant, effective_until instant?, daily_entry_cents?, weekly_entry_cents?, open_exposure_cents?, daily_entry_count?, self_excluded_until?, status enum)`. Versions are immutable and time-bounded.
- `LimitUsage(user_id FK, jurisdiction_id FK, kind enum, window_start instant, window_end instant, consumed integer, reserved integer, version integer, PK(user_id,jurisdiction_id,kind,window_start))`. This is the authoritative materialization for concurrent enforcement.
- `EntryComplianceSnapshot(entry_id PK/FK, policy_id FK, jurisdiction_id FK, location_attestation_hash string, checked_at instant)`.
- `IdempotencyReceipt(actor_id uuid, operation_key string, operation_type enum, state enum, result_json object, created_at instant, retain_until instant, PK(actor_id,operation_key))`. Money and payout receipts are retained with the financial ledger.

**Sports and frozen rules**

- `Slate(slate_id uuid PK, sport enum, name string, first_lock_at instant, state enum)`.
- `Game(game_id uuid PK, slate_id FK, home_team string, away_team string, current_schedule_version integer, lock_at instant, state enum OPEN|LOCKED|IN_PROGRESS|FINAL|CERTIFIED, lock_generation integer, certified_at instant?)`.
- `GameScheduleVersion(game_id FK, version integer, scheduled_start_at instant, source_event_id string, published_at instant, PK(game_id,version))`. Append-only and time-bounded by the next version.
- `GameLockEvent(lock_event_id uuid PK, game_id FK, generation integer, previous_state enum, new_state enum, effective_at instant, reason enum, actor_type enum, source_event_id string?, created_at instant)`. Append-only.
- `Selectable(selectable_id uuid PK, game_id FK, kind ATHLETE|DST, external_id string, display_name string, team string)`.
- `RosterCatalogVersion(catalog_id uuid PK, slate_id FK, version integer, frozen_at instant, content_hash string)`.
- `RosterCatalogItem(catalog_id FK, selectable_id FK, salary_cents integer, eligible_slots set<enum>, active boolean, PK(catalog_id,selectable_id))`.
- `ScoringRuleVersion(rule_id uuid PK, sport enum, version integer, rules_json object, points_scale integer, frozen_at instant, content_hash string)`.

**Contests and lineups**

- `Contest(contest_id uuid PK, slate_id FK, catalog_id FK, rule_id FK, name string, entry_fee_cents integer, capacity integer, minimum_entries integer, max_entries_per_user integer, salary_cap_cents integer, entry_close_at instant, prize_pool_cents integer, state enum DRAFT|OPEN|LIVE|FINALIZING|PAYING|PAID|VOIDING|VOID, active_entry_count integer, current_leaderboard_generation uuid?, version integer)`. `active_entry_count` is derived but transactionally maintained; authoritative admission may instead consume `ContestSeat` rows.
- `PrizePosition(contest_id FK, position integer, amount_cents integer, PK(contest_id,position))`; the amounts sum to `prize_pool_cents`.
- `ContestSeat(contest_id FK, seat_no integer, entry_id uuid? UNIQUE, state enum FREE|HELD|ACTIVE|RELEASED, PK(contest_id,seat_no))`; exactly `capacity` rows exist.
- `UserContestUsage(contest_id FK, user_id FK, active_entries integer, next_user_entry_no integer, version integer, PK(contest_id,user_id))`.
- `Entry(entry_id uuid PK, contest_id FK, user_id FK, seat_no integer, user_entry_no integer, status enum ACTIVE|WITHDRAWN|VOID|SETTLED, current_lineup_revision_id uuid, purchase_transaction_id uuid, created_at instant, UNIQUE(contest_id,user_id,user_entry_no), UNIQUE(contest_id,seat_no))`.
- `LineupRevision(revision_id uuid PK, entry_id FK, revision_no integer, base_revision_id uuid?, committed_at instant, commit_snapshot_token snapshot_token, device_id uuid?, total_salary_cents integer, lineup_hash string, UNIQUE(entry_id,revision_no))`. Revisions are append-only.
- `LineupSlot(revision_id FK, slot enum QB|RB1|RB2|WR1|WR2|WR3|TE|FLEX|DST, selectable_id FK, salary_cents integer, game_id FK, PK(revision_id,slot), UNIQUE(revision_id,selectable_id))`.
- `MembershipVersion(entry_id FK, selectable_id FK, game_id FK, slot enum, revision_id FK, valid_from snapshot_token, valid_until snapshot_token?, PK(entry_id,selectable_id,valid_from))`. This versioned inverted relation drives fan-out.

**External statistics, scoring, and ranking**

- `StatFeedEvent(feed_event_id uuid PK, provider string, provider_game_id string, source_event_id string, source_revision integer, source_sequence integer, received_at instant, payload_hash string, payload object, supersedes_feed_event_id uuid?, status enum ACCEPTED|PENDING_GAP|QUARANTINED, UNIQUE(provider,source_event_id,source_revision))`. Append-only.
- `FeedCursor(provider string, game_id FK, contiguous_sequence integer, correction_sequence integer, stale_since instant?, version integer, PK(provider,game_id))`.
- `ScoringEpoch(epoch_id uuid PK, slate_id FK, provider_watermark object, opened_at instant, state enum BUILDING|PUBLISHED|SUPERSEDED, source_digest string, published_at instant?)`. Epochs are immutable after publication.
- `PlayerScoreVersion(epoch_id FK, rule_id FK, selectable_id FK, score_milli_points integer, component_json object, source_digest string, PK(epoch_id,rule_id,selectable_id))`. Derived and immutable.
- `LeaderboardGeneration(generation_id uuid PK, contest_id FK, epoch_id FK, lineup_snapshot_token snapshot_token, prior_generation_id uuid?, state enum BUILDING|PUBLISHED|FINAL, entry_count integer, score_bucket_count integer, rank_index_root string, checksum string, published_at instant?)`.
- `ProjectionWork(generation_id FK, shard_no integer, kind enum MEMBERSHIP_FANOUT|FULL_RECOMPUTE|RANK_BUILD, state enum READY|RUNNING|DONE, attempts integer, input_digest string, output_checksum string?, lease_until instant?, PK(generation_id,kind,shard_no))`.
- `EntryScoreVersion(generation_id FK, entry_id FK, lineup_revision_id FK, score_milli_points integer, breakdown_ref string, PK(generation_id,entry_id))`. This is a logical immutable generation view; unchanged records may be shared physically.
- `ScoreBucketVersion(generation_id FK, score_milli_points integer, entry_count integer, higher_entry_count integer, PK(generation_id,score_milli_points))`. `competition_rank = higher_entry_count + 1`.
- `LeaderboardMember(generation_id FK, entry_id FK, score_milli_points integer, display_order_key string, PK(generation_id,entry_id))`. The ordered index is `(generation_id, score_milli_points DESC, display_order_key)`; display order does not break score ties.

**Money and settlement**

- `WalletAccount(account_id uuid PK, owner_type USER|CONTEST_ESCROW|PLATFORM, owner_id uuid, currency string, status enum)`.
- `WalletTransaction(transaction_id uuid PK, kind enum ENTRY_FEE|WITHDRAWAL_REFUND|VOID_REFUND|PRIZE|RAKE|OVERLAY|ADJUSTMENT, business_key string UNIQUE, created_at instant, metadata object)`.
- `WalletPosting(transaction_id FK, posting_no integer, account_id FK, amount_cents signed_integer, PK(transaction_id,posting_no))`. Transactions and postings are append-only, and postings for each transaction sum to zero.
- `WalletBalance(account_id PK/FK, available_cents integer, version integer)` is a transactionally maintained derivative of postings.
- `SettlementRun(settlement_id uuid PK, contest_id FK, generation integer, final_epoch_id FK, lineup_snapshot_token snapshot_token, correction_window_ended_at instant, state enum BUILDING|SEALED|PAYING|RECONCILED|FAILED, result_digest string?, allocation_total_cents integer?, UNIQUE(contest_id,generation))`.
- `ContestResult(settlement_id FK, entry_id FK, score_milli_points integer, competition_rank integer, tie_size integer, occupied_position_start integer, occupied_position_end integer, payout_cents integer, remainder_cent boolean, PK(settlement_id,entry_id))`. Immutable after seal.
- `PayoutInstruction(instruction_id uuid PK, settlement_id FK, entry_id FK, recipient_account_id FK, kind PRIZE|REFUND, amount_cents integer, state READY|PROCESSING|POSTED|FAILED, wallet_transaction_id uuid?, UNIQUE(settlement_id,entry_id,kind))`.
- `EntryTerminalEffect(entry_id FK, effect_class NORMAL_PAYOUT|VOID_REFUND, instruction_id uuid UNIQUE, PK(entry_id))` prevents mutually exclusive terminal effects.

Authoritative history consists of lineup revisions, schedule and lock events, feed events, rule versions, compliance snapshots, and wallet postings. Player scores, membership indexes, entry scores, score buckets, ranks, balances, and counts are derived and repairable. Final results and sealed payout instructions are immutable settled records.

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Browse the contest lobby**

   **Inputs:** optional user, sport, slate, fee range, contest type, page cursor.  
   **Authorization:** Public contest facts are readable by anyone; account eligibility and remaining personal limits are returned only to that user.

   **Steps:**

   1. Select `OPEN` contests whose `entry_close_at` has not passed.
   2. Read each contest’s frozen rules and public capacity materialization.
   3. For a signed-in user, separately compute `may_enter` from account, jurisdiction, self-exclusion, and published personal cap without exposing private reason details to anyone else.
   4. Return a lobby watermark with `active_entry_count`, capacity, fee, prizes, and rule identifiers.

   **Semantics:** The page is one read snapshot, but fill counts may lag purchases by up to two seconds; admission never trusts that count. Nothing is optimistic. A contest may sell out after the read, in which case purchase returns `CONTEST_FULL` without charging.

2. **Purchase one or many entries**

   **Inputs:** `contest_id`, 1–150 complete nine-slot lineups, `operation_key`, location attestation, funding account.  
   **Authorization:** The authenticated owner must satisfy age, identity, jurisdiction, account, responsible-gaming, and contest eligibility requirements.

   **Steps:**

   1. Look up `IdempotencyReceipt`; if completed, return it before rechecking current time or capacity.
   2. Verify the contest is open and server time precedes `entry_close_at`.
   3. Validate every lineup against the frozen catalog, slots, uniqueness, and $50,000 cap.
   4. Let `n` be the lineup count and `fee = n × entry_fee_cents`; check the user cap, daily and weekly spend, open exposure, entry count, and wallet balance against authoritative rows.
   5. In one admission transaction, claim `n` contest seats, advance `UserContestUsage`, reserve/consume limit buckets, create a balanced fee transaction into contest escrow, create all entries and initial lineup revisions, update membership rows, and complete the receipt.
   6. Enqueue the entries for the next scoring generation.

   **Semantics:** All entries, limits, seats, fee postings, and the receipt are atomic. Lobby counts and leaderboard inclusion are eventual. The UI may display “submitting” but not “entered” before acknowledgment. Concurrent buyers serialize on scarce permits; losers receive no seat, exposure, or debit.

3. **Edit a lineup or make a late swap**

   **Inputs:** `entry_id`, `base_revision_id`, a set of slot replacements, `operation_key`, `device_id`.  
   **Authorization:** Only the entry owner may edit an active entry.

   **Steps:**

   1. Return an existing idempotent result before examining present locks.
   2. Load the base revision and current revision.
   3. If they differ, allow a three-way merge only when every touched slot still equals its value in the base revision; otherwise return the current lineup as a conflict.
   4. Apply the patch to the current lineup.
   5. Serialize against the relevant `Game` rows and require every changed old and new selection to remain `OPEN`; preserve every already-locked selection in its exact slot.
   6. Revalidate all nine slots, selection uniqueness, eligibility, and total salary.
   7. Append a complete `LineupRevision` and nine `LineupSlot` rows, close and add membership intervals, update the entry pointer, and store the receipt.

   **Semantics:** The revision, pointer, memberships, and receipt are atomic with lock checks. Live score catches up in the next generation. The UI may show a clearly labeled unsaved local draft but cannot show “saved” optimistically. Overlapping concurrent edits conflict; non-overlapping edits merge only if the combined lineup remains valid.

4. **Withdraw entries before contest close**

   **Inputs:** up to 150 `entry_id` values and `operation_key`.  
   **Authorization:** The owner may withdraw active entries only while the contest permits withdrawal and before `entry_close_at`.

   **Steps:**

   1. Resolve idempotency and validate ownership and status for the entire batch.
   2. Serialize against contest close and seat admission.
   3. Mark entries `WITHDRAWN`, release their seats and per-user contest allowance, and close current membership intervals.
   4. Append one balanced refund transaction per entry or one transaction with entry-level child references.
   5. Release open-exposure usage, but do not restore daily or weekly paid-entry consumption; a platform-voided contest restores both under operation 12.
   6. Complete the receipt and enqueue count repair.

   **Semantics:** The requested batch is atomic. Public fill count is eventual; wallet balance is immediate. No refund appears optimistically. A concurrent lock or settlement wins or loses as a whole, and a duplicate request returns the original refund.

5. **Read an entry and its visible lineup**

   **Inputs:** `contest_id`, `entry_id`, optional `generation_id`.  
   **Authorization:** The owner sees all nine slots; another user sees a slot only after that slot’s game has locked and only after the contest is live.

   **Steps:**

   1. Resolve the requested or current published leaderboard generation.
   2. Load the lineup revision used by that generation.
   3. Replace every unauthorized selection, salary, team, and identifying field with the same fixed `UNREVEALED` representation.
   4. Return the entry’s generation-consistent total and public rank.
   5. Return a generic unavailable response for nonexistent and wholly unauthorized direct-entry requests.

   **Semantics:** Visibility and score are evaluated under one read snapshot. A just-locked selection may remain hidden for one refresh, but an unstarted selection must never appear early. There is no optimistic state. A concurrent lock may place the response wholly before or after disclosure, never partly through inconsistent endpoint checks.

6. **Advance a game lock**

   **Inputs:** `game_id`, expected `lock_generation`, reason, official source event or scheduled invocation.  
   **Authorization:** Only the trusted scheduler, official-start adapter, or a separately audited operator command may invoke it; ordinary users and application administrators cannot reopen games.

   **Steps:**

   1. For a schedule change, append a schedule version only if the game is still open and the change was accepted before the existing deadline.
   2. At `lock_at`, or upon an earlier trusted start signal, serialize on the game’s state and increment `lock_generation`.
   3. Append `GameLockEvent` and change `OPEN` to `LOCKED`.
   4. At the slate’s first lock, atomically stop new contest admission; move sufficiently filled contests to `LIVE` and underfilled contests to `VOIDING`.
   5. Publish the transition to clients and enqueue any newly void contests for refunds.

   **Semantics:** Game state and its lock event are atomic, while client notification and void processing are eventual. The UI may show a countdown but cannot assume it won the boundary. A lineup edit serializes either before the lock and succeeds or after it and fails; lock never silently reverses.

7. **Ingest an external stat event or correction**

   **Inputs:** signed provider payload, provider game, source event ID, source revision and sequence.  
   **Authorization:** Only the authenticated feed adapter may write feed events.

   **Steps:**

   1. Validate source authentication, schema, game mapping, payload hash, and allowed event type.
   2. Return success for an already stored identical provider identity and revision.
   3. Quarantine a conflicting payload with the same identity and revision.
   4. Append the new `StatFeedEvent`; corrections point to the superseded event rather than mutating it.
   5. Advance `FeedCursor` only across a contiguous sequence and mark gaps or stale games.
   6. Atomically enqueue score derivation after durable receipt, then acknowledge the provider.

   **Semantics:** The feed event, safe cursor advance, and enqueue are atomic; player, entry, and rank projections are eventual. The UI never optimistically applies an unaccepted feed event. Out-of-order events wait behind a gap, duplicates are no-ops, and a correction creates a newer scoring epoch.

8. **Build and publish a live scoring generation**

   **Inputs:** a slate, target provider watermark, and generation identifier.  
   **Authorization:** System workers only; generation and shard leases are fenced.

   **Steps:**

   1. Choose the highest contiguous feed watermark and a transaction snapshot for committed lineup revisions, coalescing obsolete intermediate live watermarks when behind.
   2. Derive absolute `PlayerScoreVersion` rows for changed selectables under every scoring-rule version used by the slate.
   3. Find affected entries through `MembershipVersion` at the lineup snapshot, plus every entry whose lineup changed since the prior generation.
   4. Partition affected entries into resumable shards.
   5. For each entry, load its exact nine-selection revision at the snapshot, sum absolute player scores, and write an idempotent `EntryScoreVersion`; never add an unguarded delta.
   6. Move entry multiplicities between immutable score buckets and build the generation’s ordered, subtree-counted rank index.
   7. Verify every shard, require nonnegative bucket counts, require bucket multiplicities to sum to active entries, and compute a checksum.
   8. Atomically publish `Contest.current_leaderboard_generation` only after the barrier passes.

   **Semantics:** A player score, a bounded shard, and the final pointer swap have separate atomic boundaries; the entire fan-out is deliberately not one transaction. No partial generation is visible, and the UI shows the prior generation until publication. Newer feed or lineup changes go into the next generation; stale fenced workers cannot overwrite it.

9. **Read the live leaderboard**

   **Inputs:** `contest_id`, optional `generation_id`, page size up to 100, cursor, optional owned `entry_id` for “around me.”  
   **Authorization:** Contest participants may read the live leaderboard after first lock; public spectators receive only contests marked public.

   **Steps:**

   1. Pin the current generation or validate the cursor’s generation.
   2. Traverse the ordered index by `(score DESC, display_order_key)` without recomputing scores.
   3. Compute competition rank as one plus the multiplicity of all higher score buckets.
   4. Return at most 100 rows, a generation-bound cursor, feed watermark, publication time, and stale indicator.
   5. For “around me,” locate the entry in the same generation and return neighboring order positions without revealing hidden selections.

   **Semantics:** The page is immutable within its generation; score, rank, and cursor are consistent. Nothing is optimistic. If a generation has expired from live retention, pagination returns `GENERATION_EXPIRED` and restarts instead of mixing pages; a new score generation never mutates the pinned one.

10. **Change responsible-gaming controls**

   **Inputs:** expected policy version, requested limits, cooling-off request, or self-exclusion duration, plus `operation_key`.  
   **Authorization:** Only the user may change self-service controls; mandated restrictions require a separately authorized compliance actor and audit record.

   **Steps:**

   1. Resolve idempotency and validate the expected policy version.
   2. Compare the request with the currently effective policy and jurisdictional minimums.
   3. Make reductions and self-exclusion effective immediately; assign increases an `effective_from` at least 24 hours later or the longer jurisdictional delay.
   4. Append a `ResponsiblePolicyVersion` and serialize the change against paid-entry admission on the user’s exposure state.
   5. Cancel abandoned admission reservations, but do not cancel already accepted contests unless the governing rule requires it.
   6. Store the receipt and notify the user’s other devices.

   **Semantics:** The policy version and effective interval are atomic. Device notifications are eventual. The UI may preview requested limits but must distinguish pending increases from effective ones. Concurrent entry admission is ordered wholly before or after an immediate restriction; stale policy edits conflict.

11. **Finalize results and seal payout allocations**

   **Inputs:** `contest_id`, final certified feed watermark, settlement generation.  
   **Authorization:** A scheduled system process may begin only after all games are certified and the published 60-minute correction window has elapsed.

   **Steps:**

   1. Fence one `SettlementRun`, freeze final feed and lineup watermarks, and move the contest to `FINALIZING`.
   2. Recompute all entry totals from canonical locked lineups and final stat facts, independently of live projections.
   3. Compare the result with the live index, repair discrepancies in the settlement generation, and perform an exact full ordering.
   4. For a tie of `k` entries beginning at rank `r`, sum prizes for positions `r…r+k−1`, using zero for positions beyond the paid range.
   5. Assign each entry `floor(tie_pool_cents/k)` and allocate the remaining cents to the lowest immutable contest seat numbers within the tie.
   6. Persist every `ContestResult` and `PayoutInstruction`.
   7. Verify result count, tie multiplicities, source digest, and `sum(payout_cents) = prize_pool_cents`.
   8. Seal the digest and move the contest to `PAYING`.

   **Semantics:** Building is resumable and invisible; sealing the complete result set and status is atomic. The UI shows “results under review,” not projected winnings as paid. A correction before seal invalidates and restarts the run; after seal, later provider changes are recorded but do not alter payouts except through a separately approved compensating settlement.

12. **Execute payouts or void refunds in bulk**

   **Inputs:** settlement or void-run ID and a bounded shard.  
   **Authorization:** Fenced system payout workers only; responsible-gaming status cannot deny the credit.

   **Steps:**

   1. Claim a shard lease and verify the sealed generation or void fence.
   2. For each instruction, use `(contest_id, settlement_or_void_id, entry_id, kind)` as the unique business key.
   3. In one bounded transaction, append balanced wallet postings, update wallet materializations, link the wallet transaction, mark the instruction `POSTED`, and create the mutually exclusive `EntryTerminalEffect`.
   4. On retry, treat an existing business key as the completed effect.
   5. After all shards finish, reconcile instruction totals against wallet postings and settlement or refund totals.
   6. Transfer rake or platform overlay through explicit balanced postings.
   7. Only after reconciliation, atomically mark the contest `PAID` or `VOID`.
   8. For a platform void, reverse daily, weekly, and open-exposure usage exactly once; an ordinary voluntary withdrawal does not reverse historical paid-entry consumption.

   **Semantics:** Each bounded credit batch is atomic, but a 500,000-entry payout is not. Wallet credits and final contest status are eventually consistent over minutes, with `PAYING` visible meanwhile; credits are never shown optimistically. Crashes and competing workers are harmless through leases, fences, and unique ledger keys, while underfunding halts reconciliation rather than inventing money.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful Sunday slate has:

- 2 million active users, 5 million paid entries, 20,000 contests, and several contests between 100,000 and 500,000 entries.
- Up to 150 entries per user and 4.5 million current selection edges in one 500,000-entry contest.
- Roughly 45 million current lineup slots per slate; at 1.5 saved revisions per entry, about 67.5 million append-only slot rows.
- Approximately 600 selectable athletes and defenses across 14 games.
- About 200 raw feed messages per second, with 20–50 scoring-relevant changes per second and bursts during simultaneous games.
- Two-second live scoring epochs, or about 5,400 potential generations during three hours of play.
- A popular selection owned by 60% of a 500,000-entry contest, producing 300,000 affected totals from one scoring change and potentially several million across contests.
- 50,000–100,000 leaderboard reads per second during major plays, concentrated on the top 100 and users’ own neighborhoods.
- Up to 100,000 paid positions in one contest and roughly 2 million prize or refund instructions across a large slate.

Naively updating 300,000 entries every two seconds for one player requires 150,000 dependent writes per second before ranking. Five simultaneous popular-player changes can exceed a million random writes per second. Re-sorting 500,000 distinct totals costs roughly 9.5 million comparisons per contest per refresh; changing one total can alter the ordinal rank of nearly every entry.

The expensive operations are fan-out membership traversal, recomputing nine-player totals, moving entries through the ordered score distribution, retaining immutable generations for pagination, final full reconciliation, and payout ledger writes. Admission also creates hot contention on the last contest seats, one user’s wallet, and that user’s limit buckets.

Grouping identical lineup signatures can reduce computation by moving a multiplicity once, and coalescing superseded live epochs can reduce work during bursts. Neither is a correctness dependency: the worst case is 500,000 distinct lineups, and finalization must process every entry. At 5,000 durable payout credits per second, 2 million credits take about seven minutes even without failures.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Versioned unbounded fan-out with a publication barrier.** Ordinary row triggers or reactive dependencies do not cleanly turn one player revision into hundreds of thousands of guarded entry recomputations while coexisting with late swaps, retries, and corrections. A product may concede a 10–30-second labeled live delay and coalesce intermediate states. It becomes a cheat if only visible or top-ranked entries update, if final scores rely on best-effort deltas, or if mixed revisions are called one current leaderboard.

2. **Exact rank over changing computed values with snapshot pagination.** The backend needs an order-statistic structure or score histogram carrying multiplicities, immutable generation roots, prefix counts, and generation-bound cursors; ordinary `ORDER BY ... OFFSET` neither computes cheap global rank nor remains stable. A product may expose approximate live rank, top-N plus “around me,” and exact final rank if approximation is explicit. It becomes a cheat to cap contests below the stated scale, omit ties, or label an internally inconsistent ordinal as exact.

3. **Serializable temporal locking across lineup aggregates.** A lineup commit and a game-lock transition need one authoritative order, while retries must recover the original pre-lock result and stale devices must merge against immutable history. A product may replace per-game lock with one slate-wide deadline or remove late swap entirely. It becomes a cheat to use client timestamps, delayed stat arrival, a mutable `locked` Boolean without history, or automatic last-write-wins.

4. **Atomic admission across capacity, user cap, wallet, and regulatory exposure.** These resources have different contention patterns, yet acceptance must own all or none. A backend may use leased reservations and compensating cleanup instead of one distributed transaction, temporarily underfilling a contest while uncertain. It becomes a cheat if limits are checked asynchronously, the contest may oversubscribe, a failed batch may remain charged, or compensation can release exposure belonging to a committed entry.

5. **Fenced bulk settlement with exactly-once business effects.** Final ranking, deterministic tie allocation, hundreds of thousands of wallet credits, crash recovery, and monetary reconciliation cannot honestly be one request or one database transaction. A product may delay payouts, settle in scheduled batches, and show `PAYING` for several minutes. It becomes a cheat to pay from the live index, assume jobs run once, mutate old ledger entries, omit cent conservation, or mark the contest paid before every instruction reconciles.

### 8. Confidence

The roster template, salary cap, per-game late swap, frozen contest rules, external-stat corrections, integer scoring, competition ranks, tie-pool payout, append-only financial ledger, idempotent jobs, correction window, multi-entry caps, and responsible-gaming enforcement are standard or direct consequences of a real-money fantasy product.

The exact two-second epoch, five- and ten-second freshness thresholds, 60-minute correction window, all-or-nothing 150-lineup purchase, immutable generation model, score-bucket order-statistic index, seat-number cent remainder, and particular scale figures are concrete choices invented for this exercise. They are intended to remove implementation ambiguity, not claims about one operator’s current rules.

The most design-specific requirement is generation-coherent live ranking. Some production systems accept visibly eventual individual rows, but requiring one score/rank watermark is deliberate: without it, the primary fan-in and ranking stress can be hidden behind transient wrong answers. Jurisdiction-specific definitions of paid-entry consumption, limit cooling-off, contest voids, post-settlement corrections, and lineup disclosure would require legal and product validation before launch.

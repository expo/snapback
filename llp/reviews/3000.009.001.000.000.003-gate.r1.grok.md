## Ruling (verbatim answer text)

## Q-A — the Stage R gate

Replace the absolute 4,525/A gate. Production slices R1–R5 start only when the candidate strictly beats both self-hosted Supabase and self-hosted Convex-on-Postgres, in one interleaved expo-build-450b campaign, on all six S1 Postgres write cells, and does not regress flag-off main on the six read cells or on W5 exact.

A rung qualifies only when all five repetitions pass the unchanged S1 rule: intended-arrival p99 ≤ 100 ms for writes and ≤ 50 ms for reads, measured errors ≤ 0.1%, and exact durable counters against every acknowledgement including warm-up ([LLP 3000.009.001.002 §6](llp/3000.009.001.002-scale-limits-against-postgres-controls.rfc.md); the pass rule restated in `results-20261027-s1-r0-authoritative/README.md`). A 4/5 fails. Median p99 does not rescue a rung. Three-repetition scouts may find the bracket; the qualifying ceiling and the first failing rung above it are deciding and use five repetitions. Arm order rotates, with a fresh service every measurement. The candidate, flag-off main of that same commit, Supabase, and Convex all run in that window. Advocates may re-tune only before the window. 450a can stop the program and cannot pass it (RFC 003 §8.2, §20).

A **win** is a strictly higher qualifying offered rate than Supabase and a strictly higher qualifying offered rate than Convex. The headline ladder stays 100·2^(k/2). Quarter rungs 100·2^((2k+1)/4) are used only when the candidate and the best competitor share a headline rung (ruling R1 / S1 ruling 9, RFC 003 §9). A quarter rung never turns a headline-rung loss into a win.

A **tie** is that shared rung after the next rung has been run and both fail it: the quarter rung, when they tied on a headline rung; otherwise the next headline rung. Latency, sent mean, CPU, and WAL do not break a tie. An unrun next rung leaves the cell untested. A tie fails this gate.

**Non-regression** applies to W1, W2, W3, and W5 exact, both links. The candidate must pass 5/5 at every rung that same-commit flag-off main passes in the same campaign, and its qualifying rung must be at least main’s. One failed repetition fails the cell. Read cells are run with `SNAPBACK4_READ_GRANT_CLOCK_BOUNDS=1000,250` on both the candidate and main, because that is the configuration that actually beats Convex on W2 and W3 (`results-20261025-s1-reads/README.md`). The read cells must also be strict wins against both controls in that same campaign. §10.3 already refuses a new claim scored against a historical control.

If a competitor fails a rung it passed in the latest accepted same-method campaign, that cell is inconclusive. One disclosed advocate check and one full rerun are allowed. The second result stands. Pinned rungs: Supabase W1 A/B 2,000, W2 A/B 2,000, W3 A/B 2,828, W4/A 3,200, W4/B 2,262, exact A 8, exact B 70, hot1/A 800, hot1/B 2,690; Convex W1 A/B 500, W2 A/B 8,000, W3/A 8,000, W3/B 5,656, W4/B 141, exact A/B 25, hot1/A 400, hot1/B 25. Convex W4/A has no historical pass (`results-20261024-s1-onehost-controls/README.md`), so it is simply run at the deciding rungs.

The 4,525/A ≤6.0 ms test is retired as a gate, including the ≤6.4 ms default-on mean. Restored R0 already passes W4/A at 3,805/s, 5/5, sent-mean upper bounds 5.84–6.12 ms, while Supabase’s best same-window rung is 3,200/s, 5/5, and both are 0/5 at 4,525/s (`results-20261027-s1-r0-authoritative/README.md`). Supabase’s five 4,525 means sit near 1 s; R0’s five means are 7.01–7.13 ms and every conservative lower bound is above 6.0 ms. Tie-break controls show the same Supabase collapse at 3,805 and 4,525 (`results-20261027-s1-tiebreak-controls/README.md`). Little’s law at 32 clients makes 7.07 ms the necessary mean at 4,525 (RFC 003 §2.2, §8.2). That rung is flush physics both products miss. The parent objective is to beat these two controls on every S1 cell ([LLP 3000.009.001.000.002 §1](llp/3000.009.001.000.002-beating-the-postgres-controls.rfc.md) and G9 in §10.8), which is also the 2026-10-27 sentence. Publish the 4,525 row. It decides nothing.

Landed R0 does not pass this replacement. hot1/B ties at 2,690/s, 5/5, with R0 4/5 and Supabase 0/5 at 3,200. Convex is absent from that window. Exact and hot1/A are single-rung checks, not ceilings. SPR8’s 450a matrix (`snapback4/reports/20261026-spike-r/REPORT.md` round 8; panel candidate `6c0fe17c6e`) is diagnostic: hot1/B 3,200 3/3+3/3 would break the tie if reproduced on 450b against both controls, and the retained queued exact-A 2/3 at 107.54 ms is why exact uses five repetitions.

Keyed W4 and the SDK 1% mix stay default-on obligations. They use this same win rule, with drains in the path. They do not block the start of R1–R5. S1’s published write path is unkeyed HTTP (parent §5). If they fail, default-on stays off and any claim says “unkeyed HTTP”.

## Q-B — Postgres CPU

Drop the +30% / ≤0.35 ms-per-write stop. Replace it, for the universal sentence and for default-on only, with one CPU-constrained cell: **W4/A-cpu**.

Same W4 workload, link A, SLO, fixture, durability (`fsync`, `synchronous_commit`, `full_page_writes`), 32 clients, and ≤32 product connections. Postgres is pinned to logical CPU 0 only. App/proxy stay on 3–4 and 9–10, generator on 5 and 11, `shared_buffers` unchanged at 8 GiB. Interleave the candidate, flag-off main, Supabase, and Convex. The candidate must be a strict win under the Q-A rule. This cell does not gate the start of R1–R5. It gates default-on and the sentence “every scenario”.

PG CPU decides the outcome when the database has about one core: a small managed instance, or many tenants sharing one Postgres. It does not decide the landed S1 cells. Those give Postgres logical CPUs 0–2 and 6–8. At the winning 3,805/s rung, authoritative R0 uses about 0.488–0.491 ms of Postgres CPU per acknowledgement, roughly 1.9 cores (`results-20261027-s1-r0-authoritative/DIAGNOSTICS.md`), inside that grant. The same-host round-4 comparison is R0 0.474 versus ordered main 0.185 ms/write, and WAL 811 versus main 616 B/ACK; raw PG on that window is 0.146 ms/write and 104 B/ACK (`REPORT.md` round 4). RFC 003 §8.1’s +30% stop, measured against round-2 main at about 0.245 ms, is already crossed. That ratio compares R to an older Snapback, not to Supabase or Convex, and it blocks a configuration that wins the cells the author named. WAL bytes stay in every report. They get no ratio stop: on this device the COMMIT gap versus raw was 0.058 ms (round 4), and syncs are about 0.09 per acknowledgement.

W4/A-cpu is the fair version of “many tenants” and “higher concurrency” that this harness can run. A noisy-neighbor multi-tenant model is out of scope; there is no interference pattern that treats all three systems fairly. If all three fail every rung down to 100/s, the cell is inconclusive and the universal sentence waits, rather than being declared from an unusable floor.

## Q-D — path

1. Amend RFC 003 as below. Score the campaign already running on 450b under that rule. A 4,525 miss does not fail a cell. A missing Convex leg, or a missing exact, hot1, or read leg, does not pass that cell.
2. Finish that campaign on the candidate that will be qualified: all six write cells as strict wins, reads and exact as strict wins and non-regressions, five repetitions, same commit flag-off, both controls. 450a SPR8 numbers do not carry.
3. If exact or hot1/B fails, fix and rerun those cells on 450b. hot1/B at a 2,690 tie ends the universal sentence until some later candidate takes a higher rung than both controls. Do not add an eighth-rung or a latency tie-break.
4. When step 2 passes, start R1–R5 behind the flag. The +30% CPU stop does not block them. Ordered L′ remains the fallback if step 2 fails.
5. R5 repeats the competitive campaign on the production candidate. Add keyed W4 and keyed mix, the 400/s mixed read, and W4/A-cpu.
6. The universal sentence becomes available only when step 5 passes, and only for that configuration. If R or G6 is still opt-in, the sentence says so. If keyed fails, the sentence says unkeyed HTTP and default-on stays off. If W4/A-cpu fails, the sentence names the dedicated three-core Postgres envelope and omits “every scenario”.
7. Keep SQLite as a separate SQW sentence. It is already 6/6 against historical natural-column controls, with the no-hop disclosure. It is not on the Postgres path.

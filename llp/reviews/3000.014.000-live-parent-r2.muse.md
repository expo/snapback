NOT READY

Revision 2 fixes the shape of the round-1 blockers but leaves the new bounded-polling machinery without executable definitions. The RFC's own §8.5 still lists three feasibility questions as unresolved, which trips its §6 NOT READY rule by itself.

What improved: poll-time enumeration replaces mutation-time fanout; time composition is refused instead of half-designed; G/A/birth supersedes the bare epoch; mixed chains get an admission matrix; revoke/regrant double-bump closes the delayed-frame hole.

Remaining blockers, all against §8 as normative:

**Security**

- Continuation and enumeration leakage are requirements, not algorithms. §8.3 says continuations are "server-authenticated" and bind `(G, A, viewer, table, last id)`, skip denied candidates "privately", and a page "may be capped without exporting a hidden continuation." No auth construction, no gap-leakage model for lexicographic skip (seeing ids 10 then 20 reveals 11–19 are denied/absent), and no resume semantics after a cap with no continuation. State the token format, unforgeability check, what a gap reveals, and what the device does after a hidden cap.
- Service-branch stripping for sync is ambiguous. §4 admitted sync "after removing whole service branches"; §8.3 says "only a single invertible parent audience is admitted." Say whether `(invertible OR service)` syncs with service stripped or is refused, and bind it in the binder. Stripped service principals must never gain a device audience per §1.
- Time-exclusion predicate is undefined. §8.1 refuses "transitive opening, authored expiry, inherited expiry, and expiring roster witnesses" but allows "membership existence." No syntactic decision procedure distinguishes a rule that probes roster existence from one that depends on its deadline or `now`. Without it the Codex-r1 hazard returns: an expiring witness ends authority with no A bump and a live descriptor survives. Give the binder predicate plus refused/accepted pairs.

**Correctness**

- A-increment predicate is imprecise. §8.3 bumps A "once for a transaction that edits any live parent policy input, parent existence, or roster table referenced by its read rule." Table-level bumps cause global restaging on unrelated roster writes; field-level bumps need a static input set and a per-transaction check that are not specified. Define the exact input set, where the hook runs, and what a non-policy parent-column write does. A miss is stale authority; over-bump is starvation.
- G/birth durability has no hook. §8.3 requires G "new on every adoption including rollback, never reused after reset/restore" and birth reservation "through existing monotonic commit history." No G generation, storage, compaction, or restore path is given, and no birth-sequence source or wire size is bound. Say what rotates G on restore-from-image and what travels in `C's own birth sequence`.
- Staging/publication lacks a frame contract. §8.4 names `Active(G,A)`/`Staging(G,A)`, idempotence by "acquisition id and page ordinal," one-store-transaction commits, and publication "only after authority enumeration and every requested child horizon have finished." No frame schema, no id/ordinal assigner, no cross-partition ordering between descriptor enumeration and per-scope `last N` acquisition, and no stale-vs-fresh verdict table for reads during staging. The existing device vocabulary in [snapshot.rs](/home/ccheever/projects/sb4-w14-rules4/snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/snapshot.rs) and [deferred.rs](/home/ccheever/projects/sb4-w14-rules4/snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/deferred.rs) has no manifest-swap term; either define the mapping or narrow the claim.
- Online-resolver isolation is underspecified. §8.2 reserves "1 parent point seek + declared decoder bound + read-rule probe price," evaluates "entire read rule under the same viewer and reserved internal ledger," then restores. It does not say whether evaluation sees the transaction's own uncommitted policy writes (§3 says it must), whether `rule_probes()` in [through_read.rs](/home/ccheever/projects/sb4-w14-rules4/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/through_read.rs:30) plus `row_copy_ceiling` is the exact worst-case bound for OR-heavy lane rules, or how a device online read finds the authority descriptor versus returning Unknown. The planner tag distinguishing live verdicts from capture-match witnesses is asserted, not specified.
- Supersession map is missing. §8 "supersedes conflicting §2–5 choices," but §§2–4 still normatively describe mutable-expiry minimums, pinned-page uniformity, aggregate inheritance, scope grants, and atomic batches. List exactly which §2–5 sentences survive; an implementer cannot guess.

**Feasibility and scope**

- The three §8.5 qualifications are open design work: full-manifest replacement versus shipped client vocabulary, parent-id acquisition under account admission via existing partition/index paths, and indefinite starvation under frequent A cuts ("can postpone fresh publication indefinitely"). Whole-manifest-or-nothing with unbounded postponement is a liveness defect for an end-game sync form. Either bound it (per-scope freshness, incremental manifest patches, priority) or record it as an accepted limitation with a progress guarantee.
- Lane fit is a scope gap. §8.1 correctly refuses sync for the actual lane 085 grievance OR and lane 007 lead/support/partner OR, covering them only via the online resolver. State plainly that initial sync ships invertible parents only and lanes 007/085 ship online-only children, with acceptance criteria. Without a binder refusal tying `sync` on a live child to the single-invertible-audience check, a later edit can silently widen either lane to one roster.
- Adoption/rollback remain unbounded prose. §8.5 "validates every live reference" under the "ordinary adoption budget" with full rollback, but gives no chunking, metering, or operator-output contract for the scan.

To flip: define A/G/birth storage and hooks, the continuation token, the time-exclusion predicate, the frame/manifest contract with a starvation bound, the service-branch rule, the §2–5 survival list, and the sync-admission refusal; then re-review those bounded representations.

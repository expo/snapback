### 1. Product contract

The product is a multi-tenant analytics service for web, mobile, and server applications. Engineers ingest behavioral events; data administrators define dimensions, metrics, and identity rules; analysts explore data; product teams consume dashboards; and security administrators control whether a person may inspect source rows, compute protected aggregates, or export results.

It makes these promises:

1. Producers may submit individual events or retryable batches. A repeated source/event identifier with the same payload counts once; reuse with a different payload is quarantined rather than silently overwriting data.

2. Producer event time and trusted receipt time are preserved separately. Accepted events normally appear in a queryable snapshot within 60 seconds, with explicit per-source freshness watermarks.

3. Analysts may filter and group across the full retained history by any registered dimension they are authorized to analyze, without requiring a purpose-built cube for each combination.

4. Counts, distinct subjects, sums, averages, ratios, top groups, and percentiles are supported. Queries may scan an unbounded number of source rows; large jobs queue or spill to background execution rather than silently sampling or truncating.

5. Analysts may define ordered funnels, fixed or dynamic cohorts, and retention curves, including breakdowns by event-time or current profile dimensions.

6. Every temporal query states its timezone, half-open time interval, event-time versus receipt-time choice, identity revision, and whether profile values mean “at event time” or “as of this run.”

7. Exact mode aggregates only rows the analyst may inspect individually. Protected mode may aggregate a broader population, but returns only privacy-protected estimates and never source rows or direct identifiers.

8. Protected results use subject-level contribution bounds, protected group-label discovery, suppression based on noisy population size, sticky noise, and a privacy budget shared across analysts, devices, dashboards, APIs, and exports in the same disclosure audience.

9. Drilling into an exact result returns its contributing readable rows. Drilling into a protected result returns only the caller’s currently readable subset and clearly says that protected contributors may be omitted.

10. Every dashboard refresh pins one data snapshot for all tiles. A late response from an older filter selection cannot replace a newer result.

11. Saving or sharing a report saves its definition, not the author’s authority. Every viewer is reauthorized, and cached results are separated by snapshot, policy version, audience, and exact-versus-protected mode.

12. Reports can be exported as CSV, JSON, or Parquet on an IANA-timezone schedule. Aggregate-only email delivery uses an authenticated expiring link; delivery to an external object store requires an administrator-approved destination.

13. Late events, historical corrections, and identity changes revise later runs rather than mutating an already issued result. Revised periods are labeled.

14. Identity merges, splits, legal erasure, dimension changes, and metric-definition changes are versioned and auditable.

15. Every result records the exact query definition, data cutoff, watermarks, schema and metric versions, identity revision, access-policy version, privacy release, and result digest needed to reproduce it.

16. Reports, dashboards, cohorts, and schedules use immutable revisions. Concurrent or multi-device edits merge only when their semantic paths are disjoint; conflicting query edits require an explicit choice or fork.

17. SDKs may queue events offline while preserving their original event times. Analysts cannot execute new analysis offline; administrator-enabled cached results are encrypted, visibly dated, and expire within 24 hours, with protected-result caching disabled by default.

18. Ingestion, interactive dashboards, exploratory scans, exports, repair jobs, and privacy erasure have isolated resource pools. A costly query may wait or fail explicitly, but it cannot return an incomplete answer labeled complete.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** No principal may learn data belonging to another tenant through rows, aggregates, labels, errors, timing, caches, or exports.

2. **[TIME]** One accepted source/event identifier represents one immutable logical event, regardless of retries or arrival order.

3. **[TIME]** The system never replaces producer event time with receipt time, even when an event arrives late or from an offline device.

4. **[TIME]** Every result is computed from one immutable snapshot, and a dashboard claiming cross-tile consistency uses the same snapshot for every tile.

5. An execution limit may stop a query, but it may never silently change the query’s population, discard groups, or present a partial result as complete.

6. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Row-read scope and aggregate-computation scope are separate grants, and neither one implies the other.

7. **[SECRECY]** If protected scope contributes to a result, the entire result follows the protected path even when some contributing rows are individually readable.

8. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** The influence of one protected subject is bounded before joins and aggregation, regardless of that subject’s event, device, alias, or tag count.

9. **[SECRECY]** Repeated, overlapping, complementary, or algebraically equivalent queries must compose under one published privacy allowance rather than exposing independent exact or noisy answers.

10. **[SECRECY]** Group labels, ranks, denominators, totals, “Other,” empty-versus-suppressed states, refusals, pagination counts, and observable cost information are disclosures subject to the same protection as numeric cells.

11. **[MONEY] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A protected money metric must declare its currency, clipping range, and per-subject contribution bound, and every clipped result must be labeled.

12. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Direct identifiers and raw events may leave the service only under current row-read authority.

13. **[TIME] [SECRECY]** Historical dimensions may be evaluated as they were at event time, but authorization is always evaluated under the caller’s current grants.

14. **[TIME] [SECRECY]** A saved definition, dashboard cache, cohort, scheduled job, or old download link never preserves authority that has been revoked.

15. **[SECRECY]** Every privacy release reserves its shared budget atomically before any data-dependent response becomes observable.

16. **[TIME]** Late events, corrections, and identity revisions create new analytical generations while previously issued results retain their recorded meaning.

17. **[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** An identity merge or split cannot silently change an in-flight query or rewrite the lineage of an older result.

18. **[TIME] [SECRECY]** A long-running query is authorized both when submitted and immediately before its result is released.

19. **[TIME]** Two devices editing the same analytical definition never resolve a semantic conflict through silent last-write-wins behavior.

20. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Erasure takes effect through a read-blocking tombstone before physical cleanup begins, while already downloaded external files are reported as irretrievable rather than falsely claimed revoked.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Exact “safe-sized” aggregates reconstruct hidden facts.**

   - **Wrong version:** Permit exact answers whenever a cohort contains at least 20 or 100 people.
   - **Why wrong:** Arbitrary filters permit differencing, complements, high-cardinality groups, and repeated exclusions; a large cohort does not prevent one person’s contribution from being isolated.
   - **Observation:** “Converted enterprise users” returns 4,201, while the same query excluding Alice returns 4,200, revealing Alice’s conversion even though both populations contain thousands of people.

2. **Applying row visibility before aggregation produces confidently false BI.**

   - **Wrong version:** Reuse the analyst’s row filter as the aggregate input filter.
   - **Why wrong:** Aggregate authority is intentionally broader than row-read authority, so filtering first changes the business population rather than protecting the result.
   - **Observation:** An analyst authorized to aggregate 4,812 customers but inspect none receives a retention denominator of zero; another analyst with access to one region receives a company-wide dashboard containing only that region.

3. **There is no single historical “now.”**

   - **Wrong version:** Use receipt time, the latest profile, and the latest identity map for every historical query.
   - **Why wrong:** Event occurrence, ingestion, profile validity, identity belief, definition publication, and query execution are different clocks.
   - **Observation:** An offline January 2 signup arrives in March and precedes a recorded purchase; it moves the person’s funnel entry and every retention period, while a concurrent identity merge changes whether two devices count as one subject.

4. **Reports and exports become authority-laundering artifacts.**

   - **Wrong version:** Let a shared dashboard reuse its creator’s cache or let a schedule continue under authority captured when it was created.
   - **Why wrong:** The artifact contains the result of privileged computation and can outlive role, region, customer-classification, or recipient changes.
   - **Observation:** EU access is revoked while an export runs, yet the weekly CSV still arrives; alternatively, an aggregate-only viewer opens an exact result cached by a privileged author.

5. **An ad-hoc aggregate is not a bounded row query.**

   - **Wrong version:** Fetch some maximum number of matching events into application memory, aggregate them, and call a timeout or row cap a successful result.
   - **Why wrong:** Input rows, per-subject sequence state, and output groups are all data-dependent and may be enormous; arbitrary dimensions defeat precomputed-cube enumeration.
   - **Observation:** A 90-day query scans 6.75 billion events and grouping by URL creates 200 million groups, while the naive implementation returns the first 100,000 rows as an apparently complete chart.

6. **Concurrent and offline edits can create a report nobody authored.**

   - **Wrong version:** Use last-write-wins or blindly merge field-level changes.
   - **Why wrong:** Funnel steps, conversion window, denominator, timezone, attribution mode, and identity mode are semantically coupled even when stored in separate fields.
   - **Observation:** Device A changes a funnel from signup-to-purchase to trial-to-renewal while offline; device B changes entry attribution and the conversion window; an automatic merge publishes a hybrid definition neither analyst reviewed.

### 4. Data model

All identifiers are opaque UUIDs unless stated otherwise; timestamps are UTC instants, while every calendar operation also stores an IANA timezone.

- `Organization(org_id PK, name, default_timezone, retention_days, authorization_epoch int64)`.
- `Principal(principal_id PK, kind user|service|scheduler, status)`.
- `MembershipVersion(org_id, principal_id, role, valid_from, valid_to?, PK(org_id, principal_id, valid_from))` and equivalent `TeamMembershipVersion`; both are append-only and time-bounded.
- `Dataset(dataset_id PK, org_id FK, name, created_at)` and `Source(source_id PK, dataset_id FK, credential_digest, allowed_event_types, status)`.

Ingestion uses:

- `IngestionBatch(source_id, batch_id, body_digest, state, accepted_count, duplicate_count, rejected_count, received_at, committed_at?, PK(source_id,batch_id))`.
- `Event(dataset_id, source_id, event_id, ingest_sequence int64, event_type_id, occurred_at, received_at, actor_key_id?, session_id?, schema_version, payload_digest, raw_payload_pointer?, PK(dataset_id,source_id,event_id), UNIQUE(dataset_id,ingest_sequence))`.
- `EventDimensionValue(dataset_id, ingest_sequence, dimension_id, string_value?|decimal_value?|boolean_value?|timestamp_value?, PK(dataset_id,ingest_sequence,dimension_id))`.
- `QuarantinedEvent(source_id, event_id, payload_digest, reason_code, first_seen_at, last_seen_at)`.
- `EventCorrection(correction_id PK, target_event_key, operation replace_dimension|exclude|restore, value?, asserted_at, effective_from_snapshot)`.

`Event`, its dimension values, and corrections are append-only. An event correction changes query interpretation through an overlay; it does not rewrite the received fact.

Semantics use:

- `EventTypeVersion(event_type_id, version, name, field_schema, valid_from, valid_to?, PK(event_type_id,version))`.
- `DimensionVersion(dimension_id, version, stable_key, label, scope event|subject|account, value_type, classification public|internal|confidential|direct_identifier, source_expression, attribution_modes, valid_from, valid_to?, PK(dimension_id,version))`.
- `MetricVersion(metric_id, version, name, expression_tree, aggregation, unit, currency?, protected_eligible, clipping_min?, clipping_max?, max_contributions_per_subject, PK(metric_id,version))`.

An incompatible type or meaning receives a new dimension or metric identifier; labels may change by version without changing identity.

Identity and profile history use:

- `Subject(org_id, subject_id, PK(org_id,subject_id))`.
- `IdentityKey(org_id, namespace, value_hmac, encrypted_value?, destroyed_at?, PK(org_id,namespace,value_hmac))`.
- `IdentityAssignmentVersion(identity_key_id, subject_id, effective_from, effective_to?, system_from, system_to?, identity_revision, PK(identity_key_id,system_from))`.
- `SubjectAttributeVersion(subject_id, dimension_id, typed_value, effective_from, effective_to?, system_from, system_to?, source_priority, PK(subject_id,dimension_id,effective_from,system_from))`.
- `IdentityGraphRevision(org_id, revision, prior_revision, created_at, reason, PK(org_id,revision))`.

The `effective_*` interval records when a fact is asserted to be true in the product’s world; the `system_*` interval records when the analytics service believed it. These tables are bitemporal and append-only.

Authorization and aggregate disclosure use:

- `AccessPolicyVersion(policy_id, version, row_read_predicate, aggregate_scan_predicate, allowed_event_types, allowed_dimensions, allowed_metrics, privacy_policy_id?, created_at, superseded_at?, PK(policy_id,version))`.
- `PolicyGrantVersion(grant_id, version, principal_or_team_id, policy_id, policy_version, valid_from, valid_to?, PK(grant_id,version))`.
- `PrivacyPolicy(policy_id PK, privacy_unit, horizon_days, epsilon_cap, delta_cap, minimum_noisy_units, release_epoch_duration, maximum_groups_per_unit, noise_mechanism)`.

The default protected policy uses resolved subject as the privacy unit, a rolling 365-day accounting horizon, total \(\epsilon=4\), \(\delta=10^{-6}\), a noised release threshold of 50 subjects, daily fixed data-release epochs, at most 20 countable events per subject per UTC day per cell, and at most eight contributed groups per subject per query. Sums use the metric’s declared clipping interval; a funnel or retention cell receives at most one contribution per subject. Charges do not reset per user, browser, or calendar day.

- `PrivacyLedger(audience_id, policy_id, accounting_window, normalized_query_hash, release_epoch_id, snapshot_id, epsilon_debit, delta_debit, state reserved|released|abandoned, result_digest?, noise_seed_reference, PK(audience_id,policy_id,release_epoch_id,normalized_query_hash))`.

Equivalent queries in the same release epoch reuse one sticky result. A dashboard refresh stream reserves its future disclosure schedule rather than receiving free independently noised snapshots.

Query state uses:

- `DataSnapshot(snapshot_id PK, dataset_id, included_received_at, partition_manifest_digest, source_watermarks, identity_revision, schema_revision, correction_revision, erasure_revision, created_at)`.
- `QueryRun(run_id PK, principal_id, normalized_query_tree, query_kind, mode exact|protected, snapshot_id, policy_version, authorization_epoch, schema_and_metric_versions, identity_revision, timezone, status, result_pointer?, result_digest?, safe_cost_statistics)`.
- `Cohort(cohort_id PK, dataset_id, owner_id, name, head_version)` and `CohortDefinitionVersion(cohort_id, version, predicate_tree, mode dynamic|first_qualified_fixed|frozen, entry_rule, exit_rule, lookback, timezone, attribution_mode, base_version, PK(cohort_id,version))`.
- `CohortGeneration(cohort_id, definition_version, generation, snapshot_id, identity_revision, state, subject_count, PK(cohort_id,definition_version,generation))` and derived `CohortMembership(generation, subject_id, membership_start, membership_end?, qualifying_event_key?)`.

Saved objects use immutable `ReportVersion`, `DashboardVersion`, and `ExportScheduleVersion` tables. A report version stores the complete query tree, metric versions, timezone, time range, attribution and identity modes, ordering, and visualization. A dashboard version stores tile UUIDs, placements, pinned report versions, and global-filter mappings. A schedule version stores its cron expression, timezone, daylight-saving rule, report-selection rule, recipients, destination, format, freshness deadline, and generation.

`ExportRun` is unique on `(schedule_id, nominal_occurrence, schedule_generation)` and records the selected report version, snapshot, authorization epoch, artifact pointer, and digest. `DeliveryOutbox` and append-only `DeliveryAttempt` rows provide retryable external delivery.

`PrivacyTombstone(subject_id, request_id, effective_at, state)` immediately removes a subject logically. `AuditEntry` is append-only. Replaceable derived tables—columnar partitions, dimension dictionaries, common rollups, distinct-count sketches, funnel indexes, and cohort bitmaps—always name their source snapshot and generation; they are accelerators, never authoritative facts or restrictions on query shape.

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Ingest an idempotent event batch.**  
   **Inputs:** source credential, batch and chunk identifiers, and events containing source event ID, name, producer timestamp, actor key, session, and properties. **Authorization:** derive organization, dataset, and allowed event types from the credential, never from the payload. **Steps:** (1) hash and compare any existing batch receipt; (2) resolve schema at trusted receipt time; (3) validate types and quarantine malformed events; (4) HMAC identity keys and extract typed dimensions; (5) insert each new event; (6) treat an equal digest as a duplicate and a different digest under the same ID as a conflict; (7) commit chunk counts; (8) enqueue compaction and snapshot publication. **Atomic:** each event, its dimensions, and its chunk receipt. **Eventual:** snapshot visibility. **Optimistic UI:** an SDK may say “accepted,” never “included in analytics.” **Conflict:** identical retries are no-ops; conflicting IDs are quarantined.

2. **Register or evolve a dimension or metric.**  
   **Inputs:** base version, expression, type, classification, attribution modes, unit, privacy bounds, and requested historical range. **Authorization:** data administrator; lowering classification or expanding protected eligibility additionally requires a security administrator. **Steps:** (1) compare with the current version; (2) retain the identifier only for compatible changes; (3) publish the new version for future ingestion; (4) scan retained partitions into an unpublished projection generation; (5) validate coverage and conversion errors; (6) atomically publish that generation. **Atomic:** version-head and generation-head swaps. **Eventual:** historical backfill. **Optimistic UI:** show “collecting” or “backfilling.” **Conflict:** a stale base or incompatible reinterpretation fails explicitly.

3. **Change aggregate or row access.**  
   **Inputs:** base policy version, row predicate, aggregate predicate, allowed fields and metrics, privacy policy, grant interval, and reason. **Authorization:** security administrator; widening aggregate scope requires a data-administrator countersignature. **Steps:** (1) validate predicates without inspecting hidden cardinalities; (2) create immutable policy and grant versions; (3) atomically advance their heads and increment `authorization_epoch`; (4) fence in-flight publication, cached results, schedules, and download tokens; (5) enqueue physical cache deletion. **Atomic:** policy publication and epoch increment. **Eventual:** worker cancellation and cache cleanup, while every access gate observes revocation immediately. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Conflict:** stale policy versions must be rebased.

4. **Correct a profile or merge/split identities.**  
   **Inputs:** identity keys, effective interval, corrected attribute or merge/split allocation, reason, and expected graph revision. **Authorization:** trusted profile source for attribute assertions; identity administrator for merges and splits. **Steps:** (1) validate tenant ownership and interval consistency; (2) append bitemporal assignments or attribute assertions; (3) resolve overlaps by declared source priority, never arrival order; (4) create and atomically publish a new identity revision; (5) invalidate current-identity caches; (6) rebuild affected cohort and aggregate generations. **Atomic:** all assignments plus graph-revision publication. **Eventual:** historical reattribution. **Optimistic UI:** “revision accepted; rebuilding.” **Conflict:** equal-priority overlapping facts or a stale graph revision require explicit resolution.

5. **Run an ad-hoc aggregate.**  
   **Inputs:** exact or protected mode, half-open time range, arbitrary Boolean filters, group dimensions, metric versions, timezone, dimension attribution, identity mode, sorting, and requested output form. **Authorization:** validate every reference against the current policy; exact mode injects row scope, while protected mode injects aggregate scope and rejects direct-identifier output. **Steps:** (1) normalize equivalent query expressions; (2) bind one snapshot, release epoch, policy, schema, metrics, and identity revision; (3) estimate resources without exposing hidden cardinalities; (4) for protected mode, atomically reuse or reserve a privacy release; (5) scan all qualifying partitions using distributed partial aggregation and spill as needed; (6) bound per-subject contributions; (7) select group labels through a protected heavy-hitter mechanism, add sticky noise, and apply the noised population threshold; (8) make totals and child cells coherent through non-disclosing post-processing; (9) persist the full result and lineage; (10) revalidate authorization before publishing pages. **Atomic:** privacy reservation and final result publication; the scan itself uses the immutable snapshot. **Eventual:** later data appears only in later runs. **Optimistic UI:** stale authorized results and progress, never predicted values. **Conflict:** identical concurrent queries share one release; distinct queries racing for the remaining budget either commit a debit or receive no result.

6. **Evaluate an ordered funnel.**  
   **Inputs:** two to twelve event predicates, entry rule, conversion window, re-entry rule, grouping attribution point, range, and timezone. **Authorization:** aggregate permission for every event and dimension; subject sequences are never returned under protected mode. **Steps:** (1) bind the normal query versions; (2) resolve events to subjects; (3) order by `(occurred_at, source_sequence if present, received_at, source_id, event_id)`; (4) select the first valid entry and then the earliest valid later event for each step within the half-open conversion window; (5) attribute the breakdown at entry unless another point is explicit; (6) cap journeys per subject; (7) jointly release monotone step counts and conversion-time summaries. **Atomic:** privacy release. **Eventual:** late events affect a later snapshot. **Optimistic UI:** progress only. **Conflict:** cancellation stops computation but does not refund a privacy charge after any data-dependent result became observable.

7. **Create or materialize a cohort.**  
   **Inputs:** cohort ID, base version, behavioral predicate, lookback, entry and exit rules, fixed or dynamic mode, attribution, and timezone. **Authorization:** editors may reference only aggregate-authorized fields; saving never grants permission to list members. **Steps:** (1) normalize the definition; (2) append a version and compare-and-swap its head; (3) evaluate required history against one snapshot; (4) write membership intervals into an unpublished generation; (5) validate it; (6) publish the generation atomically; (7) enqueue incremental refreshes for dynamic cohorts. **Atomic:** definition-head and generation-head swaps. **Eventual:** membership construction. **Optimistic UI:** definition edits may remain local, but counts remain “building.” **Conflict:** disjoint metadata changes may merge; overlapping predicate changes create explicit competing drafts.

8. **Compute a retention curve.**  
   **Inputs:** entry cohort generation, return-event predicate, cohort range, period unit, calendar-versus-elapsed mode, period count, timezone, and re-entry rule. **Authorization:** aggregate access to entry and return populations. **Steps:** (1) bind one snapshot and identity revision; (2) choose each subject’s single declared entry instant; (3) assign cohort dates in the requested timezone; (4) construct exact half-open period boundaries; (5) count a subject at most once per cohort-period; (6) use the initial eligible cohort as denominator unless survival semantics are explicitly selected; (7) return `null`, not zero, for future or watermark-incomplete cells; (8) jointly protect numerators and denominators and derive percentages afterward. **Atomic:** protected release only. **Eventual:** late returns revise later runs. **Optimistic UI:** a prior curve may remain with a stale label. **Conflict:** none; snapshot pinning makes the run deterministic.

9. **Drill from a result cell into events.**  
   **Inputs:** originating run and cell, requested columns, and pagination cursor. **Authorization:** reauthorize under current row-read scope; aggregate authority contributes nothing. **Steps:** (1) verify access to the originating result; (2) reconstruct the cell predicate against its snapshot; (3) intersect it with current row and column permissions; (4) return stable pages ordered by event time and event key; (5) state that protected contributors may be omitted. **Atomic:** immutable-snapshot reads only. **Eventual:** none. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Conflict:** an authorization-epoch change invalidates the cursor; zero readable rows beside a nonzero protected estimate is valid.

10. **Save, share, or merge a report/dashboard.**  
    **Inputs:** resource ID, base version, query or layout path operations, title, and audience. **Authorization:** current edit and share permission; every referenced field is validated. **Steps:** (1) load base and current immutable versions; (2) three-way merge disjoint paths such as title and tile position; (3) mark overlapping metric, predicate, funnel, timezone, denominator, attribution, or ordering edits as semantic conflicts; (4) append the accepted version and atomically advance the head; (5) store the audience and audit entry without attaching cached authority. **Atomic:** version insertion, head swap, and audit. **Eventual:** thumbnails and results rerun under each viewer. **Optimistic UI:** offline edits remain local and “unsynced.” **Conflict:** return base, current, proposed, and conflicting paths for resolution or fork.

11. **Load a dashboard consistently.**  
    **Inputs:** dashboard version, global filters, viewer timezone, and optional cached run ID. **Authorization:** reauthorize every tile under the viewer’s current policy. **Steps:** (1) resolve immutable dashboard and report versions; (2) choose one data snapshot and protected release epoch; (3) map global filters into each tile; (4) execute or reuse only cache entries with matching policy, audience, mode, and authorization epoch; (5) publish one versioned envelope containing all tiles and shared freshness metadata. **Atomic:** protected reservations for the envelope are all-or-none. **Eventual:** refresh creates a new run. **Optimistic UI:** a still-authorized prior envelope may display as stale; offline use requires an unexpired encrypted entitlement. **Conflict:** concurrent edits cannot affect the already selected version, and late responses cannot replace a newer run ID.

12. **Configure and execute a scheduled export.**  
    **Inputs:** base schedule generation, report-selection rule, cron, timezone, daylight-saving behavior, freshness deadline, format, destination, and recipients. **Authorization:** schedule manager plus authority to send the data classification to every destination; execution is system-only. **Steps:** (1) validate the schedule, including spring gaps and repeated autumn times; (2) verify the external destination; (3) publish an immutable schedule generation; (4) at each nominal occurrence, uniquely claim `(schedule, occurrence, generation)`; (5) reauthorize the service principal and recipients; (6) select the report version and snapshot; (7) execute with normal exact or protected rules; (8) render one immutable encrypted artifact; (9) atomically store its digest and an outbox item; (10) recheck policy and generation immediately before delivery; (11) send using a stable external idempotency key and record every attempt. **Atomic:** schedule publication, run claim, and internal artifact/outbox commit; external delivery cannot share that transaction. **Eventual:** rendering, delivery, and retries. **Optimistic UI:** draft schedules remain disabled until verification; runs show computing, ready, sending, delivered, ambiguous, or failed. **Conflict:** old generations are fenced; destinations without idempotency may receive the same artifact twice after an ambiguous timeout.

13. **Revoke access or erase a subject.**  
    **Inputs:** grant or subject identity keys, effective time, reason/request ID, and expected revision. **Authorization:** security administrator for revocation; privacy administrator for erasure. **Steps:** (1) for revocation, append the new grant interval and atomically advance `authorization_epoch`; (2) for erasure, resolve every matching identity revision and atomically insert tombstones plus a durable work manifest; (3) block new reads and result publication immediately; (4) destroy identifiers or keys and purge raw payloads; (5) rebuild affected partitions, cohorts, caches, and future privacy epochs; (6) expire hosted artifacts; (7) record external files that cannot be recalled. **Atomic:** epoch advance or tombstone plus work manifest. **Eventual:** cancellation, physical purge, and rebuild. **Optimistic UI:** “effective; cleanup pending,” never premature “fully erased.” **Conflict:** request IDs are idempotent; concurrent erasures coalesce while retaining every legal request.

14. **Seal snapshots and rebuild derived state periodically.**  
    **Inputs:** committed ingestion sequences, correction and erasure revisions, source watermarks, prior snapshot, and generation token. **Authorization:** scheduler-only. **Steps:** (1) identify fully committed partitions; (2) compact event and dimension columns; (3) apply correction and erasure overlays; (4) update dictionaries, common rollups, sketches, funnel indexes, and dynamic cohort deltas; (5) validate counts and source coverage; (6) write an immutable partition manifest; (7) atomically publish a new snapshot; (8) retire obsolete generations only after no run references them. **Atomic:** manifest publication. **Eventual:** all bulk derivation; queries may fall back to raw columns while an accelerator rebuilds. **Optimistic UI:** watermarks advance only after publication. **Conflict:** only the worker holding the current generation token may publish; obsolete workers discard their output.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment has roughly:

- 1,500 organizations and 75,000 monthly analysts or viewers.
- 200 billion retained events over 400 days.
- 500 million new events per day: about 5,800 per second on average, 40,000 per second at normal peaks, and 150,000 per second during retries or warehouse replays.
- A largest tenant with 30 billion retained events, 75 million new events per day, 500 million subjects, and several billion identity aliases and profile versions.
- Approximately 900 bytes per raw event with 10–30 sparse properties: about 180 TB of logical payload and roughly 3 trillion property values before replicas, indexes, sketches, and rollups.
- Up to 300 registered dimensions per tenant; URL, account, session, search-term, and person-like dimensions may have tens or hundreds of millions of distinct values.
- 300,000 dashboards, 2 million reports, 50,000 cohorts, and 60,000 scheduled runs per day.
- Morning bursts in which 10,000 viewers open 15-tile dashboards within a minute, producing 150,000 tile requests and roughly 100–400 distinct coalesced jobs per second.
- Around 1% of events arriving more than ten minutes late and 0.05% arriving more than a day late.

A largest-tenant 90-day query can inspect 6.75 billion events and scan 300 GB–1.5 TB of compressed columns. Grouping by three dimensions can create more than ten million intermediate groups; grouping by URL or search term can produce 50–500 million output groups.

A five-step 30-day funnel may order 2.25 billion events by subject while retaining partial state for tens of millions of subjects. A 52-week retention curve can require distinct-subject state for thousands of cohort-period-segment cells. Exact distinct counts require large distributed sets; sketches reduce cost but change the accuracy contract.

Late data and identity revisions are multiplicative repair costs. One replayed day can revise event counts, earliest-event dates, cohort assignment, every later retention cell, funnels, alerts, and dashboard caches. An identity merge can change all subject-based metrics without changing one event.

Precomputing every cube is infeasible: 100 candidate dimensions already yield 4,950 pairs and 161,700 triples before accounting for value cardinality, time grain, metric, policy scope, or cohort definition.

Common dashboards should usually answer from shared derivations within seconds. Cold arbitrary queries may take minutes or hours. Complete high-cardinality results stream asynchronously; interactive presentation may show the first 10,000 groups, but this is explicitly a view onto a complete artifact rather than a hidden answer cap.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Purpose-limited aggregate authority.** The engine must scan rows the caller cannot receive, prevent application code from observing them, inspect the requested filters and measures, and independently govern labels and output cells. Ordinary row security either filters the inputs and returns a wrong answer or grants a dangerously general bypass.  
   **Possible concession:** allow only approved bounded measures and non-identifying registered dimensions in protected mode.  
   **Cheat boundary:** using a privileged general-purpose handler and trusting it not to emit rows, filtering by row visibility, or calling exact arbitrary hidden-row aggregates “private.”

2. **Distributed, spillable ad-hoc analytical execution.** Arbitrary group-by, distinct count, percentile, top-group, ordered funnel, and retention operations over billions of rows require partition pruning, vectorized scans, distributed aggregation, disk spill, cancellation, and resumability.  
   **Possible concession:** queue large jobs, show only a paged interactive preview, and permit labeled approximate distinct counts or percentiles.  
   **Cheat boundary:** silent sampling, hidden row or group caps, fetching history into application memory, or requiring a new cube whenever an analyst chooses a new dimension.

3. **Composable disclosure accounting.** The backend must canonicalize equivalent queries, bound subject contribution before joins, discover group labels safely, atomically debit a budget shared across every delivery surface, and prevent concurrent independent noise samples from being averaged.  
   **Possible concession:** fixed release epochs, slower protected-dashboard refreshes, finite query budgets, and a smaller protected metric algebra.  
   **Cheat boundary:** per-browser budgets, daily resets that ignore overlapping historical data, minimum-cell rules without composition, or free refreshes whenever one new event changes the snapshot.

4. **Versioned temporal derivation with retractions.** Events, corrections, profiles, identities, definitions, erasure, cohorts, rollups, and watermarks must converge without rewriting previously issued results. Funnels and retention need ordered per-subject history, not independent row aggregates.  
   **Possible concession:** a declared correction window, forward-only identity merges, and visibly provisional recent periods.  
   **Cheat boundary:** accepting late data while leaving derived metrics stale, applying the current profile as historical truth, or mixing identity revisions within one result.

5. **Snapshot-bound asynchronous jobs and governed external artifacts.** Long jobs must bind immutable definitions and snapshots, survive retries, reauthorize before release, and coordinate a local result with an external delivery that cannot be transactionally committed.  
   **Possible concession:** at-least-once delivery of one immutable artifact and independently labeled tile freshness when a consistent dashboard snapshot is too expensive.  
   **Cheat boundary:** mixing pre- and post-backfill tiles under one freshness label, emailing permanent attachments while promising revocation, or letting one exploratory query starve ingestion and other tenants.

### 8. Confidence

Immutable event logs, idempotent ingestion, columnar analytical execution, event-time watermarks, bitemporal identity and profile history, snapshot lineage, versioned report definitions, optimistic concurrency, workload isolation, outboxes, and asynchronous exports are established analytical-system practices.

The exact deployment figures, 60-second freshness target, merge rules, 24-hour offline expiry, privacy thresholds, contribution bounds, release cadence, and \((\epsilon,\delta)\) defaults are concrete design choices invented for this exercise and would need workload measurement, privacy review, and user research.

The incompatibility between exact unlimited adaptive aggregation and strong individual non-inference is not invented. The protected mode is deliberately opinionated because omitting noise, contribution bounds, and compositional accounting would make “aggregate-only” an interface distinction rather than a secrecy boundary.

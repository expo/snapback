For ordinary application tables, the server argument is sound: capture and parent lookup share the transaction; references and captured inputs cannot change; structural deletion checks survive system authority; and recursive minimum deadlines preserve the invariant through three or more levels. `insert parent; insert child; delete parent` refuses. Deleting the child first permits deletion. Upsert patches cannot retarget the relationship. Nonexpiring maintained-column totals reach zero before reuse.

The remaining findings are:

1. **High — §§2–4, 7: generated aggregate rows are not safe parents under this lifetime mechanism.**

   The RFC does not restrict parents to ordinary application tables. The binder admits references to generated maintain tables through its [table catalog](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/catalog.rs:166).

   **Probe:** A public count view has one contributing row, expiring at 100. Insert a child reading through that aggregate row. The aggregate table declares no expiry, so the child captures “never.” At 100, the parent’s ordinary read becomes absent through [live aggregate recomputation](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:241), while the child remains live and publicly readable. Sweeping the last source subsequently deletes the aggregate through [aggregate maintenance](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/maintains.rs:206).

   Routing physical deletes through the new guard would not fix the earlier logical disappearance.

   **Smallest change:** restrict `read through` targets to ordinary application tables in this revision; explicitly refuse maintained and other engine-owned parent relations.

2. **High — §3.3 and §5: server cleanup does not prevent a reused parent from meeting retained device children.**

   **Probe:** A device holds nonexpiring issue `p` in org A and child `c`, capturing A. Children retain delivered history. The viewer leaves A. The server then deletes `c`, deletes `p`, and recreates `p` in B—all permitted because no physical server child remains. The viewer joins B and receives the replacement parent.

   The device can still hold retained `c` from A. Its deadline is never, so advancing the expiry clock changes nothing. Retention across replacement acquisition is intentional in [snapshot handling](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/snapshot.rs:12).

   Now `c`’s captures differ from its locally live parent. The claimed single-parent verdict cannot classify every stored row selected by `.issue = p` as belonging to B’s current relation. Conversely, applying the resolver’s unconditional empty result after withdrawal hides history that the child’s leave policy preserves. Existing [retained-read authorization](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:314) deliberately distinguishes this policy.

   **Smallest change:** scope the equality proof and parent-verdict optimization to current acquired child images. Specify a separate retained-history path that preserves the child’s leave policy and cannot contribute old images to the replacement parent’s current pages, counts or completeness proof. The demonstrated defect is incorrect relationship/completeness semantics; it does not establish disclosure to a never-authorized viewer.

3. **Medium — §7, step 2: first-adoption audience correspondence is under-specified.**

   **Probe:** The existing child uses `tenant`:

   ```quarry
   read <- exists members[.tenant, viewer]
   ```

   Its parent uses `org`. Child `c` has `tenant = A`, references `p`, and `p.org = B`. Adopt `read through .issue`, dropping `tenant`.

   Comparing existing columns “by the parent rule’s field names” finds no child `org`. A plausible name-intersection implementation skips the comparison, then captures B and publishes A’s old content to B. Comparing before projection preserves the evidence, but does not identify which evidence to compare.

   **Smallest change:** define the correspondence between previous child audience inputs and parent inputs. Refuse initial adoption when that correspondence is missing or ambiguous; an explicit mapping can support differently named copies. Missing correspondence must never mean “nothing disagreed.”

4. **Medium — §10 versus §5: the revised chain fixture cannot satisfy its device-prediction acceptance criterion.**

   **Probe:** Projects read under `exists members[.org, viewer] and not .archived`; tasks and comments inherit that complete rule. The conjunction is not one of the three exact sync forms. Consequently tasks cannot supply the live, covered synced parent required to predict a comment. This follows directly from [audience inference](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/schema.rs:293) and [recording coverage](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/recorded.rs:5).

   **Smallest change:** split the acceptance fixture: the conjunct-bearing chain proves complete rule preservation and server-only residency; a second, exactly invertible chain proves device capture, prediction and coverage behavior.

Whole-rule substitution, live membership probes, exact sync admission, unchanged grant checking, protected captures, early reservations and atomic activation are sound choices. I found no additional defect in their stated ordinary-table authorization or cursor-confidentiality contracts.

**Overall verdict: NOT READY.**

# Doorlist — events with roles

Archetype: a Partiful/Luma-style events app. Axis under test: per-event
role-scoped authorization with field-level grants; pre-identity principals and
their later claim; guest-list disclosure choices; capacity and waitlist
promotion.

### 1. Product contract

**What it is and who uses it.** Doorlist lets anyone create an event, invite
people by phone number or email, give away or sell tickets, run a
capacity-bound waitlist, and check guests in at the door with a QR code; an
event stands alone or belongs to a recurring series. *Hosts* (exactly one per
event) hold every power; *co-hosts* hold a granted subset, per event or per
series; *door staff* hold one power, check-in; *guests* RSVP, bring plus-ones
and may attend privately; *invitees without an account* — the ordinary first
case — act through a link sent to their phone or email until they sign up,
when everything they did becomes theirs.

**Promises.**

- P1. You can invite anyone by phone number or email. They can see the event,
  RSVP, add plus-ones and get a QR code without installing anything;
  notifications reach them by SMS or email until they have the app, push after.
- P2. When an invitee later signs up with that number or address, their
  invitations, RSVPs, plus-ones, tickets and any co-host powers granted to it
  become theirs, everywhere, without redoing anything.
- P3. Other guests see only what the host chose — names, a count, or nothing —
  and only if the host opened the list to everyone, guests, or hosts alone. A
  guest attending privately is never named to other guests.
- P4. Every number shown — "47 going", "you're #3 on the waitlist", "2 spots
  left" — is exact and is the same number the host sees.
- P5. When an event is full you join a waitlist and keep your place. When
  enough spots open for your whole party you get an offer with a deadline; the
  spots are held until it passes.
- P6. A party (you plus your plus-ones) is admitted whole, never two of three,
  and raising or lowering capacity never bumps anyone already going.
- P7. Hosts can edit anything. Guests get one notification per burst of edits,
  describing the final state. A change of date, time or place is announced to
  everyone invited and lets every ticket holder take a full refund.
- P8. Co-hosts have exactly the powers the host gave them, for that event.
  Removing a power applies to everything they do next and nothing they already
  did.
- P9. Removing a guest removes their plus-ones, voids their offers and tickets,
  and refunds their money.
- P10. Refunds follow the policy shown at purchase, measured against the
  event's start time as it stands when you ask. A host cancellation refunds
  everyone.
- P11. Door check-in works with no connectivity. Duplicate scans across doors
  are detected when devices reconnect; a partly admitted party shows as such.
- P12. Series: RSVP once for every future occurrence, skip any single one; a
  host can change one occurrence or all that follow.
- P13. Your RSVP is the same on every device; a device acting on stale
  information is shown the current state and never silently overwrites.
  Offline you can read what you have loaded; an RSVP made offline is sent on
  reconnect and may be refused if the event has filled — you will be told.
- P14. Asking about someone you may not see gets the same answer as asking
  about someone who is not there.
- P15. Contact details a host types are seen only by that host and by co-hosts
  explicitly granted contact access. Door staff see names and party sizes only.

**Concurrency, offline and multi-device expectations.** Two guests racing for
the last spot: one gets it, the other is waitlisted. A host and co-host
editing different fields at once: both land; the
same field: the second editor sees both values. A guest cancelling on one
device while accepting an offer on another: arrival order wins and the losing
device refreshes.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: [money] [secrecy] [time] [others] (other people's data).

1. At every instant an event has exactly one active host. [time]
2. A phone number or email is bound to at most one user at any instant; a
   binding exists only because possession of the channel was verified when it
   was created. [time] [others]
3. Every act on an event records the authority it ran under (self, a link, a
   role grant, a system job), which was active at the act's time; later role
   changes never rewrite this. [time]
4. `confirmedSeats` is the sum of party sizes of going RSVPs; `heldSeats` is
   the seats under open offers and unpaid holds. With a capacity set,
   `confirmed + held ≤ capacity`, except that `confirmed` alone may exceed a
   capacity lowered afterwards; then `held = 0` and nothing promotes. [time]
5. A party's seats are granted or withheld as a unit.
6. Waitlist order is join order and never reorders. A party skipped because
   it does not fit keeps its place. Growing a waitlisted party sends it to the
   back; shrinking does not.
7. An open offer holds exactly its seats until accepted, declined, expired or
   voided. No seat is simultaneously held and confirmed.
8. Every count shown is computed over all RSVPs of the event, regardless of
   what the viewer may see by name. [secrecy]
9. A guest attending privately is counted in totals and named only to holders
   of the manage-guests grant and to the door. [secrecy]
10. Host-typed labels and contact addresses are shown only to holders of the
    view-contacts grant. Unclaimed guests appear to other guests under the
    name they typed themselves, never under the host's label. [secrecy] [others]
11. A viewer without manage-guests receives an identical response for "invited
    but not visible to you" and "not invited". [secrecy]
12. The refunds against one payment never exceed it, and go to the payer, not
    to whoever holds the ticket now. [money]
13. Refundability is decided against the event's start time at the moment of
    the request; a material change (date, time, place) reopens a full-refund
    window for every ticket bought before it. [money] [time]
14. Across all check-ins a party is admitted at most its party size; every scan
    is recorded, including ones that admit nobody. [time]
15. Removing a guest cascades to their plus-ones, offers and tickets. A guest
    with any seat admitted cannot be removed, only flagged.
16. Revoking a grant affects future acts only; scheduled acts are
    re-authorized when they execute, not when they were scheduled. [time]
17. A role granted to a phone number or email is exercisable only by the user
    bound to it, never by whoever holds the invitation link. [secrecy]
18. Series grants and series RSVPs apply to every occurrence created while they
    are active, and to already-created future occurrences; a per-occurrence
    choice always wins over the series. [time]
19. Edits to one event within a ten-minute window reach each recipient as one
    notification carrying the final state. [others]
20. Once a contact is claimed, link-possession authority for it lapses within
    seven days; when a binding ends, the former user loses that contact's rows
    immediately. Rows are never re-keyed as a side effect; re-attribution is
    an explicit, recorded act. [time] [secrecy]
21. A claimed contact brings its live and recent rows, not its ancient
    history: events that ended more than 180 days before the binding are not
    shown, and grants older than 90 days are suspended until the granting host
    reconfirms. [secrecy] [others]

### 3. The traps, ranked

**Trap 1 — "Who is this guest?" is a binding with a start and an end, not a
foreign key.** *Wrong version:* `rsvps.userId` is nullable; invitations store
the phone number; at sign-up, backfill `userId` on every row whose contact
matches. *Why wrong:* (a) numbers and addresses are recycled — whoever signs
up with the number eight months later inherits a stranger's RSVPs and, worse,
a co-host grant to a private party; (b) the backfill erases that those acts
were done by link possession, so the audit shows a user acting before it
existed; (c) unbinding becomes impossible — remove the phone and the rows stay
yours; (d) someone who RSVP'd via an email link and again in the app under
their phone holds two going rows and two seats; (e) a co-host grant sent to a
phone is exercisable by whoever holds the SMS link. The correct shape:
contacts are principals; a user is the union of the contacts bound to it
*now*; link possession is a narrower authority; duplicate rows merge by rule
at claim. *Observation:* sign up with a number invited long ago and find
yourself co-host of a stranger's event; or count the guest list before and
after one person claims and watch 47 become 48.

**Trap 2 — Counts are about the resource; names are about the viewer.**
*Wrong version:* "47 going", "you're #3" and "2 spots left" are computed over
the rows the viewer may see — the natural thing when visibility is a filter
applied before every read. *Why wrong:* under counts-only disclosure a guest's
visible domain collapses to their own row: the count reads 1, the position
reads "#1" with thirty parties ahead, and a full event shows 99 spots left. If
the RSVP write reuses that count, it overbooks. A second wrong version
excludes privately-attending guests from the public count "to protect them":
the host sees 47, guests see 46, the discrepancy itself leaks, and capacity
arithmetic has two truths. A third: "is Alex going?" returns *forbidden* for a
hidden guest and *not found* for a non-invitee, so the refusal shape answers
the question. Structural numbers are computed over the unfiltered domain by
the mechanism the write path uses; hidden guests are counted and un-named; a
non-visible guest is indistinguishable from a non-guest. *Observation:* a
hidden guest RSVPs and the public count does not move; a waitlisted guest sees
"#1"; probe a guest list by name and diff the response shapes.

**Trap 3 — Capacity is three numbers, and promotion is an offer.** *Wrong
version:* at RSVP time, `if count(going) < capacity then going`; on
cancellation, flip the first waitlisted row to going. *Why wrong:* two RSVPs
read 98 of 100 and both write; one seat opens and the head party is three;
instant promotion admits someone to a paid event unpaid, or at 3 a.m. thirty
minutes before start; a promotion that holds no seats loses them to a direct
RSVP arriving during the offer; two cancellations run two promotions offering
the same seat twice; a host lowering capacity below the going count makes
"free" negative. Free is `capacity − confirmed − held`; promotion is a
time-bounded *offer* that holds seats, one run per event at a time, first-fit
in queue order. *Observation:* fire
two RSVPs at one free seat; cancel while an offer is open and watch a direct
RSVP take the offered seat; lower capacity under the going count and watch
promotion run.

**Trap 4 — Authority is a fact of the act; role changes cascade forward
only.** *Wrong version:* check `isCohost(user, event)` on each request, store
`actorUserId` on records, and let scheduled announcements fire with the body
saved. *Why wrong:* a co-host demoted at 9 p.m. still messages 5,000 guests at
10 p.m.; their 8 p.m. check-ins display as "by (no role)" or get re-judged; a
removal that does not cascade leaves plus-ones counted and tickets valid, so
the door admits the removed party; a series grant copied into occurrences
cannot be revoked series-wide; a host who leaves without transfer orphans the
event. Acts carry the grant they ran under; scheduled acts re-read it at
execution; cascades are transactional and enumerated. *Observation:* demote a
co-host with a scheduled message and wait for 10 p.m.; remove a guest with two
plus-ones and count seats.

**Trap 5 — Refunds follow the terms as they were, and the payer, not the
holder.** *Wrong version:* `refundable = now < startsAt − 48h`; refund to
the current ticket holder; run the host-cancellation refunds in one loop.
*Why wrong:* the host moved the event a week out after the guest bought under
other terms; a payment captured after the seat hold lapsed takes money and
gives no seat unless a refund path exists; a plus-one who claimed and now
holds a ticket is refunded money the primary paid; a 5,000-ticket refund
through a rate-limited processor fails midway and duplicates or strands
refunds. *Observation:* buy, have the host move the date, cancel at 47 hours
and be refused; kill the refund worker mid-run and count refunds against
payments.

**Trap 6 — Door check-ins merge as facts, not as a state.** *Wrong version:*
`tickets.checkedInAt = now` written by the scanning device; offline devices
sync their table; last writer wins. *Why wrong:* two offline doors admit the
same ticket and one fact is lost, so the party entered twice and nobody knows;
"two of three admitted" collapses to a boolean; a ticket voided after the
manifest download is admitted with no flag; device clocks disagree, so "last"
is meaningless. Check-ins are append-only events with device sequence numbers;
state is derived; the server re-judges each uploaded fact and records *what it
found*, never overwriting. *Observation:* take two devices offline, scan one
QR on both, reconnect both, and count check-in records and duplicate flags.

### 4. Data model

Conventions: ids are opaque; times are UTC instants; a principal is named by
`principalKind ∈ {user, contact}` plus `principalId`. *Versioned*: every change
appends a history row; *append-only*: never updated except a terminal state
field. `?` marks nullable.

- `users` (id) — displayName, avatarUrl?, createdAt.
- `contactPrincipals` (id; unique channel+address) — channel ∈ {phone, email},
  address (E.164 or lower-cased email), createdAt. The pre-identity principal.
- `principalBindings` (id; at most one row per contactPrincipalId with
  unboundAt null) — contactPrincipalId, userId, boundAt, unboundAt?, method ∈
  {otp-sms, otp-email, oauth}, endedReason? ∈ {user-removed, reclaimed, admin}.
  Time-bounded, append-only. Derived view `effectivePrincipals(user, t)` =
  {user} ∪ {c : a binding of c to user covers t}.
- `accessLinks` (id) — contactPrincipalId, eventId, purpose ∈ {invite,
  manage-rsvp, ticket}, secretHash, createdAt, expiresAt, revokedAt?,
  lastUsedAt?. Possession authorizes guest-side acts for that contact on that
  event only; never a role grant. Time-bounded.
- `eventSeries` (id) — hostUserId, timezone, rule {frequency ∈ {daily, weekly,
  monthly}, interval, byWeekday[], until? | count?}, template (every `events`
  field a host edits), materializedThroughIndex, state ∈ {active, ended,
  cancelled}, createdAt.
- `seriesRoleGrants` (id) — seriesId, principalKind, principalId, role,
  grants[], grantedAt, grantedByGrantId, revokedAt?. Standing, time-bounded.
- `seriesRsvps` (id; unique seriesId+principal while endedAt null) — seriesId,
  principalKind, principalId, partySize, hiddenFromGuestList, displayName?,
  createdAt, endedAt?. Standing intent, time-bounded.
- `events` (id) — seriesId?, occurrenceIndex?, overriddenFields: set<field>,
  title, description, locationText, locationVisibility ∈ {everyone, going,
  hosts}, startsAt, endsAt, timezone, capacity? (null = unlimited),
  waitlistEnabled, plusOnesAllowed (0–10), rsvpDeadlineAt?, guestListVisibility
  ∈ {everyone, guests, hosts}, guestListDetail ∈ {names, counts, none},
  priceCents (0 = free), currency, refundPolicy ∈ {anytime, until-7d,
  until-48h, none}, state ∈ {draft, published, cancelled, ended}, version,
  fieldVersions: map<field, version>, lastMaterialChangeAt?, createdByUserId,
  createdAt, cancelledAt?. Versioned through `eventEdits`.
- `eventEdits` (append-only) — eventId, version, actorGrantId, changes:
  map<field, {before, after}>, isMaterial, at.
- `roleGrants` (id) — eventId, principalKind, principalId, role ∈ {host,
  cohost, door}, grants ⊆ {edit-details, manage-guests, view-contacts,
  check-in, send-updates, manage-money, manage-roles}, grantedAt,
  grantedByGrantId? (null only for the creating host), revokedAt?,
  revokedByGrantId?, sourceSeriesGrantId?, suspendedPendingReconfirm. The
  host row holds every grant; door rows hold only check-in. Time-bounded.
  Derived: `effectiveGrants(user, event, t)` = union of grants over active,
  unsuspended rows whose principal ∈ effectivePrincipals(user, t).
- `invitations` (id; unique eventId+principal) — eventId, principalKind,
  principalId, invitedByGrantId, hostLabel (host-private), hostNote?, sentAt,
  deliveryState ∈ {queued, sent, delivered, failed, bounced}, revokedAt?.
- `rsvps` (id; unique eventId+principal) — eventId, invitationId?,
  principalKind, principalId, displayName (shown while the principal is
  unbound), state ∈ {going, maybe, not-going, waitlisted, offered,
  pending-payment, removed, merged}, partySize ≥ 1 (includes self),
  hiddenFromGuestList, waitlistSeq? (monotonic per event, assigned on entering
  waitlisted), offerId?, fromSeriesRsvpId?, overridesSeries, version,
  stateChangedAt, createdAt. Versioned through `rsvpHistory`. One live row per
  person per event across their principals (Op 2's merge, Op 3's lookup).
- `rsvpHistory` (append-only) — rsvpId, eventId, fromState, toState,
  partySizeBefore, partySizeAfter, actorPrincipal, actorAuthority ∈ {self,
  link:<id>, grant:<id>, system:<job>}, at, reason.
- `plusOnes` (id) — rsvpId, ordinal (1..partySize−1), name?,
  contactPrincipalId?, removedAt?. A named plus-one with a contact receives a
  ticket-purpose access link.
- `capacityLedger` (eventId) — capacity?, confirmedSeats, heldSeats,
  generation. **Derived** — `confirmed` = Σ partySize over going rows; `held` =
  Σ seats over open offers + Σ partySize over pending-payment rows that have no
  offer — **but maintained in the same transaction as every change to its
  inputs.** A reconciler recomputes hourly and alarms on drift; this row
  serializes every capacity-affecting write.
- `waitlistOffers` (id) — eventId, rsvpId, seats, offeredAt, expiresAt, state
  ∈ {open, accepted, declined, expired, voided}, generation, paymentId?.
  Time-bounded holds.
- `payments` (append-only plus state) — id, eventId, rsvpId, payerUserId,
  amountCents, currency, processorRef? (unique), state ∈ {pending, captured,
  failed, abandoned}, holdExpiresAt, createdAt, capturedAt?.
- `tickets` (id) — eventId, rsvpId, plusOneOrdinal (0 = primary), paymentId?,
  priceCents, state ∈ {valid, void, refunded}, secretHash, issuedAt, voidedAt?,
  voidReason?. Every seat of a going party has one, free or paid; the ticket
  is the check-in credential.
- `refunds` (append-only plus state) — id, paymentId, ticketId, amountCents,
  reason ∈ {guest-cancel, host-cancel, host-removed, material-change,
  hold-expired, seat-dropped}, processorRef?, state ∈ {requested, succeeded,
  failed}, requestedByAuthority, requestedAt, settledAt?, attempts.
- `checkIns` (append-only) — id, eventId, ticketId, seatsAdmitted, result ∈
  {admitted, duplicate, rejected-void, rejected-unknown, admitted-then-void},
  scannedByGrantId, deviceId, deviceSeq, scannedAt (device clock), receivedAt
  (server clock). Derived per rsvp: `admittedSeats` = Σ seatsAdmitted where
  result ∈ {admitted, admitted-then-void}.
- `announcements` (id) — eventId, authorGrantId, body, audience ∈ {going,
  going-maybe, all-invited, waitlist}, scheduledFor?, sentAt?, state ∈
  {scheduled, sent, cancelled, cancelled-authority-lost}, eventVersionAtSend?.
- `notifications` (append-only outbox; unique coalesceKey while undelivered)
  — id, recipientPrincipalKind, recipientPrincipalId, eventId, kind,
  coalesceKey, payload, notBefore, deliveredVia? ∈ {push, sms, email},
  deliveryState, createdAt, deliveredAt?.

**Relationships.** Series → events (by occurrenceIndex); event → one ledger,
many roleGrants, invitations, rsvps, offers, tickets, checkIns, announcements;
rsvp → plus-ones, tickets, an optional offer and payment; contactPrincipal →
bindings over time, links. Rows are keyed to principals, never re-keyed
automatically (I20).

**Derived values.** The name shown for an rsvp = the bound user's displayName
if its principal is bound *now*, else `rsvps.displayName` (waitlist position:
Op 7). `refundable(ticket, now)` = policy allows at `now` against the
current `startsAt` (anytime; until-7d: now ≤ startsAt − 7d; until-48h: now ≤
startsAt − 48h; none: never) **or** ticket.issuedAt < lastMaterialChangeAt and
now ≤ max(lastMaterialChangeAt + 7d, startsAt − 48h).

**Time-bounded values.** Bindings, links, role grants, series grants, series
intents, offers, payment holds, the rsvp deadline, the refund window, and the
check-in window (startsAt − 4h to endsAt + 1h).

### 5. The hard operations

Capacity-affecting steps run under the event's ledger row lock —
"transaction" below means that; unlimited-capacity events take it only for
offers and holds, since plain increments commute.

**Op 1 — Invite by contact (bulk).** *Inputs:* eventId, up to 500 rows of
{channel, address, hostLabel, hostNote?}, actor. *Authorization:* actor holds
an active grant with manage-guests. *Steps:* (1) resolve the actor's grant G;
event state ∈ {draft, published}. (2) Per row: normalize; upsert
contactPrincipal C; if an unrevoked invitation(event, C) exists, report
duplicate; else insert invitation {invitedByGrantId: G, hostLabel},
accessLink(C, event, invite, expiresAt = endsAt + 7d) and notification
{recipient C, kind invite, coalesceKey (C, event, invite)}. (3) Delivery
worker: C bound now → push and in-app; else SMS or email carrying the link;
provider callbacks update deliveryState. *Atomic:* each row's three inserts,
not the batch — the response lists per-row outcomes. *Eventual:* delivery
state. *Optimistic UI:* rows appear as "queued". *Conflict:* two co-hosts
invite the same contact at once; the unique key makes one a duplicate.

**Op 2 — Claim a contact (sign-up, or add a contact to an account).**
*Inputs:* user U (new or existing), channel, address, OTP. *Authorization:*
possession of the channel proven by an OTP verified now with the SMS/email
provider (external). *Steps:* (1) verify OTP; load or create C. (2)
Transaction on C: (a) if an active binding (C, A) exists with A ≠ U: unboundAt
= now, endedReason = reclaimed, notify A through its other bound contacts,
invalidate A's cached grants; (b) insert binding (C, U, now); (c) every active
roleGrant or seriesRoleGrant to C older than 90 days gets
suspendedPendingReconfirm = true and its granting host is notified; (d)
accessLinks for C: expiresAt = min(expiresAt, now + 7d). (3) For every unended
event holding an rsvp keyed to C and one keyed to U, one transaction per
event: keep the row whose state ranks highest (going > offered >
pending-payment > waitlisted > maybe > not-going), set the other to merged,
release its seats (void its offer), append history reason claim-merge,
enqueue promotion. (4) U's view now includes C's rows within the 180-day
horizon (I21). *Atomic:* step 2; step 3 per event. *Eventual:* notifications,
cache invalidation. *Optimistic UI:* none — "linking…" until acknowledged.
*Conflict:* two users verifying C at once serialize on C; the later wins and
the earlier is reclaimed.

**Op 3 — RSVP (create or change).** *Inputs:* eventId, principal P (the
user, or the link's contact), desired ∈ {going, maybe, not-going}, waitlistOk,
partySize, hiddenFromGuestList, displayName (required when P is an unbound
contact), baseVersion?. *Authorization:* P is invited and not revoked, or the
event admits open links and the actor holds one, or the actor holds a
manage-rsvp link for P, or P ∈ effectivePrincipals(actor); event published;
partySize − 1 ≤ plusOnesAllowed; after rsvpDeadlineAt only cancel/shrink
unless the actor has manage-guests. *Steps (transaction):* (1) find the live
rsvp R for the event under any of the actor's effective principals (or the
link's contact); create at version 0 if none; if baseVersion is given and ≠
R.version, reject STALE with R. (2) desired ≠ going: if R was going, confirmed
−= R.partySize and released = true; if offered, void the offer and held −=
seats; if waitlisted, leave the queue. Set state and partySize; append history
with the actor's authority; paid seats are refunded only if refundable(ticket,
now). (3) desired = going: (a) need = partySize − (R going ? R.partySize : 0);
if R is offered with offer.seats ≥ partySize, continue as Op 5 accept; (b)
free = capacity − confirmed − held (∞ if unlimited); (c) if need ≤ free: free
event → state going, confirmed += need, issue tickets for the new seats; paid
→ state pending-payment, held += need, then Op 11; (d) else if waitlistEnabled
and waitlistOk: if R was not waitlisted or its party grew, waitlistSeq = next;
state waitlisted; (e) else reject FULL. (4) A going party shrinking: confirmed
−= delta, void the dropped plus-ones' tickets, seat-dropped refunds if
refundable, released = true. (5) version++; if released, enqueue promotion.
*Atomic:* all of it. *Eventual:*
promotion, notifications, ticket delivery. *Optimistic UI:* show the requested
state greyed as "confirming" when last-known free ≥ need; never a solid
"going" before the ack; position only from the server. *Conflict:* the ledger
lock decides; the loser gets FULL (and is waitlisted) or STALE.

**Op 4 — Promotion and expiry sweep (scheduled/background).** *Trigger:*
enqueued by any seat release, capacity increase or offer expiry, plus a 60 s
scan for offers and unpaid holds past their deadline. *Authorization:* system.
*Steps, one worker per event at a time (transaction):* (1) Expire: open offers
with expiresAt ≤ now → expired, held −= seats, rsvp → waitlisted with a *new*
waitlistSeq (back of the line), notify; pending-payment holds past
holdExpiresAt uncaptured → payment abandoned, held −= partySize, rsvp → its
prior state. (2) If now ≥ startsAt − 1h, stop: no offers in the last hour
(managers admit walk-ins through Op 3). (3) free = capacity − confirmed −
held; if free ≤ 0, stop. (4) Walk waitlisted rows by waitlistSeq: for each
with partySize ≤ free, insert offer {seats: partySize, expiresAt: min(now +
24h, startsAt − 1h)}, rsvp → offered, held += partySize, free −= partySize,
notify (not coalesced); larger parties are skipped and keep their seq; stop
when free = 0 or the list ends. (5) generation++. *Atomic:* 1–5. *Eventual:*
notifications. *Conflict:* a second trigger finds nothing to do; the job
re-reads under the lock, so stale triggers are harmless.

**Op 5 — Accept or decline an offer.** *Inputs:* offerId, decision, actor.
*Authorization:* the offer's rsvp principal ∈ effectivePrincipals(actor), or a
manage-rsvp link for it. *Steps (transaction):* (1) if the offer is not open
or expiresAt ≤ now, reject EXPIRED. (2) Decline: offer → declined, held −=
seats, rsvp → not-going, enqueue promotion. (3) Accept, free event: offer →
accepted, held −= seats, confirmed += seats, rsvp → going, issue tickets,
history. (4) Accept, paid: create a payment for seats × priceCents with
holdExpiresAt = offer.expiresAt; rsvp → pending-payment; the offer stays open
and keeps holding. On capture (Op 11 step 3): if the offer is still open,
perform step 3; if it expired meanwhile, refund in full (hold-expired) and
leave the rsvp where the sweep put it. *Atomic:* 1–3, or 1 + 4 (the processor
intent is created first; if the transaction fails it is cancelled).
*Optimistic UI:* "you're in" only after the ack; the paid path shows a
countdown. *Conflict:* accept versus sweep expiry — first to the lock wins.

**Op 6 — Edit event details (field-level, mergeable).** *Inputs:* eventId,
patch: map<field, value>, baseVersion, actor. *Authorization:* edit-details;
priceCents and refundPolicy also need manage-money. *Steps (transaction):* (1)
for each field f in the patch, if fieldVersions[f] > baseVersion, reject
CONFLICT with the current values — you must have seen the latest value of
every field you touch; disjoint edits merge. (2) Apply; version++;
fieldVersions[f] = version; append eventEdit. (3) isMaterial = the patch
touches startsAt, endsAt, timezone or locationText; if so, lastMaterialChangeAt
= now (opening the I13 window) and one notification per invited principal,
kind event-changed, coalesceKey (recipient, event, event-changed), notBefore =
now + 10 min — a later edit in the window replaces the payload, so each
recipient gets one message with the final state. (4) capacity changed:
ledger.capacity = new; increased → enqueue promotion; decreased below
confirmed → nothing else (I4). (5) priceCents changed: existing tickets keep
their price. *Atomic:* 1–4. *Eventual:* notifications, promotion. *Optimistic
UI:* apply locally; on CONFLICT show both values per field.

**Op 7 — Read: the event page as a principal (the disclosure read).**
*Inputs:* eventId, viewer ∈ {user, link holder for contact C, anonymous}.
*Authorization:* event published and the viewer is invited, holds an open
link, or holds any grant; anonymous viewers of private events get not-found.
*Steps:* (1) resolve effective principals and grants; role class ∈ {manager
(manage-guests), staff (any other grant), going-guest, other-guest}. (2) The
viewer's own rsvp R in full: state, partySize, plus-ones, tickets; if
waitlisted, position = 1 + count(waitlisted rows with waitlistSeq <
R.waitlistSeq) over *all* rows; if offered, the offer and deadline. (3) Counts
over all rows regardless of viewer: going = confirmedSeats, going parties,
maybe count, spots left = capacity − confirmed − held (holds appear as taken,
never as holds). Shown iff guestListVisibility admits the viewer's class
(everyone; guests = R.state ∈ {going, maybe, offered, pending-payment}; hosts
= staff/manager) and guestListDetail ≠ none; staff and managers always. (4)
Names iff guestListDetail = names and visibility admits (staff always):
{displayName per §4, partySize, plus-one names, going|maybe} over rows with
state ∈ {going, maybe} and hiddenFromGuestList = false; managers also see
hidden rows, marked. Never hostLabel; addresses only with view-contacts. (5)
Location iff locationVisibility = everyone, or going and R.state ∈ {going,
offered, pending-payment}, or the viewer is staff/manager. (6) Waitlist depth:
managers only. *Direct lookups* (guest by id, search by name) apply step 4's
projection first and report a miss exactly as a nonexistent id (I11).
*Consistency:* read-your-writes for R; counts come from the ledger row, so
they are the number the last write used.

**Op 8 — Read: the manager guest list.** *Inputs:* eventId, filters (state,
admitted, search), page, actor. *Authorization:* manage-guests; view-contacts
adds the address column; a door grant instead receives the door manifest —
{ticketId, secretHash, displayName, partySize, admittedSeats, state} for every
ticket, hidden guests included (hidden means hidden from the *list*, not from
the door) — and nothing else. *Steps:* invitations ⋈ rsvps ⋈ tickets ⋈
admittedSeats; per row: displayName plus hostLabel, claimed (bound now?),
deliveryState, state, partySize, hidden, waitlist position, offer deadline,
ticket and refund states, admittedSeats; addresses only with view-contacts;
page by (state, waitlistSeq | displayName). *Consistency:* live; counts
identical to Op 7 by construction.

**Op 9 — Change roles and remove guests (the cascades).** *Inputs:* eventId,
action ∈ {grant, revoke, transfer-host, remove-guest, detach-plus-one},
target, grants[], actor. *Authorization:* manage-roles for grant/revoke (a
co-host may grant only grants they hold); host only for transfer;
manage-guests for remove and detach. *Steps, each one transaction:*
- grant: insert roleGrant {principal (user or contact), role, grants,
  grantedByGrantId}; a contact principal gets an invitation and link if it has
  none and can exercise the grant only once bound (I17).
- revoke: set revokedAt; that grant's scheduled announcements →
  cancelled-authority-lost, host notified; its door manifest tokens are
  invalidated; its check-ins, edits and invitations stand.
- transfer-host: (i) the host records a transfer offer to a *bound user*; (ii)
  the target accepts within 7 days: revoke the old host row, insert the new,
  insert a cohost row with all grants for the old host — one transaction, so
  I1 holds at every instant.
- remove-guest: if admittedSeats > 0, reject CHECKED-IN (offer the flag). Else
  rsvp → removed; plus-ones removedAt; open offer → voided, held −= seats; if
  going, confirmed −= partySize; tickets → void; paid tickets get refund rows
  (host-removed, full, regardless of policy); invitation and links revoked;
  enqueue promotion; notify the guest.
- detach-plus-one: create an rsvp for the plus-one's contact, going, partySize
  1, no capacity check (the seat is already confirmed); primary partySize −=
  1; move that ticket's rsvpId; the payer is unchanged, so a later refund
  still goes to the primary (I12).
*Eventual:* refunds, notifications, manifest invalidation reaching devices.
*Conflict:* a revoke racing an in-flight act by that grant — the act re-reads
the grant under the event lock and fails if revoked.

**Op 10 — Door check-in by QR (online and offline).** *Inputs:* token =
ticketId ∥ HMAC(secret), seatsToAdmit (default: remaining seats), deviceId,
deviceSeq, scannedAt, actor. *Authorization:* a grant with check-in;
within the check-in window; offline, a manifest token issued to that grant.
*Online steps (transaction per scan):* (1) verify the HMAC; unknown → append
rejected-unknown, return. (2) Load ticket, rsvp, admittedSeats; if
ticket.state ≠ valid → append rejected-void, return "void — see host". (3) Any
ticket of the party opens the party view; remaining = partySize −
admittedSeats; k = min(seatsToAdmit, remaining). (4) Append admitted with k,
or duplicate if remaining = 0. (5) Return {displayName, partySize, admitted,
remaining, prior scans}. *Offline steps:* (a) the device
holds the Op 8 door manifest as of T, refreshed every 60 s while online; (b)
each scan is judged locally against manifest plus own log and appended with
deviceSeq; (c) on reconnect, upload the log in order; the server re-judges
each entry against current state at receipt: valid with remaining → admitted;
already admitted elsewhere → duplicate (both rows kept — the host sees
"scanned at Door A 20:02 and Door B 20:04"); voided after T →
admitted-then-void, flagged, nobody un-admitted. *Atomic:* one scan, one
transaction. *Merge:* append-only; state is derived. *Eventual:* other doors'
view of admittedSeats. *Optimistic UI:* the device's verdict shows at once —
it is the truth at that door — and may later be annotated "duplicate".
*Conflict:* none by construction; duplicates are facts.

**Op 11 — Buy tickets (external payment).** *Inputs:* eventId, partySize,
actor — a bound user (a link holder is asked to sign up first, Op 2, so the
payer is a durable principal). *Authorization:* as Op 3 with desired = going. *Steps:* (1) Op 3 step 3c reserves the hold: rsvp →
pending-payment, held += need, payment {pending, holdExpiresAt = now + 15
min}. (2) Create the processor intent with idempotency key = payment.id; store
processorRef. (3) Webhook captured, idempotent by processorRef, in a
transaction: if already captured, ack; if rsvp.state = pending-payment and the
hold is unexpired: payment → captured, held −= need, confirmed += need, rsvp →
going, issue tickets; else (the sweep abandoned the hold): payment → captured,
then a full refund row (hold-expired) and a notification — money is never
kept without a seat. (4) Webhook failed: payment → failed, held −= need, rsvp
→ its prior state. *Atomic:* each of 1, 3, 4. *External:* 2 and the refund.
*Optimistic UI:* "holding your spot — 14:59" countdown; never "you're in".
*Conflict:* sweep versus webhook is decided under the ledger lock; the
late-capture refund path makes the losing order safe.

**Op 12 — Cancel the event (bulk, external).** *Inputs:* eventId, message,
actor; for a series occurrence, scope ∈ {this, following}. *Authorization:*
host only. *Steps:* (1) transaction: state = cancelled; open offers → voided;
scheduled announcements → cancelled; ledger frozen; append eventEdit. (2)
Enqueue a refund job: one refund row (host-cancel, full) per valid paid
ticket, up to 5,000. (3) Refund worker: requested rows at the processor's
rate (10/s), idempotency key = refund.id; success → succeeded, ticket →
refunded; failure → attempts++ with backoff, five tries, then failed and shown
to the host as "3 refunds need attention". (4) Announcement to
all invited, immediate, not coalesced. (5) following: steps 1–4 on each
materialized occurrence with a larger index, and the series rule's until
becomes this occurrence's date; ended occurrences are untouched. *Atomic:*
step 1 per event. *Eventual:* refunds, as a resumable job with per-row state
— the event page shows "refunded 4,312 of 5,000". *Conflict:* an RSVP after
step 1 is rejected; a capture webhook after cancellation refunds immediately
(Op 11 step 3).

**Op 13 — Materialize series occurrences (scheduled).** *Trigger:* daily at
03:00 series-local time, and on series create or edit. *Authorization:*
system. *Steps, per active series:* (1) compute occurrence dates from the rule
up to now + 56 days; for each index not yet materialized, one transaction: (a)
insert the event from the template with seriesId, occurrenceIndex, state
published, and its ledger row; (b) for each active seriesRoleGrant insert a
roleGrant with sourceSeriesGrantId; (c) for each active seriesRsvp in
createdAt order run Op 3 with desired = going (waitlisted if full),
fromSeriesRsvpId set, authority system:materialize; (d) advance
materializedThroughIndex. (2) End sweep: any event with endsAt ≤ now → ended.
*Idempotent* by the unique (seriesId, occurrenceIndex). *Eventual:* horizon
lag under one day. *Standing-intent rules, enforced by the RSVP and role
operations:* creating a seriesRsvp also applies to every already-materialized
future occurrence (through Op 3, each under its own capacity); ending it
applies not-going to future occurrences whose rsvp has fromSeriesRsvpId and
overridesSeries = false, releasing seats and enqueuing promotion per
occurrence; a per-occurrence rsvp change sets overridesSeries = true; revoking
a seriesRoleGrant revokes its derived grants on unended occurrences; a
template edit goes through Op 6 on each future occurrence for every field not
in its overriddenFields, with material-change notifications coalesced per
recipient per series (coalesceKey (recipient, seriesId, event-changed)) so a
time change across eight occurrences is one message.

**Op 14 — Send an update (immediate or scheduled).** *Inputs:* eventId, body,
audience, scheduledFor?, actor. *Authorization:* send-updates. *Steps:* (1)
insert announcement {authorGrantId, state scheduled}. (2) At send time — now,
or when the scheduler reaches scheduledFor — transaction: re-read the author's
grant; if revoked or suspended, state → cancelled-authority-lost, notify the
host, stop; else state → sent, eventVersionAtSend = version. (3) Fan-out in
batches of 500 with a cursor: recipients by audience as of now (going;
going-maybe; all-invited = unrevoked invitations; waitlist = waitlisted or
offered); insert notifications with coalesceKey (recipient, event,
announcement:id), never coalesced with edits. (4) Delivery routes per
recipient: bound now → push and in-app; else SMS or email via the link.
*Atomic:* 1; 2; each batch, resumable without duplicates because coalesceKey
is unique while undelivered. *Eventual:* delivery. *Conflict:* revoke versus
send is decided by the grant row read under the event lock in step 2 (Trap 4).

### 6. Scale and cost

Steady state of a successful deployment:

- 3M users; 12M contact principals (most never bind); 4M bindings; 60k binding
  changes a day, ~200 of them reclaims of recycled numbers.
- 600k events a year (1.6k a day); median 35 invitees, p99 800, maximum 20k
  for a ticketed festival. 25M invitations, 15M RSVPs, 10M tickets (2M paid)
  a year.
- 60k active series, 56-day horizon → ~480k materialized future occurrences;
  the daily materializer creates ~9k and applies up to 200 standing intents
  each (typically 300k rsvp inserts a day, worst case 2M).
- Peak: a viral event takes 20k RSVPs in 30 minutes — 11/s sustained, 100/s
  in bursts — on **one** ledger row; at ~5 ms per transaction that is half the
  row's capacity. Unlimited-capacity events avoid the lock; capacity-bound
  ones accept a queue at the row.
- Notifications: ~2M a day; a time change on a 5,000-guest event is 5,000
  outbox rows, target delivery within 60 s; retention 90 days.
- Check-in: 5,000 guests through 8 doors in 90 minutes is ~1 scan/s; an
  offline device may upload 2,000 facts at once on reconnect.
- Waitlists: p99 depth 500, maximum 5,000; the first-fit walk is bounded by
  free seats but, when free = 1 and every party is larger, scans the whole
  list under the lock — 5,000 indexed rows, acceptable.
- Refunds: 5,000 at 10/s is 8–9 minutes of resumable work.

Expensive at these sizes: Op 7's named list at 20k guests (paged at 100,
cached per (event, version, roleClass) with the viewer's own row overlaid);
Op 12's bulk refunds; Op 13's fan-out of a
standing-intent change across eight occurrences; Op 2's claim-merge across
every event a contact was ever invited to (p99 50); Op 14's 20k-recipient
fan-out; and effective-principal resolution on every request, which needs a
60 s cache invalidated on binding or grant change.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Time-bounded principal indirection.** Every authorization subject is a
   principal — contact, user, link holder or system job — and "is this row
   mine" resolves through a binding valid at *this* instant, with a horizon
   (I21) and a narrower link authority (I17, I20). A row-level policy
   `row.userId = me` cannot state it. *Concession:* backfill `userId` at
   claim. *Cheat boundary:*
   honest only if contacts are never reclaimed and links never issued — not
   this product; otherwise a recycled number steals rows, the audit shows a
   user acting before it existed, and a grant to a phone is exercisable by the
   SMS holder.
2. **Unfiltered structural numbers in a visibility-filtered world.** The going
   count, spots left and the viewer's waitlist position must be computed over
   rows the viewer may not read, returned without leaking the rows, and be
   *the same number the write path used* to admit or waitlist. *Concession:* a
   denormalized ledger row maintained in the same transaction as every change
   to its inputs — what §4 does, and honest. *Cheat boundary:*
   maintaining the counter asynchronously (the write no longer checks the
   number the reader sees, so it overbooks) or computing it over the filtered
   domain (a confidently wrong integer).
3. **A per-event system actor with holds, deadlines and fencing.** Promotion
   runs under system authority, one at a time per event, triggered by writes
   and the clock; it mutates many principals' rows atomically, holds seats
   with expiry, is idempotent under retries, and admits an external payment
   round-trip. *Concession:* promote
   synchronously inside the cancelling transaction. *Cheat boundary:* honest
   for free events with short waitlists; a cheat once the paid path exists
   (the transaction cannot await the processor) or once the 20k-guest event
   turns the cancel path into a serial scan under the row lock.
4. **Acts carry their authority, and scheduled acts re-authorize.** Records
   store the grant id, not the user id; cascades on revoke are enumerated and
   transactional; a scheduled act re-reads its grant at execution under system
   authority. *Concession:* store `actorUserId` and check roles at request
   time only. *Cheat boundary:* anything scheduled or delegated — a demoted
   co-host still messages 5,000 guests at 10 p.m., and the audit cannot say
   under which grant a check-in happened.
5. **Append-only merge of offline facts with server re-evaluation.** Door
   devices write events, not states; the server accepts them out of order,
   duplicated and against a stale manifest, re-judges each and records the
   verdict alongside the device's. *Concession:* last-writer-wins on
   `checkedInAt`. *Cheat boundary:* honest with one online door; a cheat with
   two doors or one offline one, because the duplicate fact and the
   voided-after-download fact are exactly what a state overwrite loses.

Field-level grants — one invitation row projected four ways (manager with
contacts, staff without, door with hidden guests' names but no list, guest by
disclosure) — are routine if every read goes through a projection; the cheat
is returning the row and trimming on the client.

### 8. Confidence

*Standard practice in shipping products:* invitations to phone or email with
later account claim (Partiful, Luma); guest lists with names/counts/none and
per-guest hiding, hidden guests still counted (Partiful); waitlists with
time-limited offers and held spots (Eventbrite, Luma); refund windows relative
to start time; offline check-in with manifests and post-hoc duplicate
detection (Eventbrite organizer app); series materialization over a rolling
horizon with per-occurrence overrides and "this and following". The scale
figures are plausible extrapolations, not measurements.

*Invented for the exercise, or made explicit where products leave it
implicit:* contacts as first-class principals with interval bindings, the
180-day claim horizon and 90-day grant re-confirmation; rows never re-keyed
as a side effect; link possession as a distinct, narrower authority that
lapses after claim; the three-bucket ledger as the one serialization point
shared by reads and writes; ordered first-fit promotion with expired offers
going to the back; "acts carry their grant" and execution-time
re-authorization of scheduled acts as stated rules; the material-change refund
reopening formula; per-field versions for mergeable edits;
`admitted-then-void` as a recorded verdict. These are the parts most likely to
need adjustment — and the parts the axis exists to stress.

# Photo library — archetype design

Axis under test: per-principal data volume (a user's *own* 100k rows) against a bounded-closure sync model; assets at scale; sharing rules over machine-derived data; owner-partition retention and offline search.

### 1. Product contract

**What it is.** A personal photo and video library in the Google Photos mould. Each account owns one library (its *partition*): 100k+ items for a heavy user, backed up automatically from phones and desktops into an object store, with machine-derived labels, faces and places, a timeline, search that works offline, non-destructive edits, favorites, archive, trash with 60-day expiry, duplicate cleanup, memories, one designated *partner* who receives photos continuously by rule, shared albums with members, links, comments and likes, quotas, export, and account deletion.

**Who acts.** *Owner* (the account holder, via several devices); *device* (a phone uploader with a revocable token, acting as the owner in the background); *partner* (one other account); *member* (of a shared album; dozens per album); *link viewer* (anonymous token holder); *system* (derivation, schedulers, purge — no human principal).

**Promises.**
1. Every photo or video taken on the phone is backed up in the background; it appears in the timeline immediately with a "backing up" badge, which clears when the server has the bytes.
2. The timeline scrolls the whole library by capture date on every device, offline included for what the device has synced; the app is usable seconds after first sign-in and fills in older years behind the user.
3. Search the library by content ("dog", "beach"), people, places, dates, favorites, and text in photos — online and offline. Offline results state the date range the local index covers.
4. Faces are grouped into people automatically; the user names, merges, splits, and hides people; corrections survive re-grouping and model upgrades.
5. Edits are non-destructive: the original is always recoverable, every save is kept, edits appear on all devices.
6. Favorites, archive, edits, trash and restore sync across devices; the user's latest action wins even when one device was offline for days.
7. The same bytes never enter the library twice; near-duplicates are suggested for cleanup, never deleted automatically.
8. Trash holds deleted items 60 days, restorable with their album placements intact; then they are permanently deleted. "Empty trash" is immediate and final.
9. The usage meter includes trash and archived items; when the quota is full, backup pauses with a clear message and nothing is silently dropped; emptying trash frees space at once.
10. Shared albums: invitees and link-joiners can view and, if the owner allows, add photos, comment, and like; the owner controls members and links and can disable a link without evicting anyone who already joined.
11. Partner sharing: one partner receives all photos, or photos of chosen people, from a chosen date, automatically and continuously; the partner may save items to their own library; either side can end sharing; saved copies remain the partner's.
12. Sharing a photo never reveals the owner's person names, place names, or coordinates unless location is on for that album; removing access (untagging a face, trashing, ending a share, removing a member) takes effect within seconds.
13. Memories (this day, trips, a person over the years) are automatic and never include a hidden person or date or a trashed or archived item; hiding withdraws memories already generated.
14. Export delivers every item plus metadata as downloadable archives within hours, valid for 7 days.
15. Account deletion: 30-day grace, then everything the user owns is purged; items they contributed to others' albums are withdrawn; copies others saved remain; their comments remain, attributed to "Deleted user".
16. Nothing the user cannot see is revealed by counts, orderings, errors, or timing.

**Concurrency, offline, multi-device expectations.** Two devices backing up the same photo produce one item and both report success. Offline, the timeline and search run over the synced tiers; placeholders for every item and the ~5,000 most recent thumbnails are cached; full-size images load on demand. Offline actions (favorite, trash, edit, album add, comment) queue and apply on reconnect; one refused because permissions changed meanwhile is reported, never silently dropped. Uploads resume across restarts and network loss. Other devices' changes, shared-album activity, and partner arrivals appear within seconds when online.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: [$] money, [S] secrecy, [T] time, [O] other people's data.

1. Original bytes of a backed-up item remain retrievable until the user permanently deletes it or the account is purged; a blob is deleted only when no item in any account references it. [T]
2. For one owner, one content hash yields at most one item; a retried or concurrent backup of the same bytes returns the existing item and never fails as "duplicate".
3. Every edit save is retained and the original never modified; "revert" is always possible; a save on one device never discards an unsynced save from another — both are kept, the later shown.
4. A trashed item is absent from timeline, search, albums, memories, partner views, and suggestions immediately; it is purged no earlier than 60 days after its most recent trashing; restoring reopens its album placements. [T]
5. Storage used = charged bytes of all live and trashed items the user owns (saved partner copies and archived items included), counted once per item, plus bytes reserved by open uploads; an upload that would exceed quota is refused with a reason; an abandoned upload releases its reservation within 24 hours. [$][T]
6. A viewer of a shared item learns neither the owner's person names, nor place names, nor coordinates, unless the album has location sharing on; derived data inherits the secrecy of its source. [S][O]
7. The partner sees exactly the items the rule currently selects: removal (face un-assigned, item trashed or archived, date edited out of range, rule ended) is visible within seconds; addition within minutes. [O][T]
8. A partner's saved copy is the partner's: it survives the end of sharing and the sharer's deletion, and never changes when the sharer edits or trashes the source. [O]
9. Album membership is an interval; after removal or leaving, a former member sees nothing of the album, including via a link they used before, and their contributed items are withdrawn. [O][T]
10. Revoking a link stops admission; it does not remove members who joined through it. [T]
11. Comments and likes attach to one stay of one item in one album; removing the item hides them; re-adding the same photo starts a new stay with no comments. [O][T]
12. A comment is deleted only by its author or the album owner; a like is removed only by the liker; an album item is removed only by its contributor or the album owner. [O]
13. Across devices, the later action by wall clock wins for favorite, archive, trash/restore, capture-date edit, and edit head; a stale action arriving later from an offline device never overrides it. [T]
14. Offline search never presents a partial index as complete; every offline result set carries its coverage watermark.
15. Online and offline search agree for every item both have indexed at the same model generation.
16. A user's face confirmation or rejection outranks machine assignment and persists across re-clustering and model generations.
17. A hidden person or date never appears in memories or share suggestions; hiding withdraws existing memories that include it.
18. Account purge removes everything in the owner's partition, ends partner shares both ways, closes memberships, withdraws contributed items, anonymizes comments, and releases blob references — and touches nothing another account owns. [O][T]
19. An export contains every item live at the request moment, exactly once, and nothing created after it. [T]
20. A revoked link and a nonexistent link are indistinguishable; a viewer's counts are computed over the viewer's visible set; the owner's storage and people counts over the whole partition, trash, archive, and hidden included. [S]

### 3. The traps, ranked

**T1 — "Photos of Alice" is three derivations deep, and every layer has a different author** (temporal, authority, visibility — the sharing-over-derived-data axis).
*Wrong version:* store the partner rule as a list of face-cluster ids, evaluate it once when a photo is uploaded, insert a share row. *Why wrong:* the extension of "photos of Alice" is `faces → cluster → person → rule`, and the three arrows are rewritten by three authorities: the model (re-clusters and reassigns at every generation), the owner (confirms, rejects, merges, splits, hides), and product state (trash, archive, capture-date edits, scope changes). A cluster id does not survive a generation switch; a rejection must beat the model forever; a merge silently widens the rule; a date edit moves a photo across `fromCaptureDate`. *Observation:* the owner marks a face "not Alice" and the partner still sees the photo; after a model upgrade the partner's Alice feed empties or fills with strangers; the partner's like remains on a photo they can no longer see.

**T2 — 100k own rows against a bounded replica: offline search that silently searches a subset** (the volume axis).
*Wrong version:* replicate "the rows the user may read" (1.8M derived rows) or replicate "the most recent N items" and search over it. *Why wrong:* search is meaningful only over the whole library; a recency-bounded replica gives confident empty results for old years; a complete replica takes 40 minutes and 800 MB. *Observation:* offline "Lisbon 2019" returns nothing while online returns 412, with no indicator — or first launch never finishes syncing.

**T3 — Assets are not rows** (concurrency, money, authority, external system).
*Wrong version:* upload = put bytes then insert a row; delete = delete row and blob; quota = `SUM(size)` at upload time; partner "save" copies the row. *Why wrong:* two devices back up the same photo concurrently and both see "duplicate" errors or two items appear; a crashed upload holds quota or orphans a blob; the partner's saved copy shares bytes with the original, so the sharer emptying trash deletes the partner's photo; a sum over 100k rows per upload is a scan; two concurrent uploads each pass the check. *Observation:* a broken image in the partner's library after the sharer empties trash; a usage meter over quota; "storage full" that clears itself a day later.

**T4 — Server arrival order is not user intent order; trash is a state machine; edits are a log** (merge).
*Wrong version:* last write by server receipt on the whole row. *Why wrong:* phone A (offline) favorites at 10:00; phone B (online) unfavorites at 10:05; A syncs at 11:00 and the favorite returns. With trash it is worse: A's stale `trash` re-trashes a photo B restored, and the purge job deletes it 60 days later. Two offline edits: the row-level winner discards the loser's crop. *Observation:* a resurrected favorite; a photo that "deletes itself"; a vanished edit.

**T5 — Interval membership everywhere: items in albums, people in albums, comments on stays** (temporal).
*Wrong version:* `albumItems` and `albumMembers` as plain join tables with physical delete; comments keyed by `(albumId, mediaItemId)`; link revocation deletes the members who joined via the link. *Why wrong:* removing and re-adding a photo resurrects comments, including from a member who left; "who was in the album when this was written" is unanswerable. *Observation:* a re-added photo shows a year-old comment by someone no longer in the album; revoking a link evicts the owner's mother.

**T6 — Sensitivity inherits through derivation** (visibility, secrecy).
*Wrong version:* strip coordinates from the shared item but keep `placeId`, let "Lisbon" into the viewer's search, show the album on a map, show the owner's person names as chips, return "link revoked" versus "link not found". *Why wrong:* the place name *is* the location; the names are private annotations; distinct refusals confirm existence. *Observation:* a viewer searches a shared album for "Lisbon" and finds the photo whose location was hidden.

Ranking: T1–T2 are the axis; T3–T4 are money and silent data loss; T5–T6 are wrong but visible.

### 4. Data model

Neutral types: `id` (opaque unique), `ts` (UTC instant, ms), `hlc` (64-bit hybrid logical clock stamp from the writing device: 48-bit wall ms + 16-bit counter; ordered numerically, ties broken by `deviceId`), `seq` (per-partition monotonic integer), `json`.

**Partitions.** Every row outside the global tables carries `ownerId` — the *retention partition*, purged as a unit. Some rows also have a distinct *sync partition* (`syncOwnerId`): the principal in whose change log they appear.

Global tables:
- `blobs` — `id` (SHA-256 of bytes), `sizeBytes`, `mimeType`, `storageKey`, `state` (reserved | committed | purging), `refCount` (derived: referencing `mediaItems`), `committedAt`.
- `labelVocabulary` — `labelId`, `name`, `synonyms` json, `parentLabelId`. ≈5,000 rows.
- `places` — `id`, `name`, `level` (poi | city | region | country), `parentPlaceId`, `lat`, `lng`.

Per-owner tables:
- `users` — `id`, `email`, `displayName`, `quotaBytes`, `state` (active | deletionRequested | purging | purged), `deletionRequestedAt`, `activeGeneration` (model generation currently indexed), `settings` json.
- `devices` — `id`, `userId`, `kind`, `name`, `tokenHash`, `createdAt`, `lastSeenAt`, `revokedAt`, `backupEnabled`, `maxAcceptedHlc`.
- `uploadSessions` — `id`, `ownerId`, `deviceId`, `blobId` (claimed hash), `sizeBytes`, `localAssetId`, `externalUploadId`, `state` (open | finalizing | done | expired | failed), `expiresAt` (created + 24 h). Open rows are quota reservations; time-bounded.
- `mediaItems` — `id`, `ownerId`, `blobId`, `kind` (photo | video), `contentHash` (unique with `ownerId`), `perceptualHash` (derived), `captureTime` ts, `captureOffsetMinutes`, `captureTimeSource` (exif | file | user), `captureTimeHlc`, `width`, `height`, `orientation`, `durationMs`, `uploadedAt`, `uploadedSeq`, `sourceItemId` (set on partner-saved copies), `favorite` + `favoriteHlc`, `archived` + `archivedHlc`, `trashState` (live | trashed), `trashGeneration`, `trashHlc`, `trashedAt`, `trashSeq`, `purgeAfter` ts (`trashedAt` + 60 d; null when live), `lat`, `lng`, `locationSource`, `locationHlc`, `placeId` (derived), `currentEditId` (derived from `editSaves`), `chargedBytes`, `indexVersion` (bumped on any change to the synced projection), `purgedAt` (tombstone, retained 90 d).
  *Sync projection* (tier T carries only this): `id, captureTime, captureOffsetMinutes, kind, width, height, orientation, durationMs, favorite, archived, trashState, purgeAfter, currentEditId, thumbHash (24 bytes), indexVersion`.
- `renditions` — `(mediaItemId, kind)`, kind ∈ thumb256 | preview1024 | preview2048 | video720 | edited:`editSaveId`; `storageKey`, `width`, `height`, `sizeBytes`, `state`. Derived; not synced (keys are computed).
- `editSaves` — `id`, `mediaItemId`, `ownerId`, `baseEditSaveId` (null = original), `deviceId`, `hlc`, `serverSeq`, `operations` json (crop, rotate, adjustments), `fork` bool, `renditionState`. Append-only. Head = max `hlc`.
- `itemLabels` — `(mediaItemId, labelId, generation)`, `ownerId`, `confidence`, `source` (ml | user). Derived per generation; retired generations dropped.
- `faceInstances` — `id`, `mediaItemId`, `ownerId`, `faceKey` (box quantized to 1/64 of the image; stable across generations), `box`, `generation`, `clusterId`, `embeddingRef` (opaque, never synced). Derived.
- `faceClusters` — `id`, `ownerId`, `generation`, `personId` (null = unnamed group), `representativeFaceId`, `faceCount` (derived), `state` (active | merged | retired), `mergedIntoId`.
- `persons` — `id`, `ownerId`, `name`, `hidden` + `hiddenHlc`, `mergedIntoPersonId` (chain, followed to its root), `anchors` json (≤ 50 `(mediaItemId, faceKey)` pairs the owner confirmed), `createdAt`. Persons outlive clusters; rules reference persons, never clusters.
- `faceAssertions` — `(ownerId, mediaItemId, faceKey)`, `kind` (confirm | reject | notAFace), `personId` or `rejectedPersonId`, `hlc`, `deviceId`. User authority over machine output; keyed by `faceKey` so it survives generations; last-writer by `hlc`.
  *Effective person of face f* := if a confirm assertion → its person's root; else `clusters[f.clusterId].personId` root, unless an assertion rejects that person → none.
- `itemSearchIndex` — `(mediaItemId)`, `ownerId`, `generation`, `labelIds` packed, `personIds` packed (effective roots, non-hidden), `placeId`, `placeAncestorIds` packed, `captureDayLocal` int, `textTokens` packed, `favorite`, `archived`, `trashState`, `indexVersion`. ≈80 bytes; derived from six tables; the row tier S syncs.
- `storageUsage` — `(ownerId)`: `usedBytes`, `trashBytes`, `reservedBytes`, `itemCount`, `trashCount`, `updatedAt`. Maintained counters, written in the same transaction as every event that changes them; reconciled by scheduled full scan.
- `albums` — `id`, `ownerId`, `title`, `coverMediaItemId`, `shared`, `collaborate`, `allowComments`, `allowLikes`, `shareLocation`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `deletedAt`.
- `albumItems` — `id`, `albumId`, `ownerId` (album owner), `mediaItemId`, `mediaOwnerId`, `addedByUserId`, `addedAt`, `removedAt`, `removedByUserId`, `suspended` (item currently trashed; placement retained), `orderKey` (fractional string). Interval rows; at most one *open* row per `(albumId, mediaItemId)` (partial unique index on `removedAt is null`). Sync partition: every open member.
- `albumMembers` — `id`, `albumId`, `ownerId` (album owner), `userId`, `role` (owner | member), `joinedAt`, `joinedVia` (invite | link:`linkId` | owner), `leftAt`, `removedByUserId`, `muted`, `lastSeenActivityAt`. Interval rows; one open row per `(albumId, userId)`. Sync partition: the member.
- `shareLinks` — `id`, `albumId`, `ownerId`, `tokenHash`, `permits` (view | join), `createdAt`, `revokedAt`, `joinCount` (derived).
- `comments` — `id`, `albumId`, `ownerId` (album owner), `albumItemId` (null for album-level), `authorUserId`, `body` (≤ 2,000 chars), `createdAt`, `editedAt`, `deletedAt`, `deletedByUserId`. Attached to the *stay*, not the photo.
- `likes` — `(albumItemId, userId)`, `albumId`, `ownerId`, `createdAt`, `removedAt`. Open row = liked.
- `activity` — `id`, `albumId`, `ownerId`, `kind`, `actorUserId`, `subjectIds` json, `createdAt`. Append-only; read cursor per member.
- `partnerShares` — `id`, `ownerId` (sharer), `partnerUserId`, `state` (invited | active | ended), `scope` (all | persons), `personIds` json (sharer's persons), `fromCaptureDate`, `includeLocation`, `partnerAutoSave` (none | all), `acceptedAt`, `endedAt`, `endedBy`, `reconciledThroughSeq`, `reconciledGeneration`.
- `partnerShareItems` — `(partnerShareId, mediaItemId)`, `ownerId` (sharer; retention), `syncOwnerId` (partner), `visibleSince`, `visibleUntil`, `reason` (all | person:`personId`), `savedAsMediaItemId`. Interval rows; the materialized derived set. Open row ⇔ the partner may see the item now.
- `duplicateGroups` — `id`, `ownerId`, `kind` (exact | near), `memberItemIds` json, `suggestedKeepId`, `state` (open | resolved | dismissed | stale), `generation`.
- `memories` — `id`, `ownerId`, `kind`, `title`, `itemIds` json (≤ 30), `personIds`, `dateRange`, `generatedAt`, `showFrom`, `showUntil`, `dismissedAt`, `invalidatedAt`.
- `jobs` — `id`, `ownerId`, `kind` (derive | reindex | partnerReconcile | purgeTrash | accountPurge | export | memories), `state`, `cursor` json (resumable), `snapshotSeq`, `attempts`, `startedAt`, `finishedAt`.
- `exports` — `id`, `ownerId`, `requestedAt`, `snapshotSeq`, `state`, `parts` json, `sizeBytes`, `itemCount`, `expiresAt` (+7 d).
- `syncLog` — `(syncOwnerId, seq)`, `table`, `rowId`, `op` (upsert | tombstone | tierReset), `tier` (T | S | A | P), `indexVersion`, `at`. Append-only per sync partition; entries older than 90 days are compacted (a cursor older than the horizon forces a tier reset).

**Time-bounded values.** `uploadSessions.expiresAt`, `purgeAfter`, tombstones (90 d), the `syncLog` horizon (90 d), `memories.showUntil`, `exports.expiresAt`, `deletionRequestedAt` + 30 d, and every interval row's `[since, until)`.

**Client replica — the bounded closure made explicit.** Per device, per tier: `{cursorSeq, backfillWatermark (captureTime), complete}`.
- Tier T (timeline): the sync projection of all own `mediaItems`, trashed and archived included as flags. Backfill recent-first, 2,000 rows per page; 100k rows ≈ 10 MB.
- Tier S (search): `itemSearchIndex` rows, same order; ≈ 8 MB at 100k. `complete` only when the watermark reaches the oldest item at the active generation.
- Tier A (albums): own `albums`, open `albumMembers`, open `albumItems` of member albums (2,000 most recent per album locally, the rest paged); comments, likes, activity fetched per opened album.
- Tier P (partner inbound): open `partnerShareItems` plus the sharer's sync projection for those items; recent-first; local cap 20,000 rows, the rest paged.
- Tier F (detail): per item on demand — EXIF, faces with boxes, labels with confidence, edit history, rendition keys. LRU cache; never bulk-synced.
A `tierReset` entry (generation switch, horizon expiry) makes the client discard and re-backfill one tier.

### 5. The hard operations

Conventions: `now` is server time; every write to a synced row appends a `syncLog` entry in the same transaction for each affected sync partition; "atomic" means one transaction.

**Op 1 — Backup upload** (device authority; external object store; dedupe; quota).
Inputs: `deviceId`, `localAssetId`, `contentHash`, `sizeBytes`, `mimeType`, EXIF summary. Authorization: an unrevoked device of an `active` user.
```
begin:
  if mediaItems(owner, contentHash) exists and not purged: bind localAssetId; return {itemId, status:'exists'}   -- idempotent
  u := storageUsage(owner)
  if u.usedBytes + u.reservedBytes + sizeBytes > quotaBytes: refuse QUOTA_EXCEEDED
  atomic: uploadSessions.insert(state:'open', expiresAt: now+24h); u.reservedBytes += sizeBytes
  if blobs[contentHash].state == 'committed': return {sessionId, skipBytes:true}
  return {sessionId, target: externalStore.createResumableUpload(key: contentHash)}
client uploads bytes to target (resumable across restarts)
finalize(sessionId), atomic:
  s := session with state 'open' (else return its terminal result — idempotent)
  unless skipBytes: verify externalStore.object(contentHash) exists with matching size and hash, else fail HASH_MISMATCH
  blobs.upsert(contentHash, state:'committed')
  item := mediaItems(owner, contentHash) if exists (another device finished first)
       else insert(captureTime from EXIF else file time, chargedBytes: sizeBytes, uploadedSeq: next seq);
            blobs.refCount += 1; u.usedBytes += sizeBytes; u.itemCount += 1
  u.reservedBytes -= sizeBytes; s.state := 'done'; syncLog(owner, T, item)
enqueue jobs.derive(item); return {itemId}
expiry (hourly): open sessions with expiresAt < now → 'expired'; reservedBytes -= size; abort the external upload
```
Atomic: session with reservation; finalize. Eventual: renditions, labels, faces, place (seconds to minutes). Optimistic: the local asset shows in the timeline at its EXIF time with a badge; `exists`/`done` binds it to the item. Conflict: two devices racing on one hash — `(ownerId, contentHash)` uniqueness makes the second finalize adopt the first's item; both succeed. Near-duplicates (perceptual hash within Hamming distance 6) become an asynchronous `duplicateGroups` suggestion and never block upload.

**Op 2 — Derive one item** (system authority; background; external geocoder).
Inputs: `itemId`. Authorization: system only; runs at the owner's `activeGeneration`.
```
compute labels (~15), faces (boxes, embeddings), perceptualHash, thumbHash, renditions; geocode lat/lng → placeId
for each face: clusterId := nearest cluster of this owner+generation within threshold, else a new cluster
atomic:
  itemLabels.insertAll; faceInstances.insertAll; mediaItems.set(placeId, perceptualHash, thumbHash, indexVersion+1)
  itemSearchIndex.upsert(labels, effective persons, place ancestors, captureDayLocal, flags)
  syncLog(owner, T), syncLog(owner, S)
  for each active partnerShare of owner: applyPartnerRule(share, item)        -- Op 8, single-item form
```
Atomic: the item's derived rows and index row together — a reader never sees labels without faces. Eventual: attaching a *new* cluster to a person waits for the owner ("name this person") or the anchor matcher. Conflict: a concurrently trashed item still gets its derived rows (needed on restore); `applyPartnerRule` adds nothing.

**Op 3 — Sync pull and offline search** (read).
Inputs: `deviceId`, per-tier `{cursorSeq, backfillWatermark}`, `pageSize ≤ 2,000`. Authorization: a device of the user; tiers A and P return only rows whose `syncOwnerId` is the user.
```
per tier:
  if cursorSeq < horizon(syncOwnerId) or a tierReset entry follows cursorSeq: return {reset:true}   -- client discards the tier
  changes := syncLog(syncOwnerId, seq > cursorSeq, tier) joined to current rows (latest indexVersion wins; tombstones for purged rows)
  backfill := tier rows with captureTime < backfillWatermark, order captureTime desc, id desc, limit pageSize
  return {changes, newCursor, backfill, newWatermark, complete: backfill exhausted}
```
Offline search (client): parse the query into label ids (vocabulary, synonyms, parent expansion), person ids (local `persons` copy), place ids (local gazetteer subset of places referenced by own items), a date range, and flags; intersect over the local `itemSearchIndex`; exclude trashed, and archived unless asked; sort `captureTime desc`; label results with the tier-S watermark ("covering photos since 2019-03-02" while `complete = false`). Online search runs the same query server-side at `activeGeneration`, paging 200; the client merges by item id and keeps the label. No optimism; conflict-free.

**Op 4 — Favorite / archive toggle** (merge).
Inputs: `itemId`, `field` ∈ {favorite, archived}, `value`, `hlc`, `deviceId`. Authorization: the owner.
```
if hlc > now + 5 min: refuse CLOCK_SKEW (device re-stamps and retries)
atomic:
  item := mediaItems[itemId]; if purged: return GONE
  if hlc > item[field+'Hlc'] (tie: deviceId):
      item[field] := value; item[field+'Hlc'] := hlc; indexVersion+1; itemSearchIndex[field] := value
      if field == 'archived': for each active share: applyPartnerRule(share, item)     -- archive removes synchronously
      syncLog(owner, T), syncLog(owner, S)
  else: no-op (stale intent loses)
  devices[deviceId].maxAcceptedHlc := max(., hlc)
```
Capture-time and location edits take the same shape with `captureTimeHlc`/`locationHlc`, and additionally call `applyPartnerRule` and re-geocode. Atomic: the field, its clock, the index row, partner rows. Optimistic: the client flips immediately; the server echo confirms, or a newer value from another device overrides. Conflict: field LWW by hlc — two devices converge to the later wall-clock action regardless of arrival order.

**Op 5 — Trash, restore, empty trash, scheduled purge** (state machine; scheduled; bulk).
Inputs: `itemIds` (≤ 500), `action` ∈ {trash, restore}, `hlc`; or `emptyTrash`. Authorization: the owner. A trash launched from a `duplicateGroups` suggestion first re-validates that every member is still live; otherwise the group is marked `stale` and the action refused, listing the changed items.
```
trash / restore, atomic per item:
  if hlc <= item.trashHlc: skip (stale)
  trash and live:   trashState := trashed; trashGeneration += 1; trashHlc := hlc; trashedAt := now; trashSeq := next seq; purgeAfter := now + 60d
                    u.trashBytes += chargedBytes; u.trashCount += 1
                    open albumItems rows of the item: suspended := true            -- placements survive
                    for each active share: applyPartnerRule(share, item)          -- removal now
                    memories containing the item: invalidatedAt := now
  restore and trashed: trashState := live; trashHlc := hlc; purgeAfter := null; counters reversed; suspended := false
                    for each active share: applyPartnerRule(share, item)
  indexVersion+1; syncLog(owner, T, S); syncLog(member, A) for each affected album's open members
emptyTrash: pages of 200, atomic per page: purgeAfter := now for the owner's trashed items; then run purge for that owner now
purge (daily 03:00 owner-local, and on demand; resumable): items with purgeAfter < now, pages of 200, atomic per page:
  open albumItems → removedAt := now, removedBy := system (comments and likes stay on the closed stay)
  open partnerShareItems → visibleUntil := now (savedAsMediaItemId untouched)
  delete the item's itemLabels, faceInstances, faceAssertions, itemSearchIndex, renditions; drop from duplicateGroups
  blobs.refCount -= 1 for original and edited blobs; refCount 0 → 'purging' (external delete after 24 h)
  u.usedBytes -= chargedBytes; trashBytes, itemCount, trashCount decremented
  mediaItems.purgedAt := now (tombstone 90 d); syncLog(owner, T, S tombstones); syncLog(A), syncLog(P) for affected principals
```
Atomic: each item's transition with its counters and album/partner effects. Eventual: external blob deletion. Optimistic: the client moves the item to its local trash. Conflict: hlc fencing — a stale offline `trash` cannot re-trash something restored later; `restore` of a purged item returns GONE and the client says "already permanently deleted".

**Op 6 — Save edit** (append-only log; merge).
Inputs: `itemId`, `baseEditSaveId`, `operations`, `hlc`, `deviceId`. Authorization: the owner.
```
atomic:
  head := mediaItems.currentEditId
  save := editSaves.insert(base: baseEditSaveId, hlc, serverSeq: next, fork: baseEditSaveId != head, renditionState:'pending')
  if head is null or hlc > editSaves[head].hlc: mediaItems.currentEditId := save.id; indexVersion+1; syncLog(owner, T)
enqueue render(save) → renditions[(item, edited:save.id)]; on ready: renditionState := 'ready'; syncLog(owner, T)
revert := a save with operations [] (history stays monotone)
```
Atomic: the save and the head pointer. Eventual: the rendition (the device shows its own render meanwhile). Conflict: two offline saves against one base both persist; the later hlc is the head; the other is listed as "edited on <device>" and can be made head by saving it again.

**Op 7 — Face assertion, person merge, hide** (user authority over machine output; synchronous removal).
Inputs: `itemId`, `faceKey`, `kind`, `personId` or `rejectedPersonId`, `hlc`; or `merge(A, B)`; or `setHidden(personId, value, hlc)`. Authorization: the owner only. Members and the partner can never write into the owner's persons, clusters, or assertions; their "who is this?" view of shared items uses their own library's faces.
```
assert, atomic:
  faceAssertions.upsert by (owner, item, faceKey) if hlc newer
  if confirm: persons[personId].anchors += (item, faceKey)   (cap 50, oldest dropped)
  recompute effective persons of the item → itemSearchIndex.personIds; indexVersion+1; syncLog(owner, S)
  for each active share: applyPartnerRule(share, item)       -- a rejection removes NOW; a confirm adds NOW
merge(A, B), atomic: persons[B].mergedIntoPersonId := A; A.anchors += B.anchors; clusters with personId B → A
  enqueue jobs.reindex(personsOnly) and jobs.partnerReconcile(reason: merge)
setHidden, atomic: persons.hidden := value (hlc LWW); enqueue memories invalidation and reindex(personsOnly)
```
Atomic: the assertion, the item's index row, that item's partner rows. Eventual: a merge's bulk effects (thousands of index rows; partner additions), expected within minutes. Optimistic: the face moves under the new person immediately. Conflict: per-key hlc LWW; a merge and an assertion on two devices converge because assertions store person ids that are resolved to the merge root at read time.

**Op 8 — Partner-rule application and reconcile** (system authority; derived set; single-item and bulk).
Inputs: `share`, `item` (single-item form, called inside Ops 2, 4, 5, 7 and the edit paths); or `share` alone (bulk).
```
selected(share, item) :=
  share.state == active and item.trashState == live and not item.archived and item.purgedAt is null
  and (share.fromCaptureDate is null or item.captureTime >= share.fromCaptureDate)
  and (share.scope == all
       or ∃ face ∈ faceInstances(item, owner.activeGeneration): root(effectivePerson(face)) ∈ roots(share.personIds))
applyPartnerRule(share, item), inside the caller's transaction:
  open := open partnerShareItems row (share, item)
  if selected and open is null: insert(visibleSince: now, reason); syncLog(partner, P)
  if not selected and open exists: open.visibleUntil := now; syncLog(partner, P)
reconcile(share) job — on rule create, scope or date change, person merge/split, generation switch, share end:
  pages of 1,000 own items by captureTime desc (jobs.cursor): atomic per page: applyPartnerRule for each
  share.reconciledThroughSeq := current seq; reconciledGeneration := activeGeneration
  share end: close every open row (pages of 1,000); state := ended; partner's tier P receives tombstones; saved copies untouched
notify (daily 18:00 partner-local): open rows with visibleSince in the last 24 h → one "N new photos" notification
```
Atomic: one item's row with the triggering write — this is what makes removal prompt. Eventual: bulk additions (minutes); bulk removals from a split or narrowed rule run first and with priority, targeting under one minute at 100k items. Optimistic: none. Conflict: triggers for one item serialize on the item row; `reconcile` is idempotent and resumable.

**Op 9 — Album add, remove, reorder, comment, like** (interval rows; other people's data; notifications).
Inputs: `albumId`, `mediaItemIds`, `afterOrderKey`; `albumItemId` (remove, comment, like); `(albumItemId, newOrderKey)`; `body`. Authorization: add — the album owner, or an open member when `collaborate`, and only for the adder's own live items; remove/reorder — the album owner or the row's `addedByUserId`; comment/like — an open member or the owner, on an open unsuspended row, with `allowComments`/`allowLikes` on; delete a comment — its author or the album owner; unlike — the liker only.
```
add, atomic per item:
  require mediaItems[item].ownerId == caller and trashState == live
  if an open albumItems row exists for (album, item): return it (idempotent)
  insert(addedAt: now, addedBy: caller, orderKey: between(afterOrderKey, next)); activity.append('itemsAdded')
  syncLog(member, A) for every open member; notifications to unmuted members, coalesced per actor per 10 min
remove, atomic: removedAt := now; removedBy := caller       (comments and likes stay on the closed row, hidden with it)
reorder, atomic: orderKey := newOrderKey (fractional string between neighbours); collision → append a suffix character
comment, atomic: comments.insert(albumItemId, author: caller); activity.append; syncLog(member, A); notify the stay's contributor and prior commenters unless muted
like, atomic: likes.upsert open row (idempotent); unlike: removedAt := now
delete comment, atomic: deletedAt := now; deletedBy := caller (body retained 30 d, then blanked)
```
Atomic: each row with its activity entry. Eventual: notifications. Optimistic: the client inserts the item with a provisional key and shows a comment as "sending"; a refusal (item removed, member removed) is shown and the draft kept. Conflict: concurrent adds of one photo converge to one open row; concurrent reorders converge by key (ties by row id); an add by a member removed a moment earlier fails the membership check and the client reports "you are no longer in this album"; like toggles from two devices converge to the last server-applied state — likes are not intent-critical, so row LWW is accepted here and stated as such.

**Op 10 — Share link create, revoke, resolve, join** (visibility; refusals).
Inputs: `albumId`, `permits`; or `linkId`; or `token`. Authorization: create/revoke — album owner; resolve/join — anyone holding the token.
```
create: token := 128-bit random; store tokenHash; return URL
resolve(token): l := shareLinks by hash
  if none, or revokedAt set, or album deleted: return NOT_AVAILABLE   (one code, same latency, rate-limited 20/min per address)
  else return the viewer projection of the album
join(token): as resolve; if l.permits == join and caller signed in and no open membership:
  albumMembers.insert(joinedVia: link:l.id); joinCount += 1; activity 'memberJoined'; syncLog(joiner, A) (backfills the album)
revoke: revokedAt := now; members remain (a separate action removes link-joined members)
viewer projection of an item: id, captureTime, dimensions, rendition keys, contributor display name;
  lat/lng and placeId only if album.shareLocation; never labels, persons, or edit history beyond the current rendition
```
Atomic: the membership insert with `joinCount`. Conflict: two joins by one user race on open-membership uniqueness; both receive the same membership.

**Op 11 — Save partner item to own library** (cross-partition blob reference).
Inputs: `partnerShareId`, `itemId`. Authorization: caller is `partnerUserId` of an active share with an open `partnerShareItems` row for the item.
```
atomic:
  src := mediaItems[itemId]; blob := blobs[src.blobId]
  if usage(caller).usedBytes + reservedBytes + blob.sizeBytes > quota: refuse QUOTA_EXCEEDED
  copy := mediaItems(caller, src.contentHash) if exists (idempotent) else insert(
     ownerId: caller, blobId: src.blobId, contentHash, captureTime and offset copied,
     lat/lng only if share.includeLocation, sourceItemId: src.id, chargedBytes: blob.sizeBytes, uploadedSeq)
  on insert: blobs.refCount += 1; usage(caller) updated
  partnerShareItems.savedAsMediaItemId := copy.id; syncLog(caller, T), syncLog(caller, P)
enqueue jobs.derive(copy)   -- the caller's own labels, faces, clusters; the sharer's persons, assertions, and edits are never copied
```
Atomic: copy, refcount, counters. Eventual: derivation. Optimistic: the item appears in the partner's timeline with a badge. Conflict: the sharer trashing the source concurrently closes the share row, but the copy is already the caller's, and its blob reference survives the sharer's purge. `partnerAutoSave = all` runs these steps in a job from Op 8's insert path, stopping with a notification when the partner's quota is full.

**Op 12 — Model generation switch and re-index** (bulk; system; one sync event).
Inputs: `ownerId`, `newGeneration`. Authorization: system.
```
job, jobs.cursor resumable:
  pages of 500 own items (live and trashed) by id: compute labels and faces at newGeneration; insert rows tagged newGeneration (old rows untouched)
  cluster newGeneration faces (~150k embeddings)
  for each root person p: attach every newGeneration cluster containing a face at an anchor (item, faceKey), or overlapping an anchor box at IoU ≥ 0.6; a rejected face never attaches
  build staged itemSearchIndex rows at newGeneration (pages of 1,000)
  atomic switch: users.activeGeneration := newGeneration; syncLog(owner, S, op: tierReset)      -- exactly one event
  enqueue reconcile(share) for each active share; enqueue memories regeneration
  after 7 days: delete itemLabels, faceInstances, faceClusters rows of the old generation
```
Atomic: the switch. Eventual: everything else; until the switch the owner searches and shares at the old generation; after it the client re-backfills tier S (recent-first, 8 MB) instead of consuming 100k row events. Conflict: assertions and person edits made during the job target persons, which are generation-independent, and are honoured because attachment runs from anchors at the end; a tail pass re-derives items uploaded during the job at the old generation.

**Op 13 — Account deletion and export** (scheduled; bulk; external).
Inputs: `requestDeletion`, `cancelDeletion`, `requestExport`. Authorization: the account holder after fresh re-authentication; purge and export are system jobs.
```
requestDeletion, atomic: users.state := deletionRequested; deletionRequestedAt := now; other devices revoked
  partnerShares in either direction: open partnerShareItems rows closed (visibleUntil := now); reopened by reconcile on cancel
cancelDeletion (within 30 d), atomic: state := active; enqueue reconcile for each share
purge (daily; deletionRequestedAt + 30 d < now): state := purging; job resumable:
  1. partnerShares both directions: state := ended; close open rows; syncLog(partner, P)
  2. owned albums: deletedAt := now; close every albumMembers row; syncLog(member, A)
  3. memberships elsewhere: leftAt := now; own contributions removedAt := now; likes removedAt := now; comments.authorUserId := DELETED_USER (body kept)
  4. mediaItems in pages of 200: as in Op 5 purge, live items included; refCount decrements; tombstones
  5. persons, clusters, assertions, index rows, memories, duplicate groups, jobs, exports deleted; storageUsage zeroed; devices revoked;
     users.state := purged (row kept 90 d for tombstone consistency, then deleted)
requestExport, atomic: exports.insert(snapshotSeq: current owner seq, expiresAt: now + 7 d); enqueue job
export job: own items with uploadedSeq <= snapshotSeq and purgedAt null and (trashState == live or trashSeq > snapshotSeq), pages of 200:
  stream originals from the external store into 2 GB zip parts with per-item metadata JSON
     (capture time, location, labels, person names, albums, edit operations, flags)
  exports.parts appended per completed part; state := ready when done; parts deleted after expiresAt
```
Atomic: state transitions; each purge page. Eventual: the purge (hours at 100k items) and the export (hours for hundreds of GB). Conflict: an item trashed after the snapshot is still exported (`trashSeq > snapshotSeq`); an item uploaded after it is not; a partner trying to save during the grace period is refused because the open rows were closed at request time.

### 6. Scale and cost

Sizes for a successful deployment: 2M accounts, 600k monthly active, median library 12k items, top decile 60k, heavy users 100k–300k; ≈ 30 billion items fleet-wide. Per heavy user (100k items, 5% video): originals ≈ 190 GB photos (2 MB each) + 200 GB video (40 MB each) ≈ 390 GB; renditions ≈ 60 GB; derived rows ≈ 15 labels + 1.5 faces + 1 index row per item ≈ 1.8M; server posting-list index ≈ 12 MB; the two synced tiers ≈ 18 MB.
Rates: an active phone uploads ≈ 50 items/day with bursts of 3,000 after a trip; fleet ≈ 30M uploads/day, 350/s average, 4,000/s peak. Derivation ≈ 150 ms CPU per photo, ≈ 10 s per video. Album fan-out: one event in a 50-member album writes 50 log entries and up to 50 notifications; partner arrivals batch into one notification per day.

Expensive at these sizes:
- First sync of tiers T+S at 100k items: 50 pages, ≈ 18 MB, 30–90 s on LTE — acceptable because it is recent-first and the app is usable after page one. Tier F is never bulk-fetched (1.8M rows).
- Generation switch: 100k × 150 ms ≈ 4 CPU-hours per heavy user; 30B × 150 ms ≈ 140 CPU-years fleet-wide. A model upgrade is a months-long rolling program — hence §7.5's concession.
- Bulk partner reconcile on a rule or person change: 100k item reads, ≈ 10 s; a split that removes visibility must finish under a minute (priority queue).
- Trash purge: fleet ≈ 15M items/day; per user seconds; external blob deletion is the long pole.
- Account purge: ≈ 2M row deletes, 100k refcount updates, 100k tombstones; ≈ 10 minutes per heavy user, resumable.
- Counter reconciliation: a full scan per user is 100k rows; fleet 30B rows — shard to weekly per user.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Tiered, prioritized, completeness-aware partial replication of one principal's 100k-row partition.** The replica is not "the rows this user may read": it is compact projections in tiers, backfilled recent-first, with per-tier cursors, a tombstone horizon, an explicit reset signal, and a coverage watermark the product displays. An ordinary query-and-subscribe backend either ships the reachable closure (1.8M rows) or pages from the server and has no offline search. *Concession:* replicate only the last 12 months or 10k items and page the rest, with search labelled "offline: photos since <date>". *Cheat:* an unlabelled partial index, or an "offline mode" that is a cached last screen.

2. **A live derived set over machine-derived facts with layered authority and asymmetric propagation.** The partner's view is `rule ∘ persons ∘ clusters ∘ faces ∘ product state`, rewritten by the model, the owner, and the system; its removals must be synchronous with the owner's action (a rejection, a trash) and its additions may be eventual; and it needs a history, because the partner's likes and saved copies refer to a stay. Ordinary backends offer a four-way join per read (no history; 100k-row scans per partner page) or a hand-maintained materialization with nine invalidation triggers that someone forgets. *Concession:* evaluate the rule at derive time and on explicit edits, and reconcile hourly for everything else. *Cheat:* any removal that waits for the hourly job — "not Alice" while the partner still sees the photo.

3. **Assets as a transactional resource spanning an external store and several partitions.** Content-hash dedupe under concurrent uploads, time-bounded reservations counted into a maintained sum, a two-phase commit whose middle step is another system, cross-account blob references, and a purge that must respect other partitions' references. Ordinary backends have "an upload URL and a row". *Concession:* no cross-account sharing of bytes (a save copies the object, charged to the saver) and a quota check against the maintained counter without reservations, allowing overshoot bounded by concurrent uploads × maximum file size. *Cheat:* deleting a referenced blob, leaking unreferenced ones forever, or computing quota with a sum over the partition per upload.

4. **Intent-ordered field merge and per-row state machines.** Field-level last-writer by device HLC with server clock clamping, trash as a generation-fenced state with a purge deadline, edits as an append-only log with a computed head, fractional order keys. Ordinary backends resolve by arrival order. *Concession:* arrival-order LWW for favorite and archive, where conflicts are rare and reversible. *Cheat:* any path where a stale offline operation can cause a permanent deletion or discard an edit.

5. **Bulk re-derivation as a generation switch the sync sees as one event.** A model upgrade rewrites 1.8M rows per heavy user; the search index must be consistent at one generation; persons must survive by anchors; clients must receive one reset, not 100k updates. Ordinary backends emit a change per row. *Concession:* apply the new model only to new uploads and say so ("older photos use older labels"). *Cheat:* mixing generations silently, so "dog" finds 2025 photos and not 2019 ones with no explanation.

One count rule: storage used and "Alice — 1,204 photos" are *structural* counts over the unfiltered partition (storage includes trash, archive, and hidden; people exclude trash but include archive); the partner's "1,204 photos of Alice" is a *policy* count over their visible set. Visibility-filtering the first pair undercounts silently and confidently.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice (high confidence): content-hash dedupe with an idempotent finalize; resumable uploads with expiring reservations; non-destructive edit stacks with an always-recoverable original; trash with a fixed expiry and a usage meter that includes trash; persons as owner-named entities distinct from machine clusters, with user assertions outranking the model; partner sharing by "all" or "people" from a date with save-to-library copies that outlive the share; interval rows for memberships and album items; fractional order keys; HLC-stamped field LWW; refcounted blobs; per-partition change logs with tombstone horizons; generation-based re-indexing. Scale figures are order-of-magnitude estimates.

Invented or sharpened for the exercise (medium confidence): the explicit tier/watermark completeness contract for offline search and the tier-reset signal; separating the retention partition from the sync partition for inbound partner rows; comments attached to a stay rather than a photo (real products vary); the stated asymmetry that removals run inside the triggering transaction while additions queue; anchors keyed by `(item, faceKey)` with IoU re-attachment across generations; the export snapshot rule via `trashSeq`; and every numeric cap (HLC clamp, reservation and horizon lengths, tier caps). Least confident: holding bulk removals after a person split under one minute at 100k items without a per-person posting list — if that misses, add `personId → itemIds` postings.

## New fundamental and major findings

### N1. Clock pins excluded from footprints make `Time` validation a silent pass across batches — MAJOR — committer §6; read-sets §3, §4

Read-sets §3 excludes monotone time-audience pins from footprints, yet §4 validates point entries (`Row`, `Seq`, `Receipt`, `Auth` "and the rest," which includes `Time(clock)`) by looking up the window maps and conflicting only if the latest revision exceeds S. A floor advance therefore leaves no window entry, so a member whose snapshot predates a clock bump and whose effective time now sits below the floor still passes window validation. Committer §6's pin rule ("accepted only if its effective time is at or after the pin of the members accepted before it in revision order") needs the pre-batch pin as input, but its initialization from the durable floor and its storage across batches are unspecified — pins were just excluded from the structure that carries cross-batch state. Publication footprints cover `Time(audience, key)` but not the floor itself, even though the floor is advanced by a durable write ([time_audience.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/time_audience.rs:7)) and committed per member `at` ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:729)). Direction: validate `Time(clock)` predicate-style against the batch transaction's floor read (like N3), and specify the persistent pin watermark; keep the exclusion for change-evidence only.

### N2. Allocation counters live only in memory, with no crash, takeover, or adapter-reconciliation story — MAJOR — committer §4.5, §5

§4.5 assigns sequence allocations at commit "from its in-memory counter for that parent, in revision order." Nothing specifies write-through to the durable `_sequences` row ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:1091)), recovery of counters after a committer kill or takeover, or reconciliation with the two sequence sources §2.1 documents (SQLite `MAX(seq)+1` ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:714)) versus Postgres cached head + 1). After a restart the counter reinitializes while committed rows and index keys already carry the old allocations, so the same values can be assigned twice; later `sequence_value` readers ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:1087)) also see the stale durable row. Recording resolutions in the transcript keeps the replay oracle exact but does not recover the counter. Direction: make counters durable write-set entries applied in the batch transaction with recovery from durable state, before K3.

### N3. Predicate re-evaluation reads the wrong source — MAJOR — read-sets §4; committer §3.4, §4.5

§4 re-evaluates predicates "against the current value, meaning the committed value plus the deltas of the members already accepted in this batch." But §3.4 applies accepted members' write sets into the batch transaction in revision order and runs in-place members directly over it, so the batch transaction already contains every accepted effect. If "committed value" means batch-transaction state, adding accepted deltas double-counts; if it means the pre-batch snapshot, the formula misses in-place members' direct occupancy writes, which never pass through overlay deltas (cap counts update on every put and delete ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:690)) via the physical counter ([caps.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store/caps.rs:5))). An in-place sweep, view chunk, or administrative member earlier in the same batch can therefore move occupancy past a cap without tripping a later predicate member, committing an over-cap batch. Direction: define predicate validation as a single read of the batch transaction's current value, and confirm adapter-recorded in-place footprints include cap and maintain records.

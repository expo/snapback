## Verdict: SOUND WITH MAJOR ISSUES

The direction is sound and the prior panel's fixes were correctly absorbed. Four major gaps remain that could waste the committer build, break large graduations, or leave safety refusals undetectable. None requires a different architecture. Fix detection, SLO-gating, blob retention, and incremental verification, then proceed in the staged sequence.

## Fundamental and major findings

### F1. Async-promotion refusal has no detector — MAJOR — [the store port, §5](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:238)

Child A §5 records the replication guarantee at creation and says promotion on an asynchronous store makes `serve` refuse until recovery mints a new incarnation. No detection mechanism is specified. The document's own argument is that a head-regression check cannot establish continuity: lost commits can be refilled under the same sequence numbers, and session revocations and other non-head writes can vanish with no head change at all.

Evidence for the claim structure is in the parent ([failover continuity](llp/3000.009.001-snapback4-on-postgres.rfc.md:124)) and child A ([the two bullet proof](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:243)). The P1b evidence row plans a "refusal after promotion on an asynchronous" provider ([P1b witnesses](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:519)), which will force the design, but as written the mechanism cannot achieve what it claims: after an async failover with no head regression, `serve` has no stated way to distinguish promotion from an ordinary restart, so it can silently continue the same incarnation with revoked tokens valid again.

Recommended direction: define promotion detection now (for example, record the database system identifier, timeline, and last flush position at open and lease renewal; on reconnect compare them; any timeline or identifier change on an async-guarantee store forces refusal plus new-incarnation recovery before serving, never a head comparison). Add a witness that revokes a session, fails over with loss, and shows the revoked token refused or the store refusing. Until detection lands, refuse to adopt or open async-guarantee stores as same-incarnation-capable rather than calling them "supported with refusal."

### F2. Blob bytes have no joint retention rule with database PITR — MAJOR — [the store port, §4.2](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:145)

The design puts asset bytes and release trees in a `BlobStore` by digest, written before the claiming commit, with orphans reclaimed by `_asset_gc`, and says restore and graduation reconcile the ledger against the blob store and refuse while any digest is missing. Refusal after loss does not restore user bytes. The missing case: an asset uploaded, later deleted, its bytes garbage-collected, then PITR revives the earlier ledger entry that references them. The revived incarnation is missing data no refusal can recreate. Whether today's `_asset_gc` deletes deleted-asset bytes or only never-claimed orphans is UNVERIFIED (I could not open `assets.rs`), so the hole may be larger or smaller than stated, but no rule in the documents prevents it.

Recommended direction: add a blob-retention rule tied to the recovery window (never hard-delete bytes referenced by any commit within the PITR and graduation-revert horizons, or use a versioned blob store with joint ledger-plus-blob restore), constrain `_asset_gc` accordingly, and test PITR revival of a deleted asset and a restored store serving its bytes. Keep the refusal as fail-closed behavior, not as the recovery plan.

### F3. The committer decision is not SLO-constrained and S0 omits production write sets — MAJOR — [the scale RFC, §§6–9](llp/3000.009.001.002-scale-limits-against-postgres-controls.rfc.md:205)

Child C §6 defines sustainable throughput as the highest rate meeting all SLOs including writes p99 ≤ 100 ms. The §8 W4 rule then commissions committer (a) when the serial batch at "its best measured batch size" falls below 1,000 commits/s or 25% of raw Postgres. "Best" is unqualified. A large batch (S0 sweeps 1–64) amortizes the flush for throughput while the first-queued mutation waits through N serial programs, each paying `k·RTT` plus execution under the reference and batch protocols ([the bound](llp/3000.009.001-snapback4-on-postgres.rfc.md:176), [the batch](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:288)). The best-throughput batch can therefore violate the write SLO while winning the rule, overstating (0) or miscommissioning (a).

Separately, S0's engine-level cells (reference, serial batch, precomputed-write-set prototype "no overlay") omit the write sets child A §6.3 point 4 puts through the committer: every durable transaction, including read-triggered time-audience pins, asset views, and receipt-only writes ([the ordering requirement](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:336)). That read paths commit on `/changes`, `/sync`, `/q/`, `/m/` is asserted via `serve.rs` in the parent disposition and is UNVERIFIED here. A prototype without time, authority, and receipt-only writes bounds an easier problem than (a) must solve, and single-row clock contention is unmeasured before the expensive C2 overlay is commissioned.

Recommended direction: qualify the rule as the best batch size meeting all §6 SLOs and publish the throughput–latency curve per batch size. Add to S0 (or as a pre-commissioning gate) a time-publishing read-plus-write mix with full write sets, or an explicit clock-row contention cell, and state the clock design (per-mutation pin versus coalesced batch pin) before commissioning (a). If the full-write-set prototype still misses the bound, revisit (b) or scope instead of building (a).

### F4. Pre-cutover verification contradicts the no-long-snapshot rule and can break the pause budget — MAJOR — [graduation, §4.2 versus §§4.4–6](llp/3000.009.001.001-graduating-a-live-store-to-postgres.rfc.md:109)

The copier deliberately uses short SQLite read transactions so no long-lived snapshot holds back WAL checkpoints ([the rule](llp/3000.009.001.001-graduating-a-live-store-to-postgres.rfc.md:109)). The barrier design then holds a second-connection source snapshot open for the entire complete comparison: per-table, log, and family digests plus the shadow oracle sample ([barriers](llp/3000.009.001.001-graduating-a-live-store-to-postgres.rfc.md:154), [complete comparison](llp/3000.009.001.001-graduating-a-live-store-to-postgres.rfc.md:248)). For the 10⁸–10⁹-row stores named as motivation ([why Postgres](llp/3000.009.001-snapback4-on-postgres.rfc.md:88)), that digest takes minutes to hours while the source keeps serving: WAL piles up behind the pinned snapshot, and the journal delta since the barrier grows, so the in-pause O(delta) comparison ([delta step](llp/3000.009.001.001-graduating-a-live-store-to-postgres.rfc.md:260)) can exceed the 10 s pause budget ([cutover budget](llp/3000.009.001.001-graduating-a-live-store-to-postgres.rfc.md:223)). W13 measures pause at size but not source WAL growth or verification time ([W13 rule](llp/3000.009.001.002-scale-limits-against-postgres-controls.rfc.md:213)).

Recommended direction: make verification incremental (per-family or per-chunk digests under short seq-bounded reads plus journal-delta re-verification at successive barriers, with a re-barrier cadence), add preflight WAL and delta budgets with monitoring, and record verification time, source WAL bytes, and delta size alongside the pause in W13. Bound the largest qualified graduation size by those, not by the pause alone.

## What the documents get right that should not change

- A store as a logical identity, with graduation preserving it and restore minting a new one, including the replication-guarantee condition on same-incarnation failover.
- The port as a semantic contract for all durable state with extraction on SQLite first and the no-connection-above-the-port test.
- The commit path as the first scale risk, the serial batch as the first committer behind the seam, S0 calibrating rather than the reference deciding, and production Postgres on the lock-first reference ruled out. The head-lock premise matches the code ([Serialized takes the head row lock at begin](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/store.rs:115)).
- P1 as one process with a fenced standby before P2, with every new coordination point required to name its P2 mechanism.
- Graduation as capture-before-copy with triggers, chunked logical copy, transaction-aligned barriers, a crash-safe two-store state machine with a sealed target, reverse capture armed in the handoff, and O(delta) work inside the pause.
- Scale as predicted ceilings with formulas, same-semantics controls on separate hosts with swept RTT, a ledger classifying semantic cost versus design choice versus implementation, and decision rules written before measurement.
- WAL decoding set aside as commit order, and retention as checkpoint-plus-floor outside P1 rather than deletion below a floor.

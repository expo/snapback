**The lifetime rule holds for authoritative server state under the specified checks.** The attempted counterexamples resolve as follows:

- **Deletes, jobs and same-mutation writes:** `insert parent; insert child; delete parent` refuses because the structural check sees the uncommitted child. Deleting the child first permits deletion. Inserting a child after deleting its parent fails the live-parent guard. System authority cannot bypass these checks.
- **Expiry and three levels:** With grandparent deadline 100, middle-parent authored deadline 200 or absent, and leaf deadline 300, both descendants expire at 100. The middle parent’s authored expiry must be immutable. A mutable leaf deadline can shorten its life but cannot extend it beyond its captured parent deadline.
- **Sweep and replacement:** Physical children block same-id reuse, including replacement of an expired predecessor. Child-first sweeping removes their physical contributions before reuse. A replacement therefore cannot encounter the previous parent’s expired population through keyed reads or aggregate charges.
- **Upserts:** Updates preserve references and captures. An expired target takes the insertion path and must establish a live parent again.
- **Seeds and imports:** Ancestor-first capture against final batch images resolves ordering. Replacing parent inputs while physical children retain different captures refuses.
- **Restores and activation:** Restore recomputes effective deadlines and preserves validated captures. Activation judges previous-schema expiry before recapture, compares adoption inputs before projection, fills captures before validation, and publishes derived state with one generation. An expired child cannot be revived by extending its parent’s lifetime.

The expiry treatment also preserves [LLP 3000.007 §20.9.3](../../llp/3000.007-relaxed-quarry-and-explicit-residency.rfc.md:3585): expired sources stop contributing to live answers before their physical contributions are removed. Effectively expiring sources remain forbidden for maintained columns.

**That invariant does not literally hold for every device-stored image.** Independent logs, horizons and retained history can leave a locally live orphan or a child beside a replacement parent with different captures. Revision 7 accommodates this explicitly:

- Advancing the expiry clock handles finite deadlines when authoritative changes arrive.
- Mismatched live current children cannot borrow the replacement parent’s verdict; the page becomes incomplete and falls back.
- Retained-only children follow their own policy.
- Parent authorization supplies no child-coverage proof.
- Prediction requires current parent evidence or a replayed provisional dependency; parent deletion additionally requires complete child coverage.

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a good proposal and, more unusually, a good *plan*: it converts a genuinely strong evidence base (a 12-cell web matrix plus a 16-cell Apple-native extension, all with executable acceptance) into a sequenced program whose every claim I could trace to a witness, a file, or an owning document. I verified an atypically large fraction of its factual load-bearing claims directly — bundle bytes, line counts, matrix arithmetic, harness defects, runtime primitives, cross-repo counterpart text, and the status of every cited authority — and found **zero material factual errors**. That is remarkable for a 1,126-line document making claims across two repositories, a demo corpus, and a Rust runtime.

The Plan's three best decisions are structural. First, it treats its own evidence skeptically: it identified the two witness-integrity defects in the Partitime harness (the skip-exits-zero runner and the seeded-satisfiable RSVP oracle) that I independently confirmed in code, and it refuses to let convergence or push count as ranking inputs until corrected witnesses exist (§Evidence and limits item 1; Sequence step 0). Second, its measurement governance — method frozen at step 0, numbers computed by preregistered formulas from the first attribution run, a no-loosening ratchet with a ratified invalidation escape for provably wrong baselines — is preregistration discipline roadmap documents almost never have. Third, its cross-repository coordination is real, not aspirational: Exact 0373 §5.1 (verified in `/Users/ccheever/projects/exact/llp/0373-contract-and-exact-after-partitime.plan.md`) mirrors this Plan's Step 0, adopts the same sibling/dedup/three-fact contracts nearly verbatim, and carries a symmetric four-week staleness trigger.

Is anything wrongheaded? I looked hard and found nothing *definitely* wrongheaded. The three most debatable strong choices — the conjunctive step-0 floor under every implementation workstream, the never-loosen threshold ratchet, and a full-north-star graduation bar requiring LLP 0012 Parity (including deterministic local handler execution) on both mobile OSes — are each deliberate, bounded, and escape-hatched (an initial-category attribution scope so step 0 is not a full metrology program; the invalidation/rebaseline procedure; three named graduation outcomes with mandatory LLP 0000 reconciliation for narrowing). My remaining concerns are all minor and non-blocking.

## 3. Concerns

**C1 (minor — risk scoping): step 0's own stall has no explicitly scoped trigger.** Step 0 is "a conjunctive prerequisite for **every** implementation workstream" (§Sequence), and its heaviest work (attribution instrumentation, witness repair, protocol authorship) is Snapback-side. R6's mitigation text ("any blocked entry gate older than four weeks forces the named review") textually covers steps blocked on "step 0 done", but R6 is framed and titled as **cross-repository** stall on Exact amendments, so a Snapback-side metrology slip would not naturally be "surfaced as this named risk". Exact 0373 rev 7 added "a four-week phase-0 staleness trigger … symmetric with 0063's R6" — the symmetry currently exists only on the Exact side. *Resolution:* one sentence in Sequence step 0 or R6 stating the four-week review applies to step 0's own exit regardless of which repository the stall is in, or resolve open question 8 with exactly that answer.

**C2 (minor — method completeness): the instrument-correction taxonomy omits instrument *unavailability*.** §Product choice covers "drifted-but-valid" (re-derive, only tighten) and "provably invalid" (ratified invalidation) instruments, and workload growth (prospective rollover). It does not cover an instrument that becomes *unavailable* while still valid — most plausibly the "pinned agent configuration" for the agent-authoring-effort metric (model deprecation over a multi-step program) or a lost hardware class. A deprecated pinned agent is neither an instrument bug nor workload growth, so the letter of the rule leaves no lawful path to a replacement baseline. *Resolution:* extend the correction taxonomy with a replacement/unavailability case using the same discipline (documented cause, version-bumped method/threshold IDs, prospective re-run of both comparison arms and controls).

**C3 (minor — operational feasibility): frozen gateway instruments depend on live third-party services and credentials at every step exit.** §Sequence: "the gateway cells are frozen instruments re-run at step exits", and each step exit "re-runs the Partitime harness against the same fixture". The Supabase and Convex gateway cells require reachable hosted services and healthy credentials indefinitely — and credential fragility was this build's own top-documented friction (REPORT.md §5.1 publishable-key 401; §15.3 "Credential failures are architecture events"). With skip-is-not-pass correctly in force after step 0, a credential outage blocks a step exit on third-party state. *Resolution:* declare the posture in §Sequence or the step-0 protocol — e.g., a credential-health preflight with named, receipt-recorded skip semantics for remote gateway cells at step exits (distinct from required-gate aggregation), a hermetic replay substitute, or an explicit maintained-credentials commitment with an owner.

**C4 (minor — document maintainability): the Revised header is now ~4,500 words of normative-sounding prose.** Seven same-day reconciliation entries restate rules that also live in the body ("mandatory claim IDs … freeze at step 0", the invalidation procedure, the sibling-attempt rule). Everywhere I checked, header and body agree today — but at this size, divergence is a matter of time, and a future reader cannot tell which text governs. *Resolution (non-blocking):* compress each Revised entry to one dated line pointing at its round's review artifact (the details already live in `llp/reviews/`), and/or state explicitly that body text governs on any divergence.

## 4. Suggestions (non-blocking)

1. **Land the two harness fixes now, ahead of step-0 ratification.** The corrected-oracle pattern already exists in `native-exact-acto.mjs` (read current status, select a different one, wait for the transition) and the skip fix is small. Neither depends on protocol preregistration; step 0 would then *retain the receipt* rather than gate on authoring the fix. Every day the web suite stays green on a rendering-grade oracle compounds evidence-quality debt.
2. **Mechanize the moving-evidence rule.** The Plan's binding paragraph relies on discipline ("any further extension … requires a dated Revised entry with refreshed hashes"). A ~20-line CI check that recomputes the two evidence hashes and fails when they differ from the ones printed in this Plan would make the third-rebaseline scenario impossible to miss — especially given the concurrent-fleet reality of this repository.
3. **Surface the witness class in check names/output.** Exact 0373 §5.1 defines witness classes (`rendering`, `mutation-convergence`, `transport-provenance`, …). If the Partitime checks carry their class in the check name or result artifact, a future oracle weakening (convergence → rendering) becomes a reviewable diff instead of a silent semantic change.
4. **For OQ4's agent-effort formulas, prefer paired relative measurement.** Same-event, same-agent paired runs of both arms (ratio or difference), re-paired at each named measurement event, are robust to agent drift; absolute turn counts frozen from a pinned configuration are not. This also softens C2.
5. **Clarify the step-5 exit wording on publication.** The entry column says publication "need[s] only step 0" while WS6 orders **stable** publication behind WS4/WS2 with prereleases allowed. Both are coherent by design; adding "(registry install may be satisfied by clearly-labeled prereleases; stable publication closes under the cumulative manifest when WS4/WS2 land)" to the step-5 exit cell would prevent a hurried misreading.
6. **Name the line-counting rule for baselines.** The diary and report disagree slightly on non-Snapback backend counts (diary ~77/78/79 vs report 87/88/133 — the report includes scoped test cleanup and the Convex HTTP adapter). The Plan consistently uses report numbers, and the WS6 gate is relative to Snapback's own 168, so nothing is wrong — but the step-0 protocol should pin REPORT.md §4.2's counting method as the method of record.

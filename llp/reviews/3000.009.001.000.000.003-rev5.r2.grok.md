## Round-1 resolutions

**Session fence and takeover (§6.5–§6.6, witness 5) — holds.** One transaction holds `pg_advisory_xact_lock` through the token update and commit, so there is no acquire/unlock/update gap. Shared lockers granted while that exclusive request waits still appear in `pg_blocking_pids` and are in the terminated set; once the exclusive lock is granted, a new shared lock waits rather than barges. After commit, a later `READ COMMITTED` snapshot sees the new token. A commit that finishes before exclusion is included by the certificate’s re-read of the maximum `_commits` identity after exclusion and hole fill, so acknowledged 501 cannot be dropped and its sequence reused. A backend that holds neither the session lock nor the owner-row fence is out of spec; old binaries are refused or require a stopped upgrade.

**Evidence retention at N=1 (§6.4, witness 7) — holds.** `E = max(1000, ceil(q·T) + burst)` with `T≥5s` is about 22,625 sequences at 4,525/s, not 221 ms. Admitted cursors pin `F`, catch-up publishes `H` and `F` together, and a snapshot opened before prune keeps rows by MVCC. The 500-row cliff has to become a full interval in one snapshot. Restart is not allowed to forget a pin.

**Restored R0 gate (§8.2, §10.1) — holds on protocol content, not on host.** Ordered acknowledgements, footprints through `W`, two-image writes, the attempt row, freshness, catch-up, retention, and the 29-loan pool are inside the ≤6.0 ms native-mean gate. An unrestored round-3 pass does not move the odds. The host gap is finding 1.

**Keyed default-on (§6.7, §10.3, §13) — holds.** Keyed W4 and the 1% mix, including SDK keyed traffic and the drains it causes, must hit the same rungs as unkeyed W4 (4,525/A at ≤6.4 ms, 2,262/B). Otherwise default-on stays blocked. Unkeyed-only opt-in is an author ruling, not a side effect of a headline cell.

**Enriched `_commits` (§4.4, §6.1, §6.3) — holds.** One row, inserted in the same transaction as the images, with the existing unique `seq` key: commit and void cannot both become visible, and an abort rolls the early insert back. A void that arrives first makes the original insert fail before any image write. `23505` is not decisive until the winner’s state and attempt identity are read. A committed winner is never rerun. Merging the attempt row does not weaken that race.

## Findings

**1. MATERIAL, IN-DELTA, §8.2, §8.4, §10.1.** Restored R0 can still authorize production work from the wrong host.

Scenario: R0 runs on 450a, where all Spike R numbers were taken, with the restored write protocol, and three native sent means come in at 5.9 ms. Section 10.1 then starts R1–R5, and §8.4 moves the 4,525 odds, while §8.2 treats the remaining 0.4 ms as the transfer margin to the 6.4 ms ceiling. Section 8.2 never names expo-build-450b. Section 10.3 does, but only as the R5 qualification host. That is the transfer §3.5 already refuses for arm E: a non-authoritative pass starts the expensive slices. The 0.4 ms figure is also not a measured 450a→450b delta on this shape.

Fix: state that the three ≤6.0 passes that move §8.4 and start R1–R5 are expo-build-450b, link A, the §10.3 runner, and the real ≤32 pool. A 450a result may only stop the program. A pass there does not raise the odds and does not start production slices.

**2. MINOR, IN-DELTA, §6.6.** Qualification can still share the lock-wait snapshot if it is not a new command.

Scenario: a session in `REPEATABLE READ`, or one simple-query string whose snapshot is taken before `pg_advisory_lock_shared` returns, runs the token check in that same snapshot. The lock waits across takeover. The check still sees token T1, caches qualification, and a later body commits under the new owner. Witness 5 forbids the outcome, and the prose says the lock-wait snapshot cannot qualify, but it does not say that a multi-statement simple query and a non-`READ COMMITTED` session default are the same bug. The spike’s own `SELECT pg_advisory_lock_shared(...); DO ...` string is safe only under per-statement `READ COMMITTED` snapshots.

Fix: require the check in a later statement whose snapshot is taken after the lock grant returns, and forbid qualifying inside the lock statement, a CTE, or any snapshot opened before the grant.

**3. MINOR, PRE-EXISTING, §4.5 and §5.2 G6.** A lock-free sample of `W` can become visible before the invalidations installed with it.

Scenario: the publisher stores `W=11` and then invalidates query Q, or another CPU sees the new `W` first. G6 is a hit on the generation installed at `W=10` while the client observes prefix 11 and misses write 11. “Sampled atomically” covers the `(r,s)` pair, not happens-before with G5/G6 installation. The publication lock does not help readers who never take it.

Fix: invalidations for a prefix are visible to every reader before that reader can observe the `W` that names the prefix (the publication lock, or a release store of `W` after the invalidations and an acquire load before any cache lookup).

## The five checks

Serial equivalence for managed readers holds. A reader binds one pair `V`, then `image` returns `data` only when `orev` is null or `≤ V.r`, `odata` when `V.r` is in `[owm, orev)`, and `sb4_stale` otherwise. Footprints stay in `U` until `W` covers them, so a row has at most one unpublished writer and `odata` is the image on that interval. `/changes`, sync, and receipts are bounded by that same pair. Raw `r` and the inspect views are disclosed non-equivalent surfaces; they are not managed readers. The planted census is in witness 6.

Holes and takeover do not lose an acknowledgement or publish an abort under the rules as written. `W` moves only over a resolved prefix. A void and the original commit share one `seq` key. The certificate re-reads the max attempt after exclusion. Trailing uncommitted intent is not acknowledged and is not replayed. Unkeyed retry after process loss can apply again; that is the existing unkeyed contract, and keyed replay is the SDK path.

Routing holds. Same-key work waits on `U` or moves to O only through the drain. Ordered acknowledgements make the per-client minimum normally already true; the minimum is what prevents a later read, reconnect, or rebase from serving an older prefix. It is not an early success path.

Budget: 4,525/s is not credible from the measured components, and the document says so (native mean 7.04–7.24 ms, odds conditional, CPU already about 0.44 ms/write against a +30% stop). Restoration is charged by putting it inside the native-mean gate, not by an additive model. There is no evidenced cheaper design that still hides commits above `W`. Batched C and ordered C never reached 3,200/s on link A. A global read drain measured about 4.3–4.7 ms. Two images are the minimum hiding mechanism once readers cannot drain the world. Early acknowledgement is the only large residence lever, and §13 correctly leaves it to the author because it changes the success/visibility contract without removing two-image or footprint holds.

The witness matrix and default-on list cover the failure modes above before default-on. R0’s rate gate does not substitute for witnesses 4–7. Default-on still requires the author CPU ruling, keyed rungs, collision return within 100 ms, and no retention-driven revocation storm.

I found three fundamental concerns. I support centralizing the rules, but settlement needs an explicit event reducer.

1. **The settlement inputs omit the history needed to produce the outputs.** Receipt arrival, persistence completion, and late evidence after peer settlement require different effects. The proposed inputs contain neither persistence phase nor which effects have already run; `completion` cannot represent completion by this runtime. Today, the [receipt bookkeeping](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/progressive-writes.ts:56) and [settlement observation](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/progressive-writes.ts:301) supply that history. Authority also gets [rechecked at settlement](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:2007), after persistence may have yielded.

   This is fundamental because returning `settle: once` cannot enforce once-only execution. Without explicit state, the executors retain the conditionals this proposal intends to remove. Use a pure reducer `(writeState, event, currentContext) → (nextState, effects)`, recording custody, applied evidence, and outstanding responses. Keep per-invocation reply delivery separate from per-id settlement. Generate event-sequence tests: a Cartesian matrix of receipt attributes cannot prove ordering, deduplication, or safe eviction.

2. **The refusal actions cannot express terminal restore handling.** The proposed property permits read fencing only for `named: third/absent`. However, an accepted `E_STORE_RESTORE` or `E_DEV_RESET` can require fencing with the same store identity; [the current acceptance check](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:1946) explicitly handles this. More significantly, `keep` versus `retire-and-recover(keep-same-store-intents)` omits retirement without automatic recovery and retirement of all pending intents. Those distinctions exist in [journal retirement](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/app-online.ts:508); [app recovery](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/app.ts:675) deliberately excludes restore.

   Store identity equality does not establish that its execution history survived. Retaining and replaying intents after restoration can therefore violate execution guarantees. Separate three outputs: read fencing, retirement scope (`all` or `foreign-to-adopted-store`), and permission to reopen. Confirmed restoration must express terminal retirement without automatic replay.

3. **The lifetime rule treats an already-durable capture like a new admission.** Invariant 3 says captured clicks also receive the state’s typed refusal when paused or closed. But [capture acquires durable custody synchronously](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/online-capture.ts:119), before the opening gate and journal transaction finish. A capture can therefore precede close while its journal transfer follows it. The existing path [preserves custody and returns pending](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-pws-review-a1/snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/app-online.ts:292) when persistence fails or an admitted operation loses its active lifetime.

   A blanket refusal either abandons accepted work or reports failure while leaving executable work behind. Make admission disposition depend on custody phase as well as lifetime. Fence new intake and dispatch; preserve already-captured or journaled work for recovery, returning its known outcome or pending. Session-authority cancellation and terminal store retirement remain separate decisions.

1. **High — §9; obsolete-scope filtering invalidates acquisition positions.**  
   Stream from a watermark preceding an old group scope, after rebinding the audience, with a new windowed group join later in the same page. `expand_joins` retains the old scope in its output and computes acquisition `at` positions from that output’s length ([partition.rs:1148](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:1148), [partition.rs:1175](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:1175)). `stream` then removes the obsolete scope without adjusting those positions ([partition.rs:1011](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:1011)).

   If the join’s expansion ends the page, its closing marker now points beyond `events.len()`. Both device apply paths reject the page through [boundary.rs:163](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/boundary.rs:163); catch-up cannot drain. With subsequent events, positions can remain in range while marking the wrong event boundaries.

   **Change:** filter obsolete scopes before expansion computes acquisition positions, or remap every position when removing events. Extend `a_scope_of_an_earlier_audience_is_not_streamed` with a windowed union, a post-epoch join, and actual device application at page sizes 1 and 1000. The round-4 disposition removes the obsolete scope correctly but incompletely.

2. **High — §9; the first post-epoch renewal loses an already-held group’s history.**  
   This affects the single-group audiences to which the new epoch mechanism also applies; §8’s expiring-union exclusion does not prevent it.

   Start with an existing, live, expiring membership for Alice. Activate a schema that adds or retargets `notes` to that membership table, then snapshot Alice under the new schema, including note `n`. In the next commit, continuously renew Alice’s membership and delete `n`.

   The renewal emits a `join` without a scope ([partition.rs:355](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:355), [partition.rs:405](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:405)). After epoch filtering, there is no preceding `notes` membership event. Because the first transition is a join, `intervals` sets `held_at_watermark` to false despite Alice having acquired the group by snapshot ([partition.rs:864](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:864), [partition.rs:878](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:878)). The join becomes an acquisition, whose group span starts **after** its commit ([partition.rs:940](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:940)). Consequently, `n`’s deletion is excluded. Expansion sends current rows but neither clears existing copies nor synthesizes a scope without a predecessor ([partition.rs:1160](/tmp/union-r6-review/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:1160)).

   After draining, Alice still holds readable local `n`; a horizonless page or count can report `complete` with an extra row absent from the server. This is a normal schedule beginning with a snapshot under the current generation.

   **Change:** preserve enough predecessor state to distinguish a first post-epoch continuous renewal from a genuine acquisition—for example, renewal metadata recording the predecessor’s authority and lifetime, or an epoch baseline. Simply assuming every first join was previously held would reopen the acquiring-commit D2 leak. Add activation → snapshot → renew-and-delete coverage, alongside genuine acquisition and expired replacement. The epoch fallback disposition and renewal dispositions are individually reasonable but do not compose.

### 1. Product contract

The product is a longitudinal health-record, consent, disclosure, and compliance system for a regional care network. It stores patient identity, encounters, diagnoses, medications, results, notes, attachments, consent, access history, legal holds, and retention state. Users include patients and representatives, physicians, nurses, registration and billing staff, privacy and legal officers, and authorized external recipients.

Authority is deliberately not row-shaped. A treating nurse can receive a patient’s demographics and chart while `ssn_full` remains inaccessible; registration can update `ssn_full` without seeing clinical narratives; billing can receive diagnosis codes without note text; privacy staff see access metadata unless a case-specific grant permits content. A user holding multiple roles must choose an active role and purpose rather than silently unioning every privilege.

The product promises:

1. A patient has one chronological chart while every retained earlier version remains available to appropriately authorized reviewers.
2. Every returned field is permitted by the intersection of organization, active role, current care relationship, purpose, consent, field classification, and case-specific restrictions.
3. A nurse never receives, compares, clears, searches, or indirectly learns `ssn_full`.
4. Search, counts, sorting, exports, caches, notifications, conflict messages, and change feeds obey the same field rules as chart rendering.
5. Consent identifies the grantee, purpose, data categories, allowed fields, authorization interval, and a separate interval describing which records it covers.
6. Consent intervals are inclusive at the start and exclusive at the end, including scheduled future grants.
7. Revocation stops new server disclosures at the accepted server time without rewriting the legality of earlier disclosures.
8. Representatives may grant or revoke consent only while their appointment covers that act, and both identities are recorded.
9. Every protected server response is released only after an immutable access event has committed.
10. An on-duty clinician may break glass for one patient and documented emergency for at most 30 minutes.
11. Every break-glass response records its exact record versions and returned fields and enters mandatory privacy review.
12. Break glass bypasses a missing care relationship or ordinary consent only for an emergency clinical allowlist; it never reveals full SSN, payment credentials, or unrelated records.
13. Every accepted chart, identity, consent, policy, authority, hold, and deletion change creates an attributable immutable version or event.
14. Concurrent changes never silently overwrite one another; disjoint writable fields can merge, while same-field conflicts require resolution.
15. Hidden-field changes neither get overwritten by a lower-privileged edit nor produce a conflict that reveals the hidden value.
16. Legal holds preserve existing and future matching content but grant no additional permission to read it.
17. Retention screens show the controlling rule, earliest deletion time, hold deferrals, and eventual destruction certificate.
18. Managed devices may keep an encrypted ordinary-care packet for at most eight hours and compose drafts, but full SSN, restricted detail, and break-glass content are never available offline.
19. Every reconnecting device rechecks current authority and current field versions; stale commands are rejected, explicitly merged, or returned as conflicts without losing another device’s work.
20. External disclosures use a fixed version-and-field manifest, recheck authority immediately before each handoff, and never silently include later chart edits.
21. If authorization, version, or audit storage is unavailable, protected data fails closed with a generic temporary-unavailable result.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

There is no monetary invariant; the applicable marks are `[SECRECY]`, `[TIME]`, and `[OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]`.

1. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` No request may read or alter data outside its authenticated organization and named patient scope.
2. `[SECRECY] [TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A field may be returned only if every applicable current authority constraint permits it.
3. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Only the role and purpose selected for the current task apply, even when the actor holds additional roles.
4. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A nurse may never receive `ssn_full`, including through logs, indexes, caches, exports, errors, or derived values.
5. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A caller who cannot read a field cannot filter, sort, rank, aggregate, compare, or write through that field.
6. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A hidden record or field may not observably affect counts, cursors, snippets, refusal wording, response length, or change notifications unless an explicit safe aggregate permits it.
7. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Ordinary workforce access exists only while both the actor’s role and required care relationship are active.
8. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Consent authorization time and consent record-coverage time are separate half-open intervals and both must match.
9. `[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Routine revocation is prospective from its server commit time and never erases the grant or disclosures that preceded it.
10. `[TIME] [SECRECY]` Viewing old clinical content uses the caller’s present authority, while investigating an old disclosure uses the authority facts recorded for that disclosure.
11. `[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A later correction to clinical truth, consent, assignment, or policy is appended with a new recorded time and does not rewrite what was previously known.
12. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` No protected response bytes may leave the server until an access event naming the actor, patient, purpose, snapshot, versions, and returned fields has committed.
13. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A repeated request identifier creates at most one access event, and response replay is allowed only while the original authorization generation remains current.
14. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Break-glass authority is bound to one actor, device, patient, incident, and reason and ends after 30 minutes, explicit closure, role loss, or shift end.
15. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Every subsequent response during a break-glass episode requires its own committed access event.
16. `[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Every accepted authoritative change appends an immutable logical version even if physical storage uses reconstructable deltas.
17. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A write may merge only caller-touched fields that have not changed since its base version, while current hidden fields remain untouched.
18. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Offline synchronization rechecks present authority, consent, field permissions, and touched-field conflicts instead of trusting the downloaded packet.
19. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` A local offline view must append a signed device access record before decrypting its packet, and that record must upload at reconnection.
20. `[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` An active legal hold preserves every retained version and future matching version within its predicate scope until that hold is released.
21. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Creating, viewing, or releasing a legal hold grants no clinical read authority.
22. `[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Content may enter destruction only after all applicable retention deadlines have passed and no overlapping hold exists at the deletion ordering point.
23. `[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Releasing one of several overlapping holds never makes content deletable while another applicable hold remains.
24. `[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Lawful destruction removes decryptability from all versions, blobs, indexes, replicas, and restore paths in scope while retaining only non-content tombstones and destruction evidence.
25. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Each external handoff revalidates authority, and a revocation ordered before handoff prevents it while one ordered afterward cannot retract bytes already released.
26. `[TIME] [SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE'S DATA]` Audit corrections are new events, and neither the original audit event nor its manifest may be overwritten.

### 3. The traps, ranked

**1. Authority belongs to fields and derivations, not rows**

- **Wrong version:** Authorize a `patient_chart` row, fetch the complete object, and remove `ssn_full` or restricted narrative in the serializer or client.
- **Why wrong:** Hidden values can still influence search, sorting, counts, cache keys, logs, exports, uniqueness failures, and whole-object updates.
- **Observation:** A nurse sees no SSN on screen but can search by it, learns a restricted note exists from pagination totals, or clears the SSN by saving a phone-number edit.

**2. A protected read is also a mandatory write**

- **Wrong version:** Return data from a replica or cache and enqueue an audit entry afterward, or audit only when an emergency session starts.
- **Why wrong:** A crash or queue failure leaves a real disclosure without evidence, and later break-glass responses may contain different versions or fields.
- **Observation:** Fail the process after response serialization but before audit insertion; the clinician receives `note:v184`, yet no committed event identifies it.
- **Required ordering:** Pin the authorized snapshot, persist the exact response manifest and access event, commit, and only then serialize protected values; a failed delivery may therefore over-audit but never under-audit.

**3. Consent and chart history have multiple clocks**

- **Wrong version:** Store `consent_active`, one `updated_at`, or evaluate a historical record using the authority that existed when the record was written.
- **Why wrong:** Clinical effective time, authorization time, record-coverage time, and system-recorded time answer different questions.
- **Observation:** Consent is scheduled through January 31 but revoked January 15 at 14:03; a 14:02 disclosure remains authorized, a 14:04 disclosure is denied, and viewing a January 10 note in February uses February authority.

**4. Retention deletion races predicate-scoped legal holds**

- **Wrong version:** A nightly process selects `delete_after < now`, checks one `patient.on_hold` boolean, and deletes later.
- **Why wrong:** Holds overlap, cover categories and clinical intervals, include future matching versions, and can begin after candidate selection.
- **Observation:** Pause deletion after selecting an attachment, activate a matching hold, resume, and watch the attachment disappear; alternatively release one of two holds and observe premature deletion.

**5. Full versioning and safe concurrency are not update-plus-diff**

- **Wrong version:** Overwrite the current row, save a best-effort audit diff, and use either last-write-wins or one global row revision.
- **Why wrong:** The system loses clinical history or exposes a hidden-field change as a spurious conflict to someone who cannot inspect that field.
- **Observation:** Registration changes SSN while a nurse changes phone from the same base version; a global conflict leaks hidden activity, while whole-row last-write-wins restores the obsolete SSN.

**6. Authority snapshots outlive their legitimacy**

- **Wrong version:** Authorize an offline packet, subscription, or export once and let it finish after consent, employment, care assignment, or external trust changes.
- **Why wrong:** Long-lived work turns expired authority into future disclosures and can merge edits from two devices under stale permissions.
- **Observation:** Revoke consent during a 10,000-record export or end a nurse’s assignment while two devices are offline; the naive system finishes the export and accepts both uploads.
- **Required boundary:** Reauthorize every export page or stream chunk, lease offline data briefly, and define revocation as stopping future disclosures rather than pretending to retract already delivered information.

### 4. Data model

Identifiers are UUIDs, timestamps are UTC, and every interval is `[from, until)`. `recorded_at` is when the system learned a fact; `clinical_valid_from/to` is when that fact was true clinically. Canonical business history is immutable; mutable “current” rows are rebuildable projections.

**Identity and authority**

- `organization(org_id PK, name text, jurisdiction_code text)`.
- `actor(actor_id PK, actor_type enum, home_org_id FK, created_at timestamp)` and append-only `actor_status_revision(actor_id, revision_no, status, valid_from, valid_until?, recorded_at, supersedes_revision_id)`.
- `device(device_id PK, actor_id FK, public_key bytes, registered_at timestamp)` plus append-only revocation events.
- `staff_grant_revision(grant_revision_id PK, grant_id, revision_no, actor_id, role_code, org_id, facility_id?, patient_id?, valid_from, valid_until?, state, recorded_at, supersedes_revision_id, approved_by)`; organization-, facility-, and patient-scoped grants are distinct.
- `care_relationship_revision(relationship_revision_id PK, relationship_id, patient_id, actor_or_team_id, relationship_type, valid_from, valid_until?, state, recorded_at, supersedes_revision_id)`.
- `representative_authority_revision(authority_revision_id PK, authority_id, patient_id, representative_actor_id, permitted_actions text[], valid_from, valid_until?, evidence_hash bytes, recorded_at, supersedes_revision_id)`.
- `policy_revision(policy_revision_id PK, org_id, effective_from, effective_until?, recorded_at, content_hash, first_approver, second_approver)`.
- `field_access_rule(policy_revision_id FK, record_kind, column_name, role_code, purpose_code, required_basis, action enum[read,filter,write,export], effect enum[allow,mask,deny], mask_method?, priority, PK(...))`; deny wins equal priority.

The baseline matrix is:

| Field group | Treating physician | Assigned nurse | Registration | Billing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name, DOB, contact | read/update permitted fields | read/update contact | read/write | read |
| `ssn_full` | deny | deny | read/write | masked last four only |
| Standard clinical fields | read/write | read/write within assignment | deny | approved codes only |
| Restricted detail | specialty role plus matching consent | approved summary only | deny | deny |
| Coverage/member fields | deny | deny | read/write | read/write |

Privacy and legal staff receive metadata by default and require a case-specific grant for content. Break glass receives core identity plus an emergency clinical allowlist, never `ssn_full`, billing fields, or psychotherapy detail.

**Patient chart and complete version history**

- `patient(patient_id PK, home_org_id FK, created_at timestamp)` contains no mutable clinical attributes.
- `chart_item(item_id PK, patient_id FK, record_kind enum[demographics,note,diagnosis,medication,allergy,lab,document], created_at, created_by, source_system_id?)`.
- `chart_version(version_id PK, item_id FK, revision_no, parent_version_id?, change_kind enum[create,amend,correct,retract], status, clinical_valid_from, clinical_valid_to?, recorded_at, recorded_by, reason, changed_columns text[], client_command_id, payload_hash)` with unique `(item_id, revision_no)` and `(recorded_by, client_command_id)`.
- Immutable full-snapshot payload tables include:
  - `demographics_payload(version_id PK/FK, legal_name, preferred_name, date_of_birth, address, phone, email, ssn_ciphertext bytes, ssn_lookup_token bytes, coverage_member_id_ciphertext bytes)`;
  - `clinical_note_payload(version_id PK/FK, note_type, title, narrative, diagnosis_codes text[], signed_at?)`;
  - `medication_payload(version_id PK/FK, medication_code, dose, route, frequency, start_date?, end_date?)`;
  - `lab_result_payload(version_id PK/FK, test_code, numeric_value?, text_value?, unit?, abnormal_flag?, specimen_at)`.
- `field_label(version_id, column_name, protection_class, PK(version_id,column_name))` handles version-specific sensitivity.
- `attachment(attachment_id PK, item_id FK, object_key, content_hash, media_type, data_key_id)` and `version_attachment(version_id, attachment_id, PK(...))`.
- `chart_current(item_id PK, current_version_id, revision_no)` is derived and updated atomically with version insertion.
- `entity_change_event(event_id PK, entity_type, entity_id, prior_version_id?, new_version_id?, actor_id, reason, recorded_at, command_id)` covers every authoritative change.

For a knowledge time `K`, historical selection takes the newest revision of each logical entity with `recorded_at <= K`; a clinical-time query then retains facts whose clinical interval contains `B`. A present-day historical view still applies present-day field authority.

**Consent**

- `consent(consent_id PK, patient_id, grantor_actor_id, created_at)`.
- `consent_revision(consent_revision_id PK, consent_id, revision_no, grantee_kind, grantee_id, purpose_code, authorization_from, authorization_until?, record_from?, record_until?, state, revocation_effective_at?, recorded_at, supersedes_revision_id, signed_artifact_key, artifact_hash)`.
- `consent_scope(consent_revision_id, record_kind?, column_name?, protection_class?, action enum[read,export], PK(...))`.

Consent is active only when authorization time, record coverage, grantee, purpose, action, and scope all match; grants union, but cannot widen a role-level deny.

**Audit and emergency access**

- `break_glass_episode(episode_id PK, patient_id, actor_id, device_id, incident_id, reason_code, reason_text, started_at, expires_at)` and append-only `break_glass_event(event_id PK, episode_id, event_type, effective_at, actor_id, findings?)`.
- `access_event(access_event_id PK, request_id UNIQUE, actor_id, device_id, patient_id, purpose_code, decision_at, outcome, basis_revision_ids uuid[], policy_revision_id, break_glass_episode_id?, snapshot_time, query_fingerprint, manifest_hash, integrity_hash, sealed_batch_id?)`.
- `access_manifest(access_event_id PK/FK, encrypted_compressed_entries bytes, version_count int, returned_field_classes text[])` contains exact version IDs and returned, masked, and denied field names but no values.
- `compliance_outbox(outbox_id PK, event_type, source_event_id, destination, state, attempts)` drives notification, review, and separately controlled audit export.

**Holds, retention, and destruction**

- `legal_hold(hold_id PK, matter_id, title, created_at)`; `legal_hold_scope(scope_id PK, hold_id, patient_id?, item_id?, record_kind?, clinical_from?, clinical_to?)`; and append-only `legal_hold_event(event_id PK, hold_id, event_type enum[activated,released], effective_at, recorded_at, actor_id, reason)`.
- `retention_rule_revision(rule_revision_id PK, rule_id, jurisdiction_code, record_kind, trigger_type, duration_months, minimum_patient_age_years?, disposal_method, effective_from, effective_until?, recorded_at, supersedes_revision_id)`.
- `retention_disposition(item_id PK, eligible_at?, controlling_rule_ids uuid[], active_hold_ids uuid[], computed_at, generation)` is derived; an item’s deadline is the maximum applicable deadline across all its retained versions.
- `item_data_key(item_id PK, wrapped_key bytes, generation)` makes the whole version family one destruction unit.
- `destruction_job(job_id PK, run_id, item_id, expected_generation, state, created_at)` and immutable `destruction_certificate(certificate_id PK, item_id, authorized_at, destroyed_at?, rule_ids uuid[], version_hashes bytes[], object_count, backup_expiry, outcome)`.

All versions remain readable until lawful destruction. Destruction removes keys and protected values but retains minimal tombstones, hashes, and certificates.

**Offline and exchange**

- `client_command(command_id PK, actor_id, device_id, payload_hash, received_at, result_code, result_entity_id?)` provides retry idempotency.
- `offline_packet(packet_id PK, actor_id, device_id, issued_at, expires_at, authorization_generation, encrypted_manifest_hash)` excludes prohibited fields.
- `disclosure_job(job_id PK, patient_id, target_system_id, purpose, state, created_at, idempotency_key)`; `disclosure_manifest(job_id, ordinal, version_id, allowed_columns text[], content_hash, PK(job_id,ordinal))`; and append-only `disclosure_event(event_id PK, job_id, event_type, occurred_at, receipt?, detail_hash)`.

`chart_current`, active-authority projections, authorization generations, safe search projections, and retention dispositions are derived. Revocation and policy changes advance the relevant generation atomically; stale projections may deny access but never extend it.

### 5. The hard operations

**1. Change workforce authority or field policy.** **Inputs:** affected actors/roles or complete replacement rules, scope, interval, reason, expected revision, command ID. **Authorization:** delegated security administrator plus independent privacy approval; nobody may self-grant. **Steps:** validate delegation and field names; append the grant or policy revision and change event; advance affected actor, patient, and policy generations; expire affected capabilities; enqueue notices. **Atomic:** revision, evidence, generations, and outbox. **Eventually consistent:** directory display and reporting. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Concurrent conflict:** first expected revision wins; later submissions receive the intervening revision and must rebase.

**2. Issue or replace consent.** **Inputs:** patient, grantor, grantee, purpose, field/category scopes, authorization interval, record-coverage interval, signed artifact, predecessor, command ID. **Authorization:** patient or currently authorized representative; staff may record but not invent consent. **Steps:** verify representation and signature; prevent authorization from starting before signature commit; store the artifact; append consent revision, scopes, change event, and generation change. **Atomic:** instrument, scopes, evidence, and generation. **Eventually consistent:** notices. **Optimistic UI:** “awaiting confirmation.” **Concurrent conflict:** independent grants coexist as a union; a replacement with a stale predecessor is rejected.

**3. Revoke consent.** **Inputs:** consent ID, actor, reason, expected revision, command ID. **Authorization:** patient, qualifying representative, or privacy officer with documented basis. **Steps:** lock the patient authorization fence; set `revocation_effective_at` to commit time; append the revision and change event; advance the generation; cancel disclosure jobs whose handoff has not begun. **Atomic:** revocation, fence, cancellations, audit, and outbox. **Eventually consistent:** device notices and reports. **Optimistic UI:** “revoking,” not “revoked.” **Concurrent conflict:** a disclosure ordered before revocation remains historical; one ordered afterward is denied.

**4. Read or search a chart normally.** **Inputs:** patient, requested panels and fields, permitted predicates/order, purpose, cursor, request ID. **Authorization:** current role plus current care relationship or other declared basis, evaluated per field at transaction time. **Steps:** open one transaction; resolve the patient without distinguishable absent/forbidden behavior; read authorization generations; select exact current versions; apply read, filter, mask, and aggregate rules; build the response manifest; insert allowed or denied access event; commit; then serialize. **Atomic:** authority snapshot, selected versions, manifest, and audit. **Eventually consistent:** audit analytics only. **Optimistic UI:** only an already authorized cache marked stale. **Concurrent conflict:** revocation or policy change serializes on the generation fence; a concurrent chart edit cannot alter the pinned response.

**5. Start break glass and perform its first read.** **Inputs:** patient, incident ID, coded reason, at least 20 characters of explanation, requested emergency panels, actor/device, MFA evidence, request ID. **Authorization:** active on-duty clinical role on a compliant device; service, billing, registration, and patient accounts are ineligible. **Steps:** cap expiry at 30 minutes or shift end; compute the emergency field allowlist; append episode/start event; select and redact exact versions; append access event and mandatory-review outbox; commit; return the first response. **Atomic:** episode, first response snapshot, audit, and review work. **Eventually consistent:** alerts and review. **Optimistic UI:** none, and no offline use. **Concurrent conflict:** closure, role loss, expiry, and later reads serialize on the episode generation; each later read repeats operation 4 with the episode as basis.

**6. Create, amend, correct, or retract a clinical item.** **Inputs:** patient/item, kind, typed values or touched-field patch, clinical interval, base version, reason, attachments, command ID. **Authorization:** current patient-scoped write authority for every supplied field; signed narrative correction may require an addendum role. **Steps:** deduplicate; validate types and interval; load current and base snapshots; reject changed touched fields; merge unchanged touched fields into the current server snapshot; encrypt; append a complete successor version, payload, attachment manifest, and change event; advance `chart_current`. **Atomic:** successor, payload, current projection, and command result. **Eventually consistent:** search and subscriptions. **Optimistic UI:** a provisional local draft. **Concurrent conflict:** disjoint writable fields may produce a merged successor; same-field changes require explicit rebase, and signed narrative is never automatically merged.

**7. Correct demographics without exposing hidden identity fields.** **Inputs:** patient, touched fields, base version, reason, command ID. **Authorization:** evaluated separately for each field; nurses may update permitted contact fields, while only registration may update `ssn_full`. **Steps:** reject submitted forbidden fields; compare only caller-touched fields against the base; copy all untouched values from the current server version; append a complete new demographics snapshot and change event. **Atomic:** new version and current projection. **Eventually consistent:** role-safe search tokens. **Optimistic UI:** only the submitted visible fields. **Concurrent conflict:** an intervening SSN change does not block a nurse’s phone edit, while two phone edits conflict without revealing unrelated fields.

**8. Issue an offline packet and synchronize drafts.** **Inputs:** device, requested patients, signed local access ledger, packet ID, ordered client commands with bases. **Authorization:** compliant managed device and current ordinary-care authority; break-glass, full SSN, and restricted detail are excluded. **Steps:** when online, audit and issue an encrypted eight-hour packet; before each local open, append a signed device event; on reconnect, upload that ledger, check packet expiry and current generations, deduplicate commands, and apply operations 6 or 7 independently. **Atomic:** each logical command, not the whole batch. **Eventually consistent:** local-log analytics and cache purge confirmation. **Optimistic UI:** local drafts remain visibly unsynced. **Concurrent conflict:** stale authority rejects the command; same-field conflicts remain local for resolution; accepted earlier commands are not rolled back because a later command fails.

**9. Reconstruct a chart or access decision at a past time.** **Inputs:** patient or access-event ID, `knowledge_at`, optional `clinical_at`, purpose, privacy-case ID, request ID. **Authorization:** current authority governs content; an old role never restores access, and viewing audit metadata requires a current privacy case. **Steps:** choose revisions recorded by `knowledge_at`; apply clinical intervals when requested; for access investigation load the exact recorded policy, basis revisions, and manifest; apply today’s field mask to any content; audit this historical read before response. **Atomic:** reconstructed snapshot and its new audit event. **Eventually consistent:** report rendering. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Concurrent conflict:** later corrections do not alter the pinned reconstruction and appear separately as later-recorded facts.

**10. Activate or release a legal hold.** **Inputs:** matter, patient/item/category/time scopes, action, reason, expected event, command ID. **Authorization:** legal/privacy officer; release requires a second approver. **Steps:** validate bounded predicate scopes; append hold scopes and event; advance matching deletion fences; activation cancels recoverable, not-yet-committed destruction jobs; release merely recomputes dispositions. **Atomic:** hold event and deletion fence. **Eventually consistent:** enumerated impact reports. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Concurrent conflict:** hold and destruction use the same ordering fence; if hold wins, deletion skips, while a hold ordered after destruction authorization reports the pending or completed certificate.

**11. Run the scheduled bulk retention and destruction sweep.** **Inputs:** scheduler identity, jurisdiction/shard, as-of time, cursor, page size 500. **Authorization:** scheduler only. **Steps:** select due dispositions in stable order; claim jobs; for each item lock its fence, recompute current rules and holds, verify generation, atomically append a tombstone and point-of-no-return destruction authorization, then idempotently destroy its external data key and purge ciphertext, objects, indexes, replicas, and restore paths; append completion evidence. **Atomic:** eligibility recheck, fence, tombstone, and destruction authorization per item, not the 500-item page. **Eventually consistent:** physical ciphertext cleanup and backup expiry, while key destruction makes content unreadable. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Concurrent conflict:** a hold or amendment ordered first causes recomputation; destruction ordered first permanently rejects later edits and cannot be represented as held.

**12. Disclose a fixed record set to an external system.** **Inputs:** patient, trusted recipient, exact version/field selection, purpose, consent basis, receiver idempotency key. **Authorization:** records-release role, active recipient trust, matching consent, and field-specific export permission. **Steps:** create an immutable manifest; for each bounded page, recheck consent, policy, and recipient trust under their fences; redact; seal the payload; atomically append `handoff_started` and an access event; commit; transmit using mutual authentication and the idempotency key; append the receipt or ambiguous outcome. **Atomic:** final authorization, sealed page, handoff boundary, and audit. **Eventually consistent:** network delivery and receipt reconciliation. **Optimistic UI:** “queued,” never “sent.” **Concurrent conflict:** revocation before a page’s handoff cancels that and later pages; revocation afterward cannot recall that page; later chart edits never join the manifest.

**13. Review break-glass use.** **Inputs:** hourly run ID or episode ID, review decision, reviewer, findings. **Authorization:** privacy reviewer with no involvement in the emergency episode; viewing audit details is itself audited. **Steps:** the hourly process finds unreviewed expired episodes, links their access manifests, correlates incident and shift metadata, creates one case per episode, raises risk flags, and queues required notices; the reviewer appends `appropriate`, `investigate`, or `exception` without changing the original events. **Atomic:** case, episode links, and notice outbox. **Eventually consistent:** scoring, notifications, and escalation. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Concurrent conflict:** a unique episode constraint coalesces workers, and competing review decisions require explicit superseding review rather than last-write-wins.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful regional network is designed for:

| Object or workload | Target |
|---|---:|
| Patients | 5 million |
| Workforce users | 60,000 across 250 facilities |
| Encounters | 150 million |
| Current chart items | 1.2 billion |
| Immutable chart versions | 4 billion |
| Consent and revocation revisions | 40 million |
| Time-bounded workforce, care, and proxy facts | 300 million |
| Audited protected responses | 12 million/day; 3,000/second peak |
| Clinical changes | 1 million/day; 500/second peak |
| Break-glass episodes | 100–500/day |
| Active legal-hold scopes | 10,000 |
| Retention candidates examined | 5–20 million/night |
| Items physically purged | 100,000–1 million/day |
| Attachments and images | 0.5–2 PB |

Four billion structured versions at 2–4 KiB each require roughly 8–16 TB before indexes, replicas, search projections, and backups. Audit events plus compressed manifests plausibly add several terabytes annually.

The expensive paths are chart opening—because every response couples thousands of field decisions to a durable write—policy-safe search, arbitrary bitemporal reconstruction, complete-history exports, and revocation of active streams and packets. Retention is a large temporal join across item category, clinical dates, patient age, jurisdiction, policy versions, deletion state, and overlapping hold predicates. Physical destruction fans out to keys, rows, object storage, search, replicas, caches, and restore handling.

A single global audit sequence or hash chain would serialize the system. Audit storage should be partitioned, append-only, and periodically sealed into independently stored signed roots.

| Requirement | Acceptable concession | Boundary where the concession becomes a cheat |
|---|---|---|
| **Field-level noninterference across the entire query.** Authority must cover projection, predicates, joins, sorting, aggregates, snippets, cache keys, errors, subscriptions, and writes. | Use a small set of fixed sensitivity tables or preapproved role views when the field matrix is stable and every derived index follows the same separation. | Fetching complete rows and hiding values only in the client or serializer, or letting hidden fields change counts, ranks, timing, conflicts, or errors. |
| **Commit-before-disclose reads.** A response must use one snapshot and durably append its exact access manifest before release. | Coalesce one bounded response into one audit event and permit an undelivered response to leave an over-audit record. | Best-effort asynchronous audit, logging only session start, serving an unaudited cache hit, or claiming the log proves a human actually saw the data. |
| **Bitemporal immutable history with current authorization.** Clinical truth time and recorded time apply to chart, consent, relationships, policy, and investigations. | Keep immutable versions plus atomic current projections and reserve arbitrary reconstruction for specialist endpoints. | Overwriting current rows, writing history asynchronously, keeping only one timestamp, or evaluating an old disclosure using today’s consent. |
| **Hold-fenced erasure across heterogeneous stores.** Predicate-scoped holds and destruction must have a deterministic ordering point while cleanup spans keys, blobs, indexes, replicas, and backups. | Atomically commit a tombstone and destruction authorization, then perform idempotent cryptographic erasure and cleanup under a published deadline. | Soft-deleting only primary rows, checking holds before an unfenced delay, ignoring derived copies, or retaining readable “immutable” history forever despite a valid deletion duty. |
| **Revocation-aware jobs, streams, and offline devices.** Authority can expire while work is in progress, so every page or chunk needs a current generation. | Keep restricted data online-only, use short encrypted offline leases, and define revocation as stopping future disclosures rather than erasing memory or delivered copies. | Authorizing a job once, promising instantaneous recall from disconnected devices, or allowing queued pages to continue because the job started before revocation. |

Append-only constraints must bind administrative and maintenance paths as well as ordinary operations. Exporting audit roots to separately controlled write-once storage is a reasonable compromise; allowing the same application administrator to rewrite both data and evidence is not.

### 8. Confidence

Role-, relationship-, and purpose-based access, minimum-necessary disclosure, immutable clinical amendments, access auditing, scoped consent, break glass with retrospective review, legal holds, retention schedules, optimistic concurrency, and deletion workflows are standard practices or established patterns.

The exact 30-minute emergency window, eight-hour offline lease, role matrix, field classifications, workload figures, per-item encryption keys, authorization-generation mechanism, audit-manifest format, and external-handoff boundary were chosen for this exercise. Treating every chart response as commit-before-disclose and requiring field secrecy across all query observables are intentionally stronger than many deployed systems.

Confidence is high that the design exposes the intended backend limits, medium in its operational thresholds, and low on jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions. Retention periods, proxy rights, consent requirements, specially protected categories, notification exceptions, and required audit detail need jurisdictional legal and security review; this is a fixed implementation stress test, not a compliance certification.

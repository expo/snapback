# Concourse — a commerce platform whose apps install rules on the host's own tables

Axis: rule ownership and composition across untrusted authors. Running example:
merchant **Northwind Outfitters** has installed
**Sentinel** (fraud gate), **TaxRight** (tax lines), **StackDeals** (discounts),
**ShipNimbus** (labels, tags, refunds), **StockSync** (channel that reserves inventory)
and **TextBlast** (SMS marketing, app-reported usage).

### 1. Product contract

**What it is.** Concourse is a multi-tenant commerce platform. Each **merchant** runs a
store — `orders`, `customers`, `products` — and installs **apps** from a marketplace. An
app is a signed **manifest** of things it installs *into the merchant's store*: fields on
host tables, tables of its own, declarative **rules** on host tables (validation and
access), sandboxed **programs** (gates inside the host's commit, contributors that price
orders, reactions after commit, scheduled jobs, staff-invoked actions), **event
subscriptions** delivered to the developer's servers, and **usage meters** with prices.
Apps are written by parties the platform does not trust and who do not trust each other,
and several touch the same rows.

**Who uses it.** Merchant owners and staff (web admin; an offline-capable phone/tablet
admin-and-POS app for warehouses and pop-ups); app developers (manifest, dashboard, their
own backend receiving events and calling back under an install token); platform
operators; customers, whose storefront checkout writes are ordinary host writes by the
`customer` principal.

**Promises.** M = to merchants, D = to developers, P = platform-wide.

1. **M — Your store's data is yours to see.** Every value any app stores in your store is
   readable by the owner role, with who wrote it and when. Apps keep genuinely private
   data on their own servers.
2. **M — Every app's authority is a list you can read.** Per install: what it may read
   (by field group), write, guard, do without a human, and spend — with grant history,
   what it actually read in the last 7/30 days, every write, every refusal issued or
   received.
3. **M — An app can enforce; you can always override or remove it.** App rules refuse
   writes; they never grant. A rule marked overridable can be exempted per row by
   permitted staff; any rule can be disabled; any app uninstalled, after which nothing it
   installed is enforced.
4. **M — Rules refuse; they never silently rewrite your data.** Apps change an order's
   money only through attributed contribution lines (discount, tax, shipping, fee) that
   the host folds in an order you set.
5. **M — An app's failure never stops your commerce unless you made it a gatekeeper.**
   By default a slow, broken, over-budget or revoked gate is skipped and the row is
   flagged for review. You may mark a gate `required`; then its failure refuses the
   write, and you are shown which writes that blocks.
6. **M — Revoking a permission takes effect now, everywhere.** Rules, programs and
   subscriptions depending on a revoked scope go dark in the same transaction; you are
   told which gates go dark before you confirm.
7. **M — Nothing is billed that you did not accept.** Usage is charged only under a
   price version you accepted, only while the app was active, never past your monthly
   cap for that app.
8. **M — Two apps on the same row do not clobber each other.** Tags, notes, contribution
   lines and fields have owners; concurrent writes by different owners both survive.
9. **M — Offline edits are real edits.** Staff devices queue edits offline; on sync each
   is checked against current rules and commits, conflicts (you choose), or is refused
   with the refusing rule named.
10. **D — What you were granted is what you get.** Reads and events are projected to
    your scopes; you never see another app's private fields, its rules, or that it
    exists, and no other app sees yours.
11. **D — Your rules and gates run inside the merchant's commit, on every writer,
    including the merchant.** Not after the fact.
12. **D — At-least-once, ordered per row, with a way to catch up.** Events retry for 72
    hours; if you fall behind or were suspended you receive a gap marker and a bounded
    backfill.
13. **D — Fair share, declared budgets.** Per-install budgets are published numbers;
    another app's storm cannot consume yours.
14. **D — You are paid for metered usage not disputed within 30 days**, net of the
    platform fee.
15. **P — Tenant isolation is absolute.** No query, count, event or rule spans
    merchants; an app sees its installs one merchant at a time.
16. **P — Refusals are attributed to the merchant and opaque to the writer.** A refused
    app learns that policy refused, not whose or why, unless the rule's owner chose to
    say.
17. **P — Structural numbers are exact.** Availability, reserved quantity, order counts
    are host-maintained counters updated in the same transaction as the rows they
    summarize, readable by scope without seeing the rows.
18. **P — History outlives its authors.** Every rule, grant, price and install is an
    interval; audit rows reference the versions that were in force; uninstall closes
    intervals and deletes nothing an audit points at.

**Concurrency, offline, multi-device.** Two staff devices editing the same order see each
other's committed changes within about a second when online. Compare-and-set is per
field: edits to *different* scalar fields both commit; a second edit to the *same*
scalar field is a conflict to resolve. Tag adds/removes and status transitions commute or
fail cleanly (a transition from a state the row has left is refused, not applied). App
programs are server-side and never offline; a developer's backend can be down, which the
platform treats as backlog then gap — never as a lost write on the merchant's side.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: [money] [secrecy] [time] [others' data].

1. A row belongs to exactly one merchant; every read, write, count, event and rule
   evaluation is confined to that merchant. [others' data]
2. Every field on a host row has exactly one owner — host, merchant, or one install — and
   only the owner (plus the merchant, if the owner allowed it) may write it.
   [others' data]
3. The merchant owner role can read every field and app-table row in the store; no rule,
   app, or setting hides it. [secrecy]
4. An app reads a host field only under an effective grant for its field group, another
   install's field only if that install shared it, and the list of installed apps only
   under `read:installs.list`. [secrecy]
5. Rules compose by conjunction: a write commits only if every applicable rule passes.
   No rule, by any author, widens what another layer allows.
6. Evaluation order is deterministic — platform, merchant, installs by merchant-set rank,
   declaration order — and the first refusal ends it.
7. A rule fires only on the transitions it declares (insert, update of listed fields,
   delete, named status transition); a write that does not touch what a rule guards
   cannot be refused by that rule. [time]
8. A rule is bound to the read scopes its predicate needs; when any is revoked the rule
   is disabled in the revocation's own transaction and stays so until re-granted.
   [time] [secrecy]
9. A write is evaluated against the rule versions, grants, and install states in force at
   its transaction snapshot, and its audit row records those version ids. [time]
10. A rule whose outcome cannot be determined (budget, error, unreadable input)
    follows its declared posture: `open_flag` commits and stamps a visible flag on the
    row; `closed` refuses. It never silently passes.
11. A refusal returns to a non-merchant writer only an opaque refusal id and, if the
    rule's owner chose, a message; full attribution goes to the merchant's ledger.
    [secrecy]
12. A count returned to a reader is either over the reader's visible rows, labelled so,
    or a host-maintained counter over all rows kept in the rows' own transaction. There
    is no third kind. [others' data]
13. `available_qty = on_hand − reserved_qty` at every commit; no reservation ever exceeds
    it. [money]
14. An order's total equals its subtotal plus its contribution lines folded in rank order;
    no principal writes the total directly. [money]
15. An app contributes only in kinds it is scoped for, within bounds its manifest
    declares, and never against a basis newer than the one it computed on.
    [money] [time]
16. A write initiated by a staff user through an app requires both the install's grant
    and the user's own permission; a write with no human initiator requires the
    install's `autonomous:` grant for that write. [others' data]
17. No host write waits on a reaction, delivery, or job. The only app code in the write
    path is gates and contributors, bounded at 5 ms CPU each and 20 ms CPU / 50 ms wall
    per write in total.
18. A write caused by a reaction carries its origin event and depth; at depth 4 it
    triggers no further reactions.
19. Within a merchant no install is served ahead of another with a backlog beyond its
    round-robin share; across merchants capacity is shared by plan weight, never by
    demand.
20. Usage is billable only if `occurred_at` lies inside the install's active interval and
    inside an accepted price version's interval, and billable usage per install per
    month never exceeds the merchant's cap. [money] [time]
21. App-reported usage is a claim: capped, bounded to the last 7 days, idempotent per
    key, disputable for 30 days; disputed usage is withheld from payout. [money]
22. Every usage row is invoiced exactly once. [money]
23. Uninstall closes intervals: rules, programs, grants, subscriptions and metering stop
    at one instant; fields and app tables are retained read-only for 30 days then
    purged; contribution lines, audit, refusals and usage referencing the install are
    never deleted. [time]
24. Secrets have exactly one home, the install vault, which no merchant view reads; a
    manifest cannot declare a `secret`-typed row field. [secrecy]
25. Offline intents are ordered per device, replayed in that order, each evaluated as an
    ordinary write against current rules, and their outcomes are surfaced even after
    the user has moved on.
26. A rule may condition on the writer's *kind* (staff, app, customer, platform) and on
    whether the writer is the rule's own app; it can never name or detect another app.
    [secrecy]

### 3. The traps, ranked

**T1 — Rule authority drift (authority × time).** *Wrong version:* a rule is a stored
predicate evaluated at write time over whatever it references; an unreadable field
(scope revoked) reads as null and the predicate evaluates anyway; jobs and reactions run
with the authority they had when enqueued. *Why wrong:* the merchant revoked
`read:customers.basic` from Sentinel and was told it took effect. Sentinel's rule
`refuse place if lookup(customers).risk_score > 80` now sees null; `null > 80` is false;
every order commits unguarded with no flag — or, if null is an error under `closed`,
every order is refused. Either way the merchant's model of what is enforced is silently
wrong. *Observation:* revoke the scope, place an order for a customer with `risk_score =
95`. The correct system asks at revocation time "Sentinel's risk gate (required) will go
dark — proceed unguarded or keep refusing?", records the choice, and the later order's
audit shows `sentinel.risk: disabled_scope_revoked`. A job started before revocation is
refused on its next write, because authority is re-read at each write's snapshot.

**T2 — The refusal oracle and the structural count (visibility).** *(a) Wrong version:*
every count is computed over the caller's visible rows. StockSync holds
`read:orders.own` and asks "units of SKU-18 reserved"; over its visible rows the answer
is its own 3, while 40 are held by the storefront and ShipNimbus; it lists 37 units it
cannot deliver. The count is admitted and returns a confidently wrong integer. *(b) Wrong
version:* refusals name the rule and explain ("Sentinel: risk score too high"). Any app
with `transition:orders.place` can now read Sentinel's private `risk_score` one bit per
attempted placement, per customer. *Observation:* compare StockSync's count with
`products.reserved_qty`; run a probe loop from a second app and count bits recovered.
The correct system answers (a) with a host-maintained counter readable under
`read:products.availability` and (b) with an opaque `refusal_id`, a per-(writer, rule)
hourly refusal counter that alerts the merchant above 100 and throttles the writer, and
a rule-owner choice of `reveals: none|existence`. Trigger declarations also shrink the
oracle: a `place`-transition rule cannot be probed through `note` writes.

**T3 — Shared fields as a commons (concurrency × merge).** *Wrong version:*
`orders.tags` is an array each app reads, modifies and writes; `orders.total_discount`
is a number two apps both set; a staff device syncing an offline edit writes the whole
row it cached an hour ago, app-owned fields included. *Why wrong:* last-writer-wins on a
row with six owners loses somebody's write every busy hour; two discount apps alternate
overwriting the total; the offline device resurrects a field the app cleared.
*Observation:* ShipNimbus adds `label-printed` while StackDeals removes `promo-eligible`
on the same order within 50 ms — one survives in the naive design; stack a 10% and a $5
discount twice and get different totals. The correct system stores tags as
owner-attributed assignments, money as owner-attributed contribution lines folded in
merchant-set rank, and patches as per-field compare-and-set plus commuting operations.

**T4 — Rows that predate the rule; rules that outlive the app (time).** *Wrong version:*
a rule is a state assertion evaluated on any write to the table; uninstall deletes the
app's rules and fields; reinstall creates fresh ids. *Why wrong:* TaxRight's "placed
orders have tax lines" refuses marking last year's pre-TaxRight order fulfilled; after
uninstall the refusal ledger reads "refused by (deleted)"; in the other direction one
missed filter lets a dead app's rule keep refusing. *Observation:* install a rule, update
an unrelated field of an older row; uninstall and open the ledger. The correct system
has trigger declarations (the tax rule fires on the `place` transition only), `absent`
distinct from `null` for fields added after a row existed, append-only rule versions
with `effective_from/to`, and an uninstall that closes intervals rather than deleting.

**T5 — The gatekeeper in the write path (fairness × availability).** *Wrong version:*
gates run synchronously with no budget; on timeout the write either fails (a Sentinel
outage halts checkout for every merchant that installed it) or proceeds unmarked (fraud
orders ship and nobody knows); reactions share one FIFO queue per merchant; App A's
reaction writes trigger App B's, which writes and triggers App A. *Observation:* slow
Sentinel's gate to 900 ms and measure host write p99 with the flag set; install two apps
that each retag on `orders.updated` and watch for the depth-4 stop and a merchant
notice; fill ShipNimbus's queue with 200k events and check TaxRight's latency. The
correct system has per-gate and per-write budgets, a merchant-chosen posture that is
never silent, per-install queues under weighted round-robin, coalescing then gap at
backlog limits, and cascade depth on every derived write.

**T6 — Confused deputy in both directions (authority).** *Wrong version:* an app action
clicked by a staff user runs with the app's scopes, or the union of app scopes and user
permissions; a nightly job runs with the same authority as a click. *Observation:* a
junior staffer without `refunds.create` clicks "Refund" in ShipNimbus (which holds
`write:refunds`) — the naive platform refunds. TextBlast's nightly job tags 5,000
customers with no human in the loop although `write:customers.tags` was granted for
clicked actions only. The correct system intersects install grant and initiator
permission when a human initiated, requires a separate `autonomous:` grant when none
did, and records `actor` and `initiator` on every write.

### 4. Data model

Types: `id`, `text`, `int`, `cents`, `bool`, `ts`, `enum`, `set<T>`, `map<K,V>`,
`json(schema)`. Every table not marked platform-level carries `merchant_id` and is
partitioned by it. **Append-only** tables never update or delete. **Versioned** tables
append a new version row per change; readers pick by `effective_from ≤ snapshot <
effective_to`.

**Tenancy and principals (platform-level).**
- `merchants(merchant_id, name, plan enum{starter,growth,plus}, region, status, created_at)`
- `staff_users(user_id, merchant_id, email, role_id, status)`
- `staff_roles(role_id, merchant_id, name, permissions set<text>)` — permission codes such
  as `orders.fulfill`, `refunds.create`, `override_rules`, `manage_apps`, `owner`.
- `apps(app_id, developer_id, slug, name, review_tier enum{unreviewed,reviewed,certified}, status)`
- `app_versions(app_version_id, app_id, version, manifest json, published_at, review_status)` — append-only.

**Installation and authority.**
- `installs(install_id, merchant_id, app_id, app_version_id, state enum{installing,active,suspended,uninstalling,retained,purged}, rank int, installed_at, installed_by_user_id, suspended_at, uninstalled_at, purge_after, previous_install_id)` — never deleted; `rank` merchant-set, unique per merchant.
- `scope_grants(grant_id, install_id, scope text, granted_at, granted_by_user_id, revoked_at, revoked_by_user_id, revoke_reason)` — append-only; effective when `revoked_at` is null. Scope vocabulary: `read:orders`, `read:orders.own`, `read:orders.adjustments`, `read:customers.basic`, `read:customers.pii`, `read:products`, `read:products.availability`, `read:installs.list`, `read:staff`; `write:orders.tags`, `write:orders.note`, `write:products.price`, `write:customers.tags`, `write:reservations`, `write:refunds`; `contribute:orders.<discount|tax|shipping|fee>`; `transition:orders.<place|pay|fulfill|cancel|refund>`; `guard:<orders|customers|products>`; `autonomous:<any write/transition scope>`; `report_usage:<meter>`.
- `install_vault(install_id, key, ciphertext, rotated_at)` — never rendered in any merchant view.
- `budget_windows(install_id, window_start ts, kind enum{minute,day}, cpu_ms_used, rows_read, rows_written, deliveries, and the matching limits)`.

**Schema installed by apps and merchants.**
- `field_defs(field_id, merchant_id, owner enum{host,merchant}|install_id, table, name, type, read_policy enum{owner, owner_and_merchant, apps_with_table_read, listed_apps}, shared_with set<app_id>, write_policy enum{owner, owner_and_merchant}, added_at, retired_at)` — app field names are namespaced `app.<slug>.<name>`; merchant always reads regardless of `read_policy`.
- `app_tables(table_id, install_id, name, schema json, row_limit int, added_at, retired_at)`
- `app_rows(row_id, table_id, merchant_id, data json(schema), row_version int, created_at, updated_at)` — owner install reads/writes; merchant reads.

**Rules and programs (versioned).**
- `rules(rule_id, rule_version_id, merchant_id, owner enum{platform,merchant}|install_id, kind enum{validate,access}, table, trigger json{on:[insert|update|delete|transition:<name>], fields:[...]}, applies_to set<enum{staff,app,customer,platform}>, exempt_self bool, predicate json, lookups json[≤3 declared single-hop joins], requires_scopes set<text> (derived), message_merchant text, message_writer text|null, reveals enum{none,existence}, failure_posture enum{open_flag,closed}, supports_required bool, overridable bool, cpu_budget_us int, state enum{active,disabled_scope_revoked,disabled_by_merchant,retired}, dark_choice enum{proceed_unguarded,keep_refusing}|null, effective_from, effective_to)`.
  Access rules deny only; an install's access rule touches host fields only under `guard:<table>` and never the owner role's reads. Predicates are three-valued over `before`, `after`, `actor{kind,is_self}` and declared lookups; a field added after a row existed reads as `absent`, distinct from `null`.
- `programs(program_id, program_version_id, install_id, kind enum{gate,contribute,react,job,action}, code_hash, trigger json, contribute_kind enum|null, requires_scopes, requires_permission text|null (actions), budget json{cpu_ms, wall_ms, max_writes}, state, effective_from, effective_to)` — sandboxed, deterministic, no I/O.
- `subscriptions(subscription_id, install_id, event_kinds set<text>, endpoint_url, secret_key text (vault key), state enum{active,gap,paused,ended}, cursor_seq int, backlog_limit int)`.
- `overrides(override_id, merchant_id, rule_id, row_ref, user_id, reason, created_at, expires_at)` — 24-hour exemptions.
- `pricing_acceptances(acceptance_id, install_id, pricing_version_id, accepted_by_user_id, accepted_at, superseded_at, spend_cap_cents_month)` — append-only.
- `meters(meter_id, app_version_id, name, unit, price_per_unit_cents, metered_by enum{platform,app_reported}, cap_suggested_cents)`.

**Host commerce.**
- `orders(order_id, merchant_id, customer_id, status enum{draft,placed,paid,fulfilled,closed,cancelled,refunded}, currency, subtotal_cents, total_cents (derived), pricing_version int, pricing_stale bool (derived), tags set<text> (derived from tag_assignments), note text, gate_flags map<rule_id,text>, row_version int, field_versions map<field,int>, created_by_principal, created_at, updated_at)`.
- `order_lines(line_id, order_id, product_id, qty, unit_price_cents)`.
- `order_adjustments(adjustment_id, order_id, owner enum{host,merchant}|install_id, kind enum{discount,tax,shipping,fee}, amount_cents, rank int, basis_pricing_version int, source enum{contribute_program,external}, stale bool, created_at, superseded_at)` — versioned; the fold reads the unsuperseded rows.
- `tag_assignments(row_ref, tag, owner principal, added_at, removed_at, removed_by)` — append-only; the derived set is rows with `removed_at` null.
- `customers(customer_id, merchant_id, name, email, phone, address (pii group), tags (derived), order_count int (host counter), row_version, field_versions, ...)`.
- `products(product_id, merchant_id, sku, price_cents, on_hand int, reserved_qty int (host counter), available_qty (derived = on_hand − reserved_qty), row_version, field_versions)`.
- `reservations(reservation_id, product_id, qty, owner_install_id|null, order_id, state enum{held,consumed,released,expired}, expires_at, created_at)` — time-bounded by `expires_at`.
- `refunds(refund_id, order_id, amount_cents, actor, initiator_user_id, created_at)`.

**Ledgers (append-only).**
- `events(event_seq int per merchant, event_id, merchant_id, kind, row_ref, changed_fields set<text>, actor, initiator_user_id, txn_id, origin_event_id, cascade_depth int, committed_at)`.
- `deliveries(delivery_id, subscription_id, event_seq, attempt int, state enum{pending,delivered,failed,skipped_unreadable}, next_attempt_at, response_code)`.
- `gaps(gap_id, install_id, from_seq, to_seq, reason enum{suspended,backlog,retries_exhausted}, backfilled_at)`.
- `write_audit(audit_id, merchant_id, txn_id, table, row_ref, actor, initiator_user_id, before json, after json, rule_versions_evaluated list, gates_run list, overrides_used list, outcome enum{committed,refused}, refusal_id, at)`.
- `refusals(refusal_id, merchant_id, rule_version_id, writer principal, row_ref, at, layer, overridden_by_override_id)`; `refusal_counters(writer_install_id, rule_id, hour, count)`.
- `read_audit_daily(install_id, day, table, field_group, rows_read int, sampled_row_refs list[≤20])`.
- `usage(usage_id, install_id, meter_id, quantity, occurred_at, recorded_at, source enum{platform,app_report}, idempotency_key, evidence_ref, pricing_version_id, state enum{billable,over_cap,disputed,voided}, invoice_id|null)`; `usage_daily(install_id, meter_id, day, quantity_billable, quantity_over_cap)`.
- `invoices(invoice_id, merchant_id, period_start, period_end, lines json, status)`; `payouts(payout_id, developer_id, period, amount_cents, withheld_cents)`.
- `device_intents(device_id, user_id, intent_seq, client_intent_id, table, row_ref, base_field_versions map, patch json, rules_version_seen, state enum{queued,committed,conflict,refused}, result json)`.

**Derived and time-bounded values.** Derived: `orders.total_cents`, `orders.tags`,
`orders.pricing_stale`, `products.available_qty`, `rules.requires_scopes`,
`customers.order_count`. Time-bounded: every grant, rule version, program version,
install state, pricing acceptance, override, reservation (`expires_at`), and gap.

### 5. The hard operations

Conventions. `S` is the transaction snapshot. `effective_scopes(install, S)` = grants
with `revoked_at` null at `S` ∩ scopes the current manifest requests ∩ scopes the app's
review tier allows. `may_write(ctx, table, field|transition, row)` = tenant match ∧ field
ownership ∧ principal scope (staff role permission, or install grant) ∧
initiator permission when `ctx.initiator` is set ∧ merchant access rules ∧ app guard
rules. Refusal codes to writers: `AUTHZ_REFUSED`, `POLICY_REFUSED{refusal_id, layer?,
message?}`, `RULE_UNAVAILABLE`, `CONFLICT{fields}`, `STALE_BASIS{current}`.

**O1. Install an app.**
Inputs: `merchant_id`, `app_version_id`, `user`, chosen optional scopes, `spend_cap_cents_month`, `required_gates`, `rank` (default last).
Authorization: `manage_apps`; app published; tier allows the requested scopes.
Steps: (1) Render consent: scopes with the field groups they unlock; fields with
policies; rules with description, predicate, read-set, posture, overridability;
gates/contributors with budgets; meters, prices, cap. (2) In one transaction: insert
`installs(state=active)`, `scope_grants` for required + chosen scopes, `field_defs`,
`app_tables`, `rules` versions (`effective_from=now`, `failure_posture=closed` only for
`required_gates` whose `supports_required` is true), `programs`, `subscriptions`,
`pricing_acceptances`, audit. Reject the whole install on any name collision or a rule
referencing an ungranted scope. (3) Emit `install.created`. (4) The app may call
`backfill(table, since)` (O9) under its day budget.
Atomic: (2). Eventual: (3), (4). Optimistic UI: none; the consent screen is the UI.
Conflict: a concurrent second install of the same app is refused by uniqueness on
`(merchant_id, app_id)` among non-purged installs. Upgrades reuse O1: a manifest version
needing new scopes, prices or rules waits as `pending_upgrade` until re-consented.

**O2. The gated write (every host-row insert/update/transition goes through this).**
Inputs: `principal` (staff, install, customer, platform), optional `initiator_user_id`,
`table`, `row_ref` or new, `patch = {set: {field: value}, ops: [add_tag, remove_tag,
transition{from,to}, adjust]}`, `base_field_versions` (for `set`), `client_intent_id`,
`origin_event_id`, `cascade_depth`.
Authorization: computed inside, per field (`may_write`).
Steps:
```
txn ← begin(serializable) at snapshot S
ctx ← {merchant, scopes: effective_scopes(principal,S), initiator, origin, depth}
before ← load(table,row_ref) FOR UPDATE
for f in patch.set: if base_field_versions[f] ≠ before.field_versions[f] → CONFLICT{f}
for f in fields touched by patch (set ∪ ops): if not may_write(ctx,table,f,before) → AUTHZ_REFUSED
for op in patch.ops:
  transition: if before.status ≠ op.from → CONFLICT{status}
  remove_tag: if assignment.owner ≠ principal and not may_write(ctx,table,'tags.any') → AUTHZ_REFUSED
after ← apply(before, patch)
rules ← rule versions at S for table, state ∈ {active, disabled(keep_refusing)},
        trigger matches (insert | changed_fields ∩ trigger.fields ≠ ∅ | transition name),
        applies_to ∋ kind(principal), not (exempt_self ∧ principal = owner)
order rules: platform, merchant, installs by rank, declaration order
budget ← {cpu: 20ms, wall: 50ms}
for r in rules:
  if r.state = disabled(keep_refusing) → REFUSE(POLICY, r)
  out ← eval(r.predicate, before, after, actor, lookups ≤3, cpu ≤ r.cpu_budget_us)  ∈ {pass, refuse, unknown}
  if out = refuse and exists override(r.rule_id,row_ref) unexpired → record override; continue
  if out = refuse → REFUSE(POLICY, r)
  if out = unknown or budget exhausted:
       if r.failure_posture = closed → REFUSE(RULE_UNAVAILABLE, r)
       else after.gate_flags[r.rule_id] ← reason
for g in gate programs (installs by rank) with matching trigger:
  result ← sandbox(g, project(before,after, g.scopes), cpu ≤ 5ms)
  if result.refuse → as refuse above; if error/timeout → as unknown above
  after ← after + result.own_field_writes   (fields owned by g.install only)
if table = orders and basis changed (lines, qty, earlier-ranked adjustment) → O4 fold inline
after.row_version ← before.row_version+1; after.field_versions[f] ← after.row_version for changed f
write row; write tag_assignments for ops; append write_audit(committed, rule versions, gates)
append events(kind, changed_fields, actor, initiator, origin_event_id ?? new, depth)
bump budget_windows[install].rows_written; platform meters (e.g. orders_gated) → usage_daily
commit
on REFUSE: rollback; in a separate transaction append refusals + write_audit(refused) and
  increment refusal_counters(writer, rule); if counter > 100/h → merchant alert, writer throttled
  return POLICY_REFUSED{refusal_id, layer if reveals≠none, message_writer if set}
```
Atomic: row, folds, tag assignments, audit, event append, budget bump. Eventual: fan-out
(O8), read counters. Optimistic UI: a staff device predicts with cached rule predicates
(all merchant-visible); anything needing a gate program or an unreadable lookup predicts
`unknown`, shown as "pending check". Apps get no optimism. Conflict: per-field CAS;
commuting ops never conflict.

**O3. Staff-initiated app action (e.g. "Refund" in ShipNimbus).**
Inputs: `user`, `install`, `action program`, `row_ref`, `args`.
Authorization: user's role holds `program.requires_permission` (`refunds.create`) **and**
install holds every scope in `program.requires_scopes` (`write:refunds`).
Steps: (1) Check both; refuse with `USER_LACKS{permission}` or `APP_LACKS{scope}`
(merchant-side, so attribution is fine). (2)
Run the sandbox with principal = install, `initiator = user`, budget 200 ms CPU / 2 s
wall / ≤ 20 writes. (3) Every write it performs is an O2 call with `ctx.initiator`
set, so `may_write` re-checks the intersection per field. (4) Return the result and the
committed writes to the UI.
Atomic: each write separately (programs must be idempotent on `args.idempotency_key`).
Optimistic UI: the button shows "working". Conflict: as O2. Autonomous variant: a
reaction or job doing the same write has no initiator and must hold
`autonomous:write:refunds`.

**O4. Reprice an order (rank-ordered contribution fold).** Runs inline in O2 whenever
the basis changes, and when an app posts an external adjustment.
Inputs: `order`, changed basis. Authorization: `contribute:orders.<kind>`.
Steps:
```
order.pricing_version += 1
basis ← subtotal(lines)
for p in contribute programs of active installs, by rank:
  lines ← sandbox(p, project(order, p.scopes), basis, cpu ≤ 5ms)      (returns adjustments of p.contribute_kind)
  validate: kind = p.contribute_kind; discount lines ≤ basis; |amount| ≤ manifest max; on error → posture (open_flag: omit + flag; closed: REFUSE)
  supersede p.install's prior lines; insert new lines(basis_pricing_version = current)
  basis ← basis + Σ lines (discounts negative)
for a in external adjustments not superseded: if a.basis_pricing_version < current → a.stale ← true
total_cents ← subtotal + Σ unsuperseded lines; pricing_stale ← any stale
refuse if total_cents < 0 (platform rule)
```
Post-commit: `orders.pricing_changed` to owners of stale external lines; they re-post
via `post_adjustment(order, kind, amount, basis_pricing_version, idempotency_key)`,
refused `STALE_BASIS` if the basis moved again. A platform rule refuses the `place`
transition while `pricing_stale` and any stale owner's posture is `closed`; `open_flag`
stale lines are dropped and flagged at placement.
Atomic: the fold with the write that changed the basis. Eventual: external re-posts (2 s
budget at checkout, then posture). Optimistic UI: checkout shows lines "recalculating"
until `pricing_stale` clears. Conflict: the order row lock serializes contributors; a
late post against an old basis is refused, not applied.

**O5. Reserve inventory (structural counter).**
Inputs: `install`, `product_id`, `qty`, `order_id?`, `ttl ≤ 30 min`.
Authorization: `write:reservations`; `read:products.availability` to receive the number.
Steps: (1) Load product FOR UPDATE. (2) `available = on_hand − reserved_qty` — over all
reservations, not the caller's. (3) If `qty > available` → `INSUFFICIENT{available}`
(a counter the scope permits). (4) Insert `reservations(held, expires_at=now+ttl,
owner=install)`. (5) `reserved_qty += qty` through O2 (merchant rules such as "no single
hold > 100 units" apply). (6) Commit. Consume on `pay`, release on `cancel`; a sweeper
every 30 s releases `held` rows past `expires_at` with `reserved_qty −= qty` under the
product lock, idempotent by row state.
Atomic: (1)–(6). Eventual: expiry (≤ 30 s late, never early). Optimistic UI: storefront
shows "reserved until 10:00" from the returned `expires_at`. Conflict: two apps
reserving one SKU serialize on the product row; the second sees the reduced number.

**O6. Revoke a scope (or disable a rule).**
Inputs: `user`, `install`, `scope`, `reason`.
Authorization: `manage_apps`.
Steps: (1) Compute dependents: rules with `scope ∈ requires_scopes`, programs likewise,
subscription event kinds needing it. (2) Show the merchant the list with postures; for
every `closed`-posture rule require a `dark_choice`: `proceed_unguarded` or
`keep_refusing`. (3) In one transaction: `grant.revoked_at = now`; per dependent rule
append a version `(state=disabled_scope_revoked, dark_choice, effective_from=now)` and
close the prior one; programs → `disabled`; subscription kinds removed; audit. (4) Emit
`install.scope_revoked{scope}`. Re-grant restores each dependent to `active` in one
transaction.
Atomic: (3). Eventual: (4). Optimistic UI: none; the confirm step *is* the point.
Conflict: a write whose snapshot precedes the revocation commits under the old rule set,
one after it under the new; in-flight jobs see refusals on their next write.

**O7. Override a refusal.**
Inputs: `user`, `refusal_id`, `reason`, then the original write re-submitted.
Authorization: `override_rules` permission; the refusing rule's `overridable = true`.
Steps: (1) Load refusal; if the rule is not overridable → `NOT_OVERRIDABLE` with the
install's message. (2) Insert `overrides(rule_id, row_ref, user, reason, expires =
now+24h)`. (3) Re-run the write via O2, which records the override in `overrides_used`.
(4) Emit `rule.overridden{rule_id, row_ref, initiator}` to the rule owner (initiator id
only under `read:staff`).
Atomic: (2); then (3) as its own transaction. Eventual: (4). Optimistic UI: the refused
form stays filled, with an "override" affordance only for overridable rules. Conflict:
two staff overriding one row create two rows; either suffices; both are audited.

**O8. Post-commit fan-out and fair reaction scheduling (background).**
Inputs: committed events from O2 in `event_seq` order. Authorization: each target
receives only what its scopes permit at delivery.
Steps:
```
for e in new events:
  for t in subscriptions ∪ react programs of merchant with kind match, state active:
    visible ← e.changed_fields ∩ readable_fields(t.install, S_now)
    if visible = ∅ and e.kind ≠ insert/delete → skip (no leak of another app's activity)
    if e.actor = t.install and not t.receive_own_writes → skip
    if t is react and e.cascade_depth ≥ 4 → skip; mark e.cascade_exhausted; if 3 in an hour → merchant notice
    enqueue (t, e.seq, visible) on queue[t.install]:
      coalesce with a pending item for the same (row_ref, 'updated'): union changed fields, keep latest seq
      if queue length > 100,000 → drop oldest; append gaps(install, from, to, 'backlog'); subscription.state ← gap
scheduler (per shard, continuous):
  merchant ← weighted round-robin by plan (starter 1, growth 3, plus 10)
  install ← round-robin among that merchant's non-empty queues
  item ← dequeue; if budget_windows[install].cpu_ms_used ≥ limit → park queue, notify install + merchant, continue
  react: sandbox(program, payload, cpu ≤ 50ms, wall ≤ 5s, writes ≤ 100) — each write is O2 with principal=install, no initiator, origin=e.origin, depth=e.depth+1
  subscription: hand to O9
```
Atomic: enqueue per event (idempotent on `(target, seq)`). Eventual: everything else;
lag is the merchant-visible number ("ShipNimbus is 3,400 events behind"). Optimistic
UI: none. Conflict: reactions writing one row serialize in O2; a stale assumption fails
per-field CAS and the program decides whether to retry.

**O9. Deliver to the developer's endpoint and backfill (external system).**
Inputs: `subscription`, queued `(seq, visible)` items.
Authorization: payload projected under `effective_scopes(install, S_delivery)`.
Steps: (1) Build payload `{event_id, seq, kind, row_ref, fields: project(row, scopes)}`,
HMAC-signed with the vault key. (2) POST with concurrency 50 per subscription, strictly
ordered per `row_ref`. (3) 2xx → `delivered`, advance `cursor_seq` past all delivered
lower seqs; else retry with backoff 10 s → 1 h for up to 72 h, then `retries_exhausted`:
subscription → `gap`, append `gaps`, stop delivering. (4) `backfill(table, since_seq |
since_ts, page)` — a bounded bulk read (≤ 10k rows per call, ≤ 1 M per day) under
scopes, charged to the day budget; on completion the app calls `ack_gap(gap_id)` and
delivery resumes from the current head. Fields reflect scopes at delivery, not at
commit; if nothing is readable any more the item is `skipped_unreadable` and the cursor
advances.
Atomic: per delivery attempt. Eventual: all. Conflict: endpoints must be idempotent on
`event_id`; two attempts can both land.

**O10. Scheduled job run (e.g. TextBlast nightly segment refresh).**
Inputs: `program(kind=job, cron)`, slot time.
Authorization: install scopes at each write; autonomous writes need `autonomous:`.
Steps: (1) At each cron slot create `job_run(slot, fencing_token)`, unique per slot;
slots missed during downtime run **once**, not once each. (2) Lease the run for 10 min;
run the sandbox with budget 60 s CPU / 10 min wall / 10k rows read / 1k writes. (3) Each
write is an O2 carrying the fencing token; a write from an expired lease is refused
`STALE_LEASE`. (4) On budget exhaustion terminate; completed writes stand; the program
resumes from a checkpoint row in its own app table next slot. (5) Record outcome and CPU
in `budget_windows`.
Atomic: per write. Eventual: the job's overall effect. Optimistic UI: none. Conflict:
two runs for one slot cannot both hold the lease.

**O11. Record usage; close the monthly invoice (money; periodic/bulk).**
Inputs: platform meter counters from O2/O8/O9; `report_usage(install, meter, qty,
occurred_at, idempotency_key)` from apps; the 1st of each month.
Authorization: `report_usage:<meter>` for reports.
Steps, report path: (1) Idempotency on `(install, key)`. (2) `occurred_at ∈ [now−7d,
now]`, inside the install's active interval and inside an acceptance's interval; else
`USAGE_REJECTED{reason}`. (3) `price ← acceptance.pricing_version`; if
`spend_used_month + qty×price > cap` → record `state=over_cap` (unbilled), emit
`usage.cap_reached` once, return `OVER_CAP`; else record `billable`, bump `usage_daily`.
Platform path: counters flushed to `usage_daily` every minute with the same cap check
(over-cap parks the app's *paid* features, never host commerce).
Invoice job, per merchant: (4) lines = Σ `usage_daily` per install/meter with
`recorded_at ≤ period_end + 48 h` and `invoice_id` null; stamp `invoice_id` on those
usage rows in the same transaction. (5) Assert each line ≤ cap. (6) Payout after 30 days
= Σ billable − 20% fee − disputed; a dispute flips rows to `disputed`, reducing payout,
not the issued invoice; the platform adjudicates and credits.
Atomic: each usage row; each invoice with its stamping. Eventual: rollups (≤ 1 min),
payouts. Optimistic UI: the merchant's spend meter follows the rollup. Conflict: two
reports with one key collapse; a report racing the cap check serializes on the
install's month counter row.

**O12. Uninstall, retain, purge.**
Inputs: `user`, `install`, `retain_days = 30`.
Authorization: `manage_apps`.
Steps: (1) One transaction: close every rule version (`effective_to = now`, `retired`);
programs and subscriptions `ended`; grants `revoked_at = now`; pending reactions dropped
with a `gaps(reason=suspended)` row; `pricing_acceptances.superseded_at = now`; tag
assignments owned by the install re-owned to `merchant`; `field_defs` and `app_tables`
`retired_at = now` (values stay merchant-readable, read-only); `state ← retained`,
`purge_after = now+30d`; usage stays billable up to `now`. (2) Best-effort
`install.uninstalled` delivery for 72 h; export bundle offered to the merchant. (3)
Purge job after `purge_after`: delete `app_rows` and field values in 10k-row batches;
`state ← purged`. Never deleted: the install row, contribution lines, audit, refusals,
usage, events. Reinstall within retention offers `adopt_retained_data`: field defs and
app rows are re-parented to the new install (`previous_install_id`) in one transaction.
Atomic: (1); each purge batch. Eventual: (2), (3). Optimistic UI: the app disappears
from the admin at once; a "data retained until" notice remains. Conflict: a write whose
snapshot precedes (1) still runs the app's gates; after it, none.

**O13. Offline staff-device sync (merge).**
Inputs: `device_intents` queued offline, in `intent_seq` order, each with
`base_field_versions`, `patch`, `rules_version_seen`.
Authorization: the user's role at sync time, not at edit time.
Steps: (1) Replay intents strictly in order; each is an O2 call with
`client_intent_id` (idempotent on retry). (2) Outcomes: `committed`; `CONFLICT{fields}`
→ the device shows theirs vs. mine and the user resubmits with fresh versions;
`POLICY_REFUSED` → shown with full attribution (staff are merchant-side) and an override
affordance if allowed; `AUTHZ_REFUSED` → shown. (3) If `rules_version_seen < current`,
mark the outcome "checked against newer rules". (4) Later intents on the same row as a
failed one are held and shown, not silently replayed.
Atomic: per intent. Eventual: the queue drains in order. Optimistic UI: offline edits
render as committed with a "pending" badge that clears or becomes a conflict/refusal
marker. Conflict: scalar fields by per-field CAS; tag ops and transitions commute or
fail cleanly; app-owned fields never appear in a device patch unless `write_policy =
owner_and_merchant`.

**O14. The merchant's permission-and-activity ledger for one app (read).**
Inputs: `install_id`, window (7/30 days). Authorization: `owner` or `manage_apps`.
Steps: (1) Effective scopes with grant history. (2) Readable/writable field groups,
computed as scopes × `field_defs` × merchant access rules. (3) Fields and tables the app
owns, with policies and row counts. (4) Rules with state, posture, `requires_scopes`,
predicate text, and refusal counts issued and received. (5) Programs with budget use.
(6) Subscriptions with lag and gaps. (7) Reads from `read_audit_daily` with sampled
rows. (8) Writes from `write_audit`, paged. (9) Spend vs cap. Every part is an index
lookup by `install_id`; nothing scans.
Atomic: snapshot read. Eventual: read counters lag ≤ 1 day; write audit is exact.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment: 50,000 merchants; 2,000 marketplace apps; 400,000 installs
(mean 8 per merchant, large merchants 40+); 100 M products; 500 M customers; 30 M orders
a month (≈ 12/s average, 600/s on peak days, one plus-tier merchant 50/s); 1.2 M active
rule versions (≤ ~100 per merchant); 2 B `app_rows`; 500 M events a
month; 1 B `write_audit` rows a month; 240 M deliveries a month (≈ 8 per order), reaction
cascade factor ≈ 1.5; `read_audit_daily` at 400,000 × 5 × 3 ≈ 6 M rows a day.

What gets expensive at these sizes:
- **O2's rule pass** runs on every host write and must stay under 20 ms CPU / 50 ms wall
  against a 150 ms p99 write target; it needs the merchant's rule set cached by version
  stamp and O(1) rules (≤ 3 single-hop lookups, no aggregates except counter fields). A
  merchant with 100 rules and 6 gates is the design ceiling, not the average.
- **O4 folds** re-run every contributor on each basis change during checkout editing (5
  contributors × 5 ms), so the UI batches basis changes at 300 ms.
- **O8 fan-out on peak days**: 600 orders/s × 8 targets ≈ 5k enqueues/s; the queues are
  the memory hot spot, and the 100k-per-install backlog cap with gaps keeps it bounded.
- **O11's monthly close** over ~1 B usage rows is feasible only against `usage_daily`;
  stamping `invoice_id` on raw rows runs in 10k-row batches per merchant.
- **O12 purges** of a large app's 50 M `app_rows` run for hours in batches, off-peak,
  never inside the uninstall click.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

**U1. Authorization rules written by untrusted tenants-of-tenants, evaluated inside the
host's commit, bound to a versioned scope.** In an ordinary backend, authorization is
code by one author. Here the rule set is *data*, authored by mutually distrusting
parties, versioned by interval, composed by deterministic layered conjunction, each rule
carrying a compiled read-set re-checked against the author's grants at the transaction
snapshot (or invalidated in the revocation's own transaction), with a per-rule budget and
a failure posture that is never silent. A product could concede rules as a small
declarative predicate language interpreted by the host, with arbitrary code confined to
sandboxed gates under hard budgets. The concession becomes a cheat when apps are told to
"subscribe and cancel afterwards" — the customer already saw a confirmation and "an app
can enforce" is gone — or when rules run as the platform superuser so a revoked scope
keeps flowing into outcomes.

**U2. Rows with many owners: field-level ownership, per-element attribution, folds over
attributed contributions in a merchant-set order.** Ordinary row stores have one schema
owner and last-writer-wins at row or field granularity. Here `orders.tags` is a set of
owner-attributed assignments, `total_cents` a fold over owner-attributed lines in rank
order with staleness tracking, and a patch is per-field CAS plus commuting ops. The
concession is the model above — side tables per owner, derived fields recomputed in the
transaction. It becomes a cheat when apps are told to read-modify-write an array or when
a marketplace policy of "one app per field" replaces a data-model guarantee.

**U3. Exact counts over the unfiltered domain, served to readers who cannot see the
rows, maintained transactionally.** An ordinary backend either filters counts by the
caller's visibility (T2's confidently wrong integer) or exposes raw counts (a leak).
The concession is host-maintained counter fields updated in the same transaction and
given their own read scope. It becomes a cheat when the counter is eventually
consistent (oversell) or when the app is told to count its own rows.

**U4. Fairness and blast-radius isolation among principals inside one tenant and across
tenants, including inside the commit path.** Ordinary backends have one workload owner,
FIFO queues, and rate limits at the API edge. Here app code runs inside the host's
commit under a budget, reactions run on per-install queues under weighted round-robin
across merchants, derived writes carry cascade depth, backlogs coalesce then gap, and
budget exhaustion parks the app without touching commerce. The concession is per-key
rate limiting plus one queue per install. It becomes a cheat when limits stop at the API
edge (gates and reactions are not API calls) or when a timed-out gate fails open without
a flag.

**U5. Time-bounded authority and rule versions as first-class data, with audit that
outlives the author.** Ordinary backends keep roles as enum columns, rules in code, and
delete on uninstall. Here every grant, rule, program, price acceptance, install state and
reservation is an interval; evaluation binds the versions in force at the snapshot; audit
references immutable version ids; uninstall closes intervals. The concession is
`effective_from/to` columns and soft-closed version tables. It becomes a cheat when a
`deleted` flag stands in for an interval (one missed filter and a dead app's rule keeps
refusing) or when an install's `state ∈ {active, former}` is asked "was this rule in
force when that order was placed" — an enum cannot answer a question about an instant.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice: OAuth-style scope grants with
required/optional sets; namespaced app-owned fields and tables (Shopify metafields,
Salesforce managed packages); declarative validation rules on host objects (Salesforce);
sandboxed in-platform checkout functions with CPU budgets and no I/O (Shopify
Functions); merchant-set discount combination order; signed at-least-once webhooks with
retries and idempotency on event id; per-tenant governor limits and per-app quotas;
usage-based app billing with caps and re-consent on price change; soft-delete with a
retention window; append-only audit; app data in the store visible to the admin.

Deliberate departures (product choices, not known results): apps'
access rules are **deny-only** (Salesforce sharing is additive); the **intersection**
rule for staff-initiated app actions (Shopify apps act with app scopes regardless of the
clicking staffer; Salesforce Apex runs in system mode by default — both the opposite of
invariant 16); "the merchant can read everything in the store, including app tables".

Invented for the exercise, most likely to need revision when built: rule/scope
binding with transactional invalidation and the `dark_choice` for required gates;
three-valued rule outcomes tied to a declared posture; opaque refusals with probe
counters and `reveals`; owner-attributed tag assignments and rank-ordered contribution
folds with `basis_pricing_version` staleness and a 2 s checkout budget; cascade depth 4
as the loop stop; the `absent`/`null` distinction; "first refusal stops" (merchants may
prefer every failing rule at once, at the cost of budget and leak surface); the 100/h
probe threshold and 100k backlog cap, round numbers standing in for measurement; the
scale figures likewise — plausible, not measured.

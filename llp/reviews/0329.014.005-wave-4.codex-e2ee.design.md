### 1. Product contract

The product is an end-to-end-encrypted group messenger for families, friends, companies, and high-risk communities. A group contains accounts; each account can have up to five independently keyed devices. Groups support up to 1,000 accounts.

“Accepted” means the service has assigned an event its permanent group sequence and durably created its delivery obligations. “Removed” means the authoritative membership change and successor encryption epoch have committed together.

The product makes these user-visible promises:

1. Message text, formatting, group names, attachments, reactions, quote relationships, mentions, receipts, disappearing-message settings, and search indexes are never plaintext in service databases, logs, analytics, push systems, object storage, or support tools.

2. The operator can see metadata necessary to run the service: accounts and devices in each group, roles, sender device, timing, approximate padded sizes, IP and push routes, queue status, and membership changes; this is confidential messaging, not metadata anonymity.

3. Every membership, role, or group-device change has an ordered boundary and a matching cryptographic epoch.

4. Removing an account excludes all its devices from the successor epoch atomically; no post-removal event can be accepted under the old epoch.

5. Removal protects future activity, not history: a former member can retain plaintext already obtained and may receive messages accepted for its queue before the removal boundary.

6. The service cannot silently add a decryption device: devices have independent keys, account-signed certificates, visible device-list changes, and transparency proofs.

7. A new group member receives content from its join boundary forward; prior history is shared only through a separate, explicit encrypted export.

8. A newly linked device receives history from an existing trusted device or a user-keyed encrypted backup; without either, the operator cannot recover that history.

9. Every active group device has an independent offline delivery queue retained for 45 days; one device’s delivery acknowledgement never clears a sibling device’s queue.

10. Retries do not create duplicate messages, while concurrent messages from several devices are ordered by service acceptance, not unreliable client clocks.

11. An offline draft is never silently exposed to accounts added after it was composed; the author must confirm the enlarged audience before it is encrypted and sent.

12. Plaintext search—including words, phrases, sender, date, captions, and filenames—runs on locally retained history, and the UI states whether the device has complete history or is still rebuilding.

13. Mentions are encrypted structured account references, and “mentions you” is a local index; every recipient receives the same opaque event, while the device decides whether to show a mention notification.

14. Quote replies contain an encrypted fallback excerpt; quoting material from before a current member joined requires confirmation because the quote deliberately republishes that excerpt to the current audience.

15. Reactions converge to at most one current reaction per account per message even when two devices react offline; the operator cannot see, count, validate, or compact them.

16. “Read by Alice” means one of Alice’s authorized devices rendered the message or Alice explicitly marked it read; transport delivery is not a read receipt, and peer-facing receipts can be disabled.

17. Disappearing messages support after-send and after-view timers, remove plaintext and derived local artifacts on every honest device, and never claim to defeat screenshots, exports, compromised clients, or malicious recipients.

18. Attachments are encrypted and padded before upload, are scanned or previewed only on devices, and remain available from the service for at most 45 days unless another trusted device supplies them later.

19. Push providers receive only generic wake-ups; rich previews and mention-specific alerts are produced after device-side decryption.

20. Queue expiry, missing history, failed rekeying, incomplete search, and paused groups are explicit states; the product never reports a security-sensitive operation complete when it is merely queued.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[SECRECY]** Only authorized endpoint devices ever receive the keys needed to interpret encrypted application events.

2. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Search terms, search results, mention targets, reaction values, quote targets, receipt targets, filenames, previews, and disappearing policies never leave endpoints in operator-readable form.

3. **[SECRECY] [TIME]** A member removal and the fresh encryption epoch excluding every device of that member become effective at one indivisible ordering boundary.

4. **[SECRECY] [TIME]** No application event after that boundary is accepted with the prior epoch, even from a member who remains authorized.

5. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA] [TIME]** An event accepted before removal remains part of the removed member’s historically authorized queue, while an event accepted afterward never enters it.

6. **[AUTHORITY]** No membership change is accepted without the expected current revision and an intent signed by an account authorized to make that change.

7. **[SECRECY] [AUTHORITY]** A successor epoch is generated by a device that remains authorized after the transition, never solely by the departing device or the operator.

8. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Every cryptographic roster contains exactly the certified group devices authorized for that epoch, with no invisible operator-controlled leaf.

9. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** The recipient-device set for an accepted event is immutable and is never recalculated from later membership.

10. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Delivery acknowledgement is per device and is recorded only after that device has authenticated and durably persisted the ciphertext.

11. **[AUTHORITY]** Clients display a decrypted semantic event only if its sender, role, epoch, schema, references, and encrypted policy permissions were valid at its group sequence.

12. **[SECRECY]** All application event types use the same padded outer envelope, so the service does not receive a plaintext message, reaction, receipt, quote, mention, or timer discriminator.

13. **[SECRECY]** Full-text and mention indexes are encrypted with device-local storage keys and are never replaced by deterministic tokens or blind indexes queryable by the service.

14. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA] [TIME]** A newly added account receives no pre-join group keys or history unless an existing participant explicitly reshares that material.

15. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** An offline draft whose recipient set grew is held for confirmation rather than silently re-encrypted to the larger group.

16. **[TIME]** Concurrent reactions, receipts, tombstones, and account-device operations converge from authenticated causal information and the common service sequence, not from wall-clock guesses.

17. **[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Expiry removes the message, its local decryption key, search and mention entries, thumbnails, notification text, cached media, and backup-visible state before the app can reveal it again.

18. **[SECRECY]** Attachment keys, filenames, media types, dimensions, content hashes, and message relationships exist only inside encrypted application content.

19. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A malformed or unauthorized inner event may be quarantined, but it cannot prevent later valid events from being synchronized.

20. **[SECRECY]** Telemetry, crash reporting, moderation, and customer support remain content-blind unless a user deliberately submits a narrowly scoped disclosure package.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Separating removal from rotation.**  
   **Wrong version:** Update the membership row, asynchronously rotate keys, and let normal sends continue meanwhile.  
   **Why wrong:** A send can observe the new roster while using a key still known to the removed devices; reversing the order creates the opposite mismatch.  
   **Observation:** An offline removed device decrypts an event whose service sequence is after the displayed removal. The correct transition appends the roster change, successor cryptographic commit, entitlement cutover, sequence barrier, and durable delivery outbox in one transaction.

2. **Treating an account as one cryptographic recipient.**  
   **Wrong version:** Give every device the same account private key or maintain one account queue and cursor.  
   **Why wrong:** A lost laptop cannot be excluded independently, one phone’s acknowledgement drops a desktop’s message, and a newly linked device silently inherits history.  
   **Observation:** After revoking a laptop, it can still decrypt ciphertext obtained from another source, or acknowledging on a phone makes an offline desktop miss the event. Accounts are membership principals, but devices are distinct cryptographic leaves and delivery principals.

3. **Using current membership for historical content.**  
   **Wrong version:** Recompute queue access when an offline device fetches, automatically send stale drafts to the current roster, or quote an old message without comparing audiences.  
   **Why wrong:** Former members lose content accepted while they were authorized, while new members receive content composed or originally spoken before they joined.  
   **Observation:** A newly added member sees a quoted confidential excerpt or an offline draft without its author approving the disclosure. Every event and quote must be interpreted against membership intervals at the relevant sequence.

4. **Smuggling server-side rules and indexes back into the design.**  
   **Wrong version:** Upload searchable term hashes, store a `mentioned_account_ids` field, send push only to mentioned users, or let the service enforce “reactions disabled” by inspecting event types.  
   **Why wrong:** Deterministic tokens reveal equality and query patterns, selective fan-out is itself a mention index, and plaintext type flags reveal behavior the envelope was meant to hide.  
   **Observation:** An operator can enumerate who was mentioned, correlate repeated search terms, or distinguish a receipt from a message. Honest clients must evaluate semantic rules after decryption, while the service enforces only membership, epoch, size, rate, and storage limits.

5. **Making opaque operations converge by accident.**  
   **Wrong version:** Store reactions as server-side upserts, trust client timestamps, or apply events in whatever order each device receives them.  
   **Why wrong:** The service cannot see reaction targets or receipt frontiers, clocks are untrustworthy, and per-device queues can deliver retries or unresolved references.  
   **Observation:** Two devices of one account react offline and different recipients permanently display different emoji. Encrypted operations need stable IDs, causal predecessors, tombstones, a common acceptance sequence, and deterministic endpoint reducers.

6. **Equating disappearance with deleting one message row.**  
   **Wrong version:** Start every timer at send, delete only the conversation row, or assume a powered-off device has been remotely erased.  
   **Why wrong:** Offline recipients can lose after-view messages before seeing them, while search indexes, quoted previews, thumbnails, backups, notification caches, and database journals retain the plaintext.  
   **Observation:** Search finds an expired phrase or a reconnecting device briefly shows expired content. Safety controls and tombstones must be applied before rendering or indexing, and the promise must remain limited to honest endpoints.

### 4. Data model

All identifiers are random 128-bit values, digests are 32 bytes, sequence numbers are unsigned 64-bit integers, and authoritative timestamps are UTC service times. The cryptographic group context is:

`(group_id, membership_version, crypto_epoch, roster_hash, tree_root, prior_transition_hash)`

The protocol is an audited asynchronous group-key protocol with one leaf per device, authenticated transition transcripts, encrypted update paths, and per-sender forward-ratcheting message secrets.

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Link a device and bootstrap it.**  
   **Inputs:** New device keys and QR challenge, expected account device version, trusted-device or recovery signature.  
   **Authorization:** An existing account device or user-held recovery credential must certify the new device.  
   **Steps:** (1) The new device generates independent keys; (2) the trusted device verifies the out-of-band challenge and signs its certificate; (3) the service compare-and-swaps the account roster and appends the transparency entry; (4) existing devices install the new leaf into each group through operation 2; (5) one trusted device or encrypted backup transfers history, which the new device decrypts and reindexes.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Account admission and transparency publication are atomic; group enrollment and history transfer are resumable per group.  
   **UI/conflict:** Show group-by-group progress and do not claim completion early; simultaneous links retry the account-version comparison, and absent a transfer source the device starts without old history.

2. **Add an account or linked device to a group.**  
   **Inputs:** Current group context, desired account/role or certified device, new roster, cryptographic commit, welcome packages, idempotency key.  
   **Authorization:** An active admin signs an account addition; an existing member account may authorize one of its certified devices without changing its role.  
   **Steps:** (1) Fetch and verify current membership and device transparency; (2) a retained device creates a fresh successor epoch; (3) bind the exact old and new rosters into the commit; (4) create welcome material for new leaves; (5) the service verifies the metadata permission, signatures, expected revision, and exact package targets; (6) one transaction appends the transition, opens membership/device intervals, advances the group head, assigns the transition sequence, and creates delivery work.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** The roster, epoch, entitlements, and outbox are atomic; device processing is eventual.  
   **UI/conflict:** Display “adding” until the signed receipt returns; a stale concurrent transition loses and must discard its prepared secret and rebuild, while new accounts receive no earlier history.

3. **Remove a member and rotate keys.**  
   **Inputs:** Target account, expected context, signed admin intent, resulting roster, successor commit generated by a retained device.  
   **Authorization:** A current admin may remove another member, while self-leave requires a valid leave intent and a different retained device to generate the successor secret.  
   **Steps:** (1) Remove every target-account leaf from the proposed roster; (2) generate fresh epoch material encrypted only to retained leaves; (3) bind group, prior transition, role diff, old/new roster hashes, and next version; (4) verify that the intent signer was authorized, the committer remains, and no removed leaf receives a package; (5) at transition sequence `q`, one transaction closes the target’s intervals at `q`, advances membership and epoch, appends the commit and signed receipt, and queues it to retained devices; (6) reject every later old-epoch send.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** All listed service changes are atomic; retained offline devices process the commit later and before new-epoch content.  
   **UI/conflict:** Show only a pending removal before commit; pre-`q` queue items remain deliverable, concurrent send/transition ordering is unambiguous, and if no retained device can commit, the group pauses rather than falsely completing removal.

4. **Send a message or quote, including an offline draft.**  
   **Inputs:** Logical event ID, plaintext body, optional encrypted mentions and quote, composed roster hash, timer policy, current group context.  
   **Authorization:** The sending device must be an active group leaf; the client also checks the encrypted group policy and sender’s role at the current sequence.  
   **Steps:** (1) Validate mention targets and content permissions locally; (2) compare the quote’s historical audience with the intended current audience and request confirmation before resharing an excerpt; (3) after reconnect, fetch the current context; (4) if recipients were added, hold for confirmation, while a removal-only change may be safely revalidated and rebased; (5) encrypt under a fresh current-epoch message secret and bind the visible context as authenticated data; (6) submit the expected epoch and idempotency key; (7) the service checks only outer authorization, epoch, size, rate, and retry identity, then atomically assigns a sequence, stores ciphertext, fixes the recipient obligations, and writes push outbox entries.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Acceptance, ordering, payload durability, recipient snapshot, and outbox are atomic; push, delivery, and local indexing are eventual.  
   **UI/conflict:** Offline or submitted bubbles remain visibly pending; stale epochs are rejected and re-encrypted from plaintext, uncertain retries query the event ID first, and concurrent devices display service acceptance order.

5. **Upload and retrieve an attachment.**  
   **Inputs:** Local bytes, random file key, padded encrypted chunks, upload capability.  
   **Authorization:** Any authenticated active device may reserve quota; download requires the unguessable capability carried inside an authorized encrypted message.  
   **Steps:** (1) Generate a fresh file key; (2) encrypt and authenticate padded chunks locally; (3) upload them to external object storage; (4) finalize only after ciphertext digests and lengths match; (5) place the blob capability, key, plaintext digest, filename, MIME type, and dimensions inside the message ciphertext; (6) recipients download ciphertext through the capability, verify, decrypt, and scan locally.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Object finalization precedes message acceptance, but no cross-system transaction is claimed; an unreferenced completed blob is an expiring orphan.  
   **UI/conflict:** Show local preview and upload progress but do not mark the message sent before finalization; chunk retries are idempotent, and an expired or unavailable blob displays explicitly rather than corrupting the conversation.

6. **Drain and acknowledge a device queue.**  
   **Inputs:** Device credential, group/control cursors, lease generation, requested byte limit.  
   **Authorization:** The device may fetch its immutable historical obligations even after group removal, but a revoked device cannot authenticate.  
   **Steps:** (1) Return necessary transitions and account controls before dependent application events; (2) verify context receipts, signatures, ciphertext hashes, and epoch order; (3) decrypt and durably persist valid items; (4) quarantine malformed inner events without stopping the stream; (5) update local safety state and projections; (6) acknowledge sequence ranges only after durable persistence.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Each acknowledgement-range update is atomic; payload deletion follows only after every obligation is acknowledged or expires.  
   **UI/conflict:** Show synchronization or secure-resync status; leases may duplicate delivery but event IDs deduplicate it, an expired range produces a visible history gap, and missing epoch material stalls display rather than skipping safety state.

7. **Decrypt, index, mention-alert, and search locally.**  
   **Inputs:** Authenticated ciphertext stream or a plaintext search query.  
   **Authorization:** The device must possess the relevant epoch key and historical membership context; no server request is authorized for the query.  
   **Steps:** (1) Apply membership, key, expiry, and tombstone controls first; (2) verify sender identity, role, inner schema, and encrypted policy; (3) transactionally insert the local event and update message, full-text, mention, quote, reaction, and unread projections; (4) index a mention only when its stable account ID and mention permission were valid at that sequence; (5) issue any rich notification locally; (6) execute search against local postings and filter expired results.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Each local projection update is atomic with the local event; lost indexes rebuild incrementally.  
   **UI/conflict:** Search states “complete,” “downloaded history only,” or “rebuilding”; malformed events are quarantined, unresolved quotes remain linkable, and identical replay produces identical projections.

8. **Set or remove a reaction.**  
   **Inputs:** Target message ID, emoji or tombstone, prior observed reaction operation, device counter.  
   **Authorization:** The account must have been entitled to the target and allowed to react under the encrypted policy; the service validates only current outer membership.  
   **Steps:** (1) Create an encrypted signed “set my reaction” operation; (2) send it as an ordinary opaque event; (3) recipients bind the actor to the sender’s account certificate; (4) validate target and policy locally; (5) let a causal descendant win, or for concurrent operations use service sequence followed by device/event ID; (6) retain a tombstone so delayed additions cannot resurrect a removed reaction.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Event acceptance is atomic; reaction projections converge eventually.  
   **UI/conflict:** The sender may show its reaction optimistically with a pending marker; it can change after another device synchronizes, missing targets stay unresolved, and expired targets hide all reactions.

9. **Record and publish read state.**  
   **Inputs:** Highest gap-free rendered group sequence, account receipt preference, current membership interval.  
   **Authorization:** A device may claim reads only for its own account and sequences to which that account was entitled.  
   **Steps:** (1) Clear local unread state; (2) send an encrypted self-sync frontier to sibling devices; (3) if peer receipts are enabled, coalesce an encrypted group read-through event; (4) recipients verify the signer, membership interval, and lack of skipped sequence gaps; (5) merge valid frontiers monotonically and derive per-message “seen by” lists locally.  
   **Atomic/eventual:** Local unread state and its self-sync outbox commit together; peer visibility is eventual.  
   **UI/conflict:** Local unread clears immediately, remote receipts appear only after acceptance, concurrent devices merge by maximum valid frontier, and “mark unread” remains a private reminder that cannot retract an emitted receipt.

10. **Start and enforce disappearance.**  
    **Inputs:** Encrypted timer mode/duration, event acceptance time, first qualifying render, existing expiry controls.  
    **Authorization:** Clients accept a timer or group-policy change only from a sender whose role permitted it at that sequence.  
    **Steps:** (1) For after-send, derive expiry from authoritative acceptance time; (2) for after-view, the first account device to render emits an encrypted `FirstOpened` self-sync event; (3) sibling devices adopt the earliest valid opening and optionally publish a separate receipt; (4) schedule an on-device deadline; (5) at expiry atomically destroy the message/content key, postings, owned previews, media cache, notification text, and backup eligibility; (6) persist an expiry tombstone and process it before rendering after reconnect.  
    **Atomic/eventual:** Local erasure is atomic per device and synchronizes eventually; server ciphertext follows its fixed transport retention rather than interpreting the timer.  
    **UI/conflict:** Show the exact timer and limitation; earliest valid opening wins, suspicious future clocks are clamped, expired offline content never flashes, and a later reply’s deliberate quote snapshot follows the reply’s own timer.

11. **Revoke a device across all groups.**  
    **Inputs:** Device ID, expected account roster version, surviving-device or recovery authorization.  
    **Authorization:** A surviving trusted device or recovery credential must authorize revocation.  
    **Steps:** (1) Atomically mark the device revoked, advance the account roster version, disable authentication and push, discard its undelivered queues, and append transparency metadata; (2) detect every group whose current epoch still contains that leaf; (3) reject new application events in each affected group; (4) have surviving clients generate exclude-device transitions; (5) resume each group only after its successor epoch commits.  
    **Atomic/eventual:** Account revocation is atomic; group rotations are a resumable bulk operation, but the send barrier prevents a confidentiality gap.  
    **UI/conflict:** Show affected-group progress and any paused group; duplicate revocations are idempotent, concurrent sends fail stale-context checks, and groups without an available retained committer remain paused.

12. **Dispatch generic push notifications.**  
    **Inputs:** Committed push outbox entries and device push routes.  
    **Authorization:** Only the delivery worker may lease outbox rows.  
    **Steps:** (1) Coalesce pending wake-ups per device; (2) send a fixed-schema generic token to the external push provider; (3) record only delivery codes and retry time; (4) let the device fetch and decrypt its queue; (5) construct message, reaction, receipt, or mention UI locally.  
    **Atomic/eventual:** The outbox exists atomically with event acceptance, while the external call is retried and eventually consistent.  
    **UI/conflict:** A push is never displayed as message delivery; lease generations prevent duplicate workers, and a dead push token marks only the route stale rather than revoking the device.

13. **Reclaim queues and opaque blobs periodically.**  
    **Inputs:** Acknowledged ranges, fixed 45-day queue/blob cutoff, unfinished-upload cutoff, worker fence.  
    **Authorization:** A system-only scheduled worker may apply only these metadata rules.  
    **Steps:** (1) Scan fenced batches; (2) close acknowledged delivery obligations; (3) record explicit expired ranges after 45 days; (4) delete an event payload when no live obligation remains; (5) delete unfinished uploads after 24 hours and finalized blobs after 45 days; (6) compact cursor metadata and expired outbox rows; (7) emit aggregate byte/count metrics without content-derived labels.  
    **Atomic/eventual:** Each batch is atomic and the sweep is eventually complete.  
    **UI/conflict:** Long-offline devices show gaps and request encrypted peer resynchronization; workers are idempotent, and garbage collection never guesses message type, attachment references, or semantic disappearance.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment might have:

| Measure | Realistic scale |
|---|---:|
| Registered accounts / active devices | 25 million / 60 million |
| Groups / active membership intervals | 15 million / 150 million |
| Historical membership/device intervals | 600 million |
| Opaque application events | 300 million/day, 40,000/second peak |
| Mean entitled devices per event | 14 |
| Logical device deliveries | 4.2 billion/day |
| Attachment uploads | 15 million/day, 45 TB/day padded ingress |
| Largest group | 1,000 accounts, roughly 2,400 active devices |
| Heavy endpoint history | 1 million messages, 2–10 GB local encrypted state |

The dominant cost is fan-out: content blindness prevents mention-specific or event-type-specific routing, so every current device receives and decrypts every group event. Shared ciphertext storage plus entitlement intervals can compress per-device queue rows, but network egress, acknowledgements, and generic wake-ups still scale with recipient devices.

A single 1,000-account group producing five events per second creates roughly 12,000 logical deliveries per second and needs one fenced home sequencer for membership boundaries. Reactions and receipts are full encrypted events; unbatched receipts can approach quadratic traffic, so read-through coalescing is necessary.

Membership transitions serialize a group and require cryptographic work plus delivery to its device roster. Device revocation is especially expensive for accounts in hundreds or thousands of groups. Attachment storage is once per blob, but egress multiplies by downloading devices, making ciphertext CDN caching and resumable chunks essential.

Long-offline devices extend queue retention. Blob collection cannot inspect encrypted references, and event storage cannot use semantic compaction. Full-text rebuilding costs endpoint CPU, battery, and local storage rather than server compute; a million-message desktop index can take hours unless restored from an encrypted checkpoint.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Atomic metadata and cryptographic transitions.** An ordinary mutation can update membership rows, but this product must also validate and install a client-generated successor commit, cut temporal entitlements, fence the event sequence, and create durable delivery work atomically. The acceptable concession is pausing the group until a retained device supplies a commit; declaring removal complete or permitting old-epoch sends is a cheat.

2. **First-class client-owned projections.** Search, mentions, reactions, quotes, receipts, content permissions, and disappearance are deterministic endpoint reducers over an opaque ordered stream, not server queries or upserts. Local-only search, generic push, incomplete-history labels, and encrypted checkpoints are legitimate concessions; uploading deterministic term tokens, mention bits, semantic event types, or receipt targets is a cheat.

3. **Historical per-device delivery authority.** Queue access is an immutable acceptance-time entitlement interval, not a check of current membership at fetch time. A 45-day queue limit and 1,000-member group cap are reasonable concessions; using one account cursor, silently dropping accepted events, or recalculating recipients after a membership change is a cheat.

4. **Client-assisted device revocation.** A server cannot create an unknown successor content secret, so revoking one device across hundreds of groups requires client-generated transitions and a metadata-enforced send barrier. A visible temporary security pause is honest; blocking only the lost device’s network access while continuing under a key it knows is a cheat.

5. **Privacy-compatible operations and retention.** Observability is limited to outer error codes, versions, byte counts, queue latency, and aggregate delivery state; blobs and events expire by fixed custody rules rather than semantic inspection. Client-enforced disappearance and explicit user disclosure for abuse reports are honest concessions; claiming remote erasure, server-side moderation without disclosure, or plaintext support access is a cheat.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice includes independent device credentials, account-signed device enrollment, transparency logs, audited asynchronous group epochs, per-sender ratchets, client-encrypted attachments, transactional outboxes, idempotent store-and-forward delivery, generic push, local full-text indexing, encrypted receipts, and honest-client disappearing messages.

The more novel synthesis is the exact transaction binding server-authoritative membership to a client-generated cryptographic transition, the universal opaque event stream with client-enforced group rules, audience confirmation for stale drafts and historical quotes, account-wide after-view convergence, compressed but semantically per-device queues, and send barriers during cross-group device revocation.

Those pieces require cryptographic review, failure-injection testing, large-group simulation, and user research around pauses and historical-queue semantics. No part of the design claims that the service can prove an authorized member did not leak a key, force a malicious recipient to erase plaintext, hide the membership graph, or guarantee availability against a hostile operator.

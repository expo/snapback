## (2) Other family’s repairs (X1R1) — still correct

A device or JS client cannot publish old-store rows as acquired/`fresh`/`complete` after a committed replacement observation: reset persists `device:backend-required` (`store_identity.rs:67`), `require_acquired` and `apply` refuse until `install_checked` clears it in the same transaction as the store/send capture (`device.rs:208–210,356–380,463–465`; `activation.rs` expected-meta compare). Retry, offline reopen, and a same-number generation do not excuse that (`store_reset/mod.rs:199–221`; `store-reset-review.test.mjs:100–111`). Another native owner’s adoption does not bless this owner’s in-memory programs (`device.rs:617–620`; `native.rs:297–319`).

Writes: known `sent_seq`/`observed_seq` retire and are not POSTed to B; unknown/unsent keep ids with predictions cleared (`store_identity.rs:70–81`; review tests at `store-reset-review.test.mjs:223–248`, 0 known POSTs / 1 unknown). `network()` still serializes mutate POST/settle with sync (`local.ts:268–271`). Orphan import compares persisted source and destination identities: foreign known receipts stay out of B’s outbox; unknown transfers without predictions; unresolved known receipts remain in the orphan store until the destination is identified (`web.ts:280–306`; tests `:155–220`).

Legacy fallback is bound to *that request’s* watermark and the captured persisted watermark/send revision (`store_identity.rs:36–52`). A delayed page after another owner advanced is `E_GENERATION` and keeps retained history (`native.rs:265–286`). A current identity-free reply behind its own requested watermark still resets (`:287–294`). First identification of a legacy acquired device still drops left-group history (documented; `store_reset/mod.rs:254–271`).

Legitimate same store is not a replacement: `INSERT OR IGNORE` on writable open (`store.rs:158–159`, also `Store::memory` via `prepare`); reconnect test proves no extra sync; program adoption / clocks / huge watermarks keep the id (`store_reset/mod.rs:275–297`). A matching id with a *lower* head is the restore halt, not a false `--fresh`.

Same-store client busy-loop: restore stops `follow` / `syncOnce` / `flush`. Matching identity with equal head waits on `*seq == since` until a commit or deadline — that is the ordinary long poll, not a spin. `*seq != since` returns once, then either restore-halt, replacement sync, or catch-up.

Residual, not a reconstruction of the GPT holes: `admit` (`device.rs:685–709`) still queues locally after restore because the halt is on reads and `begin_send`, not admission. Those entries never POST (`flush` and `begin_send` stop). Old clients that omit `store_id` still present a foreign `after` (`serve.rs:1025`) — the original Low additive leftover; new clients and the paging tests send the id and B clears `after`.

## Verdict: SOUND WITH MAJOR ISSUES

Revision 1 resolves two of my three round-1 findings on the mechanism: admission facts are read at S through the recorder with labeled entries, and the oracle's reference is now the seam's `run` with a transcript and publication members replayed as members. The commutative-write class (§4.5) is the right idea but does not yet reach the workload it was added for: the surface form of a maintain is a column on the owner row, which the engine rewrites as a whole image on every source write, and the boundary recorder cannot tell a program's read of that row from a read of the total. Separately, the per-member time rule (§6) makes any member that is overtaken during its execution conflict regardless of data, which under load pushes the longest programs in place. Both are fixable without changing the direction.

## New fundamental and major findings

### N1. §4.5 does not reach the maintained-column workload, and §4.1 still blocks its allocations — MAJOR — committer §4.1, §4.5; overlay §4; read sets §2, §3, §7

Four gaps, each verified against the code:

1. **The maintain surface is a column on the owner row, and the engine rewrites the owner image.** A `maintained` column declared on a table lowers to a hidden target table plus a private mirror into the owner (`snapback4-lang/src/bind/columns.rs:84–97`). `apply_aggregate` updates the hidden `value` row (`engine/maintains.rs:586–619`) and then calls `mirror_column`, which `get`s the owner row and `put`s a new whole image with the column replaced (`engine/columns.rs:78–83`). So with `rooms.messageCount`, every message insert's footprint carries `Row(rooms, id)`. Any concurrent member that reads the room row for any reason (a rule, a membership check, the room name in a result) has a `Row(rooms, id)` entry (read sets §3) and conflicts. §4.5 turns the hidden target's `value` into a delta; it says nothing about the owner image, which is where the contention actually lands. UNVERIFIED how often a message program reads its room row; it is application-specific, but it is the ordinary shape.
2. **"When the program does not read the total" has no decision procedure at the boundary.** `get` returns the whole image; which fields the interpreter then evaluates is invisible to a recorder at `Rows`, and read sets §7 rejects recording in the `Runner`. So the recorder cannot distinguish "read the room" from "read the room's total".
3. **The maintain machinery's own reads would be recorded as the program's.** `apply_aggregate`'s `get` of the target (`maintains.rs:586`) and `mirror_column`'s `get` of the owner (`columns.rs:78`) cross the same boundary. Recorded as `Row` reads, they make every pair of source writers conflict even when neither program looks at the total, which is the serial rate again. The documents do not say how these calls are excluded.
4. **Allocations always reach a key.** Schema validation requires the `$sequence:<column>` index `(parent, column)` for every sequence column (`schema.rs:1149`), so every allocation placeholder is in an index key. §4.1 makes a placeholder in a key ineligible "unless its provisional encoding is proven to sort exactly as its resolved value would. Until then" it goes in place. No document gives the proof, so as written §4.1 forecloses §4.5's allocation class for every sequence table, and the schema-contention cell would measure in-place execution.

Direction. (a) Split footprints: `Row(table, id)` for a replaced image, `Field(table, id, column)` for a delta on a maintained column; the hidden target's `value` and the owner mirror both become `Field` deltas, applied at commit by rewriting the current image and re-deriving its index keys with `stored_index_keys`. (b) Let the interpreter set one bit, not record reads: when it evaluates a field listed in `table.maintained` on a fetched row, it marks that `Field` on the row's `Row` entry. A `Row` read then conflicts with `Row` footprints, and with `Field` footprints only for marked columns. That is one scope flag like `maintain_prepaid` (`write_target.rs:383–386`), not Runner-side recording, and it is what makes "did not read the total" decidable. (c) Run the maintain machinery under a scope the handle sees (`maintain_scratch` already brackets it, `maintains.rs:276`), during which its `get`/`put` of target and owner become the delta and are not recorded. (d) State the order-preservation argument for monotone allocations: the provisional value is the counter at S plus k, the resolved value is at least that, both sort above every key under the parent at S, and within-program order is kept. Then §4.1 reads "a placeholder in a key is eligible when it is a monotone allocation". (e) Make the S1a cell a maintained column on the parent with C writers, since that is the surface form, not only a global maintain.

### N2. The per-member time rule conflicts on every overtaking, independent of data — MAJOR — committer §4.2, §6, §3.3–§3.4

The mechanism: effective time is fixed before the program runs, `T = max(admission now, floor at S)` (§4.2 step 5, §6). Revisions are assigned when a member reaches the committer after execution (§3.3, §3.4). A member is accepted only if `T` is at or after the pin of every earlier-revision member, whose pin is its own `T` (§6). So consider member M and any member X admitted after M's snapshot opened and committed before M is validated. X's `T` is at least its admission time, which is later than M's floor and M's admission, so X's pin exceeds M's `T` and M conflicts. That is every overtaking during M's execution-plus-queue window, with no data in common. The rerun does not escape: its fresh floor is the pin at S', and any X admitted after S' and committed first overtakes it again, so under steady load a member that conflicts once conflicts on each rerun and goes in place after `c = 2`. Long programs are overtaken most, so the rule biases the serial section toward the costliest members. UNVERIFIED: the rate, which depends on execution-time variance that no S1a cell measures.

The rule is also stricter than the reference. `pin_writer_time` pins only when some table has `not_before` (`engine/time_audience.rs:7–21`); for every other schema the reference records the request time and enforces nothing, and the parent's §6.4 scopes the invariant to `pin_writer_time`. Revision 0 had a batch-maximum pin; revision 1 tightened it for publication ordering, which the data entries already enforce: a member that read a due row as pending conflicts with the publication member's footprint (`Time(audience, key)`, `Row`), and §6 orders the publication first.

Direction. Assign `at` at commit as `max(T, pin)`, so monotonicity holds by construction, and validate time-dependence as a predicate: record the member's time boundary B, the minimum of the next expiry among examined rows (the `Runner` already keeps `next_expiry`, `runner.rs:134`), the pending opening of any examined or written row, and the session's `expires_at`; accept iff `at < B`. Values derived from `now` that reach a row, an effect deadline or the pending index become placeholders resolved to `at`, with the taint rule for `now` in comparisons. Scope the pin to schemas with `not_before`, as the reference does. At minimum, sort each drained batch by effective time before assigning revisions, which removes within-batch overtaking but not cross-batch. Add an S1a cell with mixed program lengths so the rate is measured.

## Small notes

1. **Allocations in the oracle** (§3.5, §4.5). Allocation and delta resolutions are listed as transcript inputs while database facts are re-read. Replay should re-derive `previous + 1` from the re-read `_sequences` (`sequences.rs:212–233`) and compare with the transcript's resolution, not inject it; otherwise an in-memory counter that diverged after a batch rollback or takeover is invisible to the oracle.
2. **Serial-section reads** (§3.6, read sets §4). The batch's receipt re-check and the predicate re-evaluation "against the current value" must come from the window's maps, with per-key delta sums kept since the oldest live snapshot, or each is a round trip inside the serial section the design exists to empty.
3. **The gate is promised, not revised.** The schema-contention and effect-per-mutation cells appear in §2.3, §8.6 and §11, but LLP 3000.009.001.002 §9 was not changed, so the sizing rule the build is gated on does not yet carry them.
4. **Transition-effect reads have no entry kind** (overlay §3; read sets §3). `narrowed::reserved` (`store/narrowed.rs:213–222`), `_backfill_active`/`_backfill_inputs` (`store/backfills.rs:545–556`) and `dormant::enabled` are read by the shared write preparation. They are activation-keyed, so say they map to the schema-generation wildcard; otherwise the K1 census fails on them with no mapping to give.
5. **Unkeyed program members.** A mutation's `op` id is optional (`serve.rs:1405`), and an unkeyed member commits in a single-member batch (§3.4). State whether first-party clients always send ids; if not, those members pay a flush each.

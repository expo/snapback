**The lifetime rule holds for authoritative server state under the specified checks.** My attempted counterexamples resolve as follows:

- **Deletes, jobs and same-mutation writes:** `insert parent; insert child; delete parent` refuses because the reverse probe sees the uncommitted child. Deleting the child first permits deletion. System authority cannot bypass the structural check. Inserting a child after deleting its parent fails the live-parent guard.
- **Expiry and three levels:** With G’s deadline 100, P’s authored deadline 200 or absent, and C’s authored deadline 300, both descendants expire at 100. A crucial qualification is already in §4.2: **P’s authored expiry must be immutable once C captures P’s effective deadline.** The permission for mutable leaf expiry does not permit shortening a middle parent underneath its descendants.
- **Sweep and same-id replacement:** Physical children block reuse even after logical expiry. Child-first sweeping removes their contributions before replacement becomes possible. The replacement therefore cannot encounter the previous tenant’s expired population through page charges or live aggregate recomputation.
- **Upserts:** Existing-row patches retain the reference and captures. An expired target takes the insertion path and must establish a live parent again; changing its authored deadline cannot silently resurrect an orphan.
- **Seeds, imports and restores:** Ancestor-first capture against final batch images closes ordering holes. Imports cannot replace captured parent inputs while physical children remain. Restores preserve a coherent graph rather than repairing it by recapture.
- **Activation:** Previous-schema expiry is checked before projection and recapture, preventing resurrection. Initial audience correspondence is checked before old fields disappear. Captures precede validation; derived state and the generation publish atomically.

Effective expiry also preserves the existing distinction in LLP 3000.007 §20.9.3: expired rows stop contributing to **live answers**, while physical contributions remain until removal. Maintained columns continue to reject effectively expiring sources.

**That invariant does not literally hold for every device-stored image.** Independent logs and retained history can leave a locally live child without its parent, or alongside a replacement parent with different fields. Revision 6 explicitly accommodates this: finite deadlines use the advancing expiry clock; mismatched current captures cannot borrow the replacement parent’s verdict; retained rows follow their separate policy. Parent authorization still supplies no child-coverage proof. This is a sound distinction between server integrity and replica state.

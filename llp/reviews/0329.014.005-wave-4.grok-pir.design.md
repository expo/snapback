### 1. Product contract

Cove is a phone-number messenger in the Signal class: people find which of their address-book contacts are on the network, exchange end-to-end encrypted messages (including groups, receipts, and typing), and recover an account onto a new device. The operator runs storage, fan-out, spam limits, and recovery — and is assumed to record every disk read, cache key, replica request, invalidation, and audit line. Users use Cove *because* that operator still does not learn which numbers were queried or which mailbox was fetched, only that a padded query of a standard shape happened.

The people in the contract are: an account holder (one E.164 number, one `account_id`, up to five linked devices); the contacts in their phone book, including people who are not on Cove; the operator and its replicas, caches, pagers, and staff tools; an SMS/carrier verifier used only at registration; and an abuse reviewer who sees a sender identity only after the recipient files a report.

User-visible promises:

1. Installing Cove and granting address-book access tells the account holder which of *their* numbers currently have Cove accounts and the identity keys to start a session. The operator does not learn those numbers, including the ones that are not registered.
2. Discovery never takes a shortcut through the registration table. Registered and unregistered numbers are queried with the same padded private-retrieval shape, so an operator who already knows the registered set still cannot see which registered users sit in a particular address book.
3. Opening the inbox, pulling a linked desktop, or background-syncing mail does not tell the operator which mailbox was read, whether it was empty, how many envelopes it held, or which account the device belongs to.
4. Sending does not tell the operator who sent (sealed sender) or which mailbox was written (oblivious drop). Group sends, delivery receipts, typing, and react-with-emoji use that same drop path; there is no server-side “user U is in conversation C” event.
5. A delivered envelope does not live on the server forever. Consuming it does not create a per-mailbox delete or “mark-read” row the operator can tail.
6. Other users do not see the holder’s phone number by default (they talk to `account_id` plus identity key). The operator does see the number at SMS registration and may ban it. That is not a license to treat registration as a discovery index.
7. Recycled numbers are a different person. If a number’s identity key changes, the client shows a break and does not send into the old session.
8. Spam is limited. Limits do not produce an operator-visible social graph, a per-destination delivery log, or a “who queried this number” counter.
9. An 8+ character registration-lock PIN recovers identity material onto a new device. The operator does not learn the PIN, the backup plaintext, or which backup slot was read. Five failed PIN attempts per UTC day lock guessing without naming the slot in logs.
10. Up to five linked devices can discover, send, and fetch concurrently. Two fetches cannot be joined by the operator to conclude they are the same mailbox. Devices reconcile mail with each other through the mailbox, not through an operator-visible device roster lookup.
11. Offline, the holder reads local history and queues sends. Discovery results are stamped with a directory epoch: after 24 hours they display as stale; after 7 days they are unusable until a successful private refresh. The app never marks a queued send “delivered” from local optimism.
12. Presence is not a product surface. Opening Cove is operator-indistinguishable from the device’s scheduled cover retrievals of the same shape and size. Push, if present, is a contentless wakeup with no mailbox, sender, unread count, or badge number.
13. Optional usernames can be reserved and looked up. Looking up a username is a private retrieval; the operator does not learn the name queried. Uniqueness is enforced without a staff “is this name taken?” console over raw names.
14. Account deletion removes the number from the discoverable directory at the next directory epoch (≤ 10 minutes), stops issuing send tokens, and leaves existing envelopes to expire. Deletion is not a per-mailbox wipe the operator can watch.
15. A recipient may report one envelope. Only then may an abuse reviewer unseal that envelope’s sender certificate. Reports do not grant a mailbox-tail or a contact-list export.
16. Official clients keep address-book hashes, epoch results, mailbox capabilities, and message plaintext in a store keyed by the registration lock. Sign-out destroys the key. A stolen device that is still unlocked is out of scope.
17. Staff tools can see aggregate rates (queries per epoch, bytes stored, tokens redeemed) and the registration table for bans. They cannot open “lookup this number,” “fetch this mailbox,” “tail this user,” or “who contacted whom.” Those operations do not exist.
18. Two devices of the same account may send and fetch at once without dropping envelopes. Retries use client envelope ids. Conflicts resolve by cell generation, never by device clock.
19. PIR responses and drops are constant-size and issued on a coarse time grid (200 ms), so hit/miss, fullness, and “not found” are not distinguishable to the operator by length, status code, or timing class.
20. The holder can use Cove on a phone and a desktop without the operator learning that those two sessions are one person from the fetch path. Control-plane actions (link-device, token issuance) are separate and expected to reveal that the account is active that day.

Concurrency, offline, and multi-device: every device independently private-retrieves the directory and its own mailbox; there is no shared server cursor that names the mailbox. Offline queues carry the directory epoch and identity-key version they observed and are refused if the recipient’s identity rotated. Linked-device join is an explicit control-plane act, not something inferred from fetch traffic.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. The operator’s readable logs, replicas, invalidation streams, and caches record that a discovery or fetch of a standard padded shape occurred, and never which slot, number, username, mailbox, or backup was addressed. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
2. Discovery of registered and unregistered numbers is indistinguishable on the wire and in operator logs; the registration table is not a discovery path. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
3. A mailbox fetch does not require an account session, does not name a mailbox in plaintext, and does not change which physical rows a replica or auditor attributes to the caller. `[SECRECY]`
4. A drop does not name sender or destination in any operator-visible field; authorization to drop is possession of an unspent anonymous token. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
5. Sealed-sender identity is unavailable to the operator except for one reported envelope, after the recipient authenticates a report. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
6. Consuming, expiring, or compacting mail does not produce a per-mailbox write log, a per-cell rebuild log, or a different I/O set than a cover write of the same epoch. `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
7. Every directory record is an interval: it is current only from `valid_from_epoch` to `valid_until_epoch`. A boolean “this number is on Cove” is not a stored fact. `[TIME] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
8. A recycled E.164 receives a new `account_id`, `phone_id`, and identity key; clients that still hold the old key treat the number as a new person. `[TIME] [OTHER-PERSON DATA] [SECRECY]`
9. Discovery results are true as of a published directory epoch, not “now.” A result older than 24 hours is stale; older than 7 days is unauthorized to send against. `[TIME] [SECRECY]`
10. Per-account discovery budget and per-account send-token budget are the only server-side numeric limits that name a person; they count padded batches and tokens, never which numbers or mailboxes were used. `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
11. A destination mailbox has a fixed number of fixed-size cells. Overflow drops the envelope for the sender; it does not create an operator-visible “mailbox full” event that names the mailbox. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
12. Two non-colluding retrieval servers, or a single-server scheme whose work is always a full pass over the published epoch image, are the only permitted readers of directory, mailbox, username, and backup stores. A point-get by slot index is a protocol violation even for staff. `[SECRECY]`
13. Replication and invalidation of those stores move whole epoch images or whole shards on a published schedule, never “the pages that were just read.” `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
14. A linked device may act as the account for token issuance, PIN recovery, and link/unlink, and may not fetch another device’s mailbox without that device’s capability. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
15. Official clients may show cached plaintext only while the local store key lives; they may not re-query the operator by account id to “refresh this inbox.” `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
16. Cover retrievals are the same operation, size class, and timing grid as real retrievals. A scheduler that skips cover when “nothing happened” is a protocol violation. `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
17. Username uniqueness is true of the published username-epoch image. A failed reservation does not tell the operator which name collided. `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
18. Abuse review may see reporter `account_id`, reported envelope ciphertext, unsealed sender `account_id` for that envelope, and the report category. It may not see the reporter’s address book, the reporter’s other fetches, or a live tail of either mailbox. `[SECRECY] [OTHER-PERSON DATA]`
19. PIN guess limits are enforced on a blinded recovery identifier, not by opening the backup slot and not by logging the PIN or the slot index. `[SECRECY] [TIME]`
20. Envelope, certificate, token, and cache lifetimes are server or epoch clocks. Device clocks do not authorize a send, a recovery, or a “still registered” display. `[TIME]`

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **The read set is the query.** *(visibility; the axis)*

   - **Wrong version:** Encrypt envelope bodies and hash phone numbers, then `get directory where e164_hash = H(number)` and `get mailbox where mailbox_id = me`. Record the keys for OCC, live invalidation, cache, audit, and replication. Or “optimize” discovery as `accounts.e164 IN (address book)` for numbers the operator already knows, and use private retrieval only for the rest.
   - **Why it is wrong:** Users care about the *set they asked*, especially the unregistered remainder of the address book and the mailbox they opened. Hashes of E.164 numbers are brute-forceable. An invalidation or replica receipt that names a key *is* the contact list or the inbox identity. Splitting registered vs unregistered queries leaks the registered subset of the graph to anyone who can see which path was taken. `independentOf`-style output filters stop values from appearing in results; they do not stop the trace.
   - **Observation:** After a 280-number discovery, dump the query log, cache keys, OCC read set, and replica traffic. If any hash, slot, shard id, or `accounts` primary key from that set is present — or if the registered 40 took a different code path than the unregistered 240 — the product is already broken. Confirm by running the same padded private retrieval against a known-unregistered number and a known-registered one and diffing operator bytes.

2. **Fetch-and-ack re-leaks the mailbox.** *(concurrency + visibility; the PIR analog of active-vs-historical membership)*

   - **Wrong version:** Private-read the mailbox, then `delete envelope where id in fetched` or `update mailbox set ack_cursor = n where mailbox_id = me`, and replicate that write by key. Optionally skip the delete and instead “mark delivered” so storage shrinks.
   - **Why it is wrong:** Ordinary mail *is* a destructive read. Users want the operator not to keep a durable copy after the device has it, *and* not to learn which inbox was emptied. The ack is a write whose access pattern is the fetch the private read just hid. Compacting only dirty pages, or rebuilding only shards that received acks, is the same leak at coarser grain. A boolean `consumed` on an envelope is not an interval and still names a row.
   - **Observation:** Deliver one envelope to Bob, let his phone fetch, and watch the write log. A key-addressed delete, a shard rebuild set of size 1, or a different I/O pattern than a cover consume in the same epoch identifies Bob’s mailbox. A design that “solves” this by never deleting (so the operator keeps mail forever) fails promise 5; one that deletes by key fails promise 3.

3. **Refusals, sizes, counts, and timing as membership oracles.** *(visibility)*

   - **Wrong version:** Return not-found for empty slots, a shorter body for empty mailboxes, HTTP 429 “too many queries for this number,” or a distinct error when a username is taken. Let free-text staff search answer “is this number registered?” Let the client show “4 of 12 contacts are on Cove” from a server-computed count.
   - **Why it is wrong:** Hit/miss *is* the discovery answer. Rate-limit text that names the target tells the operator (and a probing client) which index was tried. A visibility-filtered count of “how many of my contacts registered” computed on the server requires the contact set. Timing and length classes are just another channel. Staff search over `accounts.e164` is a discovery API with a chair.
   - **Observation:** Probe a registered number and an unregistered one, a full mailbox and an empty one, a taken username and a free one, and a discovery batch that is over budget. If status, byte length, latency bucket, or log line differs, or if a support tool answers the probe without a private retrieval, secrecy failed. If the server returns an integer count of matches, the operator had the set.

4. **Spam limits that name destination or sender.** *(authority + visibility)*

   - **Wrong version:** To stop floods, `count(*) from drops where dest = Bob and hour = H` and refuse over 100. To stop senders, require a logged-in sender id on every drop. To stop directory scraping, log `queries_by_number[hash]++`.
   - **Why it is wrong:** The anti-abuse table *is* the social graph and the query graph. Sealed sender plus destination-named rate limits still tell the operator who got mail and when, which joins to “a fetch happened 4 seconds later” and re-identifies the PIR. Sender-named drops retract sealed sender. Per-number query counters retract private discovery. Users want both “I’m not flooded” and “the operator cannot see who talks to me.”
   - **Observation:** Flood Bob, or have Alice message Bob once. If a dashboard can chart Bob’s inbound rate, Alice’s outbound graph, or “queries for +1555…,” the limit is the leak. Correct limits consume per-account padded discovery budget and unlinkable send tokens, and drop overflow inside a fixed-size mailbox without a named full-event.

5. **Treating registration as a boolean and merging by “last seen.”** *(temporal)*

   - **Wrong version:** Store `is_registered` on the client (or server) and overwrite it whenever any device gets a result. Accept sends against a year-old discovery. Re-registering a recycled number keeps the old `account_id` “so their friends still find them.”
   - **Why it is wrong:** “On the network” is true *at an epoch*. Unregister, deletion, PIN-lock, and number recycle all end an interval. Last-write-wins across two offline devices will resurrect a dead registration or bury a live one depending on clock skew. Keeping `account_id` across recycle silently delivers mail for Alice to the stranger who got her old number — other people’s data, wrongly addressed.
   - **Observation:** Bob unregisters at epoch 50; device 1 (offline since 49) still has him live; device 2 refreshes at 51 and sees empty; they sync. If Bob is live, or if a queued send to the old identity key is marked sent, the interval was collapsed. Recycle the number to Carol and watch Alice’s chat; any message that opens under Bob’s name fails the product.

6. **Linking devices, push, and “only fetch when dirty.”** *(merge + concurrency + authority)*

   - **Wrong version:** Authenticate as Alice, fetch “her” mailbox, push “new mail” to her device tokens, and skip cover polls when no drop was logged for her. Merge two devices’ inboxes by server cursor `mailbox.alice.seq`. Let desktop fetch on behalf of phone.
   - **Why it is wrong:** An authenticated fetch tells the operator who is reading even if the inner index is hidden. Event-driven push and dirty-bit polling reintroduce destination-named writes as presence. A server cursor is a mailbox identifier. Fetch-on-behalf is authority the operator can impersonate for support. Two offline devices that both consume the same cell need idempotent oblivious zeros and device-list retrieval that is itself private; last-seq-wins drops mail.
   - **Observation:** Idle a device on cover-only traffic, then open the inbox; the operator should not see a new shape. Deliver mail and watch whether a push token bound to `accounts` fires. Fetch from phone and desktop; a shared cursor, a support “impersonate fetch,” or a lost envelope on simultaneous consume exposes the miss.

### 4. Data model

Identifiers are 128-bit UUIDs unless noted. Operator time is UTC milliseconds. Directory, mailbox, username, and backup stores are **epoch images**: a published, immutable snapshot plus a pending mix. Point reads by business key against those images are forbidden. The registration table is **not** referenced by discovery, fetch, or drop.

**Control plane (operator-visible, not a discovery index)**

| Table | Fields, keys, relationships | Lifecycle |
|---|---|---|
| `accounts` | `account_id UUID PK`, `e164 TEXT UNIQUE`, `phone_id UUID UNIQUE`, `status ENUM(active, locked, pending_deletion, deleted)`, `registration_lock_verifier BYTES(32)`, `backup_capability_hash BYTES(32)`, `discover_budget_day DATE`, `discover_budget_used INT`, `discover_budget_cap INT` (default 10000), `token_budget_day DATE`, `token_budget_issued INT`, `token_budget_cap INT` (default 2000), `directory_pending_op BYTES`, `created_at`, `deleted_at` | One row per number. `e164` exists for SMS, bans, and recycle. Invariant 2: no discovery/fetch/drop path reads this table by `e164`. |
| `account_events` | `event_id PK`, `account_id`, `kind ENUM(register, re_register, lock_change, delete_requested, deleted, ban)`, `directory_epoch_at_event INT`, `at` | Append-only. |
| `devices` | `device_id PK`, `account_id FK`, `device_attestation BYTES`, `link_epoch INT`, `status ENUM(active, revoked)`, `created_at`, `revoked_at`; unique `(account_id, device_id)` | Max 5 active per account. **No push token column.** No mailbox_id column. |

Logical contents of a **directory cell** (parsed only by a client that already knows the E.164): `e164_check BYTES(16)` (keyed hash, for collision check), `account_id`, `phone_id`, `identity_key BYTES(32)`, `signed_prekey_ref BYTES(32)`, `device_mailboxes[5] of {mailbox_index UINT64, mailbox_write_epoch UINT32}` or empty, `valid_from_epoch INT`, `valid_until_epoch INT` (0 = still current), `record_version INT`. Dummy cells are random bytes with a fail checksum.

Logical contents of a **mailbox cell**: packed envelopes `{envelope_id UUID, client_envelope_id UUID, sealed_sender_blob BYTES, payload_ciphertext BYTES, created_epoch INT, expires_epoch INT}` plus padding to 4096. Empty cell = random pad + fail checksum. `expires_epoch` is created+14d.

**Derived, never stored as operator-addressable facts:** current-vs-historical registration (interval vs epoch); mailbox occupancy; unread counts; “how many of my contacts are on Cove”; whether a fetch was cover or real; which shards were “dirty.” Clients derive occupancy and unread locally after decryption.

|---|---|---|

| `local_mailboxes` | `mailbox_capability BYTES(32)`, `mailbox_index UINT64`, `last_fetch_epoch`, `seen_envelope_ids` | Seen-ids are the consume cursor. |
| `local_outbox` | `client_envelope_id PK`, `dest_mailbox_index`, `payload`, `observed_identity_key`, `observed_directory_epoch`, `state ENUM(queued, dropping, dropped, refused)` | Queued offline up to 7 days. |
| `local_cover_schedule` | next fire, size class | Jittered. |

Time-bounded values: sender certs 24h; send tokens 24h; envelopes 14 days; discovery display 24h stale / 7d dead; SMS proof 10 minutes; recovery session 10 minutes; pending deletion 7 days before directory tombstone; PIN-guess window UTC day.

### 5. The hard operations

**1. Register or re-register a number** (spans SMS/carrier).

- **Inputs:** E.164, SMS proof id, device attestation, identity key, registration-lock verifier or PIN, this device’s mailbox capability.
- **Authorization:** Carrier/SMS verifier asserts the number in the last 10 minutes. If `accounts.e164` exists and a lock is set, the caller must pass the PIN check (op 7’s guess counter). No staff impersonation.
- **Steps:** (1) Verify SMS proof with the carrier; do not store the SMS body. (2) If new: insert `accounts` (`status=active`, budgets 0 for today). If recycle of a deleted number whose 7-day hold elapsed: insert a **new** `account_id` / `phone_id` / identity key; do not reuse. If re-register of an active locked account: require PIN, rotate device set to this device only if the caller requested a reset. (3) Enqueue an oblivious directory write of the new interval record (`valid_from_epoch = current+1`, `valid_until=0`) and, if replacing, a tombstone of the old record (`valid_until = current+1`). (4) Enqueue an oblivious mailbox-image write that allocates 32 empty cells at `mailbox_index` derived from the capability. (5) Issue a 24h sender cert (blind serial) and today’s remaining send tokens (op 6). (6) Append `account_events`. (7) Return account_id, phone_id, current directory and mailbox epoch ids. Never return other accounts’ slots.
- **Atomic:** account row, event, mix enqueues, token issuance counters.
- **Eventual:** directory/mailbox visibility at next epoch publish (≤ 10 min directory, ≤ 60s mailbox). SMS is external and not in the DB transaction.
- **UI:** “Verifying…” then “Setting up.” No contact list until op 2. Do not optimistic-show friends.
- **Conflict:** Double register of the same number serializes on `e164` unique. The second either joins as device 2 (if PIN and link requested) or is refused. A late SMS callback after delete-request does not recreate a deleted account.

**2. Discover contacts** (bulk private read).

- **Inputs:** Authenticated `account_id`, client-computed list of up to 10,000 E.164s, requested size class `s/m/l/xl` corresponding to 512 / 2048 / 8192 / 24576 slot fetches, current `directory_epoch`.
- **Authorization:** Account `active`. Remaining `discover_budget_used + class_cost ≤ cap` for UTC day. Class costs: s=500, m=2000, l=8000, xl=10000. The operator must not see the E.164s; they never leave the device in the clear.
- **Steps:** (1) Client maps each E.164 to three cuckoo slot indices and pads the index list with random dummy indices to the size class. (2) Client submits one batched private retrieval against `directory_slots` at the stated epoch to both retrieval servers (or one SimplePIR query vector). (3) Server(s) run a full-pass retrieval whose I/O set is the entire epoch image; append `query_receipts` with kind=directory, size class, epoch, ephemeral connect id — no indices. (4) Charge `discover_budget_used`. (5) Client decrypts/parses cells it can recognize via `e164_check`, ignores dummies and collisions that fail the check, and upserts `local_contacts` with the returned interval and identity key. (6) If a stored identity key disagrees, mark `recycled` and freeze the old conversation.
- **Atomic:** budget charge + receipt. The retrieval itself is a pure read of an immutable epoch image.
- **Eventual:** a registration from op 1 may not appear until the next directory epoch; the UI says “updated as of [epoch time].”
- **UI:** Show last local results immediately, labeled stale if epoch age > 24h. Replace in place when this op returns. Never optimistic-add a contact as “on Cove.”
- **Conflict:** Two devices discovering at once each pay budget and each write local state; merge by higher epoch, then `record_version`, never by device time. Over-budget is a **class-uniform** refusal (“try tomorrow”), not “reduce your list.”

**3. Fetch a mailbox** (private read; ordinary inbox open).

- **Inputs:** `mailbox_index` and capability (device-local), `mailbox_epoch`, size class always `m` (64 cell fetches: 32 real cells + 32 cover cells in other slots).
- **Authorization:** Possession of the capability, expressed only inside the retrieval query. **No account session.** Staff cannot pass an `account_id` to this op.
- **Steps:** (1) Client builds a private retrieval for its 32 cells plus 32 random cover cells. (2) Servers full-pass `mailbox_cells` at that epoch; append a mailbox `query_receipts` row with no index. (3) Client authenticates cells it can MAC under its capability, drops cover, decrypts envelopes, discards expired (`expires_epoch < epoch`), discards `client_envelope_id` already in `local_seen`, appends new ones to local store, updates `seen_envelope_ids`. (4) If any new envelope should leave the server, client schedules op 5 in the same mix window. (5) Repeat on a jittered 45s (foreground) / 8 min (background) timer; this *is* also the cover schedule (op 14).
- **Atomic:** none on the server beyond “answer against one epoch image.” Local insert of envelopes + seen-ids is one device transaction.
- **Eventual:** envelopes dropped in the current mix are visible next mailbox epoch (≤ 60s).
- **UI:** Cached plaintext shows immediately. New mail appears when this op returns. Never show a server-provided unread count.
- **Conflict:** Two devices fetching the same mailbox both decrypt the same cells; seen-ids are local. Consume (op 5) is idempotent zero. No server cursor.

**4. Drop a sealed envelope** (send, receipt, typing, group fan-out).

- **Inputs:** One unspent send token (Privacy-Pass style), sealed-sender blob, payload ciphertext, destination `mailbox_index`, dest cell hint (random in 0–31), `client_envelope_id`, observed recipient identity key and directory epoch.
- **Authorization:** Token redeems against `token_redemptions` (unspent nonce, unexpired epoch). No account session on this path. Client must refuse locally if `local_contacts.state` is not `live` or directory epoch is > 7 days old.
- **Steps:** (1) If local identity/epoch check fails, stop; do not hit the network. (2) Redeem token: insert nonce; duplicate nonce is a no-op success (idempotent retry). (3) Enqueue `pending_mix` item for mailbox store: an oblivious write that, at apply time, loads the dest cell, rejects if `client_envelope_id` already packed, packs the envelope or fails-full internally, increments `generation`. (4) For a group of size G, the client repeats steps 2–3 G times (G tokens, G mix items). (5) Return `accepted_for_epoch = current_mailbox_epoch+1` or `full` as a **client-decrypted** apply receipt carried in the next fetch, not as a named error. A uniform `ok` on the drop socket is required; fullness is learned by the sender’s own later fetch of a confirmation cell, or by packing locally in a write-PIR that returns a private result.
   Practical private result: two-server write returns a client-unblinded 1-bit “packed / not packed” that servers cannot decode (XOR of shares). Servers log only `ok`.
- **Atomic:** nonce spend + mix enqueue.
- **Eventual:** visible to recipient at next mailbox epoch.
- **UI:** Bubble shows “sending” until `ok`; “sent” after mix enqueue ack; “delivered” only from an E2EE receipt envelope later fetched. Never “delivered” from HTTP 200.
- **Conflict:** Two senders packing the same cell: higher `generation` wins that cell; loser retries a different cell in the next epoch with a **new** token if the first pack bit was 0; if the retry would double-spend a `client_envelope_id` already packed, it no-ops. Device clocks are unused.

**5. Consume envelopes** (oblivious rewrite; the destructive half of fetch).

- **Inputs:** Same capability as op 3, list of `envelope_id`s the device now holds, cover no-ops to pad to 32 cell writes.
- **Authorization:** Capability inside the write. No account session.
- **Steps:** (1) Pad the write set to all 32 of the mailbox’s cells: real zeros for cells whose envelopes are all seen-and-held or expired; identity rewrite (same ciphertext, `generation+1`) for the rest; this is indistinguishable from a full-mailbox rewrite. (2) Enqueue one mix item that replaces those 32 cells in the next epoch image. (3) Always do this on the consume schedule even if nothing was seen (cover consume), at the same size.
- **Atomic:** mix enqueue of a fixed 32-cell rewrite.
- **Eventual:** cells empty for everyone at next epoch. A device that has not yet fetched may miss mail: therefore a device may consume a cell only after **all** linked devices have advertised (via E2EE device-sync envelopes in the same mailbox) that they hold those ids, or after 7 days, whichever first. Until then consume is cover-only rewrite.
- **UI:** Local delete may be immediate. Server occupancy is not a UI surface.
- **Conflict:** Concurrent consumes: both write zeros; generation compare-and-set, retry next epoch. Never resurrect a zeroed envelope.

**6. Issue a day’s send tokens.**

- **Inputs:** Authenticated `account_id`, VOPRF blinding of `count` token nonces, `day`.
- **Authorization:** `accounts.status=active`, `count + token_budget_issued ≤ cap` for that UTC day. Linked device session.
- **Steps:** (1) Recompute day rollover. (2) Run VOPRF sign on blinded nonces; increment `token_budget_issued`. (3) Insert `token_issuance`. (4) Optionally issue a fresh sender cert (op 1 step 5).
- **Atomic:** budget + issuance row + VOPRF outputs.
- **Eventual:** none. Tokens redeem in op 4.
- **UI:** none, besides “cannot send, daily limit” as a uniform local state when cap is 0 remaining.
- **Conflict:** Two devices issuing: serialize on account row. Sum cannot exceed cap.

**7. Recover account from PIN** (spans the recovery/HSM or VOPRF rate-limit service).

- **Inputs:** E.164, SMS proof, PIN, device attestation.
- **Authorization:** SMS proof of the number plus PIN. Guess counter: 5 per UTC day per `backup_capability_hash`, maintained by a VOPRF/HSM that does not log PIN or slot.
- **Steps:** (1) Verify SMS. (2) Derive `backup_slot` from PIN+e164 client-side; submit a **backup-store** private retrieval of size class `s` (identical to a dummy backup poll). (3) HSM increments guess count on the blinded verifier; on fail, return the same padded miss as an empty slot (client distinguishes by MAC). On fifth fail, set `accounts.status=locked` without logging the slot. (4) On success, client unwraps identity seed, mailbox capabilities, device list; calls op 1 re-register path to replace devices if requested. (5) Append `account_events` kind `re_register` only — not “recovered slot 42.”
- **Atomic:** guess counter; separately, any re-register as in op 1.
- **Eventual:** backup image is epoch-published (writes after PIN change appear next backup epoch, 60s).
- **UI:** “Recovering.” Never optimistic-unlock.
- **Conflict:** Two recoveries: first to commit re-register wins the device set; second must PIN again. Replay of an old unwrapped backup cannot roll identity keys backward: `record_version` must increase.

**8. Link or unlink a device.**

- **Inputs:** Authenticated account, existing device attestation, new device identity, new mailbox capability, or a revoke `device_id`.
- **Authorization:** An already-linked device, or a just-recovered session from op 7. Max 5 active.
- **Steps:** (1) Insert/revoke `devices`. (2) Enqueue directory mix write updating `device_mailboxes` on the current number’s record (new interval version). (3) Enqueue mailbox mix write allocating empty cells for a new mailbox_index. (4) E2EE-notify existing devices via their mailboxes (op 4) so they learn the new sibling; this is ordinary sealed mail, not a control-plane fan-out list.
- **Atomic:** device row + mix enqueues.
- **Eventual:** discoverability of the new mailbox at next directory epoch.
- **UI:** “Linking…” on both screens; desktop waits until it can complete op 3 on its new mailbox.
- **Conflict:** Sixth device refused uniformly. Concurrent unlink/link serializes on account; a revoked device’s later fetch still works if it holds the capability — **revoke must also enqueue op 5 zeros and a capability rotation** so the old capability’s cells MAC-fail after the next epoch.

**9. File an abuse report.**

- **Inputs:** Authenticated reporter, envelope ciphertext as fetched, category, optional note ciphertext for reviewers.
- **Authorization:** Reporter’s linked device. The envelope must MAC under a mailbox capability of this account (client-side check); the server additionally checks the sealed-sender blob can be opened with the **report** key and not otherwise.
- **Steps:** (1) Insert `abuse_reports` with `unsealed_sender_account_id` filled iff the sender used a reportable certificate. (2) If unseal fails, still store the ciphertext and mark `unsealed_sender_account_id` null (sealed-sender without reportability is allowed; then the operator still does not learn the sender). (3) Do not enqueue any mailbox fetch. (4) Token-budget or ban actions, if any, go through `accounts.status` and budget fields, never through a destination log.
- **Atomic:** the report row including the optional unseal.
- **Eventual:** reviewer queue.
- **UI:** “Reported.” No optimistic ban.
- **Conflict:** Duplicate report of the same `envelope_id` by the same reporter is a no-op.

**10. Publish a directory (or username) epoch** (scheduled, every 10 minutes).

- **Inputs:** Scheduler lease, `target_epoch = current+1`, mix items with `store=directory|username`.
- **Authorization:** System only.
- **Steps:** (1) Close the mix for `target_epoch`. (2) Start from the previous immutable image. (3) Apply every pending oblivious write, plus a matching number of dummy writes so item count is padded to a published mix size class. (4) **Rewrite every slot** into the new image (identity rewrite if untouched). (5) Publish `directory_epochs` row with digests and a retrieval hint for the whole image. (6) Drop the previous-previous image (keep one back). (7) Accounts in `pending_deletion` whose 7 days elapsed become `deleted` and their slots dummy.
- **Atomic:** one epoch row + image digest is the swap. Applying 2^27 slots is a bulk job with a single publish pointer at the end; readers never see a half image.
- **Eventual:** clients learn the new epoch on their next op 2.
- **UI:** none.
- **Conflict:** One publisher per epoch (lease). Crash-retry from mix (idempotent apply by item id).

**11. Rebuild the mailbox store** (scheduled bulk, every 60 seconds).

- **Inputs:** Scheduler lease, `target_epoch`, pending mailbox mix including op 4 drops and op 5 consumes.
- **Authorization:** System only.
- **Steps:** (1) Close the mix; pad mix size. (2) Apply all writes into a **complete** new `mailbox_cells` image: every `slot_index` × 32 cells is rewritten, including unmodified cells (new nonce, same logical contents). Expiry: while rewriting, drop envelopes with `expires_epoch < target_epoch` inside the cell before re-padding — this is a uniform sweep, not a per-mailbox job. (3) Publish `mailbox_epochs`. (4) Keep one previous image for in-flight fetches. (5) Shards, if any, all rebuild every epoch (no dirty-shard set).
- **Atomic:** epoch pointer swap.
- **Eventual:** this *is* the durability path for drops.
- **UI:** none. A sender’s “sent” may sit up to 60s before a recipient fetch can see it.
- **Conflict:** Same as op 10. A late mix item misses the epoch and goes to `target+1`.

**12. Delete account.**

- **Inputs:** Authenticated account, PIN, confirmation.
- **Authorization:** Account holder with PIN.
- **Steps:** (1) Set `pending_deletion`, `deleted_at = now+7d`. (2) Revoke devices. (3) Enqueue directory tombstone (`valid_until = next epoch`) and 32-cell zeros for every device mailbox. (4) Stop op 6 issuance. (5) Do not scan `accounts` into discovery.
- **Atomic:** status + mix enqueues + device revoke.
- **Eventual:** directory epoch makes the number undiscoverable; envelopes expire by op 11.
- **UI:** Immediate local wipe. “Account will be undiscoverable within 10 minutes.”
- **Conflict:** A concurrent send token already issued may still drop until it expires (24h); cells still MAC-fail after tombstone. Re-register of the same E.164 before 7 days is refused.

**13. Reserve or look up a username.**

- **Inputs:** Lookup: username string, size class `s`. Reserve: authenticated account, username, token of cost 1.
- **Authorization:** Lookup is the same anonymous retrieval as op 2 (may charge discovery budget if issued from a session, or a separate 20/day anonymous IP budget that does not see the name). Reserve requires active account and unused name in the next username epoch.
- **Steps:** Lookup is op 2 against `username_slots`. Reserve enqueues an oblivious write of `account_id` into the cuckoo slot; apply fails closed on collision; client learns collision from a private 1-bit as in op 4. Operator logs only size class.
- **Atomic:** mix enqueue; uniqueness is on the published image (cuckoo insert).
- **Eventual:** next username epoch (10 min).
- **UI:** “Available” is optimistic only as a local cached miss from the last epoch; reservation waits for epoch.
- **Conflict:** Two reserves of the same name: one cell, one winner; loser gets private miss and uniform `ok`.

**14. Cover poll** (scheduled on every device).

- **Inputs:** None beyond the device’s cover schedule.
- **Authorization:** None (anonymous retrieval).
- **Steps:** Fire ops 3 and 5 in cover form, and with 10% probability a dummy op 2 of the device’s usual size class against random indices, on the same 200 ms grid as real use. Must run whether or not local outbox or push-wakeup happened.
- **Atomic:** same as 3 and 5.
- **Eventual:** n/a.
- **UI:** none. Suppressing cover in battery saver is a protocol violation for official clients; the setting does not exist.
- **Conflict:** none; cover is the same as real.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful independent deployment, not a hyperscale WhatsApp clone:

- 80 million registered numbers, 20 million MAU, 6 million DAU, 8 million linked devices (cap 5, observed mean 1.3).
- Directory image: 2^27 slots × 288 B ≈ 36 GiB, plus a same-size retrieval hint if single-server PIR; fits in RAM on each of two retrieval servers.
- Username image: 2^26 × 160 B ≈ 10 GiB.
- Mailbox image: 80 million × 32 × 4 KiB = 10.24 TiB logical; 2× erasure ≈ 20.5 TiB stored. Must be RAM- or NVMe-resident on the retrieval cluster because every epoch and every query full-passes.
- Address books: median 280 numbers, p90 1,200, p99 4,000. Discovery 2×/day when connected plus install: ~40 million discovery batches/day, almost all size class `m` (2,048 slot fetches).
- Mailbox fetches: foreground 45s × ~1.5 million concurrent open clients ≈ 33k fetches/s; background 8 min × 6 million ≈ 12.5k/s; cover matches the same, so ~90k mailbox retrievals/s at peak local daytime.
- Envelopes: ~800 million/day (≈ 130 per DAU including receipts/typing); peak drops ~15k/s; each drop is a mix item, each consume a 32-cell rewrite.
- Groups: max 1,000 members, p99 256; a p99 send is 256 tokens and 256 mix items.
- Mix rebuild: every 60s, rewrite 10.24 TiB (plus erasure). That is ~170 GiB/s sustained write — the dominant bill, on the order of a small HPC/NVMe pod, not a row store.
- Discovery budget 10k/day caps scraping at 10k numbers per account per day (800 billion pair-probes/day theoretical if every account maxed; real is ~10 billion).
- Backup image: 80 million × 64 KiB = 5.12 TiB, polled rarely; still full-pass, so it is cheaper to fold backups into the mailbox cluster’s schedule than to point-get.

What gets expensive: (a) mailbox full-image rewrite every 60s, (b) 90k full-pass PIR queries/s against 10 TiB, (c) group fan-out as N oblivious writes, (d) dummy writes so mix size and shard I/O do not track traffic. Directory PIR at 36 GiB RAM is cheap next to mail. A backend that point-gets 90k mailboxes/s is cheap and wrong.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Reads that do not produce a key-level read set.** Discovery, fetch, username lookup, and backup open must be full-image (or cryptographically equivalent) retrievals whose logs are `query_receipts` without indices. Ordinary `SELECT WHERE pk=?`, cached gets, traced OCC read sets, and “live queries” fail invariant 1 on the first inbox open. A reasonable concession is epoch freshness of 60s for mail and 10 minutes for directory, so clients do not see a write in the same second. Treating hashed point-gets, per-shard PIR without dummy shards, or value-encryption with a traced key as “private retrieval” is a cheat — the operator who records what was read still learns the query.

2. **Invalidation, replication, and audit that move only whole epoch images.** The product forbids row-level invalidation of directory and mail (“mailbox X changed, notify its last readers”), read-your-writes via a replica that fetched the dirty page, and staff tail. The concession is a published epoch pointer and one-image-back retention. Shipping “the pages this client just read” to a replica, or building a CDC stream of mailbox_id, is a cheat even if values stay encrypted, because that stream is trap 1.

3. **Destructive reads whose write set is not the mailbox identity.** Op 5 has to empty cells without a `DELETE FROM mailbox WHERE id=…` and without rebuilding only dirty shards. The unusual mechanism is a periodic full-image rewrite that also expires 14-day mail. The concession is 60s occupancy lag and “consume only after sibling devices have a copy (or 7 days).” Per-key ack, per-shard dirty rebuild, or “we won’t delete, so we don’t leak” (operator keeps mail forever) are cheats of promises 3+5.

4. **Authorization without naming the row, plus limits without naming the target.** Fetch is authorized by a capability inside the query, drop by an anonymous token, discovery by a padded budget. There is no `WHERE mailbox.owner = viewer`. The concession is that token *issuance* and SMS registration do reveal that an account is active that day, and that a reporter can unseal one sender. Per-destination rate counters, authenticated fetches, support impersonation of op 3, or a server count of “matches in this address book” are cheats.

5. **Interval membership in the directory, with recycle as a new person.** “On Cove” is `(valid_from_epoch, valid_until_epoch)` at a published epoch, merged across devices by epoch, not a boolean and not `accounts.status` sampled at query time through a point-get. The concession is 10-minute discovery lag and a 7-day hold before a deleted number can belong to someone else. Reusing `account_id` on recycle, last-write-wins `is_registered`, or serving discovery out of `accounts.e164` “because the operator already knows the number” are cheats: they either deliver other people’s mail to a stranger or turn the registration table into the query.

Event-driven push, dirty-bit polling, and “skip cover when quiet” are the same cheat as (1) in a pager.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice: Signal-class sealed sender and report-only unseal; registration lock and secure-value recovery; phone-number privacy (`account_id` vs E.164); number-recycle as a new identity; Privacy Pass / VOPRF rate-limit tokens; hashed-CDS considered broken at E.164 entropy; private information retrieval and two-server PIR; Path-ORAM / epoch rebuild as the write analog; padded, constant-size responses; official-client encrypted stores wiped on sign-out.

Deliberate product numbers invented here: 32×4 KiB cells, 60s mailbox epochs, 10-minute directory epochs, 24h/7d discovery staleness, 14-day envelope TTL, 10k discovery and 2,000 send-token daily caps, size classes 512/2048/8192/24576, 45s/8 min poll, five devices, 7-day sibling consume delay, 5 PIN guesses/day, 200 ms timing grid, and the 80M / 6M DAU envelope.

The unsoftened composition — fetch not bound to an account session, drops and consumes as full-image rewrites, cover that cannot be disabled, no event-driven push, registration table banned from discovery — is the exercise. Each piece exists in literature or in production messengers; running all of them as ordinary inbox-open and address-book-sync, against an operator who is *defined* to record what was read, is the load-bearing claim. Confidence is high that a queries-and-mutations backend fails traps 1–3 on the first cut; medium that 10 TiB full-image epochs every 60s is the actual cost of not cheating trap 2 at this scale; low on the exact mix padding that would defeat a determined traffic analyst joining SMS-time registration with diurnal load, which is why cover and uniform rebuilds are contract, not decoration.

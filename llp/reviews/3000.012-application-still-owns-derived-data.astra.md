**Numbered findings**

1. **High — RFC §1 and §3.2: immutable columns do not make reference attribution reconstructible.**

   Consider a post `P` with immutable author `A`, and a like whose contribution resolves through `P`. Delete `P`. The RFC says the like continues contributing to `A`, using its recorded key. But adoption rebuilds by reading each surviving source row’s target: that target no longer exists. Recreating `P` with author `B` makes the discrepancy worse: incremental maintenance retains `A`, while rebuilding finds `B`.

   This is permitted by the current engine. `immutable` restricts updates, not deletion and recreation: [write.rs:341](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/write.rs:341). Inserts also replace expired predecessors: [write.rs:62](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/write.rs:62). Current maintain rebuilding deletes the aggregate and folds source rows again: [maintains.rs:71](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/maintains.rs:71).

   **Resolution:** choose an explicit semantic contract. Either attribution is a contribution-time fact that remains durably recoverable, or it is a relationship to a currently live target, with defined deletion and expiry behavior. Specify target ID reuse, source updates after target disappearance, and adoption over pre-existing dangling references. “One point read” does not resolve these choices.

2. **High — RFC §3.2: the existing contribution index is not the proposed frozen-attribution ledger.**

   Today that index is installed only for expiring sources. Its membership and keys are recomputed from the source row through `selected(row)` and `source_key(row)`; it does not preserve independently resolved foreign-row facts. See [maintain.rs:113](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/maintain.rs:113) and [rows.rs:152](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4-core/src/rows.rs:152).

   Recording a group key alone also does not completely specify predicates through references. An inverse delta needs the old selection outcome and contribution value. Adoption after changing a predicate may require referenced facts that were never recorded, including for previously excluded rows.

   Source expiry adds another path: current live aggregates recompute before physical sweeping, using source selection and keys again. Merely teaching deletion to subtract a stored key leaves this read path unresolved: [read.rs:187](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:187).

   **Resolution:** describe the persistent contribution representation, lookup by source identity, interpretation of predicates, rebuild triggers, and treatment of excluded contributions. Account for its storage, indexes, privacy, repair, and device projection. Reusing the existing index’s name is insufficient.

3. **High — RFC §1 and §3.2: bounded reference lookup does not establish safe disclosure.**

   Typed references deliberately do not imply target existence: [validate.rs:102](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/validate.rs:102). Refusing a source write when a reference does not resolve adds a new observable existence constraint.

   For example, a writer allowed to insert public source rows could supply guessed IDs of private targets. Success versus the new missing-reference refusal reveals target existence even when the aggregate itself is unreadable. Extending a warning about group-key disclosure does not eliminate this channel.

   Existing protections are insufficient as a drop-in solution. `N_MAINTAIN_DELTA` currently skips public sources and unreadable aggregates, while maintain reservation skips public sources too: [residency.rs:286](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/residency.rs:286), [write_target.rs:288](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/write_target.rs:288). The accepted maintained-column RFC explicitly rejected missing-parent refusals because of forward references, seeding order, unheld parents, and existence disclosure: [3000.007.001 §5](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/llp/3000.007.001-maintained-columns.rfc.md:447).

   **Resolution:** make authority and permitted disclosure admission conditions alongside bounded work. Specify whose authority resolves the target, whether the writer may learn its existence, and how hidden predicates affect work, errors, and touched tables. A narrow first scope with public targets is much easier to justify. Preserve any independent source authorization when removing copy-verification clauses.

4. **High — RFC §3.2: an unheld reference target is an unknown dependency, not necessarily an unknown aggregate base.**

   The device may already hold the aggregate’s value while lacking the target needed to identify its group. Current aggregate-base handling runs only after the group key is known: [maintains.rs:157](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/maintains.rs:157).

   Conversely, holding a target as delivered history does not prove that it is currently live or currently authorizes a new contribution. The maintains card already distinguishes retained history from complete live aggregate knowledge: [maintains.md:130](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/maintains.md:130).

   **Resolution:** specify whether an unresolved device dependency queues the whole mutation without prediction or skips an explicitly server-only derived effect. Include implicit reference dependencies in residency analysis and define replay behavior when target coverage changes. Cover target expiry, retained-only targets, source deletion after target loss, and incomplete contribution coverage. The existing prediction refusal class may be reusable; that does not make these policies automatic.

5. **High — RFC §3.3: the proposed logical merge is not equivalent to the recipe being relocated.**

   A merge over followed authors’ posts suggests all matching posts are available through pagination. The recipe backfills at most 865 recent events. Following an author with 900 older pushed events therefore does not reproduce that merge’s historical domain.

   Promotion is also permanent, rather than a reversible function of current in-degree. A follow can be refused indefinitely because another follower has exhausted their pull slots. Source deletion creates a tombstone immediately and removes private copies through a later bounded job. These are observable product semantics, not implementation details.

   Evidence: [feeds.md:398](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:398), [feeds.md:441](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:441), [feeds.md:494](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:494); `CAL16/lane/snapback/feed.q:150`.

   **Resolution:** state the timeline’s historical domain, permanent promotion behavior, capacity refusals, event identity, and deletion/cleanup contract. Either preserve these explicitly or commission changed semantics. Define the oracle against that contract. “Expand the recipe” and “materialize this merge” currently describe different promises.

6. **High — RFC §3.3: public sources alone are too broad a first scope.**

   The existing integration admits public, fully synchronized, non-expiring application tables, including a public follow graph. It constrains updates to mapped identity and ordering fields. See [feeds.md:630](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:630).

   F3 requires public sources but leaves edge privacy, source/edge expiry, horizons, and mutable ordering or selection fields unspecified. Public post bodies do not authorize disclosing private follows through routing state, promotion outcomes, or work-dependent refusals. An expiring public source also becomes unreadable before physical deletion; a copied inbox row needs an equivalent lifetime rule.

   Prediction is equally specific. The recipe predicts posting when required rows are held, but follows and unfollows queue without rows because slot accounting is online-only: [feeds.md:565](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:565).

   **Resolution:** initially preserve the recipe’s full supported shape: public graph and routing disclosures, immutable event metadata, no source/edge expiry or horizons, and its existing prediction boundary. Preserve private routes and public slot-count distinctions. Broader combinations need their own proof. Existing partition events can carry the resulting rows; that does not automatically validate the transformation producing them.

7. **High — RFC §1 and §3.3: the advertised bounds do not cover every lifecycle operation.**

   A cap per reader bounds that reader’s edge count. It does not alone bound source history removed on unfollow, copies deleted during cleanup, retained tombstones, source updates, or total bytes emitted by an atomic operation.

   The recipe has additional caps for events per actor, copies per event, private entries per reader, global broadcast rows, and pending tombstones. Its purge depends on at most 1,001 private copies per event. These appear in [feeds.md:494](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:494) and `CAL16/lane/snapback/feed.q:62`, `:96`, `:117`.

   **Resolution:** provide an operation matrix covering source insert/update/delete, edge insert/update/delete, promotion, backfill, cleanup, and adoption. Include rows, indexes, bytes, and composed derivations. Explicitly refuse unsupported lifetime and update operations. Moving constants into engine policy removes application implementation work; it does not remove the resulting capacity limits or recovery obligations.

8. **Medium — RFC §4; Research §2.5 and §3: “one substrate” is reasonable, but “no new mechanism” is an unsuitable rewrite test.**

   The shared row-change tail is useful. It is not already a generic derivation executor. `Origin::Maintained` deliberately skips downstream maintains and asset placement; engine-owned-table refusal recognizes maintain targets specifically: [write.rs:301](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/write.rs:301), [write.rs:497](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/write.rs:497).

   F1 already has deliberately different policies: no first-build device derivation, a private aggregate plus mirror pass, and event-free activation followed by snapshot reacquisition. Ordinary maintain rebuilding emits events. See [3000.007.001 §3.7](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/llp/3000.007.001-maintained-columns.rfc.md:300) and [§3.11](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/llp/3000.007.001-maintained-columns.rfc.md:376).

   **Resolution:** require one executor, transaction boundary, catalog, and sync protocol, with explicit per-form lifecycle policies. A descriptor needs dependencies, ownership, durable provenance where necessary, device behavior, and activation policy—not just delta, bound, rebuild, and audience. Needing another policy hook is not evidence that Snapback needs a successor.

**Suggestions and novel ideas**

- **Treat attribution as an engine-authored historical fact when that is the intended semantics.** A small durable record keyed by source/contribution identity can preserve attribution after target loss. Aggregates can then rebuild from those facts. This could keep the proposed authoring surface while admitting that its provenance is not disposable cache state.
- **Separate a timeline’s answer contract from its delivery strategy.** Define history, identity, ordering, deletion, and completeness first. Push/pull selection can then change without silently changing what “Following” contains.
- **Use incremental-versus-rebuild equivalence as the first design test.** Compare the two after target deletion/recreation, expiry before sweep, predicate changes, promotion, and follow/unfollow. These small counterexamples are more decisive for the RFC than another holistic calibration.
- **Revisit keys-only all-pull serving as a measured alternative.** The recorded absolute increase of roughly 2.5 ms deserves consideration beside the unfavorable ratio. This does not authorize ignoring the current 3× bar; it argues for an explicit product judgment about latency, acquisition bytes, and implementation complexity.
- **Give the substrate explicit lifecycle hooks, not a promise of uniform behavior.** Sharing transactions, ownership enforcement, dependency reporting, and generation publication is valuable even when one form snapshots attribution and another has asynchronous cleanup.
- **Make the guide reset independently reviewable.** Preserve a complete effect-only path, move specialized OIDC/action material, and compare actual lookup output. A capability directive should not become a second security boundary or a reason to conceal necessary core APIs.

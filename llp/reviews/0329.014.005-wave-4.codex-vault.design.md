### 1. Product contract

This is a personal, family, and team vault whose authoritative state lives on enrolled devices. The service is an encrypted relay, mailbox, and blob store; it is not an item database and cannot decide who may read, edit, share, delete, or merge an item.

The product makes these user-visible promises:

1. Once a full-mode device reports “offline ready,” it contains every authorized current item, password history, attachment, policy proof, and key needed for indefinite local use without check-ins, leases, trusted clocks, or server tokens.
2. Unlocking, search, autofill, reveal, copy, create, edit, delete, and conflict resolution work while the service is unreachable.
3. “Saved” means the revision, its audit commitment, and its sync outbox entry are durably committed on this device; “delivered” and “accepted into shared history” are separately reported states.
4. IDs and encryption keys are generated on devices, so offline creation never needs server allocation or uniqueness checks.
5. Devices can reconcile through the service, direct local transfer, or an encrypted sync bundle after an arbitrarily long partition.
6. The server never receives plaintext item data, plaintext item names, content keys, recovery secrets, or intelligible item-access rules.
7. The server may observe account and network identity, opaque mailbox identifiers, traffic timing, padded blob sizes, storage totals, and upload/fetch activity; the product does not call this metadata invisible.
8. Every item has its own effective reader, editor, sharing, deletion, and administration policy; vault roles and groups are conveniences that devices expand into cryptographic capabilities.
9. A viewer may possess a decryption key but cannot create an accepted edit without a separately verifiable write capability.
10. A new grant defaults to “current state and future revisions”; sharing historical password versions requires an explicit choice.
11. Users without access do not learn an item’s title, fields, history, conflict state, or existence through client-visible counts or differentiated refusals.
12. Concurrent item edits are never resolved by server arrival order or wall-clock last-writer-wins.
13. Independent edits may merge automatically only when their conflict domains are provably disjoint; competing passwords, OTP seeds, private keys, secure-note bodies, deletes, and replacements remain explicit branches.
14. Concurrent access-policy edits use security-preserving rules: unrelated changes commute, while removal beats a concurrent grant and demotion beats a concurrent promotion for the same subject.
15. Revocation prevents disclosure of causally later key epochs, but cannot erase plaintext or keys already copied, stop an isolated device from using its old replica, or prove whether an old-epoch edit was made before or after revocation.
16. A late edit from someone who remains authorized is bridged into the current epoch; a removed actor’s late branch is preserved for inspection but never silently enters current shared state.
17. Account authentication recovery and vault-data recovery are distinct: support can restore an account shell, but only a surviving device, recovery kit, or configured trustee quorum can restore encrypted data.
18. Emergency access is pre-provisioned and explicitly scoped; outage-independent access uses human trustees, while an enforceable waiting period requires an available release witness and is not advertised as outage-independent.
19. Team audit strongly covers accepted writes, grants, revocations, merges, imports, deletions, and key transitions, but labels offline read telemetry as client-reported rather than claiming every plaintext view is provable.
20. Strong travel mode replaces the device’s ordinary identity and keyring with a travel-scoped identity containing only selected items; restoring excluded items requires a different trusted device, recovery artifact, or release service.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[SECRECY]** No server-held value is sufficient to decrypt an item, attachment, access policy, audit body, or recovery archive.
2. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A device discloses an item key only to a principal authorized by a valid item-policy chain that the device itself verifies.
3. **[TIME]** A full-mode device’s local read and write authority never expires merely because it has not contacted a server.
4. **[TIME]** Wall clocks, upload times, and server arrival order never decide revision ancestry, item conflicts, or whether an offline edit preceded a revocation.
5. **[AUTHORITY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Every accepted mutation is signed by a device with the required capability under the policy head referenced by that mutation.
6. **[SECRECY, TIME]** Revocation protects causally later secrets but never claims to retract secrets or keys previously delivered.
7. **[TIME, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A removed actor’s old-epoch write remains recoverable evidence but cannot automatically modify the current shared item.
8. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Concurrent policy changes never broaden access merely by taking the union of two access lists.
9. Every reported successful local write has an immutable revision, complete encrypted dependencies, an audit commitment, and an outbox record in one local transaction.
10. No valid revision, conflicting secret, or delete-versus-edit branch is discarded merely because another revision arrived first.
11. A merge revision resolves only the heads it names, and any concurrent unnamed head remains unresolved.
12. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A current-and-future grant reveals no historical item epoch unless the grantor explicitly includes it.
13. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A tombstone prevents automatic resurrection but does not destroy a concurrent offline edit.
14. **[SECRECY]** Unknown or undecryptable data is shown as pending, locked, or invalid rather than being interpreted as an empty item or deletion.
15. **[SECRECY]** Resetting a service password cannot produce a vault decryption key.
16. **[SECRECY, OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Every recovery or emergency authority capable of decrypting another person’s data is disclosed to that person before the data is placed under that authority.
17. **[TIME]** A scheduled expiry or emergency waiting period is not a hard security boundary unless a named trusted time witness is available.
18. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** An audit report states the exact device frontiers and event classes it proves and never describes unobservable offline reads as complete.
19. **[SECRECY]** Strong travel mode leaves no self-sufficient path on the traveling device for recovering excluded keys.
20. An external password change is never marked complete in the vault until the external service has accepted and the client has verified the new credential.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Offline authority versus revocation**

   - **Wrong version:** The server marks a device revoked, rejects its later uploads, and the product says access ended immediately.
   - **Why wrong:** An isolated device cannot distinguish “still authorized” from “revoked elsewhere”; it must keep decrypting and writing in both worlds. Its clock cannot prove when an edit occurred, and rotation cannot erase previously learned plaintext.
   - **Observation:** Disconnect device A, revoke it from device B, change a secret on B, and edit the old secret on A six months later. A must still work locally; after reunion, A cannot decrypt B’s new epoch and A’s branch must be quarantined rather than silently accepted or destroyed.

2. **A ciphertext-blind server mistaken for the item authority**

   - **Wrong version:** The server uses an account row or opaque item ID to decide whether an upload, read, count, or deletion is allowed.
   - **Why wrong:** The server does not possess the encrypted item policy, its causal history, or its key epochs. If it can make the semantic decision, the item rule is not actually device-held; if clients accept whatever it relays, replayed policies and revoked writes become authoritative.
   - **Observation:** Replay an old grant, upload a correctly formed revision from a viewer, fork two policy histories, and ask an unauthorized member for item counts. Current devices must reject or quarantine the records locally, while the unauthorized client learns neither the item nor a differentiated reason.

3. **Secret-bearing fields treated as ordinary mergeable columns**

   - **Wrong version:** Field-wise merge or last-writer-wins combines two offline edits into one item.
   - **Why wrong:** A username, password, URL, OTP seed, and recovery code can form one credential transaction; mixing pieces from different branches may create a credential that never worked. Delete-wins loses a valid rotation, while update-wins resurrects intentionally deleted data.
   - **Observation:** Device A changes the password and username at a website while device B changes the OTP seed or deletes the item. Reunion must preserve both causal versions and require an explicit credential-domain decision.

4. **Recovery and emergency access presented as free capabilities**

   - **Wrong version:** Support resets the account and restores plaintext, or an emergency contact automatically gains access after 14 days even if every service and owner device is offline.
   - **Why wrong:** Recovery after loss of every secret is information-theoretically impossible without pre-existing escrow, a recovery artifact, or trustees. A delay needs a trusted observer of elapsed time; a pre-delivered offline secret can be exercised immediately.
   - **Observation:** Destroy every device and recovery share, then ask support to decrypt the server blobs; or isolate the emergency contact and advance its clock. Either plaintext remains unrecoverable, or the design had an undisclosed keyholder or unenforceable timer.

5. **A complete audit promised for devices that hold plaintext**

   - **Wrong version:** Every secret view, copy, or export appears in an authoritative team audit.
   - **Why wrong:** A modified authorized client can read cached plaintext, photograph it, or suppress a local event. Device timestamps are advisory, and an offline device may withhold events indefinitely.
   - **Observation:** Read and copy a cached password using a modified client with networking disabled. Mutation commitments can later expose forged or missing writes, but no remote system can prove the plaintext view occurred.

6. **Travel mode or deletion implemented as hiding rows**

   - **Wrong version:** Travel mode removes navigation entries while keeping a full decrypting identity, or “delete everywhere” removes the server copy and claims all replicas are erased.
   - **Why wrong:** A coercive examiner can use any restoration capability still on the device; flash remnants, backups, exports, and dormant replicas remain outside remote control.
   - **Observation:** Enter travel mode, power-cycle, block the network, and attempt full restoration using only that device. If restoration succeeds, excluded keys were never truly removed; if an offline replica later silently resurrects a deleted item, the tombstone protocol was incomplete.

### 4. Data model

Types used below are `Id256`, `Hash256`, `Bytes`, `UInt64`, `Boolean`, `Enum`, `Timestamp`, `Set<T>`, and `Map<K,V>`. A `Timestamp` from a device is only a display hint; only a signed witness or server receipt establishes that an event was observed no later than a particular time.

| Table or record | Important fields | Keys, relationships, and lifecycle |
|---|---|---|
| `AccountShell` | `account_id`, authentication methods, billing tier, quota, opaque mailbox tokens | Server-visible and mutable; controls transport and storage only, never item authority. |
| `PrincipalIdentityCommit` | `principal_id`, `kind: person/group/team/trustee`, encryption/signing public keys, encrypted label, `parents[]`, `signatures[]` | Append-only identity DAG; current identity state is derived. |
| `DeviceGovernanceCommit` | `commit_id`, `parents[]`, admitted and revoked `device_id`s, roles, quorum rule, signer set | Append-only; admission and revocation are causally bounded, not certificate expirations. |
| `DeviceCertificate` | `device_id`, `principal_id`, signing and encryption public keys, `admitted_by`, format suites | Non-expiring certificate referenced by revisions; revocation is derived from governance commits. |
| `PrincipalKeyEpoch` | `epoch_id`, `principal_or_group_id`, `parent_epoch`, encrypted member set, device envelopes, reason | Append-only; rotates when a device or group member is removed. |
| `Vault` | `vault_id`, encrypted name/settings, governance head, default access-group references, recovery posture | Stable anchor; default access is expanded and validated on devices. |
| `ItemAnchor` | `item_id`, `vault_id`, creation policy head, random audit alias | Stable identifier; vault relationship is inside encrypted records when hiding grouping matters. |
| `ItemPolicyCommit` | `policy_id`, `item_id`, `parents[]`, encrypted grants `{subject_id, rights, history_floor}`, `item_key_epoch`, envelope-set hash, signer set | Append-only policy DAG; rights include `read`, `edit`, `share`, `delete`, and `admin`. |
| `ItemKeyEpoch` | `item_id`, `epoch_id`, parent epoch, random audience key, recipient/group envelopes | The plaintext audience key exists only on authorized endpoints; grouping identical audiences is an optimization. |
| `ItemRevision` | `revision_id`, `item_id`, `kind`, `parents[]`, `policy_head`, `key_epoch`, `author_device_id`, `author_sequence`, `previous_device_event`, encrypted field operations, attachment roots, claimed time, signature | Immutable revision DAG; `revision_id` commits to the canonical encrypted record. |
| `EncryptedFieldOperation` | stable `field_id`, `conflict_domain_id`, operation enum, ciphertext, schema tag | Login-core fields normally share one conflict domain; tags and independent custom fields may use separate domains. |
| `KeyEnvelope` | `envelope_id`, key epoch, anonymous recipient epoch reference, encapsulated key bytes | Delivered through opaque recipient mailboxes; server cannot interpret the recipient’s item role. |
| `AttachmentChunk` | random `chunk_id`, ciphertext, padded length, integrity hash | Immutable and content-verified after decryption; revision manifests define order and filenames. |
| `PurgeBarrier` | `item_id`, tombstone revision, policy head, retained hash commitment, optional opaque storage-GC capabilities | Append-only and retained indefinitely so ancient devices cannot silently resurrect a purged item. |
| `RecoveryPolicy` | `policy_id`, scope, recovery public key, threshold, trustee commitments, encrypted recovery-manifest reference | Versioned; each vault declares whether recovery is personal-only, trustee-based, or explicit organization escrow. |
| `EmergencyGrantEvent` | `grant_id`, parents, grantor, delegate, encrypted scope, mode, quorum/witness requirements, emergency-key envelopes, status, signatures | Append-only grant/request/revoke DAG; scope completeness is derived from covered item epochs. |
| `AuditEvent` | `event_id`, vault, actor device, device sequence, causal parents, action, encrypted item alias and details, policy head, mutation commitment, signature | Append-only; every accepted mutation commits to one, while read events remain honest-client telemetry. |
| `AuditCheckpoint` | checkpoint ID, covered device frontiers, Merkle root, signer set, optional server receipt | Append-only and mergeable; supports gap, rollback, and fork detection after devices exchange checkpoints. |
| `SyncBlob` / `DeliveryRef` | random blob ID, opaque route or mailbox token, padded ciphertext, encrypted pointer, upload receipt, optional GC-capability hash | Server-visible storage layer; relationships, parents, ACLs, and item semantics remain encrypted. |
| `TravelProfile` | physical device ID, distinct travel `device_id`, encrypted allowed-item set, restoration-capsule digest, entry/exit events | Device-local plus ordinary item grants to the travel identity; the full identity is absent while active. |
| `ExternalChangeIntent` | intent ID, item, base heads, encrypted old/new candidates, origin, state, remote receipt hash, transition signatures | Append-only local saga used to survive ambiguous website password changes. |

Derived, never authoritative, tables include `CurrentItemHeads`, `EffectivePolicy`, `AuthorizedItemIndex`, `ConflictQueue`, `RekeyCoverage`, `EmergencyCoverage`, `AuditFrontier`, and the local search/autofill index.

All revisions, policies, governance changes, key epochs, audit records, and barriers are append-only. A device computes current state from valid maximal DAG heads. Device authorization and item access are bounded by causal commits, not wall-clock intervals. Scheduled dates create signed intents; they do not invalidate an isolated device by themselves.

A normal device retains the current snapshot, its own branch bases, policy proofs, tombstones, and historical keys promised by the user’s retention setting. Server-side compaction may replace old ciphertext with signed hash commitments only if returning devices can provide a self-contained base-and-branch package; silently discarding that evidence would impose a hidden offline horizon.

### 5. The hard operations

For every local write, “atomic” means a transaction in the device’s encrypted store; the service never supplies the item commit point.

1. **Unlock, search, read, and autofill offline**

   - **Inputs:** Local unlock factor, query or item ID, optional requested revision.
   - **Authorization:** Possession of the device and its protected keyring; biometrics may release but do not derive the storage key.
   - **Steps:** (1) Unlock the device keyring; (2) verify the stored governance and policy frontier; (3) open the encrypted local index; (4) select valid readable heads; (5) decrypt the chosen revision and cached attachments; (6) append a signed read event if team telemetry is enabled.
   - **Atomic:** The read observes one locally consistent snapshot.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Freshness and read-event delivery.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Full local UI, labeled “current on this device” with last successful frontier.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Autofill never silently chooses among competing credential-domain heads; it uses an explicitly selected local head with a warning or waits for resolution.

2. **Create or edit an item offline**

   - **Inputs:** Destination vault, fields, attachments, base heads, and locally known policy head.
   - **Authorization:** A valid local device certificate plus `edit` capability under that policy.
   - **Steps:** (1) Generate an item ID if needed; (2) assign stable field and conflict-domain IDs; (3) generate a random revision key; (4) encrypt fields and chunks; (5) wrap the revision key under the item’s audience epoch; (6) create a revision naming every base head; (7) increment the device counter and sign; (8) append the revision, audit event, and outbox record.
   - **Atomic:** Revision, complete local dependencies, audit commitment, and outbox entry.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Delivery, recipient validation, and merging.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Show immediately as “saved on this device.”
   - **Concurrent conflict:** The edit becomes another DAG head; only disjoint conflict domains may auto-merge.

3. **Synchronize after any partition**

   - **Inputs:** Local blob frontier, policy/audit checkpoints, and outbox.
   - **Authorization:** Transport authentication controls mailbox and quota access; devices independently validate every item operation.
   - **Steps:** (1) Exchange opaque frontiers with a server, peer, or bundle; (2) fetch missing blobs; (3) verify hashes, signatures, device chains, counters, policy lineage, and key epochs; (4) stage incomplete dependency sets; (5) classify complete records as accepted, auto-mergeable, conflicted, stale-authority, or invalid; (6) decrypt only possessed envelopes; (7) atomically advance local materialized heads; (8) upload the local outbox and retain receipts.
   - **Atomic:** A receiver exposes a revision only with all required parents, policy proofs, envelopes, and chunks.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Replica delivery, audit checkpoints, and policy coverage.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Distinguish queued, uploaded, delivered, accepted, conflicted, and quarantined.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** DAG ancestry determines concurrency; server order has no semantic effect.

4. **Resolve content conflicts**

   - **Inputs:** All known heads, their common ancestors, and user choices per conflict domain.
   - **Authorization:** `edit` capability under the current unambiguous policy.
   - **Steps:** (1) Three-way compare every stable field; (2) merge untouched and provably disjoint domains; (3) require a choice or explicit preservation for each competing secret domain; (4) treat tombstone-versus-edit as a decision, not an automatic rule; (5) create a new encrypted revision whose parents include every resolved head; (6) sign and commit it.
   - **Atomic:** The resolution revision and audit event.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Other replicas learning that resolution.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Preview the exact resulting credential before commit.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Two simultaneous resolutions become competing heads and require another merge; neither is discarded.

5. **Grant item-level access**

   - **Inputs:** Item, recipient or group identity, rights, and history scope: current-and-future, selected history, or full history.
   - **Authorization:** `share` capability; applicable team governance may additionally require an owner quorum.
   - **Steps:** (1) Verify recipient keys out of band or through the authenticated directory; (2) require resolution or explicit inclusion of all current secret heads; (3) create a policy child adding the subject; (4) for current-and-future access, rotate the item epoch and publish a current-state checkpoint; (5) wrap that epoch only to eligible recipient/group epochs; (6) add explicitly selected historical envelopes; (7) publish encrypted delivery references and an audit event.
   - **Atomic:** Policy child, checkpoint, envelope set, and local delivery intent form one local commit.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Recipient delivery and acknowledgment.
   - **Optimistic UI:** “Granted locally; delivery pending,” followed by item/key coverage.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** A concurrent removal or demotion for the same subject wins until a causally later regrant.

6. **Revoke access or a device and bulk-rekey**

   - **Inputs:** Subject, item/group/device scope, reason, and affected-item index.
   - **Authorization:** Matching `share`/`admin` authority; sensitive teams may require an offline-capable signature quorum.
   - **Steps:** (1) Sign the removal in the relevant policy or governance DAG; (2) generate new principal/group and item audience epochs excluding the subject; (3) require causally later writes to use fresh revision keys under those epochs; (4) batch current-state checkpoints for affected items; (5) distribute new envelopes only to remaining principals; (6) record per-item coverage and acknowledgments; (7) flag externally reusable credentials for rotation at their external services.
   - **Atomic:** The initiating device commits the removal and its new root epoch together; each item checkpoint is separately atomic.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Bulk checkpointing, key delivery, acknowledgments, and external password rotation.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Show “removed from future policy,” “new epoch active,” and rekey coverage separately; never show “forgotten everywhere.”
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Old-epoch work from a still-authorized actor may be bridged; a removed actor’s branch is quarantined, and secrets created by an unaware old-epoch device may already have been exposed to the former audience.

7. **Resolve concurrent policy changes**

   - **Inputs:** All maximal policy heads and the common policy ancestor.
   - **Authorization:** The item or vault governance quorum defined by the common ancestor.
   - **Steps:** (1) Merge operations on unrelated subjects; (2) apply removal over concurrent grant and demotion over concurrent promotion for the same subject; (3) freeze ambiguous group restructures at the most restrictive known access; (4) have the quorum choose the final subject set and roles; (5) create a policy commit referencing every policy head; (6) rotate the audience epoch if any reader was removed.
   - **Atomic:** Resolution policy and new audience-epoch declaration.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Item checkpoints and recipient envelopes.
   - **Optimistic UI:** No optimistic expansion; disputed access is visibly restricted.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Another concurrent resolution creates a new dispute rather than winning by timestamp.

8. **Delete, restore, or purge an item**

   - **Inputs:** Item heads, mode, and optional retention rule.
   - **Authorization:** `delete`; purging shared history may require `admin`.
   - **Steps:** (1) Create a tombstone revision over every known head; (2) hide the item from normal views; (3) sync the tombstone; (4) restore by creating a revision over the tombstone and selected content heads; or (5) purge by destroying reachable current keys, sending opaque per-blob garbage-collection capabilities, and retaining a signed purge barrier.
   - **Atomic:** Tombstone or restore revision plus audit event.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Replica hiding, provider-side blob collection, and key destruction.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Immediate move to Deleted, with sync and purge scope stated.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** A concurrent edit produces “deleted here, edited there”; it cannot silently resurrect the item, but an authorized user may salvage it into a new current revision.

9. **Enroll a new device**

   - **Inputs:** New device public keys and an existing trusted device or recovery authority.
   - **Authorization:** Existing device approval or the configured recovery quorum; server login alone is insufficient.
   - **Steps:** (1) Establish a proximity/QR-authenticated channel; (2) verify the human on both endpoints; (3) sign a non-expiring device certificate; (4) add the device to applicable principal/group epochs; (5) transfer governance frontiers and authorized key envelopes; (6) hydrate and verify the encrypted replica; (7) acknowledge offline-ready coverage.
   - **Atomic:** Certificate admission on the approving device and protected keyring installation on the new device.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Full ciphertext hydration and other-device awareness.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Progressive item coverage, never a false “complete.”
   - **Concurrent conflict:** A concurrent revocation from the same governance state dominates; enrollment-versus-recovery forks require explicit resolution.

10. **Recover an account onto a new device**

   - **Inputs:** Recovery kit or trustee shares, an encrypted recovery manifest, and a ciphertext snapshot from a device, backup, peer, or service.
   - **Authorization:** Recovery secret or configured threshold; identity-service checks may supplement but cannot replace it.
   - **Steps:** (1) Reconstruct recovery authority; (2) verify and decrypt the recovery manifest; (3) generate fresh device keys; (4) sign a recovery-generation commit; (5) decrypt the available personal-vault snapshot; (6) revoke lost devices and rotate principal epochs; (7) checkpoint personal items; (8) request separate team re-admission where team governance requires it.
   - **Atomic:** Creation of a usable recovered local identity and vault snapshot.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Rotation, coverage, service upload, and team re-admission.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Show recovered item count, snapshot date, missing attachments, and rekey progress.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Two recoveries from one generation create a security-critical fork; preserve both data branches but pause privileged sharing until recovery authority resolves and rotates again.

11. **Exercise emergency access**

   - **Inputs:** Emergency grant, delegate proof, ciphertext snapshot, and either trustee approvals or a release-witness assertion.
   - **Authorization:** A pre-existing scoped grant; emergency access cannot cover team data the grantor lacked authority to delegate.
   - **Steps:** (1) Verify that current item writes have maintained emergency-envelope coverage; (2) have the delegate request release; (3a) in outage-independent mode, collect the configured trustee threshold, or (3b) in delayed mode, obtain a signed elapsed-time release from the named witness after the veto window; (4) reconstruct the scoped emergency key; (5) decrypt covered items from any available replica; (6) record signed approvals and use events for later synchronization.
   - **Atomic:** Threshold reconstruction and local installation of the emergency key; there is no global transaction.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Notifications, audit delivery, and future-item coverage.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Show exact scope, snapshot completeness, approvals, and whether the mode depends on an available witness.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Revocation protects later epochs but cannot retract already released shares or plaintext; concurrent exercise and revocation is reported as such.

12. **Enter and exit strong travel mode**

   - **Inputs:** Selected travel items, step-up authentication, and a restoration path held elsewhere.
   - **Authorization:** The user may include only items they may delegate to a travel identity.
   - **Steps:** (1) Generate a distinct travel device keypair; (2) grant selected items to it and hydrate a complete travel replica; (3) place the full-device restoration capsule on another trusted device, recovery token, or service; (4) verify that path; (5) atomically switch to the travel profile; (6) destroy the ordinary keyring, decrypted index, caches, thumbnails, and excluded attachment keys on a best-effort basis; (7) continue reading and writing travel items offline; (8) exit by recovering or reenrolling the ordinary identity and then syncing travel branches.
   - **Atomic:** Profile switch and replacement of the active key index.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Physical erasure, policy propagation, and post-travel reconciliation.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Excluded items disappear only after the restoration check and travel replica commit.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Travel edits use normal DAG semantics; excluded ciphertext received in transit remains opaque, and revocations learned on exit classify stale branches normally.

13. **Build periodic audit checkpoints and perform maintenance**

   - **Inputs:** Audit frontiers, pending rekeys, missing envelope coverage, and retention policy.
   - **Authorization:** Any designated audit/checkpoint device; rekey work additionally requires the relevant key authority.
   - **Steps:** (1) Run after a local event threshold or approximately every 24 hours when a qualifying device is awake; (2) validate signatures, device counters, mutation commitments, and causal dependencies; (3) flag gaps and equivocation; (4) create a Merkle checkpoint covering named device frontiers; (5) co-sign or gossip it to other auditors; (6) resume idempotent rekey and emergency-coverage batches; (7) export reports with their exact completeness frontier.
   - **Atomic:** Each checkpoint and each item-level maintenance result.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Cross-device completeness, server receipts, and bulk coverage.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Events are “uncheckpointed” until covered; no global-now completeness claim.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** Concurrent checkpoints merge by taking both roots as parents; a missing offline device remains explicitly outstanding.

14. **Change a password at an external website**

   - **Inputs:** Item heads, website origin, generated password, and authenticated browser session.
   - **Authorization:** Item `edit` capability plus the user’s authority at the external site.
   - **Steps:** (1) Durably create an encrypted `ExternalChangeIntent` containing the old and candidate credentials; (2) submit the change to the site; (3) verify the new credential with a fresh authenticated request without discarding the old session; (4) on confirmed success, append an item revision and finalize the intent in one local transaction; (5) on timeout or ambiguous response, retain both candidates and enter reconciliation instead of retrying blindly.
   - **Atomic:** The arbitrary website and vault cannot be atomic; only preparation and later local finalization are atomic.
   - **Eventually consistent:** Vault synchronization and team audit delivery.
   - **Optimistic UI:** Show “changing at site,” not a new current password, until verification succeeds.
   - **Concurrent conflict:** A simultaneous credential-domain edit remains a conflict; site confirmation does not justify overwriting that branch.

### 6. Scale and cost

The design should support these concrete operating points:

- A personal account: 800 active items, 15,000 revisions, five devices, 10 GB of encrypted attachments, and one device returning after five years.
- A family: six people, twelve devices, 5,000 items, 50,000 revisions, and hundreds of distinct item audiences.
- A large team: 50,000 people, 150,000 devices, 5 million active items, 200 million retained revisions, and roughly 1 billion audit events per year.
- A successful service: 20 million active users, 60 million devices, billions of live items, tens of billions of immutable objects, and peak transfer bursts above 300,000 objects per second.
- A long-absent device may present 250,000 missing objects, obsolete formats, multiple policy epochs, and thousands of local revisions in one reconciliation.

The expensive paths are:

- Arbitrary item ACLs: direct per-device wrapping is proportional to item–principal–device edges; group epochs reduce ordinary fan-out but make removal a group rotation.
- Revoking one member from a group touching 100,000 items: the root group epoch is small, but current-state checkpoint coverage can require 100,000 item operations.
- Maintaining indefinite policy commitments, tombstones, device histories, and audit frontiers because no absent device can be assumed dead.
- Validating an ancient branch: policy-chain traversal, signature-suite compatibility, ancestor discovery, key bridging, and attachment transfer all occur together.
- Client-side search and authorization over millions of team items, requiring encrypted local indexes partitioned by readable audience.
- A blind server’s inability to distinguish useful ciphertext from item-unauthorized junk, requiring coarse account quotas, proof-of-storage capabilities, and rate limits.
- Audit fan-out: mutation events are modest, but optional view/copy telemetry can exceed content-write volume by two orders of magnitude.

Storage pricing must reflect retained encrypted history instead of silently imposing a reconciliation cutoff. User-directed compaction may discard old ciphertext after retaining commitments, but it must state which historical reads or ancient-device merges then require that returning device’s own branch package.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **The device is the commit authority.** Local transactions, IDs, keys, policies, and revision DAGs must remain complete without a server lease, and later sync must union histories rather than replay commands against a central row.

   A product could concede a 30-day offline lease or read-only offline mode. It becomes a cheat when described as indefinite offline read and write.

2. **Authorization is encrypted application data.** The backend can authenticate a transport session, store opaque blobs, and enforce quota, but only endpoints can decide whether a revision’s capability chain permits reading, writing, sharing, deleting, or merging an item.

   A product could give the server plaintext ACLs or an escrowed policy key. It becomes a cheat when it still claims that the server cannot evaluate item-level rules.

3. **Revocation is causal key evolution, not a mutable flag.** The system must rotate audience epochs, preserve concurrent old-authority branches, bridge still-authorized authors, quarantine removed authors, and expose incomplete rekey coverage.

   A product could reject every old-epoch upload or require online revocation checks. It becomes a cheat when it also promises both lossless ancient-device reconciliation and unbounded offline authority.

4. **Conflict state is durable product state.** Immutable multi-head items, structured conflict domains, delete-versus-edit handling, policy-conflict rules, permanent barriers, and human-authored merge revisions do not fit a single current row plus `updated_at`.

   A product could use last-writer-wins or discard late branches. It becomes a cheat when users are told no password change or deletion decision can be silently lost.

5. **Recovery, time, audit, and erasure require named authorities and bounded claims.** Threshold key recovery, offline emergency trustees, optional clock-bearing release witnesses, signed audit frontiers, and externally held travel restoration material must remain distinct from the ciphertext relay.

   A product could use provider escrow, server timers, mandatory online hardware attestation, or remote wipe. Those are legitimate disclosed products; they become cheats when labeled device-only recovery, outage-independent delayed access, complete offline-read audit, or guaranteed deletion from disconnected replicas.

### 8. Confidence

The foundation is standard practice: envelope encryption, independent device keys, signed device admission, per-item or per-audience key epochs, immutable encrypted records, causal DAGs, three-way merge, local encrypted indexes, outboxes, recovery kits, threshold secret sharing, hash/Merkle audit commitments, and best-effort cryptographic erasure.

The impossibility boundaries are also standard consequences of the threat model: copied plaintext cannot be revoked, an isolated device cannot observe remote revocation, clocks cannot establish when an offline edit occurred, total secret loss is unrecoverable without escrow, arbitrary external websites cannot join a vault transaction, and a plaintext-holding client cannot prove every read.

The exercise-specific parts are the exact combination and UX: stale-authority candidate branches, per-field conflict domains, removal-dominates policy merging, current-and-future sharing checkpoints, explicit rekey and emergency coverage, frontier-qualified audit reports, and travel mode as a distinct device identity. Those require formal protocol modeling, independent cryptographic review, adversarial implementation testing, and user research before production; they are product semantics, not a claim of a novel cryptographic primitive.

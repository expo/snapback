## Round 1 (verbatim)

## Verdict on the thesis
The measure — application-owned infrastructure removed — is the right primary target, and the thesis is broadly supported. The per-category evidence in `benchmarks/runs/2026-09-06-clique-improvement/evidence/astra-008-interim-holistic/score.md` is exactly a primitives-vs-composition split: Snapback leads on authorization (92 vs 82) and media (94 vs 76), is close on writes (89 vs 93) and background work (89 vs 88), but trails badly where composition lives — queries and live (65 vs 87), client integration (63 vs 83), diagnostics and dev loop (65 vs 87). That matches "primitives competitive, plumbing is the gap."

Two qualifications. First, the measure underweights the connected path. Convex's cited strength is "the cleanest mutation-to-subscription path" with "no invalidation plumbing," and its queries/live note stresses subscriptions propagating "without invalidation plumbing" plus shared watermarks. Removing offline workarounds alone does not close that gap; the ordinary online subscribe-render-revalidate loop must also require zero coordinator code. Second, the measure needs a companion: correction-loop cost. Setup/docs (68 vs 83) and diagnostics (65 vs 87) — stale-guide errors, opaque `E_OPAQUE` on the reactions collision, architectural isolation for delivery conflicts — are what turn plumbing gaps into blown budgets. I would keep the document's measure and add "fresh-builder correction loops attributable to Snapback" as a co-measure, consistent with the loop bar in `rules/NOT-DOING.md`.

One nuance the document handles well but could state louder: offline sync is a Snapback lead (68 vs 48 — Convex and Supabase both required app-owned offline subsystems). The problem is not the offline primitives but that the lead does not survive integration: the same run still required "an app-owned inbox cache and explicit resume anchor."

## The four moves (keep / change / drop / add)
**Move one (client coherence): keep, first.** The inbox-cache / resume-anchor / second-tab-database findings are application architecture changes, not verbosity, and they directly explain the client-integration and offline-sync deltas. This is the largest structural opportunity. Agree with high confidence on need.

**Move two (execution and certification agree): keep, but reframe as cost-follows-plan.** The cap-metering ticket (`issues/20260906-snapback2-cap-metering-makes-multi-row-inserts-uncertifiable-at-large-caps.md`) and the feed-ratio history are genuine composition failures: a declared bound on one axis (memberships per principal) vetoes atomic work on another (members per conversation). But "maintained cardinality checks" and "shared rule probes" carry their own write-maintenance costs that must be priced, per the document's own warning. I would scope this to: no natural bounded Clique write certifies at a cost its execution does not actually pay. Do not let this become a general optimizer project.

**Move three (Quarry owns semantics): keep but narrow sharply.** The reactions collision (`issues/20260906-snapback2-quarry-operation-table-name-collision.md`) is real leakage of lowering identity into the public language, and the search-postings episode (client-authored index maintenance changing completeness guarantees) is the larger instance. But the typed-IR-vs-strengthened-lowering comparison should be explicitly biased toward the bounded fix first: distinct generated binding identities, source-located diagnostics, preserved effects/once-only/refusal/twin-agreement/check-budget. A full direct-lowering rewrite is not justified by one collision. Keep the `fn` deferral.

**Move four (whole-application evolution): change to defer.** The survivor-uniqueness trigger (`issues/20260906-snapback2-rebuilt-survivor-uniqueness.md`) is, by its own record, a precise static finding with no runtime reproduction, and the score file states "M7 was not run in any lane." Evolution coherence is real work, but it has near-zero demonstrated Clique score impact today. Keep the direction, defer implementation behind moves one and two and the loop bar.

**Add (missing):** (a) the React hook / query-composition surface — the document names "hundreds of different per-row queries" and "enriched pages and query composition" but assigns them no owner; per-row query fan-out and the inbox-vs-thread-vs-search composition is where builders felt the queries/live gap, and `snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/react-core.tsx`, `drain.ts`, `live.ts` deserve explicit treatment; (b) diagnostics and dev-loop actionability as a first-class slice (opaque module-first-line failures, delivery-conflict isolation recipes, resume-refusal rewrites), given 65 vs 87; (c) guide worked examples for drain/inbox-restore/multi-tab, given setup/docs 68 vs 83 — this is small, high-leverage, and currently homeless across the four moves.

## Ordering and the first slice
Agree with client coherence first, then certified composition; Quarry simplification third but in bounded form; evolution last. That matches both confidence and score opportunity, and respects `rules/RULES.md` and `rules/NOT-DOING.md` (no new scope without a trade).

The highest-leverage one-to-three-day slice: **make the packaged client do inbox-drain → thread-coverage → predicted-send → restart → revocation → reconnect with zero builder-owned cache, anchor, or polling loop, on React web, plus a documented two-tab policy.** Concretely it removes from the builder's plate: the separate inbox client/cache, the permanent ascending resume anchor, the corrective fresh time-dependent query paths, and the per-tab database scoping with its unmerged secondary queue. The witness is the document's own success criterion: cold offline reads/search, predicted writes, restart, revocation, reconnect, and two tabs recovering both queues. That is visible in the next Clique run because it deletes named files and loops from the lane rather than improving a ratio. Second priority, in parallel only if capacity allows: the 49-row-into-`cap-1000` regression fixture must certify atomically, since non-atomic 50-member group creation is the most legible composition failure after the client.

## Move one: decomposition
The proposed primitive set — one transactional ownership boundary; delivery identity separating durable changes from clock reevaluation; platform-owned tab policy; shared subscriptions plus composition — is the right set at the right level. I would not replace it with a single-log-plus-single-reducer or a leader-tab-with-thin-mirrors as the mandated decomposition; both are candidate implementations of the same boundary, and the document is right to leave leader-election vs shared-storage undecided while demanding the observable policy (both queues survive, writer-close recovers, documented).

On current code shape, from a time-boxed skim: the direction of travel is right and the fragmentation is the problem. `snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/offline.ts` (862 lines) already centralizes the runtime, `store/range-store.ts` (1,274 lines) owns the swap abstraction the document wants extended to versions/coverage/answers/outbox/invalidation, `store/outbox.ts` exists as the settlement ledger, `live.ts` (215 lines, `SharedSse`) already shares one physical stream, and `drain.ts` (215 lines) owns watermark-then-tail. But `client.ts` (1,490 lines, at the 1,500-line file cap in `rules/RULES.md`) sits atop `client-offline.ts`, `client-outbox.ts`, `client-auth.ts`, `client-wire.ts`, plus `react-core.tsx` (448 lines) — coordination spread across at least six modules with the transactional boundary the document calls for not yet enforced in one place. The delivery-identity episode shows why the boundary matters: ordering by execution read-time rather than durable sequence alone. Where the code fights itself most is exactly the seam the document names: row versions, coverage facts, held answers, outbox settlement, and invalidation each have a home, but no single swap owns their joint transition, so the builder reimplemented the joint (the inbox cache and anchor). I did not audit `twin.ts` / `twin-hydrate.ts` line-by-line; I did not check whether the twin-hydration path already has the right reducer shape to absorb this.

Query composition, not subscription dedup, is the harder half of the fourth bullet and should be promoted: dedup of identical subscriptions (`live.ts` sharing) cannot fix N distinct per-row queries; enriched pages / composed queries that ride drained coverage can.

## Biggest risk and what to cut
The largest effort-without-outcome risk is the typed Quarry IR path becoming a compiler rewrite that preserves every invariant (effects, once-only evaluation, refusals, native/twin agreement, subsecond check) while the Clique outcome still hinges on the client and certificate. The document hedges correctly ("a rewrite is not established as a prerequisite"), but an orchestrator primed to implement will read "evaluate a typed IR" as license. Cut or defer: any direct-lowering rebuild until the bounded hygiene fix (distinct identities, source locations) lands and a fresh census shows remaining semantic-leakage cost; all of move four beyond rehearsing existing operator-guide/J2 machinery on a populated app; and any new registry, gate, or digest apparatus — the document rightly proposes none, and `rules/NOT-DOING.md` forbids it without a trade.

Also watch the byte budget: the R8 note of a 72,555-byte gzip candidate against the 66,000-byte ceiling (`rules/NOT-DOING.md` fixed complete-runtime boundary) means "shared ownership and incremental updates" is a constraint, not just a preference. Any client consolidation that grows the local runtime past ceiling fails the offline bar it serves.

## Summary for the author (one paragraph)
Keep the thesis and its measure, add correction-loop cost as a co-measure, and hold the order: client coherence first, certified composition second, bounded Quarry hygiene third, evolution deferred. The first slice should delete the builder-owned inbox cache, resume anchor, polling loops, and per-tab database workaround in one documented packaged-client behavior including a two-tab queue-recovery policy — that is the visible Clique win. Constrain move two to cost-follows-plan with the 49-row atomic regression as its witness, shrink move three to binding identity plus source-located errors with the IR rewrite explicitly deferred, and do not let move four or any new apparatus consume the window; update the clock-ticket premise to repaired-on-main before the orchestrator plans from it.

## Round 2 (verbatim)

## (a) Tab policy — verdict and the sharpest objection

**Verdict: keep the minimal policy, but narrow the promise.** Waiting on the Web Lock with an online-only follower beats Gemini/Astra's BroadcastChannel mirroring (a second protocol plus a second retained copy against the 66,000-byte gzip ceiling) and beats Grok's build-nothing (the per-tab-database hack stays). But it does **not** pass the second-tab issue's own regression — two tabs, one offline write each, both settle (`issues/20260906-snapback2-second-browser-tab-cannot-share-the-offline-carrier.md`): follower offline writes refuse by design, so only the *leader's* queue recovers. Say that plainly or the next diary scores the refusal as a bug.

**Sharpest objection: the follower is a behavioral fork, not a degraded mode.** The same component code observes different contracts per tab: no held answers, no outbox, so `useOutbox` entries/`ready` and `useMutation` pending (`snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/react-core.tsx`) mean something different, and every offline write takes an error branch the happy path never exercises. "Online-only until takeover" is honest only if the follower announces itself in the existing status vocabulary (a new `OutboxStatus.reason`, cf. `snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/client-outbox.ts`) so queue-count UI can branch — silent-empty is a trap. Two further breaks: account transition while waiting (follower waits on partition A, user re-authenticates as B — takeover must be gated on same-principal unseal per the `client-offline.ts` rewrite, else it blocks on or opens the wrong partition; I did not check the waiting path handles auth-generation change), and leader crash vs close (the lock releases on both, but crash can interrupt a carrier swap; I did not check IndexedDB atomicity/recovery on that path — `snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/store/indexeddb.ts`, `snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/store/locks.ts`). StrictMode double-create is the non-problem: `react-core.tsx` already pins creation in a ref with deferred close; cross-tab locking is `navigator.locks`, a different mechanism.

## (b) Cap metering — verdict and the sharpest objection

**Verdict: keep the maintained-count probe; per-distinct-key alone is arithmetically dead.** The benchmark case is 49 inserts under `cap 1000 by principal` — 49 *distinct* keys — so charging N once per key still refuses at `check`. Reading the maintained row (one examined row, exact, reserve 1) is the honest fix, with Twin falling back to today's scan when the row isn't held.

**Sharpest objection: "exact at probe time" is an ordering claim I did not verify.** The maintained row must reflect the *current transaction's own writes* (creator seat plus 49-member loop in one `groupCreate`) before `check_constraints` reads it; otherwise the probe reads stale and admits over-cap or refuses valid. Rollback, the seed path (skips rules but not constraints, LLP 1017 §2.3), and successor rebuild (§2.5 counts base rows, never index entries) must all recompute rather than trust counts — successor admission especially must not read a maintained row the migration's drops already invalidated. I did not check maintain-vs-check ordering in the engine code. Secondarily, requiring a `maintain` declaration couples certification to a second declaration; acceptable only with the planned `check` rewrite pasting the exact line — a hidden engine-owned count would instead tax every capped write whether probed or not.

## One thing in another panelist's position that is wrong and matters

Muse's premise that row versions, coverage, held answers, and outbox settlement have "no single swap" owning "their joint transition" — contradicted by `OfflineRuntime.commitMany` serializing deliveries, watermarks, and settlements into one `store.commit` swap (`snapback2/packages/snapback2/src/offline.ts`), the boundary Astra, Claude, and Grok all agree already exists. It matters because that premise licenses the range-store rewrite the panel converged to cut.

## Anything that should change my direction (or "nothing")

Three narrow amendments, otherwise nothing: (1) give follower mode a first-class waiting status reason in the `useOutbox`/`useMutation` contract, documented as degraded; (2) gate takeover on same-principal unseal and specify account-switch-while-waiting; (3) state up front that the two-offline-tabs regression stays open with forwarding as a ticket. Keep move-one session work and the cap-first-slice ordering.

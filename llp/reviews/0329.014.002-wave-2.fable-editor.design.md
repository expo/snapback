# Tessera — collaborative documents and whiteboards

Archetype: collaborative editor / whiteboard. Axis under test: **merge semantics
beyond optimistic concurrency and last-writer-wins** — concurrent edits must
*compose*. The design is written in neutral product / data-model / operation
terms so a later implementer can build it without guessing.

The design's central move: a document is **one identity-addressed sequence** of
items (characters, block breaks, mark boundaries, shapes), and *every* user
action — typing, formatting, splitting a paragraph, dragging a section, undo,
restoring a version — is an ordinary operation on that sequence. Nothing is
ever a snapshot overwrite, nothing is ever a physical delete, and no position is
ever a number.

### 1. Product contract

Tessera is a workspace tool. Teams write documents (headings, paragraphs,
lists with nesting, to-dos, code, images) and draw on whiteboards (shapes,
sticky notes, connectors, groups) together — in real time, offline, and from
several devices at once. Users are workspace members holding a per-document
role (owner, editor, commenter, viewer) and link guests (viewer or commenter).

Promises users would state:

- **P1.** Everyone with a document open sees each other's keystrokes within
  about a second; no one is ever blocked from typing by someone else's typing.
- **P2.** Two people editing the same sentence at once both keep their text.
  The result contains both, in a stable order, with no conflict dialog and no
  duplicated paragraph.
- **P3.** Editing works fully offline — typing, formatting, reordering,
  commenting, drawing — and merges on reconnect without losing anything you or
  anyone else did meanwhile.
- **P4.** Your own two devices (laptop offline on a plane, phone offline in a
  cab) can both edit the same document; both sets of edits survive the merge and
  you are never shown a "conflict copy" of your own paragraph.
- **P5.** Undo undoes *your* last action on *this device*, and never removes
  anything another person — or your other device — wrote.
- **P6.** Dragging a block (a paragraph, a section, a list item with its
  children) to a new place keeps whatever anyone typed into it meanwhile; a
  block is never duplicated or lost by a drag.
- **P7.** Formatting composes: bold from one person and italic from another on
  overlapping text both apply.
- **P8.** Comments stick to the text they were written about through any
  surrounding edits; if that text is deleted the comment survives in the margin
  showing what it quoted.
- **P9.** Collaborators' cursors and selections are drawn at the right place
  even while you hold unsynced edits of your own.
- **P10.** Presence shows who is looking *now*; someone who closed the tab or
  lost access disappears within 30 seconds.
- **P11.** Every past state of the document is viewable for 90 days (named
  versions forever) with per-character authorship. Restoring one is itself an
  edit: others keep working through it, and you can undo it.
- **P12.** Deleted content is recoverable from history for at least 90 days.
- **P13.** Mentioning someone notifies them. If they lack access you are asked
  to share first (if you may); otherwise they receive an access request that
  carries no document content.
- **P14.** Sharing changes take effect immediately: a revoked person's open tab
  stops updating within seconds, their cursor vanishes for everyone, and edits
  they made offline are refused on arrival and kept on their device — never
  merged, never silently dropped.
- **P15.** Link guests see exactly what the link grants: nothing about
  internal comments, including their counts.
- **P16.** On a whiteboard, one person moving a shape while another recolors it
  yields a moved, recolored shape; a resize racing a move yields one or the
  other, never a blend; connectors never dangle.
- **P17.** A device offline for more than 30 days is not silently merged; its
  pending changes are quarantined for manual recovery.
- **P18.** History orders edits by when the service accepted them; an edit
  authored offline appears where it arrived, labelled "authored 2h earlier,
  offline".

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: **[S]** secrecy, **[T]** time, **[O]** other people's data. (No money in
this archetype.)

1. Once two replicas hold the same set of accepted operations they render the
   same document, character for character, regardless of the order the
   operations arrived. [T]
2. No accepted keystroke is ever lost: every accepted insert is visible or
   recoverable from history for 90 days. [T]
3. Concurrent inserts into the same paragraph produce a paragraph containing
   both texts, each contiguous, in one order every replica agrees on.
4. A character, block, shape, comment, or mark keeps its identity for life;
   no operation ever re-creates content under a new identity.
5. Undo on a device reverts only operations authored on that device, one
   action at a time, and never hides another actor's content. [O]
6. Moving a block never duplicates or loses it, and anything typed into it
   concurrently moves with it.
7. The rendered document is always a single well-formed sequence: no moved
   range is ever rendered twice, zero times, or inside itself.
8. A comment's anchor is a pair of character identities; it never drifts, and
   a comment whose text is fully deleted is shown orphaned with its quote.
9. Formatting is resolved per character per mark type; concurrent formatting
   by two actors both apply where they do not contradict, and where they do the
   later-ordered one wins deterministically.
10. History is ordered by service acceptance sequence; the state at any past
    sequence number is reproducible and attributes every visible character to
    its author. [T] [O]
11. A restore is an ordinary batch of operations: edits accepted after it
    compose with it, and it is undoable like any other action. [T]
12. Content is delivered — by query, live update, presence, notification, or
    mention preview — only to principals holding an unexpired, unrevoked grant
    at the moment of delivery. [S] [O]
13. Revocation and link expiry disconnect live sessions within 10 seconds and
    remove the principal's presence from every other viewer. [S] [T]
14. Operations arriving from a principal without a current grant are refused
    with the content preserved on the sending device. [S] [T]
15. Presence is visible only to principals who share access, and only while
    the session has heartbeated within 30 seconds. [O]
16. Counts and badges shown to a viewer are computed over what that viewer may
    see; a guest's "3 comments" never reveals an internal thread exists. [S]
17. A mention notification carries document content only if the recipient
    holds a grant at delivery time, re-checked at delivery, not at posting. [S]
18. A shape's geometry (x, y, w, h, rotation) is one value: two concurrent
    geometry edits yield one of them intact; geometry and style edits compose.
19. A connector is rendered only while both endpoints are rendered; undoing an
    endpoint's deletion re-renders the connector.
20. A device whose last acknowledged sequence is older than 30 days is expired;
    its queued operations are refused and quarantined, and only then may the
    service forget tombstones older than the surviving devices' frontier. [T]

### 3. The traps, ranked

**Trap 1 — merge: a position is not a number.** *Wrong version:* text is a
string per paragraph; edits are `insert(offset, text)` / `delete(offset, n)`;
comments, cursors, and marks store offsets. *Why wrong:* an offset is only
meaningful against the exact state the author saw. Two concurrent inserts at
offsets 40 and 45 corrupt each other; every comment above an edit drifts one
word; a collaborator's cursor lands mid-word. Operational transformation
repairs this only with a central server and no offline queue longer than the
transform can handle. *Exposing observation:* two users type into the same
sentence at once, or one device inserts a paragraph above a commented range
while the other is offline; after merge the comment highlights the wrong
words. The design gives every character an identity `(replica, seq, index)`
and an `after` pointer; comments, cursors, and marks reference identities and
are resolved to screen positions locally.

**Trap 2 — merge and structure: moving a block is not delete-plus-insert,
and reorders can form cycles.** *Wrong version (a):* drag = delete the block's
text and re-insert it at the destination under new identities. Anyone typing
into the block concurrently either loses the text or sees it appear at the
*old* location next to tombstones; comments anchored inside are orphaned;
undo history splits. *Wrong version (b):* blocks carry `parent_id` and a sort
key with last-writer-wins. Two concurrent drags — A nests section 2 under 3, B
nests 3 under 2 — produce a cycle; both sections vanish from render on every
device and nothing reports it. *Exposing observation:* start typing in a
paragraph on one device while another device drags that paragraph elsewhere;
and the crossed-drag test. The design keeps identities under a range-move
override (§4) and makes the service a sequencer for moves alone, refusing the
one that would nest a range inside itself — loudly, to the losing device.

**Trap 3 — concurrency and authority: undo is an inverse of *my* operation on
*this replica*, not a return to a previous state.** *Wrong version (a):* undo
restores the previous snapshot — B's concurrent typing disappears. *Wrong
version (b):* undo stacks keyed by user id — Alice's laptop undoes the
paragraph her phone wrote. *Wrong version (c):* undo of "bold" issues
"un-bold" — which also strips the bold B applied to a superset. *Why wrong:*
the unit of authorship is the replica, not the user, and the inverse must
compose with everything that happened since. *Exposing observation:* A types,
B types after it, A presses undo: B's text must survive. Alice's two offline
devices sync, then laptop presses undo twice: only laptop actions revert. The
design implements undo as retraction operations that name the operation they
retract.

**Trap 4 — temporal: there are two clocks, and a version is not a snapshot.**
*Wrong version:* history is a table of snapshots stamped with wall-clock
arrival time; "restore 3:00 pm" overwrites the document. *Why wrong:* an
offline device that syncs at 5:00 pm carries edits authored at 2:40 pm;
whether the "3:00 pm version" contains them depends on when you ask. An
overwrite discards whatever B typed between the restore's start and its
landing, and cannot be undone without another overwrite. *Exposing
observation:* view the version list, sync an offline device, view it again; or
restore a version while a collaborator is mid-sentence. The design orders
history by acceptance sequence, shows author time as a label, and computes a
restore as a *diff expressed as operations*.

**Trap 5 — visibility and authority: a live subscription, a presence row, an
offline queue, and a mention all outlive a grant unless something re-checks.**
*Wrong version:* access is checked when the document is opened, when the
notification is created, when the offline batch was authored. *Why wrong:*
grants are intervals. Bob revoked at 10:00 keeps receiving every keystroke on
his open tab; his cursor stays on everyone's screen; his 30-minute offline
batch is accepted at 10:05 because his session token is still valid;
"@carol" in an internal thread mails Carol the quoted paragraph though she was
never granted access; the guest's badge reads "3 comments" when the guest can
see one. *Exposing observation:* revoke a user with a tab open and a phone
offline; mention an outsider; compare a guest's badge to a member's. The design
re-authorizes on every delivery, keys presence to grant-checked sessions, and
resolves notifications at delivery time.

**Trap 6 — merge: the row is not the unit of conflict, and a container's
deletion must not swallow concurrent children.** *Wrong version (a):* a shape
is a row; any edit rewrites the row → B's recolor snaps the shape back to
where it was before A's drag. *Wrong version (b):* every column is its own
last-writer-wins register → A's resize (x, y, w, h) and B's move (x, y)
interleave into a squashed shape neither made. *Wrong version (c):* deleting a
block physically removes its text → the paragraph B typed into it during the
delete is gone and history shows nothing. *Exposing observation:* drag while
another user recolors; resize while another moves; type into a block another
device is deleting, then undo the delete on that device — B's text must come
back with it. The design groups fields into *intent registers* (geometry is one
value, fill and stroke are separate values) and makes deletion a retractable
tombstone.

### 4. Data model

Types are neutral: `id` (opaque), `seq` (64-bit ordered), `text`, `int`,
`bool`, `time`, `json`. Composite identity **OpId** = `(replica_id, replica_seq)`.
**ItemId** = `(replica_id, replica_seq, index)` — one op that inserts a run of
*n* characters creates items index `0..n-1`; every non-text item has a run of
length 1.

**Durable, mutable-by-grant**

- `workspaces(id, name)`
- `users(id, workspace_id, display_name)`
- `documents(id, workspace_id, kind: 'doc'|'board', created_by, created_at,
  deleted_at?)`. Derived: `head_seq` (last accepted seq), `snapshot_seq`,
  `prune_horizon_seq`.
- `grants(id, document_id, principal_kind: 'user'|'workspace'|'link',
  principal_id, role: 'owner'|'editor'|'commenter'|'viewer', granted_by,
  granted_at, expires_at?, revoked_at?, revoked_by?)`. **Time-bounded and
  append-only**: revocation writes `revoked_at`; a re-grant is a new row. "Held
  access at time *t*" is a query over this table.
- `share_links(id, document_id, token_hash, grant_id, expires_at, revoked_at?)`.
- `replicas(id, user_id, device_label, created_at, expired_at?)`.
- `replica_documents(replica_id, document_id, last_ack_seq, last_seen_at)` —
  the per-document frontier of each device; drives pruning and expiry.
- `sessions(id, user_id?, link_id?, replica_id, document_id, grant_id,
  connected_at)` — a live connection bound to the grant that admitted it.

**Append-only operation log** (the document's source of truth)

- `ops(document_id, accepted_seq, op_id: OpId UNIQUE, author_user_id, lamport:
  int, authored_at: time (device clock, untrusted), accepted_at: time,
  batch_id?, atomic: bool, kind, payload: json)`. Primary key
  `(document_id, accepted_seq)`; `accepted_seq` is per-document, dense,
  assigned by the service. Retained 90 days, plus any range covered by a named
  version.

Op kinds and payloads (`after` is an ItemId or `ROOT`):

| kind | payload | composes by |
|---|---|---|
| `insert` | `after, items: [{kind: 'char'\|'break'\|'shape'\|'mark_open'\|'mark_close', text?, props?}]` | identity + tree order |
| `delete` | `ranges: [{run: OpId, from: int, to: int}]` | tombstone set |
| `retract` | `target: OpId` (a `delete`, `move`, or `mark` op) | tombstone set |
| `set` | `item: ItemId, register: text, value: json` | LWW by `(lamport, replica_id)` |
| `move` | `first: ItemId, last: ItemId, dest_after: ItemId` | sequenced by the service |
| `thread`, `comment`, `resolve` | see comment tables | append / LWW |

**Materialized state** (derived from `ops`; recomputable; what queries read)

- `items(document_id, run: OpId, index_from, index_to, after: ItemId, lamport,
  replica_id, author_user_id, kind, text?, hidden_count: int)`. Runs may be
  physically split at any index without changing identities. `hidden_count` is
  the number of unretracted `delete` ops covering the item; visible iff 0.
- `deletions(document_id, delete_op: OpId, run: OpId, from, to, retracted: bool)`.
- `registers(document_id, item: ItemId, register, value, set_op: OpId, lamport,
  replica_id)` — one row per register per item, holding the current winner.
  Registers on a `break` item: `block_kind`, `indent`, `checked`, `language`,
  `image_ref`. On a `shape` item: `shape_kind`, **`geometry`** (one value:
  `{x, y, w, h, rotation}`), `fill`, `stroke`, `opacity`, `group_id`,
  `from_shape`, `to_shape` (connectors), `from_side`, `to_side`.
- `moves(document_id, move_op: OpId, first: ItemId, last: ItemId, dest_after:
  ItemId, accepted_seq, retracted: bool)`.
- `mark_pairs(document_id, open: ItemId, close: ItemId, mark_type, value?,
  lamport, replica_id)` — derived from `mark_open`/`mark_close` items.
- `snapshots(document_id, at_seq, blob, created_at)` — materialized state at
  `at_seq`; every 1,000 ops.
- `named_versions(id, document_id, at_seq, name, created_by, created_at)`.

**Comments and notifications**

- `threads(id, document_id, anchor_start: ItemId, anchor_end: ItemId,
  quoted_text, audience: 'members'|'everyone', created_by, created_at,
  resolved: bool, resolved_set_op?, deleted_at?)`. Derived: `orphaned` = both
  anchors hidden; `partial` = one hidden.
- `comments(id, thread_id, author_id, body, created_at, edited_at?,
  deleted_at?)` — append-only apart from `body` (register) and `deleted_at`.
- `mentions(id, comment_id, mentioned_user_id, created_at, state: 'pending'|
  'delivered'|'withheld'|'cancelled', delivered_at?)`.
- `notifications(id, user_id, kind, document_id?, payload, created_at,
  read_at?)` — `payload` is composed at delivery time and never copies content
  the recipient may not read.
- `access_requests(id, document_id, requester_id, requested_by_mention?,
  state, created_at)`.

**Ephemeral** (never durable, never in history)

- `presence(document_id, session_id, user_id?, guest_label?, replica_id,
  anchor: {item: ItemId, side: 'before'|'after'}?, focus: {…}?, color,
  last_heartbeat)`.

**Ordering rule (the whole merge, stated once).** The document is the pre-order
traversal of a tree rooted at `ROOT`: an item's children are the items whose
`after` is that item, sorted by `(lamport desc, replica_id desc)`; item
`index+1` of a run is an implicit child of item `index` with the run's lamport.
Hidden items are skipped when rendering but remain in the traversal (a hidden
item can still be an `after`). A block is a `break` item plus the visible items
up to the next `break`; the block's registers live on its `break`. A board's
z-order is sequence order; shapes have no text unless followed by chars.
Active (unretracted) moves are applied during traversal: on reaching `first`,
the traversal skips through `last` and through any items that follow `last`
before the next `break` positioned after `last` (so concurrent typing at the
block's end and concurrent splits inside it travel with the block); on reaching
`dest_after` the skipped range is emitted after it. A later move of the same
`first` supersedes earlier ones by `accepted_seq`. A character's format for
mark type *m* is the value of the unhidden pair (`open` before it, `close`
after it in rendered order) with the highest `(lamport, replica_id)`, or none.

Time-bounded values: `grants` rows, `share_links.expires_at`, `replicas`
expiry (30 days from `last_seen_at`), `snapshots`/`ops` retention (90 days),
presence (30 s).

### 5. The hard operations

Conventions. **Auth** names the role required, checked against `grants` at the
moment of the call. **Atomic** is a single service transaction. Client ops are
stamped `lamport = 1 + max lamport seen`, `replica_seq` strictly increasing, and
sent in batches at most every 200 ms; the service assigns `accepted_seq`.
Rejections name the op and a code; the sending device keeps the content.

**Op 1 — Open a document and subscribe.** *Inputs:* `document_id`,
`replica_id`, `last_ack_seq` (0 for first open), session credential (user or
link token). *Auth:* any grant with `revoked_at` null and `expires_at` in the
future; creates a `sessions` row bound to that `grant_id`. *Steps:* (1) find the
latest snapshot with `at_seq ≤ head_seq`; (2) if `last_ack_seq ≥ snapshot_seq`
send only `ops` in `(last_ack_seq, head_seq]`, else send the snapshot plus ops
after it; (3) upsert `replica_documents.last_seen_at`; if the replica's
`last_ack_seq` is older than `prune_horizon_seq`, respond `REPLICA_EXPIRED`
(client runs Op 5's quarantine branch); (4) register the session for live
delivery of every op with `accepted_seq > head_seq` and for presence of the
document; (5) client materializes, then re-applies its own pending ops on top
(see Op 5). *Atomic:* steps 1–4 read one consistent `head_seq`. *Eventual:*
none — a gap between snapshot and subscription is a correctness bug. *Optimistic
UI:* the locally cached replica renders immediately, marked "syncing".
*Concurrent conflict:* ops accepted between step 1 and step 4 must be delivered
by step 4 (subscribe at the read's `head_seq`, not "now").

**Op 2 — Submit an edit batch** (typing, deleting, formatting, splitting and
joining paragraphs, setting registers, deleting and restoring blocks).
*Inputs:* `document_id`, `replica_id`, ordered ops with `replica_seq`,
`lamport`, `authored_at`, optional `batch_id` + `atomic`. *Auth:* editor or
owner via the session's grant, re-read in the transaction; commenters may only
submit `thread`/`comment`/`resolve`. *Steps, in one transaction per batch:*
(1) re-check the grant; if revoked/expired reject the batch `NO_GRANT`; (2)
check `replica_seq` continues the replica's last accepted seq — otherwise
`OUT_OF_ORDER`; (3) for each op verify every referenced ItemId/OpId exists in
`items`/`ops`; a reference to a pruned identity is `STALE_REFERENCE`, any
other missing reference is `MISSING_DEPENDENCY`; (4) assign consecutive
`accepted_seq`, append to `ops`; (5) apply to materialized tables: `insert` →
`items` (a split of the containing run if `after` is mid-run; a `break` insert
is a paragraph split, a `delete` of a `break` is a join); `delete` →
`deletions` + `hidden_count`; `retract` of a delete → `retracted = true`,
`hidden_count -= 1`; `set` → replace the row iff `(lamport, replica_id)` is
higher than the current winner; `mark_open/close` → `mark_pairs`; (6) update
`replica_documents.last_ack_seq`; (7) publish the ops to subscribers. If
`atomic` and any op fails, roll back the whole batch. *Atomic:* all of it.
*Eventual:* search index, thread `orphaned` flags (recomputed within seconds).
*Optimistic UI:* the device applies every op locally before sending; the render
is identical to the eventual server render because the ordering rule is
deterministic and independent of arrival order. *Concurrent conflict:* none for
text and marks — they commute. Registers: the loser's value is visible in
history. Deletes of a block with concurrent typing: the typing lands inside the
hidden range and is restored by `retract` (Trap 6).

**Op 3 — Move a block or range** (drag a paragraph, a section with its
children, a list item; "bring to front" on a board). *Inputs:* `first` (the
`break`/`shape` item), `last` (the last item of the range as the mover saw
it), `dest_after`. *Auth:* editor. *Steps (service, in the same transaction
pipeline as Op 2 but sequenced):* (1) validate references; (2) compute the
current rendered order; (3) refuse `MOVE_CYCLE` if `dest_after` lies inside
the range `[first … last+tail]` or inside any active move's range that would,
transitively, be emitted inside this one (walk the "dest inside range"
graph; refuse on a cycle); (4) append the op, insert into `moves`, superseding
an earlier active move of the same `first`; (5) publish. *Atomic:* 1–4.
*Optimistic UI:* the client applies the move locally and shows it; on
`MOVE_CYCLE` it re-derives its state from the authoritative sequence, drops the
move, and shows "Couldn't move: it would nest a section inside itself".
*Concurrent conflict:* two moves of the same block → the later-accepted
placement wins, the earlier mover sees the block jump, nothing duplicates.
Move racing a delete → block is hidden at the destination and reappears there
if the delete is retracted. Move racing typing or a split inside the block →
both travel with it (§4 ordering rule).

**Op 4 — Undo / redo** (client-derived; submitted through Op 2/3).
*Inputs:* the device's undo stack — a list of *actions*, each a list of OpIds
authored by this replica plus, for `set`, the value they replaced. *Auth:*
same as the ops produced. *Steps (client):* pop the last action and emit its
inverse as new ops: `insert` → `delete` of exactly those items; `delete` →
`retract(delete_op)`; `set` → if `registers.set_op` is still one of mine,
`set(previous value)`, else no-op ("changed by someone else since"); mark →
`delete` of its `open` and `close` items; `move` → `retract(move_op)`;
`retract` → re-issue the original op's effect as a new op. Push the inverse
onto the redo stack. Redo of an undone insert is `retract` of the undo's
delete, so **identities are preserved and concurrent comments and cursors on
that text survive the round-trip**. *Atomic:* one action = one `atomic` batch.
*Optimistic UI:* immediate. *Concurrent conflict:* undoing my delete while B
also deleted the same chars leaves them hidden (`hidden_count` still 1);
undoing my bold while B bolded a superset leaves B's bold. Cross-device: the
phone's stack never contains laptop ops, so Alice cannot undo her other device
(Trap 3).

**Op 5 — Reconnect an offline device** (bulk). *Inputs:* pending ops (may be
thousands), `last_ack_seq`. *Auth:* current grant, checked before anything is
accepted. *Steps:* (1) Op 1 to obtain missed ops; the client rebases: reset
local state to the authoritative sequence, then re-apply pending ops in
`replica_seq` order, skipping any now-cyclic move and telling the user; (2)
submit pending ops in ≤ 500-op batches through Op 2/3; (3) on `NO_GRANT` or
`REPLICA_EXPIRED`, keep the pending ops locally, render the device's last state
read-only under a banner "Your offline changes could not be merged — Recover",
and offer an export of the pending text (the plain text of every pending
insert, grouped by block) — never discard; (4) on success, clear the pending
queue, keep the undo stack (it references accepted OpIds). *Atomic:* per batch.
*Eventual:* the user may watch others' edits land around theirs during step 2.
*Concurrent conflict:* composes by design; the same user's other device is just
another replica (Trap 3, P4).

**Op 6 — Create a comment thread with mentions** (spans the notification
providers). *Inputs:* `anchor_start`, `anchor_end` (ItemIds; or a `break`/
`shape` item for block comments), `body` with mention tokens, `audience`.
*Auth:* commenter or above; `audience = 'members'` requires a member grant (a
link guest may only post `'everyone'`). *Steps:* (1) verify both anchors exist
and are visible; capture `quoted_text` from the rendered range (≤ 200 chars);
(2) insert `threads` + first `comments` row; (3) for each mention: if the
mentioned user holds a grant → `mentions.state = 'pending'`; else if the
caller may share (editor/owner) → the UI asked *before* submit and the batch
includes a `grants` insert (viewer or commenter, caller's choice) then
`'pending'`; else create `access_requests` and set `'withheld'`; (4) enqueue
delivery (Op 7). *Atomic:* 1–3 and the enqueue. *Eventual:* delivery.
*Optimistic UI:* thread appears immediately. *Concurrent conflict:* the anchor
text may be deleted concurrently → thread renders orphaned with its quote.
Resolve racing a reply: `resolved` is LWW, the reply appends; the thread shows
resolved with a new reply.

**Op 7 — Deliver notifications** (background; external push/email providers).
*Inputs:* `mentions` in `'pending'`, batched every 5 s. *Auth:* system. *Steps
per mention:* (1) re-read the recipient's grant for the document *now*; if none
→ `'withheld'` and (if none exists) an `access_requests` row; (2) if the
comment or thread was deleted → `'cancelled'`; (3) compose `payload` from the
current comment body and document title (both readable under the recipient's
grant; a guest-audience check applies to `'members'` threads); (4) insert
`notifications` and call the provider with idempotency key `mention.id`;
(5) mark `'delivered'`. *Atomic:* 1–5 per mention in one transaction; the
provider call is at-least-once with the idempotency key, so a crash between 4
and 5 re-sends the same key. *Eventual:* delivery latency. *Concurrent
conflict:* a revocation between posting and delivery wins (Trap 5).

**Op 8 — Presence heartbeat and cursor broadcast** (ephemeral write + fan-out
read). *Inputs:* `session_id`, `anchor`, `focus` (ItemIds, not offsets),
every ≤ 100 ms while moving, heartbeat every 5 s. *Auth:* the session's grant,
re-validated by the service every 10 s and on every grant change. *Steps:*
(1) upsert the in-memory presence row; (2) fan out to sessions of the same
document whose grants are current; guests are shown as "Guest" with a color;
(3) receivers resolve the anchor against *their own* rendered sequence — this
is what keeps cursors correct while the receiver holds unsynced ops; an anchor
on a hidden item resolves to the nearest visible neighbor; (4) rows with
`last_heartbeat` older than 30 s are dropped and a removal broadcast. *Atomic:*
none. *Eventual:* all of it. *Concurrent conflict:* none; last heartbeat wins.

**Op 9 — Revoke a grant / expire a share link** (write + scheduled sweep).
*Inputs:* `grant_id` (or the sweep finds `share_links.expires_at < now`).
*Auth:* owner for owner grants; editor for lower grants they or anyone granted;
system for the sweep (every 60 s). *Steps:* (1) set `revoked_at` (append-only
history); (2) in the same transaction, mark every `sessions` row bound to that
`grant_id` as terminated; (3) after commit: close those connections with
`NO_GRANT`, delete their presence rows, broadcast presence removal; (4) any
batch from those replicas is refused at Op 2 step 1 thereafter. *Atomic:* 1–2.
*Eventual:* 3 within 10 s (I13). *Optimistic UI:* the sharing dialog updates
immediately. *Concurrent conflict:* a batch accepted before the transaction
commits is legitimately in; one after is refused. If the revoked user's other
device is offline, its later reconnect hits Op 5 step 3 — content preserved.

**Op 10 — View history at a sequence / blame.** *Inputs:* `document_id`,
`at_seq` (or a wall-clock time mapped to the last `accepted_seq` with
`accepted_at ≤ time`). *Auth:* viewer; a revoked principal sees nothing. *Steps:*
(1) load the nearest snapshot `≤ at_seq`; (2) replay `ops` in `(snapshot.at_seq,
at_seq]`; (3) render; attribute each visible character to `items.author_user_id`
and each register to its `set_op` author; (4) the version list groups ops into
sessions of ≤ 10 minutes idle per author and labels an op whose `authored_at`
is more than 5 minutes before `accepted_at` "authored offline at …". *Atomic:*
a consistent read at `at_seq`. *Eventual:* snapshots may lag (replay is
longer, not wrong). *Concurrent conflict:* none — history is immutable once
accepted (Trap 4).

**Op 11 — Restore a version** (atomic batch). *Inputs:* `at_seq`. *Auth:*
editor. *Steps (service, one transaction):* (1) render state `S` at `at_seq`
and current state `C` at `head_seq`; (2) compute ops: for each item visible in
`S` and hidden in `C` → `retract` of every unretracted delete covering it; for
each item visible in `C` and not visible in `S` (including items created after
`at_seq`) → `delete`; for each register whose winner differs → `set` to the
`S` value; for each move active in `C` and not in `S` → `retract`; for each
move active in `S` and not in `C` → a new `move` with the same range and
destination, cycle-checked; (3) submit as one `atomic` batch authored by the
restoring user's replica; (4) push the batch on that replica's undo stack.
*Atomic:* the whole batch or nothing. *Optimistic UI:* none — show a progress
state; the batch can be thousands of ops. *Concurrent conflict:* B's typing
accepted after the batch lands on top of the restored state; B's typing
accepted before is deleted by step 2 but recoverable from history — the
promise is "restore is an edit", not "restore is a time machine" (I11).

**Op 12 — Compact and prune** (scheduled, bulk; every 10 minutes per active
document, nightly for all). *Inputs:* documents with `head_seq − snapshot_seq >
1000` or with tombstones below the horizon. *Auth:* system. *Steps:* (1) write
a new `snapshots` row at `head_seq`; (2) expire replicas whose
`last_seen_at < now − 30 days` (`replicas.expired_at`); (3) compute
`prune_horizon_seq = min(last_ack_seq over unexpired replicas of this
document)`, and clamp it to `head_seq − (ops accepted in the last 90 days)`;
(4) physically drop `items` rows fully hidden by deletions with
`accepted_seq < prune_horizon_seq` whose identities are not referenced by any
`threads` anchor or `named_versions` range, rewriting `after` pointers of
surviving children to the dropped item's own `after` **with the sort key of the
dropped item preserved on the child** (so traversal order is unchanged); (5)
drop `ops` older than 90 days not covered by a named version. *Atomic:* per
document. *Eventual:* pruning is throttled. *Concurrent conflict:* an expired
device that later references a pruned identity is refused `STALE_REFERENCE`
(Op 5 step 3); a live device cannot, because the horizon is its acknowledged
frontier or older (I20).

**Op 13 — Whiteboard geometry, style, and connectors.** *Inputs:* `set` ops on
shape registers; connector shapes reference `from_shape`/`to_shape`. *Auth:*
editor. *Steps:* (1) drag or resize produces one `set(geometry, {x,y,w,h,
rotation})` at ≤ 20 Hz, coalesced locally into one undo action per gesture;
recolor produces `set(fill, …)`; (2) Op 2 applies each register independently
by LWW; (3) rendering derives connector visibility: a connector is drawn iff
both endpoint shapes are visible, and its endpoints are computed from the
endpoints' current geometry; (4) deleting a group deletes its shapes as one
atomic batch (retractable together). *Atomic:* per batch. *Optimistic UI:*
immediate; if the LWW loser is the local device (its `(lamport, replica_id)`
was lower), the shape visibly snaps to the winner when the winner arrives.
*Concurrent conflict:* move ‖ recolor → both apply; resize ‖ move → one
geometry wins whole (I18); draw connector ‖ delete endpoint → connector exists
but is hidden; retract the delete → it reappears (I19). Concurrent
`set(group_id)` by two users → LWW, no cycle possible because groups are flat.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment: 20,000 workspaces; 3,000,000 documents (2.4 M docs,
0.6 M boards); 150,000 daily active editors; 25,000 documents open
concurrently at peak, of which 3,000 have ≥ 2 live editors and 200 have ≥ 10.

Document sizes: median 8,000 characters (about 900 runs after coalescing),
p95 60,000, p99 400,000 (long specs, meeting-note logs). Boards: median 150
shapes, p99 5,000.

Rates: a typing user emits about 5 inserts/s, batched into ≈ 3 batches/s;
150 k DAU × ~2,000 ops/day ≈ 300 M ops/day ≈ 30 M batch rows/day (ops are
stored one row per op but written one transaction per batch). 90-day retention
≈ 27 B op rows ≈ 4 TB at 150 bytes; snapshots add ≈ 3 M × 40 KB ≈ 120 GB.
Materialized `items` ≈ 3 M docs × 1,500 rows ≈ 4.5 B rows before pruning.

Fan-out: a 10-editor document at 5 ops/s each is 50 ops/s in and 450
deliveries/s out; the 200 such documents cost ≈ 90 k deliveries/s at peak.
Presence is larger: cursor updates at 10 Hz from 25 k sessions with a median
of 1.4 viewers each ≈ 350 k ephemeral messages/s, which is why presence is
never durable.

Expensive at these sizes: **Op 10/11** on p99 documents (400 k chars, ~2 M ops
over 90 days) — a snapshot every 1,000 ops bounds replay to ≤ 1,000 ops but a
restore diff walks the whole sequence twice (≈ 1 s). **Op 12** on
tombstone-heavy documents (a meeting-notes doc that is rewritten daily carries
10× its visible size in hidden items until the horizon advances; a single
offline laptop holds the horizon for up to 30 days). **Op 5** with a 30-day
laptop: up to ~60 k pending ops rebased on ~200 k missed ops. **Op 1** cold
opens of p99 documents: 40 KB snapshot + tail; cache snapshots at the edge.
Mention delivery and the revocation sweep are cheap; the grant history table
grows ≈ 2 rows per share action and is never large.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

**U1 — The merge function lives on both sides and must be bit-identical.** The
client renders its own pending ops *before* the service sees them and must
arrive at the same sequence the service will materialize. A "queries +
mutations over rows" backend gives the client rows and the server the truth;
here the truth is a deterministic reduction (`§4` ordering rule) that every
replica computes. *Concession:* server-only merge — the client sends ops and
renders only what returns. *Cheat boundary:* the moment the product still
claims offline editing (P3) or sub-second local typing (P1); a server-only
merge with an offline queue is exactly the design that misplaces the queue.

**U2 — Nothing is a physical delete, and garbage collection is a distributed
frontier.** Every delete is a retractable tombstone that other actors' pending
operations may still name as an `after`. Rows can be dropped only below the
minimum acknowledged sequence of every unexpired replica, and the concept of
"a device I have not heard from in 29 days is still holding my tombstones" has
no counterpart in an ordinary backend's delete primitive. *Concession:* hard
prune at a fixed age with device expiry (this design's own 30-day rule).
*Cheat boundary:* pruning without refusing stale references — the device's
later insert then lands at an undefined position and nobody is told.

**U3 — Some operations commute and some must be sequenced, in the same
transaction, with per-device rejection feedback.** Text, marks, registers, and
tombstones need no ordering; moves need the service to act as a sequencer and
check a global invariant (I7) *against state that includes the commuting ops
accepted in the same batch*, then tell one specific replica to roll back one
specific op while keeping its other pending ops. Ordinary backends either
sequence everything (and cannot merge offline queues) or nothing (and cannot
refuse a cycle). *Concession:* accept cyclic moves and repair deterministically
(detach the cycle to the end of the document). *Cheat boundary:* a repair that
runs only on the service, so clients that applied the move optimistically
render a different document until they reload.

**U4 — The unit of conflict is an intent-sized register with causal metadata,
not a row or a column.** A shape row has fields that must merge as one value
(`geometry`) beside fields that must merge independently (`fill`), each
carrying `(lamport, replica_id)` of its winner so that a value authored after
*seeing* the current value beats it and a truly concurrent one loses
deterministically. Row-level or column-level last-writer-wins by arrival time
cannot express either the grouping or the causality. *Concession:* one row per
register with arrival-time LWW. *Cheat boundary:* when the multi-field value is
split into columns (the squashed shape) or when arrival order lets an old
offline value overwrite a newer one because it arrived last.

**U5 — Delivery is re-authorized against an interval, continuously.** Live
subscriptions, presence, and queued notifications must be cut or withheld when
a `grants` row acquires `revoked_at` or `expires_at` passes — I13 demands 10 s,
and the offline queue must be judged at arrival, not authoring. A backend that
authorizes at subscription time has a subscription outliving its grant.
*Concession:* re-check every *N* seconds. *Cheat boundary:* *N* larger than the
promise, or a re-check that covers queries but not presence fan-out or
notification payloads.

**U6 — A version is a sequence number and a restore is a computed batch.**
History must reproduce any accepted sequence prefix, attribute characters, and
express "go back" as ordinary operations that compose with concurrent edits
and are undoable. *Concession:* restore as overwrite under an exclusive lock
with a warning. *Cheat boundary:* a lock that a disconnected device can hold,
or an overwrite that silently discards edits accepted during the restore.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice, high confidence: identity-addressed sequences with
`after` pointers and `(lamport, replica)` sibling order (the RGA / causal-tree
family); tombstones with retraction; per-register last-writer-wins with causal
stamps; per-replica undo implemented as inverse operations (the Yjs
UndoManager shape); mark boundaries as sequence items; fractional-order z-index
via sequence order; snapshots plus op tail; delivery-time authorization for
notifications; grants as append-only intervals; comment anchors as identity
pairs with a captured quote.

Design choices made for this exercise, medium confidence: (a) **range moves as
a traversal override with a "tail through the next break" rule** — real
editors mostly do delete-plus-insert (Trap 2 is a known limitation of shipping
systems), and fully commutative range moves are an open research problem; the
sequencer-with-cycle-refusal compromise is invented here and its interaction
with nested concurrent moves (§4) is specified but untested. (b) **Geometry as
one register** — some shipping whiteboards use per-property LWW and accept
blends; the grouping is a product ruling, not consensus. (c) **30-day replica
expiry as the pruning frontier** and the `STALE_REFERENCE` refusal — standard
in spirit, invented in the specific numbers and the quarantine flow. (d)
**Restore as a diff-batch that retracts others' deletions** — Google-Docs-like
products behave this way but the exact op derivation (especially re-issuing
moves) is mine. (e) Sticky-format behaviour (whether typing at the end of a
bold word is bold) is left to the editor's choice of `after` and is not
otherwise specified — an implementer must pick one.

Known weak spots an implementer should not guess past: the tail rule for moves
makes a concurrent split inside a moved block travel with it — deliberate, but
a user who split a paragraph intending the second half to *stay* will be
surprised; RGA has a documented interleaving anomaly for text typed backwards
(inserting before the previous character) that this design does not correct;
the "authored offline" label relies on an untrusted device clock and is
advisory only; and audience-scoped counts (I16) cover comment badges but the
design has not enumerated every count a UI might render (presence counts are
safe because presence already requires access).

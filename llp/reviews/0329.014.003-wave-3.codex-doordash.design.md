### 1. Product contract

This is a three-sided food-delivery marketplace. Each order belongs to one customer and one restaurant; a dasher may carry up to three independent orders in a batch. Customers, restaurant staff, and assigned dashers are the three ordinary writers. Automated services and support may act only through named, audited exception operations.

1. One order has one authoritative lifecycle but three purpose-built views: customers see purchase, ETA, tracking, and refund facts; restaurants see preparation and handoff facts; dashers see offer, route, pickup, and delivery facts.

2. The lifecycle is `placed → accepted → preparing → ready → picked_up → delivered`; `accepted` means restaurant acceptance, while dasher offer acceptance changes assignment state without changing the order lifecycle.

3. `cancelled` is terminal: customers may cancel under disclosed terms before pickup, restaurants may reject or cancel with a reason, automated services may enforce deadlines, and only support may cancel after pickup for a safety or recovery exception.

4. Refunds, disputes, failed-delivery adjustments, and payout reversals do not move a delivered order backward or relabel it as cancelled.

5. Placement freezes item names, modifiers, quantities, prices, taxes, fees, discount funding, initial tip, currency, restaurant, destination, policy versions, and the original delivery promise.

6. Retrying checkout with the same placement intent creates at most one order and one payment authorization; after a timeout the UI says it is checking the outcome rather than inviting a second purchase.

7. Restaurant availability is derived at an authoritative instant from its IANA time zone, versioned hours, dated exceptions, capacity, and bounded busy or pause intervals.

8. Busy mode is an expiring interval that may pause new intake, add 0–60 minutes to future prep quotes, or do both; it never silently changes an accepted order’s recorded promise.

9. A quote lasts 90 seconds. Placement rechecks its menu, restaurant-state, capacity, delivery-zone, and pricing versions, so discovery and an expired quote are not guarantees of acceptance.

10. Restaurants may accept, reject, report a committed prep estimate, start preparation, and mark ready; they cannot edit customer payment, tip, address, dasher pay, pickup, or delivery facts.

11. Assignment examines at most 64 recorded candidates per round. Each offer is a 30-second lease carrying an assignment epoch, earnings guarantee, approximate distance, and enough pickup/drop-off information to make an informed decision.

12. Offer acceptance atomically wins the order, consumes dasher capacity, and commits any proposed batch route; an expired or concurrently lost offer cannot create temporary duplicate pickup authority.

13. A batch contains at most three orders, accepts no additions after any member has been picked up, and must preserve every member’s pickup precedence, capacity, detour limit, and delivery promise.

14. Each batched order retains its own lifecycle, customer audience, tip, cancellation, proof, settlement, and dispute; customers never learn another batch member’s identity, address, instructions, or exact stop.

15. The customer sees both the original promised window and a separately labelled current estimate. Estimates may change with prep, traffic, assignment, and location, but revisions never erase the promise used to judge lateness.

16. Customer tracking begins after pickup. It normally shows a freshness-labelled, approximately 50-meter position; during another customer’s drop-off it shows “completing a nearby delivery” and an ETA instead of revealing that stop.

17. Restaurants see only an approach distance and arrival band until their pickup is completed. Raw location history, the customer destination, tips, refunds, and courier earnings remain hidden.

18. Customers may edit drop-off instructions until pickup, tip at checkout, add a supplemental tip for one hour after delivery, cancel under current terms, and open an itemized refund request; changing the address requires support or cancellation and reorder.

19. Delivery creates separate customer-payment, restaurant-earnings, dasher-earnings, tax, and platform obligations. “Delivered,” “captured,” “payable,” and “paid out” remain distinguishable states.

20. Offline clients queue intent, not authority. Reconnected actions are rechecked against current server time, lifecycle, assignment epoch, offer expiry, field revision, and actor grant; stale actions fail without overwriting newer work.

21. Multiple devices converge on the same ordered history. Status changes are never last-writer-wins, and a deliberate tracking-device takeover invalidates the old device’s location lease.

22. Push notifications and cached projections may lag, but they never authorize actions; every consequential command is checked against authoritative state and returns the winning current state on conflict.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Customers, restaurants, and dashers may write only their named field groups and commands, never an arbitrary partial order record.

2. **[TIME]** Restaurant acceptance and dasher offer acceptance are different facts and can never substitute for one another.

3. **[TIME]** An order may follow only the declared transitions, may not skip required states, and may never leave `delivered` or `cancelled`.

4. **[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Pickup and delivery are valid only from the currently assigned dasher under the current assignment epoch.

5. **[TIME]** `picked_up` requires `ready`, and `delivered` requires `picked_up`, except through a separately named and audited support override.

6. **[TIME] [MONEY]** After pickup, ordinary cancellation cannot substitute for a refund, failed-delivery resolution, or payout reversal.

7. **[TIME]** A concurrent losing command cannot overwrite the winning transition merely because it arrives later.

8. **[TIME]** An offline command is judged when received, and its device timestamp cannot revive an expired offer, grant, quote, or cancellation right.

9. **[OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA] [TIME]** Restaurant and support authority must be active and correctly scoped at the instant an action commits.

10. **[MONEY]** One placement intent may create no more than one order, one initial authorization, and one initial set of obligations.

11. **[MONEY]** The placed order’s menu, prices, fees, taxes, discounts, tip, currency, rounding, and policy versions remain reproducible after their sources change.

12. **[TIME]** “Accepting orders now” is calculated from the restaurant’s time zone, schedule, dated exceptions, capacity, and current overrides at the decision instant.

13. **[TIME]** Busy mode expires without an expiry job and cannot retroactively change accepted orders or their original promises.

14. **[TIME]** The original promise and current ETA are separate facts, and every ETA revision records its observation time and inputs.

15. **[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Every dispatch round records a finite candidate set, and widening time or radius creates a new round.

16. **[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** At most one active assignment owns pickup and delivery authority for an order.

17. **[TIME] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** A batch addition must preserve every existing member’s constraints, not merely improve total route time.

18. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** One customer may not learn another batched customer’s identity, address, instructions, tip, support case, or exact stop.

19. **[SECRECY]** Restaurants never receive customer payment credentials, tip amounts, refund evidence, exact destination, or courier earnings.

20. **[SECRECY] [TIME]** A dasher receives exact destination and contact access only while the corresponding assignment and completion grace period are active.

21. **[SECRECY] [TIME]** Tracking has explicit start, precision, freshness, batch-suppression, and end rules, and retained history is never exposed as live location.

22. **[TIME]** An older or superseded location sample cannot move the displayed courier position or ETA backward.

23. **[SECRECY]** Counts, ranks, refusal text, subscription behavior, cursors, and timing may not reveal hidden candidates, batch members, payouts, or disputes.

24. **[MONEY]** Every ledger transaction balances exactly in integer minor units within one currency.

25. **[MONEY]** Tips belong to the dasher for their order and cannot be pooled across a batch or used silently to reduce another order’s base pay.

26. **[MONEY]** No authorization, capture, refund, payout, or adjustment may occur twice for the same financial intent.

27. **[MONEY]** Every refunded cent names its customer-facing component and whether the restaurant, dasher, platform, tax authority, insurer, or payment rail funds it.

28. **[MONEY] [TIME]** A later refund never silently rewrites or claws back an already vested earning; any recovery is a new, visible adjustment under an explicit policy.

29. **[MONEY]** Delivery, capture, restaurant payout, dasher payout, and provider confirmation remain separate states even when they usually happen in sequence.

30. **[SECRECY] [OTHER PEOPLE’S DATA]** Support access is case-scoped and time-bounded, and every exception records its actor, reason, evidence, and resulting financial treatment.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Treating the shared order as one shared mutable row.**  
   **Wrong version:** Return the same record to all clients, hide fields in the UI, and permit generic partial updates after checking a role.  
   **Why wrong:** Read and write grants drift as fields are added; a stale update can erase another principal’s work; former staff or expired assignments retain excess authority.  
   **Exposing observation:** A restaurant response contains a tip or address, a customer update removes a prep estimate, or a removed employee’s offline `ready` command succeeds.

2. **Selecting a dasher and building a batch as separate best-effort writes.**  
   **Wrong version:** Search all nearby dashers, send broad offers, assign the first respondent, then append orders to a route one at a time.  
   **Why wrong:** Cost grows with fleet size, two dashers can appear to win, stale capacity passes, and a later batch addition can break an earlier customer’s promise.  
   **Exposing observation:** Two concurrent accepts produce two navigation screens, or adding a “nearby” order makes an existing member late.

3. **Recomputing settlement from mutable totals.**  
   **Wrong version:** Keep `customer_total`, `restaurant_payout`, and `dasher_payout` on the order and overwrite them after delivery, refund, or dispute.  
   **Why wrong:** Funding sources, vesting, retry identity, promotion ownership, and already-sent payouts disappear from history.  
   **Exposing observation:** Replaying a refund changes the balance, a batch reallocates tips, or reconciliation cannot explain a one-cent difference without application logs.

4. **Representing restaurant state with `is_open` and `is_busy`.**  
   **Wrong version:** Timers flip booleans and staff leave busy mode enabled until someone turns it off.  
   **Why wrong:** Delayed jobs, restarts, DST transitions, schedule edits, and forgotten toggles create impossible state and can rewrite accepted expectations.  
   **Exposing observation:** A location stays busy the next morning, opens during a dated closure, or an existing promise changes when new-order intake pauses.

5. **Putting raw GPS on the order or batch.**  
   **Wrong version:** Order points by arrival time and expose the shared route to every watcher.  
   **Why wrong:** Offline and multi-device uploads move the marker backward; high-rate writes contend with durable transitions; a batch exposes homes and unrelated stops.  
   **Exposing observation:** An old phone reconnects and moves the dot backward, or customer A can identify customer B’s drop-off.

6. **Filtering hidden rows and then using counts, ranks, or refusals as policy facts.**  
   **Wrong version:** Count only candidates visible to the caller, return different errors for different hidden states, or expose ETA changes as if they revised the promise.  
   **Why wrong:** A privacy filter can produce a confidently wrong operational answer, while metadata exposes the very facts hidden fields were meant to protect.  
   **Exposing observation:** A restaurant infers tip size from settlement timing, a customer learns that no dasher accepted rather than simply seeing “still matching,” or an owner-only candidate count incorrectly reports zero.

### 4. Data model

Use opaque 128-bit IDs, UTC `instant` values, IANA time-zone names, integer seconds, signed 64-bit money in minor units plus ISO currency, and fixed-precision coordinates plus accuracy meters. Server receipt time governs authority; device time is evidence only. Commands carry `actor_id`, an idempotency key, a request hash, and the relevant expected component versions.

One order has many lines, revisions, transitions, ETA revisions, financial transactions, and cases; it has zero or one active assignment. A batch has one dasher and one to three active members. Every financial transaction’s debits equal its credits per currency.

The transition matrix is:

| From | To | Authorized actor and guards |
|---|---|---|
| none | placed | Customer; live quote, confirmed authorization, intake capacity reserved. |
| placed | accepted | Active restaurant staff; before acceptance deadline. |
| accepted | preparing | Active restaurant staff; committed prep estimate supplied. |
| preparing | ready | Active restaurant staff. |
| ready | picked_up | Active assigned dasher; current epoch and pickup evidence. |
| picked_up | delivered | Active assigned dasher; current epoch and delivery evidence. |
| placed | cancelled | Customer, restaurant rejection, support, or acceptance-timeout service. |
| accepted/preparing/ready | cancelled | Customer with current cancellation quote, restaurant with reason, support, or policy service. |
| picked_up | cancelled | Support/service safety exception with recovery and settlement instructions. |
| delivered/cancelled | any | Nobody; later changes are financial or support events. |

Field grants are command-specific:

| Field group | Customer | Restaurant | Dasher |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lifecycle | Create and policy-cancel; read own | Accept, prepare, ready, reasoned cancel | Pickup and deliver while actively assigned |
| Items | Choose at placement; read snapshot | Read snapshot | Read handling details while offered/assigned |
| Destination/contact | Read own; edit instructions until pickup | First name and relay only | Exact details during active assignment and grace |
| Prep/busy | Read customer-facing ETA | Write own prep and operating commands | Read pickup estimate |
| Assignment/batch | Courier identity and order-specific ETA | Approach/arrival projection | Own offers, assignments, earnings guarantee, and route |
| Location | Coarse permitted projection | Approach band before pickup | Write only current session |
| Money | Own charge, tip, refund | Own settlement only | Own guarantee, earnings, and disclosed tip |
| Cases | Own case view/evidence | Own-party evidence | Own-party evidence |

Current status, availability, location, ETA, balances, and audience views are derived caches. Order transitions, configuration publications, field revisions, candidate snapshots, route revisions, financial entries, provider events, and case events are append-only. Quotes, grants, offers, presence leases, tracking access, overrides, cancellation quotes, and support scope are explicitly time-bounded.

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Read or subscribe to an order projection.**  
   **Inputs:** order ID, caller, audience cursor. **Authorization:** owning customer, currently scoped restaurant staff, active assigned dasher within grace, or case-scoped support. **Steps:** (1) resolve the caller’s grant at server time; (2) load lifecycle and permitted component revisions from one consistent snapshot; (3) derive only that audience’s fields and audience revision; (4) attach permitted ETA/location data with freshness. **Atomic:** the durable portion is one snapshot. **Eventual:** ETA and location may lag and are labelled. **Optimistic:** retain the last version as stale. **Conflict:** reconnect from the audience cursor; nonparticipants receive the same not-found response regardless of hidden order state.

2. **Publish hours or set busy mode.**  
   **Inputs:** complete schedule revision, or mode, prep delta, requested end, expected configuration version, idempotency key. **Authorization:** active location manager; ordinary staff only if granted busy control. **Steps:** (1) validate local intervals, overnight spans, DST interpretation, and a maximum two-hour override; (2) append the schedule or override using server time; (3) advance the configuration version; (4) compute the next boundary and invalidate future quotes. **Atomic:** revision, version, audit, and cache work item. **Eventual:** discovery caches refresh, but correctness derives directly from time and intervals. **Optimistic:** show “updating.” **Conflict:** stale devices receive the intervening revision rather than merging overlapping commands.

3. **Quote and place an order.**  
   **Inputs:** item/modifier quantities, destination token, initial tip, payment token, quote ID, placement idempotency key. **Authorization:** authenticated customer acting for self. **Steps:** (1) quote from a consistent menu/configuration snapshot, route estimate, capacity, and pricing policy and persist it for 90 seconds; (2) on confirmation, revalidate all source versions and reserve one intake slot; (3) authorize the exact total externally under a deterministic key; (4) after confirmed authorization, atomically create the order, private and commercial snapshots, `placed` event, original promise, command result, and notifications. **Atomic:** final local creation is all-or-nothing; provider authorization is outside it. **Eventual:** notifications and orphan-authorization voiding. **Optimistic:** “placing order,” then “checking outcome” after ambiguity. **Conflict:** a changed menu, busy state, capacity, zone, or expired quote returns a replacement quote; same-key retry returns the original attempt.

4. **Apply a restaurant transition.**  
   **Inputs:** order, `accept|start_preparing|mark_ready|reject`, relevant lifecycle/restaurant versions, prep seconds or reason, idempotency key. **Authorization:** active staff at that location with the named command grant. **Steps:** (1) load current state; (2) check the matrix and deadline; (3) append restaurant-field and transition events; (4) advance only the lifecycle and restaurant component versions; (5) on acceptance enqueue payment capture and dispatch, and on prep/ready enqueue ETA refresh. **Atomic:** revisions, transition, capacity effect, and work items. **Eventual:** payment, matching, notification, and ETA. **Optimistic:** show a pending control, not a completed state. **Conflict:** cancellation or another staff command may win; return its current state, while a duplicate command returns its original result.

5. **Run a bounded dispatch round.**  
   **Inputs:** order and assignment versions, `as_of`, radius tier, policy revision. **Authorization:** claimed system work only. **Steps:** (1) require `accepted|preparing|ready` with no assignment; (2) select at most 64 online dashers with location no older than 15 seconds, compatible zone/vehicle, and fewer than three active orders; (3) for candidates with zero to two unpicked orders, evaluate at most 15 legal pickup/drop-off insertion pairs; (4) freeze candidates, ranks, location sequences, constraints, and policy; (5) offer the best still-eligible candidate for 30 seconds. **Atomic:** final eligibility recheck, recorded round, offer, and notification. **Eventual:** offer delivery and a later bounded radius tier after expiry. **Optimistic:** customer sees only “finding a dasher.” **Conflict:** state or assignment change ends the round; no-candidate never triggers an unbounded scan.

6. **Respond to an offer and commit a batch.**  
   **Inputs:** offer token, `accept|decline`, offer/session/batch versions, idempotency key. **Authorization:** addressed dasher in the current online session. **Steps:** (1) validate signature, expiry, current location, capacity, and assignment epoch; (2) on decline close the offer; (3) on acceptance recheck the order plus every proposed batch member and promise; (4) atomically consume the offer, create the unique assignment, create or revise the batch and route, reserve capacity, rotate the tracking epoch, and expose scoped destination access. **Atomic:** every accepted order, route stop, capacity reservation, and offer result. **Eventual:** navigation, losing-offer cleanup, projections, and ETA. **Optimistic:** “accepting” until confirmed. **Conflict:** any assignment, cancellation, pickup, route, or capacity change expires the whole proposal without partial batching.

7. **Ingest and project live location.**  
   **Inputs:** tracking session, assignment epoch, device generation, monotonic sequence, sampled time, coordinates, accuracy, speed, and heading. **Authorization:** dasher owning the current session; assignment audiences are derived server-side. **Steps:** (1) validate lease, generation, plausible time, rate, and accuracy; (2) deduplicate `(session, sequence)`; (3) update current position only if the sample is newer within the same generation; (4) retain a decimated trace and emit a coalescible event; (5) generate separate customer, restaurant, and system projections, suppressing unrelated batch stops. **Atomic:** sample acceptance and conditional current-position update. **Eventual:** 2–5 second fan-out, ETA refresh, and retention deletion. **Optimistic:** only the dasher follows device-local position. **Conflict:** duplicates succeed, stale generations are rejected, and out-of-order points never replace current position.

8. **Record arrival, pickup, or delivery.**  
   **Inputs:** order, milestone, lifecycle/assignment/route versions, location reference, evidence, idempotency key. **Authorization:** current assigned dasher; support uses a separately audited override. **Steps:** (1) verify epoch, stop, state, geofence or override, and evidence; (2) append fulfillment proof; (3) for pickup or delivery append the legal transition; (4) complete only that order’s route stop; (5) on delivery end its tracking access and create one deterministic settlement instruction; (6) replan remaining batch stops. **Atomic:** proof, transition, stop, capacity effect, settlement instruction, and work items. **Eventual:** evidence processing, settlement, projection, and ETA. **Optimistic:** offline UI says “queued,” not confirmed. **Conflict:** a duplicate returns success; cancellation, reassignment, or changed route requires authoritative reconciliation.

9. **Amend customer-owned fields or add a tip.**  
   **Inputs:** order, instruction revision or supplemental-tip amount, relevant component version, idempotency key. **Authorization:** owning customer. **Steps:** (1) permit instructions only before pickup and forbid address mutation; (2) compare only `customer_fields_version`, so restaurant work does not cause a false conflict; (3) append the revision; (4) for a positive tip within one hour after delivery, authorize/capture it externally under a stable key and append a balanced dasher-tip adjustment after confirmation. **Atomic:** instruction revision locally, or tip adjustment and external-result record locally. **Eventual:** dasher projection and payout eligibility. **Optimistic:** instructions may appear pending; money never appears paid prematurely. **Conflict:** same-field stale edits return the newer text, while retries cannot duplicate a tip.

10. **Cancel an order.**  
    **Inputs:** order, expected lifecycle/financial versions, reason, and a current cancellation quote when required. **Authorization:** owning customer under policy, restaurant with a permitted reason, deadline service, or case-scoped support. **Steps:** (1) compute or validate a 60-second quote naming customer refund, retained charge, restaurant compensation, dasher compensation, and funders; (2) recheck state and cutoff; (3) append `cancelled`; (4) revoke offers or assignment access, release capacity, and remove only this batch member; (5) create a deterministic settlement instruction and external void/refund work; (6) replan remaining members. **Atomic:** transition, releases, allocation, and work items. **Eventual:** provider action, payout, route, and notifications. **Optimistic:** “cancelling.” **Conflict:** if acceptance, ready, or pickup won, the quote is invalid and no fee is charged from it.

11. **Refresh ETAs.**  
    **Inputs:** order/batch revision, prep revision, latest location sequence, traffic snapshot, estimation policy, work generation. **Authorization:** scheduled or coalesced system work. **Steps:** (1) snapshot inputs; (2) request bounded routing information; (3) compute estimates in route order; (4) recheck every input watermark; (5) append a revision only when materially changed; (6) flag promise risk without altering the original promise. **Atomic:** ETA append, current pointer, and notification. **Eventual:** routing and audience updates. **Optimistic:** age and label the previous estimate rather than invent precision. **Conflict:** any newer prep, location, state, or route input discards the result and coalesces a replacement.

12. **Post three-way settlement.**  
    **Inputs:** terminal settlement instruction, frozen amounts, restaurant contract, dasher guarantee, tax and promotion policies, stable settlement key. **Authorization:** settlement service only. **Steps:** (1) require no prior transaction for the key; (2) calculate customer obligation `food + tax + delivery fee + service fee + tip − discounts`; (3) calculate restaurant payable from food, commission, merchant-funded discounts, compensation, and applicable tax treatment; (4) calculate dasher payable from base, bonus, compensation, and that order’s tip; (5) assign tax, promotion subsidy, processor cost, and residual platform lines; (6) require equal debits and credits; (7) atomically post all entries and enqueue capture, void, or eligibility work. **Atomic:** the complete balanced transaction or nothing. **Eventual:** provider clearing and payouts. **Optimistic:** earnings remain pending until posted. **Conflict:** duplicate terminal events no-op; later corrections append adjustments.

13. **Request and resolve a refund or dispute.**  
    **Inputs:** order, affected items/components, reason, evidence, requested amount, then a decision with per-party allocations and case version. **Authorization:** customer may request; affected parties may submit evidence; support or a narrowly limited policy service decides. **Steps:** (1) freeze captured and previously refunded exposure; (2) append the request; (3) on decision cap the amount at remaining exposure; (4) allocate each cent by component and funder using deterministic remainder ordering; (5) atomically append the decision, balanced adjustment or hold, and provider-refund work; (6) mark paid only after provider confirmation. **Atomic:** case version, exposure consumption, ledger entries, and work item. **Eventual:** evidence exchange, refund, statements, and negative future balances for explicit post-payout recoveries. **Optimistic:** “requested,” never “refunded.” **Conflict:** concurrent decisions re-evaluate remaining exposure; duplicate webhooks and requests are idempotent.

14. **Sweep payouts and reconcile providers.**  
    **Inputs:** recipient kind, currency, cutoff, provider, cursor, policy version, maximum 500 recipients per page. **Authorization:** leased periodic worker. **Steps:** (1) select one bounded page of positive eligible payables; (2) exclude holds, missing accounts, minimum failures, and prior reservations; (3) atomically move amounts to payout-in-flight and create stable transfer operations; (4) submit externally; (5) deduplicate webhooks; (6) compare daily provider statements with expected charges, refunds, and transfers; (7) append completion, reversal, or discrepancy cases. **Atomic:** each page’s reservations, batch, and work items. **Eventual:** transfers, retries with the same key, statements, and the next page. **Optimistic:** show pending, in-flight, and paid distinctly. **Conflict:** reservation uniqueness prevents double spending; a late refund creates a future adjustment rather than rewriting a submitted payout.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful national deployment might have 20 million monthly customers, 300,000 restaurant locations, one million registered dashers, 200,000 simultaneously online, and five million orders per day.

- Five million orders/day is 58/second on average and approximately 500/second during a concentrated meal peak.
- At 25 durable events/order, the system adds 125 million durable events/day, roughly 62.5 GB/day at 500 bytes/event before indexes and replication.
- Two hundred thousand online dashers sending every four seconds produce 50,000 location samples/second.
- At 200 bytes/sample, retaining every sample would produce about 864 GB/day before replication, so detailed traces need short retention and decimation.
- With 150,000 actively tracked orders, 1.5 watching devices/order, and four-second display updates, tracking produces roughly 56,000 outbound messages/second.
- A first dispatch wave at 500 orders/second, 64 candidates/order, and up to 15 route insertions/candidate requires as many as 480,000 bounded route-feasibility evaluations/second.
- Seven years at five million orders/day is approximately 12.8 billion orders, so completed operational data cannot all remain in the low-latency working set.
- Daily payout sweeps may process hundreds of thousands of recipient balances, while ledger and provider reconciliation examine tens of millions of entries.

Location ingestion and fan-out dominate continuous compute and bandwidth. Matching is expensive at meal peaks but remains bounded independently of fleet size. Financial reconciliation, interval-aware discovery, ETA routing, and audience projection become expensive through indexing and external-service calls.

The location plane should keep the latest accepted point, decimate durable traces to perhaps one point per 30–60 seconds, coalesce audience updates to 2–5 seconds, and shed intermediate points before delaying order or ledger commits. Payment, mapping, phone-relay, and messaging vendor fees should be budgeted per completed order because they may exceed core compute cost.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **One aggregate with audience-safe reads, writes, and inference control.** An ordinary row permission is insufficient because safety covers fields, commands, subscriptions, notifications, errors, counts, ranks, cursors, and timing. A valid concession is separate customer, restaurant, and dasher projections with visible version stamps. The concession becomes a cheat if clients receive a superset and hide it locally, independent copies become competing truths, or hidden state is inferable through metadata.

2. **Bounded set work followed by atomic assignment and route revision.** Candidate discovery may occur over an indexed snapshot, but acceptance must consume the offer, reserve dasher capacity, create exactly one assignment, revise every batch member and stop, rotate the epoch, and enqueue consequences together. A serialized coordinator with expiring reservations is a valid concession. It becomes a cheat if two winners are repaired later, the fleet is scanned globally, batching is removed, or route and assignment commit separately.

3. **Time as queryable state with conditionally authoritative deadlines.** Hours, exceptions, time zones, busy intervals, quotes, offers, grants, and promises require interval semantics and server-time decisions; scheduled work must carry an expected generation and become a no-op when stale. Lazy derivation and durable partitioned deadline queues are valid concessions. Process-memory timers, trusted device clocks, full-table cron scans, or delayed jobs that create truth cross the cheat boundary.

4. **A lossy live plane beside a durable fulfillment plane.** GPS is high-rate, reorderable, privacy-sensitive, and expendable, while pickup, delivery, assignment, and proof are low-rate and durable. Latest-position storage, coarse 15-second refresh, and no replay are valid product concessions. Writing every point through the order transaction, accepting an old device generation, or sending a whole batch route and hiding stops client-side is a cheat.

5. **Immutable multi-party accounting around unreliable providers.** Charges, payables, tips, refunds, holds, payouts, and provider state are distinct machines, while webhooks are duplicated, delayed, and reordered. Delayed payouts, manual review, or a simulated provider are valid concessions if stable keys, ambiguity recovery, balanced entries, and reconciliation remain. Mutable balance fields, exactly-once webhook assumptions, best-effort retries, or scalar refunds without funding allocations are cheats.

### 8. Confidence

The structural design is standard marketplace practice: explicit state machines, immutable order snapshots, scoped commands, server-time deadlines, bounded matching, assignment epochs, versioned routes, expiring presence, audience projections, idempotent external operations, append-only accounting, balanced adjustments, and reconciliation.

The exercise-specific choices are the 90-second quote, 30-second offer, 64-candidate round, three-order batch, 15-second location freshness, two-hour busy maximum, tracking precision, post-delivery tip window, cancellation policies, retention periods, refund-funding rules, and workload envelope. They are concrete implementation requirements for this specification, not claims about DoorDash’s actual internals.

Regional tax, worker-classification, payment, privacy, accessibility, insurance, and evidence rules would require jurisdiction-specific product decisions. The highest-confidence stress findings are the need for field-scoped authority, finite recorded assignment work, transactional batch acceptance, time-derived restaurant state, generation-fenced tracking, and immutable three-way settlement.

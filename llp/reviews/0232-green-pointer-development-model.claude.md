## MATERIAL findings

**M1 — Evidence inversion: the RFC's core mechanism targets the #4-ranked evidenced cost while the two top-ranked costs are deferred or ignored.** Both independent mining passes converge on the same ranking: the dominant cost is the landing-time regeneration + re-verification treadmill on content-coupled frozen records (Part 1 rank #1: 10–70 min per cycle, 42 sessions using `checkout --theirs`+regen, 16 sessions resorting to the loud bypass, gate-blocks outnumber true races ~7:1; Part 2: ~14% of 3,372 commits are integration/regeneration churn, the livelock ticket's 6+ burned cycles with a green battery every time). The #2 cost is drift-gate infrastructure fragility (128 raw "no stale-record conclusion was made; repair the gate environment" events — pushes blocked with zero drift caught; Part 1 calls it the "cheapest high-value fix in the pile"). The push race proper is #3 and measured cheap (~1–2 min recovery); broken-base triage is #4 (31/176 sessions, per-incident cost a triage detour, already partially mitigated by `ALLOWED WITH PRE-EXISTING STALENESS`). The RFC's centerpiece (D-1/D-2 green ref + branch-cut discipline) addresses #4; #1 is demoted to a non-blocking companion ticket (D-6, "not blocking this RFC's acceptance"); #2 appears nowhere in the document. The Motivation's own cost model ("three recurring costs dominate": rebase race, treadmill, ambiguity) is partially falsified by the dossier — two of its three named costs are the cheap ones. Part 1's summary is explicit: "An RFC should target making generated artifacts rebase-stable or regenerable-in-seconds, and decoupling gate freshness from the exact pushed commit — not the race itself." Under the rider's terms, this is a decision-quality defect in the RFC's core weighting, not a detail.

**M2 — D-2 plausibly *worsens* the dominant evidenced cost, and its claimed benefit is smaller than stated.** (a) Cutting from a `green` that lags HEAD by up to 12 hours means the branch's merge-base is older by roughly 75–190 commits at measured August cadence (139–379/day). The landing rebase span equals main-tip minus merge-base, so for the common short session D-2 multiplies the number of upstream commits every landing must rebase across — and the dossier shows precisely that rebase exposure is what converts a 10-second retry into a regenerate-commit-re-gate cycle (~9.4% of commits are regen churn; every extra upstream commit is another chance to dirty guide projections, receipts, metrology stamps). The RFC never analyzes this trade; OQ2 gestures at "merge conflicts grow" but does not confront that the growth feeds the mechanism both miners rank #1, while D-6 (the mitigation) is explicitly non-blocking. Adopting D-2 before D-6 completes plausibly increases total fleet cost. (b) The claimed corollary — "every failure observed on a branch cut from green is attributable to the branch" — holds only *before* the D-3 landing rebase. The moment the lander rebases onto current `main` and runs gates, the is-this-mine ambiguity returns, and the evidence locates most failure events exactly there (pre-push gate blocks, 92 baseline-staleness allowances, post-rebase clippy/test reds). D-2 buys attribution only for mid-development branch failures, a fraction of an already #4-ranked cost.

**M3 — `green` advancement semantics are undefined under chronic reds, and the pointer plausibly stalls by default.** D-1 says green advances "when a battery run completes green at commit X," unqualified. The record shows battery/proof reds are frequent, not exceptional: 25 main-red tickets in 23 days, ~15 drift/red tickets filed on the single worst day, near-duplicate backstop tickets on consecutive days, and (historically, pre-0189) 40 consecutive local-proof runs with zero successes. If advancement requires an all-green complete battery, `green` can stall for days whenever any one gate carries a known, ticketed, fix-forward red — making D-2's cut points ancient (amplifying M2) and the sheriff a permanent regime rather than an exception handler. If instead advancement tolerates ticketed known reds (the natural fix, and symmetric with LLP 0189 D-6, which already lets promotions proceed past ticketed unrelated reds), then the RFC must say so — and must then retract or qualify D-2's attribution corollary, because a branch cut from a green-with-known-reds can again observe inherited failures. Either way, the RFC's central primitive is incompletely specified on the exact axis the evidence says will be exercised constantly.

**M4 — The sheriff is undesigned against the measured cadence: it inherits the livelock, has no revert-safety procedure, and no convergence argument.** (a) Sheriff repairs and reverts of Rust-core landings are themselves heavyweight landings under D-3; the livelock ticket measures exactly this class burning 6+ cycles against an ~8-minute median landing gap ("slowest-lander starvation is structural: the heavier your change, the more likely you lose"). The process charged with restoring green is subject to the dominant unaddressed friction. (b) Default-revert against a main advancing every 5–10 minutes risks reverting commits that landed or in-flight work depends on; the livelock ticket records a near-miss of precisely this class ("one recovery rebase nearly baked in reverts of concurrent lanes' work — caught by a file-level delta audit"). D-4 specifies no dependency check, no delta audit, no rule for reverting a commit with descendants building on it. LLP 0041's "reverts are nearly free" applies to external consumers, not to a fleet stacked on the reverted commit. (c) No convergence argument: while the sheriff repairs red set R against pinned commit R, ~90+ new commits land before the next battery; the battery tests latest main, which may carry new reds from those landings. The RFC cites the C27–C33 lesson ("never chase HEAD") but its repair→land→await-battery loop can starve in the same shape when new reds arrive faster than the battery cadence closes the loop. This needs at least a designed answer (e.g., green-advance evaluated per-gate on content rather than per-battery-run on a commit).

**M5 — Coherence gap with LLP 0189 D-1: the RFC changes the operational response to battery reds without naming the amendment, creating a dual-owner collision.** The "changes nothing about claim gating" claim verifies — D-5 is consistent with 0189 D-3/D-4/D-6, and "no claim may cite green" is the right guard. But 0189 D-1 (an author-ruled Decision) says battery reds "become fix-forward filesystem tickets that the fleet fixes promptly," with escalation to the author after four consecutive red runs. 0232 D-4 makes revert the *default* response when green stalls. That is an operational amendment to 0189 D-1's doctrine, not a pure companion, and the RFC neither lists the 0189 amendment among its effects nor designs the interaction: a fleet lane mid-fix-forward on a ticketed red and a sheriff reverting the causal commit are two owners taking opposite default actions on the same red. OQ1 spots the adjacent tension (the 2026-08-10 24h fix-forward ruling) but frames it as needing only a ruling; the collision-avoidance protocol (does an open, actively-owned fix-forward ticket suppress the sheriff's revert? for how long?) is a design element, not an open question footnote.

**M6 — An evidenced near-dominant snag category is ignored outright: gate-infrastructure fragility, and the record's own concrete remedies are absent.** The 128 "repair the gate environment" block events (gate blocked the push because the *gate* couldn't run — pure overhead, zero drift caught) are the dossier's rank-#2 cost and appear nowhere in the RFC. Likewise absent — even from D-6's candidate list — are the two remedies the repo's own record already proposes and partially quantifies: content-keyed green-battery receipts honored across gate-disjoint rebases (livelock remedy 1, called out by Part 2's synthesis as "a direct application of LLP 0189's 'bind content, not HEAD' to the pre-push layer, where the evidence says the time actually goes") and prepared-runtime reuse in `auth-invalidation-report` (livelock remedy 2, projected to cut the battery from ~10 to ~2 minutes, changing race odds fleet-wide). An RFC restructuring the development model while omitting the record's best-evidenced levers fails the rider's test.

## Minor findings

1. **Staleness-bound arithmetic.** D-4 calls 12 hours "one missed battery cycle plus slack," but the battery cadence is six-hourly (0175/0189: "at least twice daily; the current six-hour cadence satisfies this") — 12 hours is two full cycles. Cosmetic, but the bound's justification should match the real cadence, and OQ2's "two consecutive battery reds" alternative is probably the better-shaped trigger anyway.
2. **Ledger inconsistency.** D-1 says advancement "appends a line to a small append-only ledger file" (reads as committed); OQ4 proposes an *uncommitted* ledger published as a battery artifact. Pick one in the body.
3. **Moving-tag mechanics.** A re-pointed tag is not just "invisible to `git pull` defaults" — clients that already fetched it will not update it without a forced fetch, so a moving `green` tag silently goes stale on every long-lived clone. A branch ref avoids this. Implementation-phase material; OQ4 already holds the question.
4. **The MVCC analogy oversells.** In MVCC, snapshot readers never merge back; here every "reader" becomes a writer that pays the full merge at landing. The analogy conceals exactly the cost M2 identifies. Rhetoric, but it shapes intuition in the wrong direction.
5. **D-2's escape clause may swallow the rule.** Fleet work is heavily continuation-shaped (lanes building on just-landed sibling commits); with green lagging up to 12h, "the task specifically requires an unlanded commit" may describe a large share of sessions. Worth an expected-conformance sentence.
6. **D-7 is premature generalization** — portability bindings before single-repo validation of the core. Cheap prose, no defective decision; altitude-rule noted, does not drive the verdict.
7. **Tag namespace accretion** (`green-<timestamp>` at 2+/day) wants a retention rule eventually. Implementation-phase.

## Evidence-fit assessment

- **D-1 (green ref): partial fit.** The dossier does support a machine-readable "known-good / known-red on main" signal (Part 1 §4 argues for exactly that), and the mechanism is cheap (a retag on the existing battery substrate). But its advancement semantics are undefined where the evidence says they matter most (M3), and its primary advertised use (branch-cut base) is the weak use; its strong evidenced use (triage signal) is unmentioned.
- **D-2 (branch-cut discipline): poor fit.** Targets the #4-ranked, per-incident-cheap snag; delivers attribution only pre-rebase; plausibly amplifies the #1 mechanism by enlarging every landing's rebase span (M2). The evidence does not justify this as the model's centerpiece.
- **D-3 (landing unchanged): good fit.** Correctly preserves the 0189 adaptation the record shows was forced by real failure (the 40-run zero-success local-proof history). But "unchanged" also means the treadmill is enshrined as-is, which is only acceptable if D-6-class work is elevated (see M1).
- **D-4 (sheriff): mixed fit.** The problem — recurring main reds needing owned repair — is evidenced (25 tickets/23 days, duplicate backstop filings). The mechanism is undesigned against the measured cadence: livelock inheritance, revert-safety, convergence, and the fix-forward collision are all unhandled (M4, M5).
- **D-5 (claims unchanged): good fit.** Verified consistent with 0189 D-3/D-4/D-6; the "green is not claim evidence" guard is right.
- **D-6 (treadmill audit): right target, wrong altitude.** Names the evidenced dominant cost but demotes it to a non-blocking ticket and omits the record's own two quantified remedies plus the #2 gate-fragility cost (M1, M6). The evidence supports the inverse structure.
- **D-7 (portability): unevidenced now.** No dossier category bears on it; harmless as prose, premature as design.

## Redesign suggestions

The evidence supports keeping the pieces but inverting the structure:

1. **Make the landing-cost mechanism the RFC's core, not companion work.** Promote to D-level design, with acceptance criteria: (a) content-keyed pre-push battery receipts honored across gate-disjoint rebases (livelock remedy 1 — the repo's own proposal, and the direct application of 0189's bind-content doctrine to where the measured time goes); (b) prepared-runtime reuse in `auth-invalidation-report` (remedy 2, ~10 min → ~2 min); (c) gate-infrastructure failure (`no stale-record conclusion`) becomes non-blocking ticket-and-proceed instead of a push block — 128 blocked pushes with zero drift caught is the cheapest evidenced win in the pile; (d) the D-6 inventory itself (rebase-stable or seconds-regenerable artifacts) with the dossier's named candidates.
2. **Keep the green ref, cheap and reframed**: primarily a machine-readable known-good/known-red signal (answering the evidenced triage cost directly), secondarily an *optional* cut point for long-running or heavyweight work where staleness is amortized. Define advancement as green-modulo-ticketed-known-reds (per-gate, content-scoped), mirroring 0189 D-6 eligibility, and state honestly what that does to attribution.
3. **Scope the sheriff down for v1**: begin as the existing backstop pattern plus an owned escalation (the 0189 D-1 four-red author escalation given an operational owner), with revert as an *available* action gated on a designed safety procedure (descendant-dependency check, the file-level delta audit the livelock ticket says should be part of the retry recipe) and an explicit suppression rule when an open fix-forward ticket has an active owner. Graduate to default-revert authority only with an amendment to LLP 0189 D-1 and evidence that fix-forward-by-fleet is too slow — evidence the current dossier does not yet contain.
4. **Split if needed**: an evidence-primary "landing economics" RFC (item 1) and a secondary "green pointer + sheriff" RFC (items 2–3). The first is justified by the dossier today; the second becomes much stronger once the first exists, because a cheap battery and receipt reuse also fix the sheriff's own landing problem.

## MATERIAL findings

**M-1. D-3 makes INFRASTRUCTURE fail-open, but the specified classifier cannot compute the boundary D-3 defines — and the change silently reverses a recorded author ruling.**
D-3 restricts non-blocking to "a gate that was selected correctly but could not produce a trustworthy content verdict," and the acceptance bar hard-codes "infrastructure failures cause zero push blocks." But in the real runner, *every* prepare-phase failure is classified infrastructure: `executeGate` returns `infrastructureReason: executionInfrastructureReason(prepared, 'prepare command failed')` — an unconditional fallback (drift-prone-gates.mjs, ~lines 901–921) — and the `INFRASTRUCTURE_OUTPUT` regex (line 45) explicitly matches `SNAPBACK_IBEX_CARGO_BUILD_FAILED`-class bootstrap codes. `auth-invalidation-report`'s prepare is `scripts/bootstrap-ibex-runtime`, which **builds the pushed revision's Rust tree**. So the single most common content failure a Rust-core landing can have — the push breaks the build — presents as INFRASTRUCTURE and, under D-3 as written, lands with a ticket instead of blocking. Today this class fail-closes (pre-push returns 2, "no stale-record conclusion was made"), and LLP 0175's recorded author ruling says so explicitly: "a green baseline, an unavailable baseline, or **infrastructure ambiguity still blocks**" (0175, D2.1 ruling block). Three defects compound here: (a) the D-3 boundary ("trustworthy content verdict") is intentional but has no specified mechanism — the runner already owns baseline-attribution machinery (`baselineRevisionsForPlan` + `runGateAtRevision`) that could corroborate an infrastructure classification at the merge-base before waving the push through, and D-3 doesn't require it; (b) the rollback tripwire list covers "content red admitted by reuse" (a D-1 failure) but has no tripwire for *content failure admitted as INFRASTRUCTURE* (the analogous D-3 failure); (c) the "Author ruling required before acceptance" section asks about the 24-hour window and revert but never names that D-3 overturns the 2026-08-07 "infrastructure ambiguity still blocks" ruling. The evidenced 128 fail-closed events with zero drift caught genuinely justify fail-open for *reproducible-at-baseline* environment failures; the RFC must say how push-introduced prepare/build breakage is kept out of that class (or accept it with eyes open, a tripwire, and the ruling named).

**M-2. D-1's "declared semantic input closure" has no declaration authority, and the registry demonstrably under-approximates the closure of exactly the gate the economics depend on.**
D-1 keys receipt reuse on "every declared semantic input has the same bytes" plus gate-disjoint upstream selection, and its acceptance bar requires that "no ineligible retry does" reuse. But nothing in the repo declares a per-gate semantic input closure: the registry entries carry `triggerRules` (selection triggers), `requiredEnvironmentPaths`, and `requiredModules` — and LLP 0175 explicitly frames trigger rules as latency-boundary *refinements* of affected groups, not input closures. For the dominant gate, the gap is concrete: `auth-invalidation-report`'s triggers name 16 files plus `src/auth/` and `src/storage/`, while its check runs an acceptance report against a binary built from the **entire Rust crate** — `Cargo.lock`, `build.rs`, `rust-toolchain.toml`, `src/runtime.rs`, `src/manifest.rs`, and most of `src/` are outside its triggers. Consequences: (i) if "declared inputs" means trigger-matched paths, an upstream landing inside the true build closure but outside the triggers is "gate-disjoint," the receipt is honored, and an interaction-caused new content red that today's forced rerun would catch (baseline green → REFUSED, correctly) lands silently — receipt reuse converting a would-be red to green, which D-1's own last sentence forbids; (ii) if instead closures must be honestly declared, the heavyweight gate's closure approximates the Rust build closure, so disjoint reuse helps the evidenced core-vs-docs race (the livelock ticket's common case) but *not* core-vs-core races — a real limit on the claimed economics that the RFC should state; (iii) the acceptance criterion "no ineligible retry does" is undecidable until "eligible" is grounded. The RFC needs to name the closure authority (per-gate declared closures with an admission/completeness bar, analogous to LLP 0189 D-4's "digest closes over the semantics-affecting inputs" rule — or an explicit, tripwired acceptance of trigger-approximation with the scheduled battery as the honesty backstop) and to bound the expected reuse win accordingly. The fixtures D-1 requires prove reuse *mechanics* on historical diffs; they cannot prove closure *completeness*, which is where this contract can actually fail.

- **D-3 (typed outcomes):** targets the second-ranked, cheapest-to-fix cost (128 zero-value blocks). Right idea; unsound as specified (M-1).

- **D-6 (fix-forward coordinator):** aligned with 0189 D-1 and the recorded 24-hour convention; activation on completed red battery, not pointer age, is the right trigger. Modest evidence base (broken-base is rank 4) but modest mechanism. Acceptable.
- **D-7 (revert protocol):** solves a problem the evidence never shows occurring (no incident in either dossier part needed an automated revert; the near-miss was a *rebase* nearly baking in reverts, which D-7's delta audit correctly absorbs as a check). Defensible as a constraint-box on a dangerous action rather than a promoted mechanism, and it is author-gated — but it is the least evidence-supported element and could be halved to its eligibility conditions without loss.

Justified only to the extent automation (D-6/D-7, ref advancement) actually acts on it; the phasing keeps it from taxing D-1–D-3, so proportionality is acceptable, not elegant.
- **Still ignored:** guide-warning saturation (minor 4); the 197 catch-up merges / 39-merge lane pattern (lane hygiene — receipts reduce the incentive but nothing addresses it directly); discretionary re-verification (minor 5).

## Redesign suggestions

1. **D-3:** require baseline corroboration before any non-blocking INFRASTRUCTURE outcome — reproduce the failure at the merge-base with the existing attribution machinery; a prepare/check failure that does *not* reproduce at baseline is treated as content evidence and blocks. Add a "content failure admitted as infrastructure" tripwire to the rollback list. Add the reversal of 0175's "infrastructure ambiguity still blocks" ruling to the author-ruling section.
2. **D-1:** add a per-gate *declared input closure* to the registry contract with an admission bar (0189 D-4's closure rule is the model); state explicitly that `auth-invalidation-report`'s honest closure includes the Rust build closure, and scope the economic claim to gate-disjoint races (core-vs-docs) accordingly. 3. **D-3 INHERITED_RED:** let the pre-push runner file/update the idempotent signature ticket itself, matching its INFRASTRUCTURE handling and genuinely preserving the current baseline rule.
4. Add a required-amendments list (LLP 0175 D2.1 ruling text and exit-2 semantics; 0189 cross-reference for the health record's relationship to D-1 tickets).
5. State that a valid receipt licenses skipping discretionary post-rebase re-verification for gate-disjoint rebases — this is where the measured 69 minutes actually went.

The rewrite's priorities are right: it now spends its weight where the evidence says the time goes, the green pointer is honestly demoted to a health record, and the revert authority is properly caged behind evidence conditions and an author ruling. But its two load-bearing new contracts each have a soundness hole exactly where they meet the real runner: D-3 fail-opens a class the classifier provably cannot separate from content failure, and D-1's reuse eligibility rests on input-closure declarations that do not exist and are demonstrably under-approximated for the gate that dominates the cost.

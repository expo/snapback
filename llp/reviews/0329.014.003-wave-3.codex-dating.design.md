### 1. Product contract

This is a safety-first dating service for adults. Members publish profiles, receive location-sensitive recommendations, swipe privately, match only after two explicit likes, and converse only while that match remains active.

1. A published profile contains two to six approved photos, exactly three prompt answers, age, coarse location, and an optional photo-verification badge.
2. Exact coordinates, verification captures, private safety state, and internal recommendation scores are never shown to another member.
3. Recommendations require mutual age and identity preferences and current geographic eligibility; they exclude blocks, safety suspensions, active matches, and pair cooldowns.
4. Active travel mode replaces physical location as the discovery origin for its stated interval and is visibly labeled with a destination city.
5. A directional like is private to its sender until the recipient either has current paid “Who Liked You” access or submits a reciprocal like.
6. Free members receive no incoming-like identities, exact count, state-dependent teaser, notification, ranking boost, or refusal that reveals whether anyone specific liked them.
7. A paid member sees the exact count and profiles of current, unresolved incoming likes, including likes received before purchase.
8. Viewing a liker, purchasing access, opening a notification, or appearing in someone’s queue never counts as a reciprocal swipe.
9. The second valid reciprocal like creates exactly one match and one new conversation atomically, including when both people swipe simultaneously.
10. A like remains active for 90 days unless matched, passed, withdrawn by a terminal pair action, blocked, or invalidated by safety status.
11. A pass terminates any current incoming like from that person and suppresses the pair for 30 days; any later encounter starts empty.
12. Messages are accepted only while their match is active, and the app never marks an offline message “sent” before server acceptance.
13. Unmatching immediately closes the conversation for both people, hides its consumer-visible history, and suppresses the pair for 180 days.
14. A later match after a pass or unmatch requires two fresh likes and opens a new conversation; old likes and conversations never revive.
15. Blocking is symmetric in visibility even though authority is directional: either person’s block makes both people invisible to each other on every surface.
16. Reporting atomically creates a confidential safety case and a block; report evidence remains available only to authorized safety reviewers.
17. A block denies all new server access immediately, causes connected official clients to purge pair data before their next render, and makes nonrenewable cached grants expire within ten minutes.
18. Other-person content is stored encrypted in controlled caches; a disconnected client without a live grant fails closed rather than displaying indefinitely cached profiles, photos, or messages.
19. Offline swipes and messages may remain pending for 24 hours, but they carry the pair generation they observed and are rejected if a pass, unmatch, block, or newer device action intervened.
20. Notifications contain no name, photo, message text, liker identity, report state, or blocker identity and are rechecked before dispatch and display.

The revocation promise covers first-party servers, official clients, controlled caches, media grants, and queued notifications. It cannot erase human memory, screenshots, photographs of a screen, or data extracted by a modified client.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Treating block as a filter on future reads.**

   - **Wrong version:** Add `not blocked` to profile and message queries and close the current match.
   - **Why wrong:** The other person’s phone, tablet, queue buffer, chat database, photo cache, media URL, search index, and notification queue may already contain the data; deleting ordinary rows may also destroy report evidence.
   - **Exposing observation:** Open a profile and chat on an offline tablet, block that user from another device, then inspect the tablet ten minutes later. Any renderable card, photo, message, or identity-bearing notification demonstrates failure. Correctness requires pair-scoped grants, monotonic revocation generations, controlled encrypted caches, notification withdrawal, and segregated safety evidence.

2. **Creating the match after separately storing and scanning two likes.**

   - **Wrong version:** Insert each like, run a later reciprocal-like query, then separately create a match, conversation, and notifications.
   - **Why wrong:** Simultaneous likes can create duplicates; one participant can observe an intermediate state; a block can land between discovery and disclosure. The second like is an atomic declassification of two secrets.
   - **Exposing observation:** Have both people like simultaneously while one also blocks. Duplicate conversations, a transient “liked you” entry, inconsistent match identifiers, or a notification surviving the block exposes the race.

3. **Protecting liker names while leaking the secret through other outputs.**

   - **Wrong version:** Hide identities but show free users an exact count, boost inbound likers in the normal queue, leave stable holes after filtering, or return different direct-lookup errors.
   - **Why wrong:** Secrecy applies to the whole observable result. Filtering a count to rows ordinarily visible to the caller can also produce a confidently wrong zero.
   - **Exposing observation:** A controlled account likes a free account and then retracts it repeatedly. If count, rank, page length, cursor, latency class, or refusal changes reproducibly, the paid secret is already disclosed.

4. **Representing the pair with mutable booleans and merging offline actions by timestamp.**

   - **Wrong version:** Store `liked`, `matched`, and `blocked` flags and let the newest client timestamp win.
   - **Why wrong:** An old offline like can arrive after an unmatch or unblock and resurrect a prior relationship; device clocks do not establish causality.
   - **Exposing observation:** Queue a like offline in generation 41, block and unblock from another device into generation 43, set the offline clock one day ahead, and reconnect. If the old like becomes current, generation fencing failed.

5. **Treating a generated recommendation queue as continuing authorization.**

   - **Wrong version:** Precompute nearby profiles once, deliver their coordinates or durable photos, and accept their cards for days without rechecking.
   - **Why wrong:** Location, travel intervals, preferences, blocks, suspensions, profile moderation, and pair cooldowns all change after ranking; repeated precise distances also permit triangulation.
   - **Exposing observation:** Prefetch a candidate, then block, suspend, move, or end travel mode before display or swipe. Showing the card, accepting the swipe, or revealing the precise movement proves the queue was mistaken for authority.

6. **Trusting asynchronous payment, verification, and safety results without temporal fencing.**

   - **Wrong version:** Let the client unlock paid data, let any successful verification callback set the badge, or perform report and block as separate actions.
   - **Why wrong:** Refunds and callbacks arrive late or out of order; photos change while verification runs; a reported person may message during a non-atomic report flow.
   - **Exposing observation:** Complete verification for photo set 7 after set 8 is published, replay an older renewal after a refund, or send a message between report creation and block. Any badge, entitlement, or message that survives shows that external authority was applied to the wrong version.

### 4. Data model

All identifiers are opaque 128-bit values, authoritative times are server-assigned UTC timestamps, and every unordered user pair has one canonical `pair_id`. Append-only histories support audit and temporal decisions; explicitly named current-state records are derived transactional projections. Search indexes and queues may omit fresh data but never authorize disclosure.

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Publish a profile or photo revision.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Owner, expected profile version, prompts, owned media IDs, order, and operation ID; only an active adult account may publish.  
   **Steps:** Finalize uploads; require approved media, exactly three prompts, and two to six photos; create an immutable profile version; if the photo-set hash changed, revoke the old verification assertion; advance profile and eligibility versions; append sync and queue-invalidation events.  
   **Atomic:** Version creation, current pointer, badge revocation, and outbox.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Scanning and thumbnails happen before publication; the prior approved version remains visible meanwhile. Draft preview may be optimistic, publication may not. A stale expected version requires explicit merge; retries return the original result.

2. **Update current location or travel mode.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Owner and either a fresh device fix or destination city and interval.  
   **Steps:** Validate device sequence, capture age, accuracy, plausible movement, and travel limits; encrypt exact coordinates; derive a coarse cell; version the effective origin; increment eligibility version; invalidate undelivered queues. Active travel overrides physical location until it ends.  
   **Atomic:** Accepted fix or travel version, effective-origin version, and outbox.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Spatial indexes and queues update asynchronously. The UI may show a pending travel selection, but recommendations use committed state. Old device sequences are ignored; concurrent travel edits compare expected versions.

3. **Serve a recommendation page.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Eligible viewer, device, generation cursor, and page size up to 25.  
   **Steps:** Select ranked items; for each, recheck current approved profile, mutual preferences, effective origins, both radii, pair generation, no active match or separation, cooldown, and safety eligibility; invalidate failures without explanation; claim valid items; issue one-use swipe tokens and viewer-specific encrypted grants; return a coarse distance band or travel-city label. Incoming-like state is not consulted for ranking.  
   **Atomic:** Claimed page, cursor advancement, and grants share one snapshot.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Refill is asynchronous. Two devices use compare-and-set claims; a short page reveals only queue exhaustion, not which rule removed an item.

4. **Commit a swipe and possibly create a match.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Actor, one-use recommendation or paid-disclosure token, like/pass, expected pair and directional versions, and operation ID.  
   **Steps:** Lock the canonical pair; deduplicate; recheck token, visibility, accounts, generation, and target eligibility. A pass records the event, terminates current intents, advances generation, and sets a 30-day cooldown. A like becomes active for 90 days; if the opposite current-generation intent is also a like, consume both, create one match and one conversation, and record both source IDs.  
   **Atomic:** Swipe, intents, pair state, match, conversation, and outbox.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** The card may disappear optimistically, but a match waits for confirmation. Opposite likes serialize; the first conflicting same-direction gesture wins; block can subsequently close and revoke a just-created match.

5. **Read “Who Liked You.”**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Viewer, device, cursor, and page size; the server must find a current `WHO_LIKED_YOU` entitlement.  
   **Steps:** Query the complete hidden incoming-like domain, then rejoin current intent, expiry, pair generation, separation, match consumption, and liker safety/profile state; compute the authorized exact count; return profiles and swipe tokens under grants expiring within five minutes and bind the cursor to entitlement version. Current distance changes do not erase a previously valid active like.  
   **Atomic:** Entitlement check, result snapshot, count, cursor, and grants.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** The projection may omit a new like but may not disclose an invalid one. No optimistic disclosure. Free and expired users receive the same locked result regardless of count.

6. **Apply a purchase, renewal, refund, or revocation.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Signed billing event with provider ID, revision, user binding, product, status, and validity; only the billing integration or an audited administrator may submit it.  
   **Steps:** Verify signature and checkout binding; deduplicate; store the event; recompute entitlement by provider revision; on loss, increment the entitlement scope epoch and append device revocations.  
   **Atomic:** Event, current entitlement, scope version, and outbox.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Payment settlement and push wakeups are external. The purchase screen remains pending until confirmation. Duplicates are no-ops, and an older webhook cannot reverse a newer refund.

7. **Open an active match or conversation page.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Participant, device, match/conversation ID, and message cursor.  
   **Steps:** Recheck participation, open match interval, current pair and visibility generations, and absence of separation; issue a ten-minute relationship grant; return the current profile version and a server-sequenced message window. Closed episodes are never returned through ordinary product access.  
   **Atomic:** Authorization snapshot, grant, and page cursor.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Read-position analytics may lag. Cached content renders only while its grant remains live. A concurrent unmatch or block advances the scope and triggers revocation of the issued page.

8. **Send a message.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Sender, conversation, expected generation, text up to 4,000 characters or approved media, client operation ID.  
   **Steps:** Lock the pair/conversation; require the current active match, participant sender, no separation, and matching generation; deduplicate; allocate the next conversation sequence; append encrypted message and generic-notification event.  
   **Atomic:** Message, idempotency record, sequence, and outbox.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** A pending bubble is optimistic but is not “sent” until acknowledged. Concurrent messages receive server order. If unmatch or block serializes first, sending fails generically; if sending commits first, the later closure immediately makes it inaccessible and suppresses its notification.

9. **Unmatch.**  
   **Inputs/auth:** Either current participant, match generation, and operation ID.  
   **Steps:** Lock the pair; confirm the current episode; close match and conversation; invalidate both intents; advance pair generation and visibility epoch; set a 180-day cooldown; append tombstones, revocations for both users, and notification cancellations.  
   **Atomic:** All authoritative closures and revocation records.  
   **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Cache purge acknowledgments and queue cleanup may lag. The initiator may hide the chat immediately. Repeats are idempotent; a concurrent message falls entirely before or after closure; an old unmatch cannot close a later episode.

10. **Block, unblock, or report-and-block.**  
    **Inputs/auth:** Actor, subject, action, operation ID, and—when reporting—category, narrative, and evidence IDs; cited evidence must come from a legitimate current or recent encounter.  
    **Steps:** Lock the pair. For a report, first create immutable safety-only evidence references. Activate or deactivate only the actor’s block source; reporting activates both a report hold and the actor’s block. On denial, close match/conversation, invalidate intents and queue items, advance generations, and create bilateral revocations. Unblocking advances to another empty generation and imposes the 180-day cooldown; it never removes another active separation source.  
    **Atomic:** Evidence hold, report/block state, pair closure, and revocations.  
    **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Safety triage and cache cleanup follow. Blocking hides immediately; reverse blocks compose; report dismissal never reconnects the pair.

11. **Synchronize a device and enforce revocations.**  
    **Inputs/auth:** Bound device session, last applied revocation sequence, and acknowledgments.  
    **Steps:** Return revocations and current scope epochs before ordinary additions. The official client applies them in one local transaction: delete controlled rows, thumbnails, notifications, wrapped keys, cards, and conversations; persist the sequence; then request currently authorized additions. A sequence gap requires a full visibility resnapshot.  
    **Atomic:** The server page has a high-water mark; purge plus cursor advancement is atomic on the client.  
    **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Push merely wakes synchronization. Devices acknowledge independently. When offline, expired grants fail closed; a generation that has been revoked is never granted again.

12. **Perform photo verification through an external provider.**  
    **Inputs/auth:** Owner, current photo-set hash, live capture, and client nonce; callbacks require a signed provider event.  
    **Steps:** Create a single-use ten-minute challenge bound to the exact photo set; issue a restricted upload; submit it externally with the attempt ID as idempotency key. On callback, verify signature, deduplicate, confirm challenge and current photo hash, record the terminal result, and create a version-bound assertion only for a valid pass.  
    **Atomic:** Challenge creation is one transaction; callback result, assertion, trust version, and outbox are another because the external call cannot participate.  
    **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Show “pending,” never an optimistic badge. Late or duplicate callbacks cannot override a newer attempt or changed photo set.

13. **Build recommendation queues in scheduled bulk shards.**  
    **Inputs/auth:** Scheduler lease, user shard, checkpoint, snapshot time, model and policy versions; system authority only.  
    **Steps:** For each user with fewer than 100 usable cards, determine a valid physical or travel origin; use spatial cells and coarse preferences to retrieve at most 2,000 candidates; exclude current matches, separations, intents, and cooldowns; score compatibility, activity, distance, and diversity without incoming-like state; publish up to 500 items with pair, origin, and preference versions and 24-hour expiry.  
    **Atomic:** Publish each user’s batch independently.  
    **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Shards retry from durable checkpoints. Changes after generation are handled by delivery rechecks; a competing batch wins by generation compare-and-set.

14. **Expire state and apply bulk safety actions.**  
    **Inputs/auth:** Scheduler shard or signed moderator/rules-engine action; service or safety authority only.  
    **Steps:** Server time expires likes, grants, travel plans, locations, verification challenges, and entitlements; each expiry advances the affected projection and emits revocations where disclosure changes. For urgent suspension, first set an account-wide deny and increment its eligibility epoch, then close affected matches and queues in idempotent per-pair batches. Repair jobs close—not create—any episode lacking its two source likes or violating separation.  
    **Atomic:** The global deny or individual expiry is authoritative immediately; no transaction spans the whole graph.  
    **Eventual/UI/conflict:** Fan-out cleanup is checkpointed. Workers compare versions and retry changed rows rather than overwriting newer state; no optimistic UI is permitted.

### 6. Scale and cost

A realistic successful deployment envelope is:

- 10 million registered accounts, 2 million monthly active users, 600,000 daily active users, and 120,000 simultaneously connected devices.
- 10 million profiles, 30 million prompts, 40 million current photos, and roughly 100 TB of originals and derivatives.
- 45 million swipes per day: about 520 per second on average and 4,000–6,000 per second at peak; one year of immutable history is 16.4 billion events.
- 100–300 million unresolved active likes, with tens of thousands targeting a single popular profile.
- 300,000–700,000 matches opened and 18 million messages sent per day, with roughly 50 million active matches.
- 60 million cards delivered per day and about 120 million queued recommendation rows.
- Three daily refreshes scoring 2,000 candidates for each daily-active user produce roughly 3.6 billion candidate-score evaluations per day.
- Five million accepted location updates, 100,000 photo uploads, 10,000 verification attempts, 50,000 blocks, and 5,000 reports per day.

The principal costs are recommendation candidate retrieval and scoring, hot incoming-like partitions, and media delivery—at 0.5–1 MB per card, 60 million card views produce 30–60 TB of daily CDN traffic. Rebuilding every derivative after a block, movement, or suspension is infeasible: the authoritative generation must change cheaply, every delivery must recheck it, and cleanup must fan out asynchronously.

Location writes need displacement and freshness thresholds to avoid indexing every GPS sample. Swipe, match, entitlement, and safety histories need partitioning, retention, and cold compaction. Report evidence must remain segregated from consumer data. Realtime pair fan-out is modest; correctness across every cache, device, notification, and media channel is harder than raw message volume.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **Atomic two-party secret disclosure.** Recording a like must inspect the hidden opposite direction and, when reciprocal, create one match and conversation without exposing the hidden row through ordinary reads, errors, counts, identifiers, or intermediate events. A dedicated serialized pair operation and short notification delay are reasonable concessions. Letting clients fetch both likes or tolerating duplicate/transient matches is a cheat.

2. **Paid queries over a hidden domain.** “Who Liked You” must combine current incoming likes, expiry, passes, matches, blocks, safety state, and a time-bounded entitlement in one logical authorization decision. An online-only screen with five-minute grants is reasonable. Client-only paywalls, visibility-filtered counts, durable photo URLs, or cursors that survive entitlement loss are cheats.

3. **Recall of already-delivered data.** Correct block behavior requires authoritative pair epochs, encrypted controlled caches, short-lived wrapped keys, bilateral revocation logs, purge ordering, media capability expiry, and notification withdrawal. The honest concession excludes memory, screenshots, adversarial clients, and permits at most the stated ten-minute disconnected lease. Calling a database filter retroactive invisibility while cached cards or previews survive is a cheat.

4. **Privacy-preserving geographic top-k with large exclusions.** Candidate retrieval combines moving points, mutual radii, preferences, thousands of prior pair decisions, safety exclusions, stable pagination, diversity, and coarse disclosure. Periodic queues, spatial cells, fixed distance bands, and delivery-time rechecks are reasonable. Sending coordinates to the client, scanning the whole population, or allowing a stale queue to bypass a block is a cheat.

5. **Generation-fenced external and bulk authority.** Unmatch, block, suspension, photo edits, refunds, verification callbacks, offline actions, and queue workers can all invalidate older work. Every action needs idempotency and an expected pair, account, entitlement, or photo generation. Cleanup may be eventual only while the authoritative deny is immediate and every read checks it. Allowing a late callback or offline gesture to resurrect superseded state is a cheat.

### 8. Confidence

Canonical pair records, directional swipe history, unique match episodes, versioned profiles, spatial candidate retrieval, precomputed queues with live rechecks, idempotent external callbacks, transactional outboxes, short-lived media authorization, and restricted report evidence are standard practice.

The 10-minute revocation lease, 5-second connected-device target, 5-minute paid grant, 24-hour queue/location windows, 30/90/180-day pair periods, exact distance bands, retention periods, workload figures, and rule that ordinary ranking ignores incoming likes are deliberate product choices invented for this exercise.

The strongest and least standard requirement is retroactive invisibility for controlled offline caches. It is technically credible only for cooperative official clients with encrypted storage and expiring keys; absolute erasure from an adversarial device is impossible and is not claimed.

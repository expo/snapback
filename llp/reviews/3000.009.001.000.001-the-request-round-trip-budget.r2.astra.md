**FUNDAMENTAL: none identified.**

The in-place ordering itself is sound: serialized receipt writers hold the head lock through their receipt write and COMMIT, so a subsequent lookup after acquiring that lock observes the preceding committed receipt. Authority resolution after the lock also prevents a revocation from committing between that check and this transaction’s completion. The separate generation command fixes the earlier adoption-snapshot problem.

1. **MAJOR — Removing preflight replay still leaves current-schema checks ahead of the replacement lookup.**

   [§3.1](llp/3000.009.001.000.001-the-request-round-trip-budget.rfc.md:121) moves replay into the durable transaction, but does not move operation lookup, delivery validation and argument decoding behind it. Today the HTTP preflight deliberately precedes [signature lookup and decoding](snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1205). The engine explicitly documents [replay before anything the current generation could refuse](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:931).

   A committed write whose response was lost must still replay after its operation is removed or its arguments change. Merely deleting the two early lookups makes that retry fail before reaching the new lookup. This is another semantic dependency on a removed check.

   **Required change:** submit the raw operation name and wire arguments to final admission. Order fresh authority and store binding, then bound receipt lookup, then current-schema validation/decoding and execution on a miss. Preserve receipt precedence over replacement ephemeral execution too.

2. **MAJOR — Replay now depends on write ownership, without a safe outcome for ownership failure.**

   The proposed replay encounters the owner fence before authentication or receipt lookup. A stale token therefore produces [the writer’s `StoreOwnership` error](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/store.rs:125), even when this operation already committed.

   That error currently has no retryable override in [refusal conversion](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/error.rs:301). The client can [settle a nonretryable refusal as a failed write](snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:1137). Keeping the current mapping would turn “this process cannot inspect the receipt through its writer” into a terminal failure for an already committed operation. It also leaves §3.1’s metadata-bearing failure path without its promised final authentication.

   **Required change:** define this branch explicitly. Either authenticate and recover the receipt through a read snapshot, or retain an unresolved, retryable outcome until the current owner resolves it. Fence failure must not establish that the original operation failed.

3. **MAJOR — “Exactly one replay check” and “hit rolls back and returns” are not valid universal rules for pre-executed members.**

   The sibling requires both [receipt absence recorded at S and a serial receipt recheck](llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:254). Those checks serve different purposes; the serial check prevents two concurrently pre-executed copies from applying.

   A serial lookup can also see a receipt written by an earlier member of the **same uncommitted batch**. Such a hit cannot roll back the physical transaction or immediately acknowledge that receipt. The sibling requires [member-scoped rollback and acknowledgement after durable batch COMMIT](llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:299).

   **Required change:** scope the single-check/physical-rollback schedule to in-place, single-member execution. Preserve S recording, serial duplicate detection, member-scoped disposition and delayed acknowledgement for shared batches.

4. **MAJOR — The two §3.6 candidates cannot share the printed B2 schedules under the stated constraints.**

   Candidate **(b)** can combine fence, head, generation and authority queries with the required internal ordering. It cannot contain the transaction framing counted alongside them: `BEGIN`, persistent member/program savepoints, releases, or terminal `ROLLBACK`. SQL functions exclude transaction-control commands; PL/pgSQL also cannot provide those cross-call savepoints. A single extended-protocol Parse cannot contain multiple top-level commands. [SQL functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/xfunc-sql.html), [PL/pgSQL transaction management](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/plpgsql-transactions.html), [extended protocol](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/protocol-flow.html#PROTOCOL-FLOW-EXT-QUERY).

   Consequently, W1’s `read + ROLLBACK` and the mutation’s `authority + savepoints` / `releases + tail` need additional waits with that mechanism. Moving savepoints ahead of the locks is not equivalent: rolling back the program could then release locks required for recording its refusal receipt.

   Candidate **(a)** can express those command sequences. However, typed `PREPARE` declarations alone do not establish rule-5 parity for interpolated `EXECUTE` literals. An adapter encoder must preserve the typed port values—including arrays, NULLs, numeric bounds and terminal NUL refusal—without replacing them with dialect-interpreted policy inputs.

   **Required change:** specify a complete schedule and budget for each admissible mechanism. Qualify the typed encoding contract for (a); count the separate framing waits for (b). Latency measurements cannot resolve this semantic difference.

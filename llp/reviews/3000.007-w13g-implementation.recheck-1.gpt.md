**High — Completion presence leaks the withheld historical floor.**  
Location: [partition.rs:668](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-w13-review/repo/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:668), with observable publication at [partition.rs:646](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-w13-review/repo/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:646).

The comparison `key >= floor` still uses the private historical selection floor. It controls whether any completion is emitted, so withholding the floor’s literal bytes does not remove its disclosure.

Concrete counterexample:

1. In both histories, `p1.at = 100`, `p3.at = 3000`; `p2.at` is **800** versus **2000**. Bob joins a `last 2` group, selecting `p3,p2`.
2. Before Bob receives the stream, delete `p2` and move `p3.at` to **1000**.
3. Both histories deliver identical current row images and deletion events.
4. With historical floor **800**, `p3` qualifies and completion names its current key **1000**. With historical floor **2000**, it does not qualify and completion is omitted.

The payload therefore reveals whether the deleted row’s old key was below 1000. Moving `p2` into an unreadable group produces the same channel. This violates the [current-readable-image rule](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback-w13-review/repo/llp/3000-snapback4.explainer.md:651). Existing paired tests keep `p3` at 3000, so both worlds satisfy the comparison and miss it.

**Smallest correct change:** establish boundary eligibility from a current readable suffix whose rows were delivered, rather than comparing against withheld historical values; leave coverage unknown when that proof is unavailable. Simply deleting the comparison is unsafe—an unselected `p1` at 1500 could then be omitted above a claimed floor of 1000. Add the paired deletion/unreadable regressions with the surviving selected row also moved, on both stores and wasm.

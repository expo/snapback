## 1. Overall assessment

The central decision is right, and it is argued the right way. "Roles are app data in a system-recognized shape" is the correct answer to LLP 0011's open question: it avoids both the staleness trap of session-claim roles (exactly the failure class LLP 0053 spent six designs eliminating) and the analyzability trap of an external policy engine that does not own the sync layer. The strongest sentence in the document is the honest statement of why this belongs in Snapback at all — revocation correctness — and the design correctly identifies that the primitive's real job is guaranteeing the role check *is* a matcher-visible data read rather than a cached flag. The scoping decisions (closed vocabularies, flat facts, no hierarchies, no Zanzibar, global roles as a degenerate scope, memberships as substrate rather than a teams product) are disciplined and well-matched to the corpus's v1 posture.

The document is not ready, for one load-bearing reason and two significant ones. First, the section the whole RFC hangs on — "Invalidation: correct by construction" — misstates how declared membership tables participate in the *Accepted* LLP 0053 contract. It implies a path-conditional `permissionBacking` (ordinary writes ride only the matcher plus delivery obligations; only "bulk paths that bypass the matcher" get permission-backing treatment) that LLP 0053 does not define: under 0053, `permissionBacking` is a static table property, and *every* committed write to such a table is authorization event 1, advancing the global version and quarantining every auth-scoped query in v1. The RFC's own Rooms bullet requires exactly that event-1 treatment for ordinary kicks. Because 0056 blesses memberships as the universal primitive, this coarse-event blast radius is the dominant runtime cost of adopting it, and the document never states it. Second, the new typed denial `SNAPBACK_AUTH_ROLE_DENIED` is claimed to sit "in LLP 0053's producer enumeration," but that enumeration is deliberately closed on a single code; as written, a role denial on a live re-query would quarantine and burn the bounded retry episode instead of producing the clean `delete` the design intends. Third, the compilation claims ("compile to analyzable, matcher-observable RLS expressions… in the structured, serializable filter language LLP 0011 pins") present a substantial amount of unbuilt and unpinned surface as if it existed — the shipped filter IR is single-table with no join/EXISTS form, the pinned v0/v1 syncable-live subset is explicitly one-primary-table, and declarative write-side rules are an explicit ratified non-goal of the frozen 0039 slice.

All three are fixable within the proposed architecture; none of them argues for a different design. The fix is a precise restatement of the 0053 mapping (owning the v1 coarse cost and pointing at shadow-mode graduation, for which memberships are — see Suggestions — the best candidate on-ramp the corpus has), a one-line denial-code correction, and an honest "what exists / what this adds / what lands first" section. With those, this should converge quickly.

## 2. Strengths

- **The core positioning (§Summary).** "Roles are app data, in a system-recognized shape" precisely threads 0011's open question, and the explicit rejection of session-claim roles — with the correct citation of why (staleness is the problem 0053 exists to prevent) — keeps 0056 coherent with the corpus's hardest-won contract.
- **The reason-to-exist argument (§Summary, §Motivation).** "A bolted-on authorization library cannot deliver that, because it does not own the sync layer" is the genuine differentiator, and "hand-rolled versions get invalidation wrong" is exactly the failure mode 0053's history documents.
- **Disciplined non-goals (§Non-Goals).** Flat facts, no relation graphs, no role hierarchies in v1, no permission matrices, teams-as-substrate-not-product — each is the right v1 cut, and each names what it defers rather than pretending it is solved.
- **The subscribe-time split is exactly right (§Rule helpers).** Resolving the *principal* at subscribe time while the *role* stays a per-evaluation data join is the correct division: the volatile part is data the matcher can see, so revocation needs no resubscribe. This is the design insight that makes the whole thing work with 0053.
- **Operator-plane separation (§Global roles).** The explicit statement that an app `admin` never satisfies `AdminRead`/`AdminWrite`/`AdminSecretRead`/`ImpersonationCapability` closes a confusion every future app author would otherwise have, and matches 0039's plane separation exactly.
- **The impersonation seam is named with an acceptance obligation (§What stays out of the session).** "An impersonated role that would never be minted for a real session must not be reachable from one" is the right negative test for the `ctx.auth.roles` pin, consistent with 0039 (`roles` always `[]` for real sessions) and 0053's sealed projection.
- **Agent composition is clean (§Agents, §Motivation).** Evaluating role checks against the effective user's memberships, with grant scopes only narrowing invocation, matches 0055's `userId`-is-the-effective-principal choice; the two drafts' cross-references are mutually consistent (0055 defers resource-scoped grant vocabulary to 0056; 0056 defers agent identity to 0055).
- **Precedence hygiene (§Status).** "Where this document restates a contract LLP 0011, 0039, or 0053 pins, those documents win" is the right clause — and it is also what makes Concern 1 resolvable without renegotiating 0053.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — §Invalidation misstates the Accepted LLP 0053 contract for membership writes, and hides the v1 blast radius of the primitive.**
Evidence: 0056 states "*Ordinary membership writes need no new mechanism.* A query gated by `hasRole` joins the membership table, so the matcher observes membership inserts/updates/deletes and wakes the subscription; a resulting shrink is protected by 0053's witnessed delivery obligation," and then assigns permission-backing treatment only to "*Bulk paths that bypass the matcher* — import, `app.reset` seed, a future policy reload." LLP 0053 defines no path-conditional permission-backing: event 1 of its closed universe is "a committed write to a `permissionBacking` table," enforced "through one commit hook used by every writer — HostPlan, Ibex, seed, admin edits, import, reset," and its card-game worked classification pins the consequence for ordinary membership writes: "a kick advances the global epoch → durable quarantine/resnapshot **and** room-authority suspension." The bulk examples 0056 cites are already events 2/3 in 0053 (activation; reset/import rotate the *incarnation*), so the bullet is redundant where it is stated and misleading where it is silent; the genuinely non-matcher writer 0053 names — admin edits — goes unmentioned. Meanwhile 0056's own Rooms bullet ("projects room reauthorization exactly as 0053 pins for membership kicks") requires ordinary kicks to be enumerated events, since 0013/0053's room barrier machinery fires only for "every writer that may commit events 1–4." Finally, because v1 has exactly one global epoch, every ordinary membership write (every join, leave, invite-accept, role change — routine traffic once memberships are the blessed primitive for messaging, games, channels, events) quarantines every auth-scoped query of every connected client until resnapshot (0053 §Blast radius). The RFC that makes memberships universal never states this cost.
Resolution criterion: rewrite §Invalidation to state the actual mapping — a `membership` declaration sets `permissionBacking: true` in 0053's sense; every committed membership write is authorization event 1 (global version advance, coarse quarantine obligations, room dependency barriers), *in addition to* the matcher wake and delivery obligation for queries that join the table; drop or correct the bulk-path framing — and add an explicit paragraph owning the v1 coarse blast radius, citing 0053's measured-blast-radius acceptance and the shadow-mode graduation path as the mitigation. (If the author instead intends ordinary membership writes *not* to advance the version, that is an amendment to Accepted 0053 and must be proposed as one — the RFC's own precedence clause forbids doing it silently, and the Rooms bullet would break.)

**2. MATERIAL — `SNAPBACK_AUTH_ROLE_DENIED` contradicts LLP 0053's closed trusted-denial enumeration.**
Evidence: 0056 §Rule helpers claims the new code is "a trusted denial in LLP 0053's producer enumeration." 0053 pins: "`SNAPBACK_AUTH_PERMISSION_DENIED` is a trusted invocation outcome… Its only v1 producers are: the declarative read/RLS permission evaluator; the room-authority reauthorization gate…; and the shared invocation gate's host-owned terminal `ctx.permissions.deny()` operation," and its dispositions table grants `delete` only to "typed `SNAPBACK_AUTH_PERMISSION_DENIED`," with "any other error immediately produc[ing] `quarantine`" plus the bounded three-attempt/15-second retry episode and manual-retry-only recovery. As specified, a `requireRole` failure waking a live re-query would emit a code the obligation machinery treats as untyped: the query lands in stuck quarantine behind an exhausted retry episode instead of the clean terminal delete the design intends — precisely the fail-closed-but-wedged shape 0053's denial class exists to avoid.
Resolution criterion: either (a) make `requireRole` produce the trusted `SNAPBACK_AUTH_PERMISSION_DENIED` through the same host-owned terminal operation, carrying the role facts (resource, required roles, present-with-wrong-role vs. absent) as structured *details* under that code, or (b) state explicitly that acceptance of this RFC amends 0053's producer/proof enumeration to admit the sibling code, and enumerate its disposition. Option (a) is cheaper and preserves 0053's "forged code quarantines" property unchanged.

**3. MATERIAL — the compilation claims present unbuilt, unpinned surface as existing machinery, and one Validation Requirement is unsatisfiable on the frozen slice.**
Evidence: §Rule helpers says the helpers "compile to membership-table joins in the structured, serializable filter language LLP 0011 pins" and are "analyzable by construction." What is pinned and shipped: `QueryPlan` is single-table (`src/manifest.rs:1143` — one `table`, `filters: Vec<FilterExpr>` of `field/op/value`); `ValueExpr`'s only auth variable is `AuthUserId` (`src/manifest.rs:1273`, `src/value_expr.rs:122`) — no join/EXISTS form, no `auth.kind`, no `auth.roles`; `TableManifest` carries no membership metadata, closed vocabulary, or unique-index capability (`src/manifest.rs:227`); `FieldType` has no id/reference kind backing `v.id("users")`; LLP 0004 §First-Slice pins the v0 syncable-live shape as "one primary table… equality predicates over an index prefix," and LLP 0008 pins the same for v1; and LLP 0039 §Non-goals records a *ratified narrowing* of 0011 deferring declarative write-side row-level rules entirely (`writeAuthorization: "handler"` interim). So `memberOf`/`hasRole` require: a new membership-predicate IR node, matcher read-summary participation for it, write-transaction evaluation, unique composite indexes, reference validators, and either an extension of the syncable-live subset or an explicit statement that role-gated queries ride the conservative re-query/`live_replace` path (which *does* exist and *is* obligation-backed under 0053 — the honest v1 story). The Validation Requirement "membership write rules are two-sided: a `guest` cannot promote themselves" cannot be satisfied before the deferred declarative write-rule surface exists.
Resolution criterion: add a short "what exists vs. what this adds" subsection: name the filter-IR extension, the matcher/read-summary work, the unique-index and reference-validator schema surface, and the write-rule dependency; sequence the RFC against 0039's ordered items (the way 0055 does — "lands strictly after"), noting that the live-revocation validation rows require 0053 item 6; and either scope v1 role-gated live queries to the conservative replace path or state the syncable-subset extension as explicit new design. No redesign needed — the claims must be dated, not changed.

**4. NON-BLOCKING — global roles inherit the one-row-per-(principal, resource) rule, so a user cannot hold `admin` and `moderator` simultaneously.**
Evidence: §Declaring a membership table pins uniqueness per `(principal, resource)`; §Global roles makes the resource the app singleton, so uniqueness degenerates to one global role per user, and the escape hatch ("declare a second membership table") is at its most awkward exactly here. The multi-role open question is listed, but the text does not acknowledge that the global scope makes it bite on day one.
Resolution criterion: acknowledge the constraint in §Global roles and either bless multiple vocabularies/tables for the global scope explicitly or note that the multi-role open question is expected to resolve first at `scope: "app"`.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — the first-host bootstrap is unstated.**
Evidence: §Rule helpers offers "'only a host can change roles' is expressible as an ordinary write rule on the membership table itself" — a self-referential rule that cannot admit the *first* host row of a new resource (no host exists to authorize it). Every app hits this at resource creation; the canonical idiom (creator-becomes-host, expressed as a disjunct referencing the parent row's creator field — itself a cross-table reference requiring the same join machinery as Concern 3) deserves a named pattern or a declaration affordance.
Resolution criterion: one paragraph naming the blessed bootstrap idiom, or an open-question entry acknowledging it.

**6. NON-BLOCKING — closed-vocabulary lifecycle on shrink is unspecified.**
Evidence: §Declaring a membership table says "vocabulary changes are schema changes, visible in review," but not what happens when a role is removed while rows still carry it: refused activation, migration requirement, or orphaned-row semantics. Since vocabulary activation is 0053 event 2 (activation), the interaction is nontrivial.
Resolution criterion: a sentence pinning the shrink behavior (refuse activation while nonconforming rows exist is the obvious fail-closed choice) or an explicit open question.

**7. NON-BLOCKING — small consistency and hygiene gaps.**
Evidence: (a) the denial payload ("the caller's actual membership state") should be reconciled with 0053's "the terminal producer… never echo[es] private permission facts to the client" — caller-own-role is defensible client-side, but the doctor-vs-client split should be deliberate; (b) the impersonation-seam acceptance is stated in §What stays out of the session but missing from §Validation Requirements; (c) §Rule helpers says an unauthenticated principal "matches no membership rows" while §Validation Requirements says the unauthenticated client "is rejected" — 0011 supports both, but pick the operative one per surface; (d) the **Related** header omits LLP 0013 and LLP 0024, both cited in the body; (e) no validation row binds the `.membership()` declaration to the room dependency registry (the kick trace lives only in 0053's acceptance), and none covers retained/offline revocation under the lease despite §Invalidation's retained bullet.
Resolution criterion: mechanical edits to the listed sections.

## 4. Suggestions

- **Name memberships as the shadow-mode graduation on-ramp.** LLP 0053's central open question (#1) asks for "a structural derivation over transactional old/new rows or an equivalently closed source" before subject-scoped narrowing/suppression of the coarse event. A declared membership table *is* that closed source: the affected subject is a declared column (`principal`), inserts/deletes name it directly, and the one-row-per-(principal, resource) rule makes updates point writes whose preimage is readable in the same transaction — sidestepping the journal's missing-preimage problem. Requiring the principal column to be a user-reference type would make derivation mechanical. This turns Concern 1's blast-radius cost into a roadmap ("coarse today, targeted after graduation") and materially strengthens the case for the primitive. It is the best answer the corpus currently has to 0053 OQ1 and the RFC should claim it.
- **Derive `permissionFields` from the declaration.** A membership table with incidental columns (`joinedAt`, notification preferences) would fire a global authorization event on every preference update. 0053 built `permissionFields` for exactly this; the declaration knows the role/principal/resource columns and should emit the narrowing automatically.
- **Resolve role ranking with compile-time sugar, not runtime hierarchy.** An ordered vocabulary plus `hasRoleAtLeast(event, "cohost")` that *expands to the explicit list at manifest compile time* gives the ergonomics of ranking with zero analyzability cost — the compiled artifact is still an explicit role list. This closes the first open question cheaply.
- **Pin the default visibility of membership tables.** State that an undeclared-rules membership table is not client-readable/syncable (fail-closed), then offer the "members see co-members" preset as the blessed opt-in. Membership rows are social-graph data; the default matters more here than for ordinary tables.
- **Say a word about optimistic/offline parity.** `hasRole`-gated mutations evaluated optimistically need membership rows in the local store (or a declared server-decides divergence); 0011's replay-parity requirement makes this worth one sentence.
- **Lean toward invitation-as-role.** Modeling "invited" as a vocabulary entry inherits the invalidation machinery for free (revoking an invite clears the invitee's preview subscription), while `hasRole(event, "host", "cohost", "guest")` lists keep invited ≠ member explicit. Worth stating as the leaning even if the question stays open.
- **Add the missing validation rows:** declaration→room-registry binding (a kick on a declared membership suspends and reauthorizes the room), ordinary-membership-write version/blast-radius behavior (whichever way Concern 1 resolves, pin it in acceptance), the impersonation seam, and offline revocation bounded by the lease.

## 5. Open questions

Beyond the RFC's own (good) list:

- What may `resourceRef` reference — a row field, a query arg, a literal — and how does a rule on a *child* table (photos in an event's album) name the parent resource? This determines both analyzability and whether resource nesting can stay "always explicit" without being unwritable.
- Are `memberOf`/`hasRole` available inside imperative Ibex handlers (as captured reads participating in the read summary) or only in declarative rules plus `requireRole`? The answer decides how much of the app corpus gets the correct-by-construction property.
- Does the declaration extend the syncable-live subset with a membership semi-join class (new matcher work), or do role-gated queries stay on the conservative re-query/`live_replace` path in v1? (Concern 3's sequencing question, but it deserves an answer as design intent, not just scoping.)
- Retrofit: what happens when `.membership()` is declared on an existing table with rows — are existing rows validated against vocabulary and uniqueness at activation, and is a violation a refused activation?
- What resource value does `scope: "app"` use in the uniqueness key, and is the singleton spelled consistently across helpers, denials, and audit?
- When this RFC is accepted, which dated amendments land in 0011 (its roles open question), 0053 (producer enumeration, if Concern 2 resolves via amendment), and 0024 (`--as-role` semantics against `hasGlobalRole` only)?

## 6. Readiness verdict

The architecture is right, the scoping is disciplined, and the document earns its place in the corpus — but its load-bearing section currently contradicts the Accepted invalidation contract it builds on (and conceals the v1 blast-radius consequence of making memberships universal), its new typed denial is outside 0053's closed enumeration with a real fail-closed-but-wedged consequence, and its feasibility claims present unbuilt filter-language, schema, and write-rule surface as existing. Under the review standard — 0011/0039/0053 win where restated, and a false READY is worse than a false NOT READY — these three must be resolved first. All are resolvable within the proposed design, most with targeted prose changes.

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a good proposal, and the right one for this corpus. The deliberate middle it takes on LLP 0011's open question — **roles are app data in a system-recognized shape** — is the only answer consistent with the rest of the system: LLP 0053 exists precisely because authorization must be checked as live data rather than baked into credentials, and LLP 0039 pins `ctx.auth.roles` to `[]` for real sessions, so session-claim roles were already foreclosed. The RFC's sharpest argument is the one it leads with: the reason to build memberships *into* Snapback is revocation correctness, which only the owner of the sync layer can deliver. That claim is true, and the §Invalidation section backs it with a precise, correct mapping onto LLP 0053's accepted machinery — both orthogonal declarations, derived `permissionFields`, event-1 semantics through the one-commit-hook rule, the room projection, and an explicitly owned global blast radius. The graduation on-ramp claim (declared memberships as the "equivalently closed source" 0053's first open question asks for) is a genuine contribution, correctly framed as input to 0053 rather than a decision made here.

I verified the "What exists, what this adds" section against the implementation, and it is accurate: `QueryPlan` is single-table (`src/manifest.rs:1143`), `ValueExpr` carries exactly one auth variable (`AuthUserId`, `src/manifest.rs:1279`) and no relationship/EXISTS form, `TableManifest` has no membership metadata and `IndexManifest` no uniqueness flag (`src/manifest.rs:227, :261`), there is no `ctx.permissions` host operation, and no `.membership()` authoring surface exists in `packages/snapback-codegen/`. The delivery-lane honesty (v1 rides the conservative re-query/`live_replace` lane; the semi-join incremental lane is a named follow-on with its 0008/0004 amendments) matches LLP 0008's DK-3 pin exactly. This is the honest seam-stating the round-1 revision promised, and it holds up under checking.

One coherence defect keeps this from READY, and it is small but load-bearing: the document claims **"LLP 0053 — no amendment"** while introducing exactly the two kinds of new protected-query input — a new declarative IR expression kind and a new runtime host operation — that Accepted LLP 0053's machine-enforced input profile **denies by default until classified**, and classification lives in 0053's profile/registry. As written, every helper-gated auth-scoped live query and every `requireRole` inside a protected query — the document's central deliverables, and most of its `[live]` validation rows — would be refused by the controlling document's default-deny rule, with no named obligation anywhere to admit them. The sibling RFC (LLP 0055) handled the parallel situation correctly with a dated 0053 amendment; this document should match. The fix is a one-bullet change to §Companion obligations. The remaining concerns are non-blocking: a default-visibility claim stated wider than v1 can enforce, acceptance fixtures that the v1 global coarse event can mask, an unnamed delegation-persistence seam, and a HostPlan-writer idiom gap.

Is anything wrongheaded? No. The opinionated choices — closed per-declaration vocabularies, one row per `(principal, resource)`, flat facts with no hierarchy or nesting, declaration-bound helpers with no global `hasRole` — are all defensible, all stated with their costs, and all reversible along named open questions. The plan is feasible and well-sequenced against 0039's activation matrix.

## 2. Strengths

- **The middle answer, argued rather than asserted** (§Summary, §Motivation): memberships as app data in a system-recognized shape, with revocation correctness as the reason it must be built in. The "a bolted-on authorization library cannot deliver that, because it does not own the sync layer" framing is the correct product argument.
- **"What exists, what this adds" is exemplary and accurate** (§What exists, what this adds): every shipped-substrate claim I checked against `src/` is true, the new surface is enumerated by name, and the v1-vs-follow-on delivery lanes are stated without overclaiming what "analyzable by construction" buys in v1.
- **The LLP 0053 mapping is precise and in 0053's own terms** (§Invalidation): both orthogonal declarations composing on one table (matching 0053's roster/card-game fixtures), `permissionFields` derived from the declaration so incidental-column updates are suppressed under 0053's known-update-only narrowing rule, inserts/deletes always firing, and reset/import inherited rather than restated.
- **The v1 cost is owned, not hidden** (§Invalidation, "The v1 cost, owned"): membership churn as a stream of global event-1 quarantines is stated plainly, tied to 0053's blast-radius measurement obligation, and paired with a credible narrowing path.
- **The graduation on-ramp claim** (§Invalidation): the observation that a declared membership is a closed subject source — declared principal column, point writes with in-transaction pre-images under the uniqueness rule — is a real answer-shaped input to 0053's open question 1, and it is correctly owned by 0053 rather than decided here.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — "LLP 0053 — no amendment" contradicts 0053's default-deny protected-query profile for this RFC's own new inputs.**
Evidence: §Companion obligations: *"LLP 0053 — no amendment: this RFC uses the existing `permissionBacking` declaration, the existing event universe, and the existing trusted code."* But §What exists names as new surface *"a membership predicate node in the structured filter IR"* and *"the `ctx.permissions` host surface."* Accepted LLP 0053's protected-query input profile pins: *"A newly added expression kind is denied for protected queries until this profile classifies it"* (profile item 2) and *"every unknown or future host op [is] also denied by default"* (profile item 4, whose registry pins op→class assignments in 0053's own text), where a protected query is *"every auth-scoped live subscription and every retainable query."* A `hasRole`-gated live subscription and a `requireRole`-bearing protected query are therefore refused by the controlling Accepted document until the new expression kind and host op are classified — and classification is 0053-owned surface that no companion obligation claims. The document's own `[live]` validation rows are unreachable under that refusal. LLP 0055 faced the identical situation (new sealed projections joining 0053's profile) and named a dated 0053 amendment (*"the protected-query input profile covers them"*); this RFC disclaims one.
Resolution criterion: replace the "no amendment" bullet with a dated LLP 0053 obligation (or an explicit 0053-blessed routing through its implementing spec) that classifies the membership predicate node as an admitted declarative read contributing to the capture/read-summary witness, and `ctx.permissions.requireRole`/`get` as `DatabaseCaptured` on success and `TerminalAuthorization` on denial. The event-universe, trusted-code, and `permissionBacking` "no amendment" claims can stand; the profile claim cannot.

**2. NON-BLOCKING — the fail-closed default-visibility claim is stated wider than v1 can enforce.**
Evidence: §Declaring: *"an undeclared-rules membership table is **not** client-readable or syncable"*; validation row *"[base] Fail-closed default visibility: an undeclared-rules membership table is not readable/syncable by any client."* In v1, all client reads are handler-mediated (function-first API per LLP 0004; the declarative rules surface is unbuilt per this RFC's own §What exists and LLP 0011 §Status), so an app-authored Ibex query handler can always read the membership table and return roster rows — the system cannot inspect handler outputs. The enforceable v1 content is narrower: refuse `.sync`/syncable/live/generated-read surfaces on an undeclared membership table. As written, the [base] row can pass trivially (no direct surface exists for any table) while the actual leak path is untestable, giving false confidence.
Resolution criterion: one or two sentences stating which surfaces the default binds in v1 (sync/live/generated read surfaces; handler reads remain app-trusted until declarative read rules land) and scoping the validation row to those surfaces.

**3. NON-BLOCKING — the v1 global coarse event masks the matcher-path fixtures; the masking disappears exactly when it matters.**
Evidence: §Invalidation pins *"The primitive's job is making sure the check **is** a captured read the matcher can see."* But in v1 every membership write is a global event 1, so every auth-scoped subscription quarantines and resnapshots regardless of whether the captured-dependency/matcher wake works at all — the `[live]` rows ("Revocation clears live results," "hasRole-gated query") can pass with a silently broken matcher path. That protection is precisely what 0053's graduation is designed to remove, and this RFC claims to be the on-ramp. The hazard is concrete in shipped code: `CapturedReadKind::Permission` exists today and is ignored by both the matcher (`src/live_matcher.rs:190`) and commit-time read-set validation (`src/storage.rs:3233-3234`) — the obvious-looking capture kind for a `requireRole` read is exactly the one that would make both pinned behaviors silently fail. The `[base]` read-set-validation row catches the mutation half; no row isolates the query-side wake.
Resolution criterion: amend the revocation `[live]` row to assert the **delivery-obligation** witness (matcher path, causing data version) independently of the authorization-event obligation, and add a fixture: a `requireRole`-gated query whose handler performs no other read of the membership table wakes on a membership change (asserted with the coarse event's effect factored out, e.g. under shadow-mode narrowing or by inspecting the obligation type).

**4. NON-BLOCKING — membership writes performed under delegation outlive the grant, including self-seating, and neither §Agents nor validation names it.**
Evidence: §Agents covers delegated *reads* ("evaluates role checks against the effective user's memberships") and the validation rows cover delegated visibility, but a delegated agent whose grant scope covers a role-management mutation can — legitimately, under the effective user's `host` authority — insert a membership row for **any** principal, including its own actor `userId`. Memberships are durable app data (this RFC, §Declaring), so that row survives grant revocation: the agent later holds `host` autonomously after the delegation that created it is gone. This is a by-design consequence of "the agent sees/does what the user could" (LLP 0055), but it is the kind of authority-persistence seam this corpus consistently names rather than leaves implicit (cf. 0055's restore-revokes-grants reasoning).
Resolution criterion: one sentence in §Agents acknowledging that delegated membership writes persist beyond the grant and are attributed via the actor/effective/grant audit tuple, plus a validation row (delegated agent seats a principal — including its own actor — as `host`; grant revoked; the membership persists and doctor/audit attributes its creation to the tuple).

**5. NON-BLOCKING — the v1 write-enforcement idiom names only the Ibex handler path; HostPlan mutation writers have no named role-check idiom.**
Evidence: §What exists ("Write-side enforcement, v1 vs. later") pins the v1 idiom as *"`await ctx.permissions.requireRole(eventId, "host")` before the membership write,"* which only exists in Ibex handlers. HostPlan mutations are first-class writers (this RFC lists HostPlan in the every-writer enforcement list; `MutationStep::{Get,Guard}` per LLP 0051 D2, `src/manifest.rs:1199`) and can write membership tables; the central vocabulary/uniqueness checks hold for them, but the *role-authorization* idiom for a HostPlan membership write (a `Get` on the membership table plus `Guard`, evaluated inside the commit transaction) is unstated.
Resolution criterion: one sentence naming the HostPlan idiom (Get+Guard against the declaration's unique index inside the commit transaction) or explicitly restricting the v1 blessed membership-write path to Ibex mutations.

- **The core answer (§Summary, §Design/Declaring).** "Roles are app data in a system-recognized shape" resolves 0011's open question without inventing a policy engine; membership tables stay queryable/joinable/exportable while the declaration buys centralized vocabulary enforcement, explainable denial, and 0053 wiring. Global RBAC as the degenerate app-scoped case (§Global roles) avoids a second mechanism.
- **The captured-dependency contract (§What exists).** Pinning the representation off the dead `Permission` kind, and pinning the empty/denied-lookup capture as an empty-result query dependency on the index range, is grounded in verified matcher behavior — the captured-`Query` short-circuit makes hidden-to-visible wakes a correctness requirement, and the document says so with the exact failure ("a newly-added member permanently invisible"). The conservative table-level dependency for row-field `resourceRef`, with "coarse wake is a re-query cost, never a correctness loss," picks the right side.
- **The two-sided, scope-safe v1 write contract (§Declaring, §What exists).** Immutable principal/resource axes plus the target-row-resource idiom close the confused-deputy shape structurally rather than rhetorically, and the pinned semantics for the eventual declarative rules keep the v1 idiom and the follow-on rules from diverging. The negative fixtures (unrelated target row, forged inputs, revocation racing the check) are in the validation matrix.
- **`requireRole` decomposition (§Rule helpers).** Splitting the check into a `DatabaseCaptured` read (both outcomes) plus the existing `TerminalAuthorization` deny op respects 0053's one-class-per-operation rule exactly, reuses the closed producer enumeration, and keeps the minimized denial payload (`kind`, `callerState`) on the right side of 0053's "never echo private permission facts" rule.
- **Honest delivery-lane accounting (§What exists, "Delivery lanes, honestly").** Stating that v1 helper-gated queries ride the existing re-query/`live_replace` lane — and enumerating what "analyzable by construction" buys in v1 (wake, doctor, vocabulary) versus what it does not (incremental deltas) — matches LLP 0008's pinned subset and prevents the document from overselling.
- **The v1 cost, owned (§Invalidation).** Making membership churn a stream of coarse event-1 quarantines is named as the deliberate 0053 trade, with per-declaration blast-radius measurement added as the evidence base for narrowing — and the graduation paragraph distinguishes row-attribution closure (supplied) from usage-side closure (not supplied), with the aggregate-check counterexample and a cheaper declaration-scoped intermediate.
- **Plane hygiene (§Global roles).** App-level `admin` never satisfying `AdminRead`/`AdminWrite`/`AdminSecretRead`/`ImpersonationCapability` mirrors LLP 0055 §Plane boundary from the other side, and the validation matrix carries the negative.
- **The impersonation seam (§What stays out of the session).** The exactly-one-app-global-declaration rule is deterministic and refuses ambiguity at setup; the test-fidelity note (impersonated roles exercise the session path, so one acceptance case must run against a real seeded row) closes a hole most designs would not notice.
- **Agents composition (§Agents).** Delegated-write persistence beyond grant revocation is stated as designed behavior rather than discovered later, and `provenance: true` gives the attribution claim a mechanism rather than a promise, with the residual (no durable log until 0019 F8) stated. The kind-gating warnings preserve both failure modes of the naive spellings, consistent with 0055's `isDirectHuman` conjunction.

## 3. Concerns

**C1. MATERIAL — `incidental` can name the declaration's own axis columns, silently defeating event-1 semantics for role changes.**
*Evidence:* §Invalidation: "A declaration may narrow with an explicit `incidental: [...]` list; declarative rules referencing an incidental column are a manifest error (analyzable), and because imperative reads cannot be statically proven, the `incidental` declaration is the app's explicit attestation that those columns never carry authorization meaning." Nothing excludes `role` (or `principal`/`resource`/the `provenance` columns) from that list. For an app using only imperative checks — no declarative rule references the role column, so the stated manifest error never fires — `incidental: ["role"]` is accepted, and every role *update* (host→guest downgrade, invite-accept) is a "known update provably within `incidental`," suppressed under 0053's narrowing rule. Inserts/deletes still fire (0053 pins that), but suppressed role changes mean: no authorization-version advance, no room-authority barrier (LLP 0013's kick-by-downgrade), and no retained-result gating for exactly the transitions the primitive exists to protect. The live matcher path survives via captured dependencies, but the version path — which rooms and retained reads ride — does not. This is a false attestation the system can refuse *structurally*, because unlike a hand-written `permissionBacking` table (where 0053 cannot know which columns carry authorization meaning), here the declaration itself names `role` as the authorization column. The document's own posture — "pinned against the confused-deputy shape, not just stated," "the derivation owns the table" — demands the refusal, and the validation matrix has no negative for it (the incidental rows test only "a genuinely incidental-only update wakes nothing").
*Resolution criterion:* Pin a floor in §Declaring or §Invalidation: the declared `principal`, `resource`, and `role` columns (and generated `provenance` columns) are never declarable `incidental` — derived `permissionFields` always includes them; violation is a typed manifest error. Add the negative fixture to the [base] validation rows.

**C2. NON-BLOCKING — the `--as-membership` facility appears to contradict the adjacent "never satisfiable by impersonation" pin.**
*Evidence:* §What stays out of the session names "a resource-qualified `--as-membership <declaration>:<resource>:<role>` facility" for the 0024 amendment, then two sentences later pins "Scoped-declaration checks are **never** satisfiable by impersonated roles — tests seed real membership rows." If `--as-membership` is an impersonation input that satisfies a scoped check, the pin is false; if it is sugar that seeds a real row (consistent with the pin), the document does not say so, and the section's framing ("Its semantics against this RFC are settled here") makes the ambiguity load-bearing rather than deferrable.
*Resolution criterion:* One clause stating what `--as-membership` is — either "sugar for seeding a real membership row under dev authority (so the never-satisfiable-by-impersonation pin holds unconditionally)" or an explicit carve-out with its own test-fidelity note, mirroring the global-role one.

**C3. NON-BLOCKING — the stale-deny direction of the check/commit race attributes its catch to a mechanism that does not run on that path.**
*Evidence:* §Rule helpers: "a denial produced under a snapshot that a concurrent grant has already superseded is likewise caught by read-set validation — acceptance carries that race in both directions," and the [base] row: "a role revoked between check and commit fails read-set validation (both directions: a stale allow and a stale deny are each caught)." Commit-time read-set validation (LLP 0004 §Write Semantics; `storage.rs`) runs at commit; a denied invocation commits nothing, so as written the stale-deny half of the row is untestable by the named mechanism. The outcome is fail-closed either way (a spurious deny is retryable; on live paths 0053's proof-witness/obligation-floor rules bound a stale deny against pending obligations), but the document either intends a new behavior — the deny op validates its captured read set at production time, the way a commit would, before the trusted denial is emitted — or it means the weaker witness/floor property, and the acceptance row should say which.
*Resolution criterion:* Respell the sentence and the validation clause to name the actual mechanism per path: allow direction = commit-time read-set validation; deny direction = either an explicit "the deny op validates captured reads before producing the trusted denial" pin (new, and then it belongs in the 0053 amendment's classification of the read primitive) or the honest fail-closed statement (a stale deny is a retryable refusal carrying a coherent witness; it is never fail-open).

**C4. NON-BLOCKING — `provenance` semantics are unpinned for non-session writers and across export/import, and the mechanism covers only creation.**
*Evidence:* §Agents: `provenance: true` adds "host-filled, immutable columns (`grantedByActor`, `grantedByEffective`, `grantedByGrant` — null grant and actor = effective for direct sessions) written by the central path on insert." The central write path also includes seed, admin edits, import, and system/cron (§Declaring "for every writer") — what the host fills for `system`, `adminBypass`, seed, and especially import (re-stamp on import versus preserve exported values, which collides with "host-filled immutable") is unstated; `grantedByGrant` references grant ids that are excluded from logical export and terminally revoked by 0055 recovery, so it should be pinned as a historical tag, not a resolvable reference (0055's `created_by` "tags, not foreign keys" precedent). Separately, the Revised header claims "durable attribution for delegated membership writes now has a real mechanism," but the mechanism is insert-only: a delegated agent's *delete* or *downgrade* of a membership (a kick, under the effective user's authority) leaves no durable trace once the row is gone — that残 residual is real and should be stated next to the insert-time claim.
*Resolution criterion:* Pin the fill rule per writer class (or restrict `provenance` fill to the invocation-gate writers and name the others' value, e.g. a tagged `seed`/`import`/`hostOps` form following 0055's provenance-tag precedent), pin import behavior (preserve, since the columns are immutable and audit-continuity is the point), state that `grantedByGrant` is a tag, and narrow the attribution claim to creation with the delete/downgrade residual named (it lands with 0019 F8, like the rest).

**C5. NON-BLOCKING — deny-at-subscribe is not empty-at-subscribe: the hidden-to-visible story quietly does not apply to `requireRole`-gated live queries.**
*Evidence:* §What exists pins the empty-result capture so "a membership insert … wakes a subscription that previously matched nothing," and validation row (b) tests it — but that mechanism presupposes a *live* subscription with an empty/filtered result. A `requireRole`-gated subscription denied at establishment produces the typed denial → `delete` disposition (0053); there is no subscription left for the later grant to wake, and 0053's terminal retirement means recreation requires an explicit resubscribe. Row (c) covers only the already-authorized direction. So the two natural authoring shapes — helper-as-filter versus `requireRole`-as-gate — have materially different grant-then-see behavior, and the document, which otherwise tells authors exactly which idiom to use, does not say so. Fail-closed, not a correctness bug — but it cuts against "correct by default" for the most obvious authoring shape.
*Resolution criterion:* One stated sentence in §Rule helpers (a denied subscription is terminated, not parked; visibility after a later grant requires app-level resubscribe — prefer the declarative helper as a filter when hidden-to-visible matters), plus a validation clause distinguishing the two shapes so acceptance does not paper over the asymmetry.

## 4. Suggestions

- **Tighten the primitive count.** §Rule helpers decomposes `requireRole` into "two primitive host operations" (the read + the existing deny), while §Companion obligations says "the two `ctx.permissions` membership primitives" and describes the read plus the *already-classified* deny — leaving `get` uncounted even though the registry rule is per-operation. Enumerate the amendment precisely: one new host operation (the membership read, backing both `requireRole`'s internal read and `get`) classified `DatabaseCaptured`; denial rides the existing `TerminalAuthorization` op unchanged.
- **State the `roles`/`pendingRoles` relationship.** The example shows them as separate lists; say explicitly that the closed write vocabulary is their union and the two lists must be disjoint (validated like the other vocabulary rules), so "pending in the vocabulary" (§Open Questions) is unambiguous to implementers.
- **Name the sweeper's owner and window.** §Rule helpers blesses the `expiresAt` sweeper shape; add that the sweeper is app-authored (cron-carried, `system` authority — consistent with 0053's "V1 builds no deadline scheduler") and that the exposure window between `expiresAt` and the sweep is the app's cadence choice, stated the way 0053 states the lease window.
- **Doctor marks the impersonated-roles path.** Since global-role checks under `--as-role` exercise the session-roles path rather than the table read, have the doctor trace label that explicitly ("satisfied via impersonated session roles, not a membership row") — it turns the test-fidelity note into an observable fact.
- **Push the `create-with-host` decision.** The RFC correctly assigns the spelling to 0004 but already concludes hand-written variance is "worth eliminating if it recurs" for a security-sensitive sequence; recommend 0004 decide it in the alignment rather than waiting for recurrence evidence.
- **State the leaning on declaration-scoped narrowing.** The open question asks whether it should land with the semi-join lane; given the per-declaration blast-radius measurement is already in acceptance and the narrowing needs no subject attribution, recording a leaning (land it with the lane) would give 0053's graduation work a concrete first target.

## 5. Open questions

Beyond the RFC's own list (which is good — multi-role at `scope: "app"`, ranking-as-sugar, roster hygiene, the authority-creating scope marker, and declaration-scoped narrowing are the right ones):

- Should the manifest check for `incidental` extend to *generated imperative* helpers' statically known column dependencies (the system knows `requireRole` reads `role`), or is the C1 floor sufficient on its own?
- What exactly does `ctx.permissions.<declaration>.get(resource)` return (the membership row? the role string? null), and is a principal-parameterized form needed for roster/admin flows, or does the app-table read cover those (as the blessed idiom implies)?
- Does the LLP 0013 note allow a protected room to bind its membership predicate to *two* declarations (the rule-level OR shape §Rule helpers blesses for queries), and does the room declaration adopt the stable declaration id?
- Is blast-radius acceptance measurement-only, or is there a numeric bar (e.g., quarantines per churn event per unrelated client) that would gate blessing memberships for the chat/game reference apps before narrowing lands?
- When the declarative two-sided write rules land and 0039's `writeAuthorization: "handler"` deferral lifts, does the blessed imperative idiom remain valid alongside them, or do declared membership tables *require* the declarative rules (fail-closed) once available?

By this document's own standard — structural refusal, not stated intent — that must be pinned before the contract is ready. The fix is one sentence plus one fixture; the remaining concerns are clearly-labeled precision polish.

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a strong document answering a question the corpus has carried since LLP 0011 ("are roles a core primitive or app-level data by default?"), and the answer it gives — **roles are app data in a system-recognized shape** — is, in my judgment, the right one. The two poles it rejects are rejected for the right reasons: pure app-land membership tables demonstrably get invalidation wrong (LLP 0053's entire six-attempt history is evidence for that), and a core role engine with its own semantics is the Zanzibar-drift path the Non-Goals correctly wall off. The middle keeps membership tables ordinary (queryable, joinable, exportable, consistent with 0011's "auth data next to app data"), while the declaration gives the system exactly the metadata needed to derive `permissionBacking`, compile analyzable checks, explain denials, and — the load-bearing point — ride 0053's Accepted invalidation machinery rather than duplicating or contradicting it. The claim that revocation correctness is the reason to build this into Snapback rather than a library is correct and well argued.

The document's distinguishing quality is that its load-bearing claims about the shipped system are **accurate**. I verified each one: `QueryPlan` is single-table (`src/manifest.rs:1143`); `ValueExpr` has exactly one auth variable (`AuthUserId`, `src/value_expr.rs:122`) and no relationship/EXISTS form; `IndexManifest` has no uniqueness flag and `FieldManifest` has `optional` (`src/manifest.rs:244–264`); the mutation IR's `Get` is a point read by id and `Guard` carries an app-supplied code (`src/manifest.rs:1218–1228`); the codegen taxonomy literally classifies "uniqueness/quota/membership guards over non-id keys" as outgrown-IR (`define.mjs:398`); the matcher proves disjointness from captured dependencies without consulting the static read summary (`live_matcher.rs:158–221`); and `CapturedReadKind::Permission` is ignored by **both** the matcher (`live_matcher.rs:190`) and commit-time read-set validation (`storage.rs:3234`), exactly as the captured-dependency contract asserts. The empty-result capture pin is genuinely load-bearing: given the shipped captured-Query short-circuit, a membership lookup that captured nothing would let the matcher prove a membership *insert* disjoint and leave a newly added member permanently invisible — the RFC identifies this precisely and pins the fix for every outcome. The stale-deny respelling is likewise honest against the code: `run_ibex_query_with_reads` early-returns on handler failure and drops `invocation.reads`, so commit-time validation genuinely cannot be the stale-deny mechanism.

Consistency with the governing pins is good: `ctx.auth.roles` stays 0039's `[]`-for-real-sessions; 0053's trusted-producer enumeration is not amended (role denials compose the existing `TerminalAuthorization` deny); the protected-profile default-deny consequence is confronted rather than dodged (the dated amendment is a named companion obligation, without which every helper-gated protected query is refused); retained role-gated reads are correctly removed from v1 because 0008's syncable subset forecloses them; and the v1 global-version cost is owned with per-declaration measurement. The 0055 composition is coherent in both directions (`authorityCreating` ↔ `provenance`, both-conjuncts kind-gating, `isDirectHuman`).

I found one place where the document contradicts itself on the security-critical write path: the **blessed expiry sweeper cannot be spelled under the document's own write contract** as written (Concern 1). It is a bounded, fixable coherence gap — one crisp writer-class table resolves it — but because it sits exactly on the structural-write contract this revision exists to make structural, and because an acceptance row depends on the sweeper, it must be resolved before this document is ready. Everything else I found is polish.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The blessed expiry sweeper is unspellable under the document's own write contract; the writer-class × write-mechanism matrix has a hole.**
Evidence: §Writes pins, unconditionally for handlers, "Raw writes to a declared membership table are refused. `ctx.db` insert/update/delete against the table fails typed… The table is written **only** through the generated, declaration-bound methods below (and the privileged **non-invocation** writers — seed, import, admin, system…)". §Writes also blesses exactly one expiry shape: "an app-authored, **cron-carried (`system` authority) job** that deletes or downgrades the row." Under LLP 0039 §Authority resolution, cron is an ordinary *invocation* under `system` authority (no user, hence no memberships). So the sweeper handler can write through none of the stated paths: raw `ctx.db` is refused; a generated method with `require:` fails (system holds no role); an unguarded call fails ("[base] … an unguarded call (no `require`) fails"); and it is not a *non-invocation* writer. The provenance rules deepen the ambiguity: `system` appears as a **non-invocation** tag, while a cron handler is an **invocation-gate** writer whose fill rule ("from the resolved authority (direct session: actor = effective…)") does not cover a userless system execution. A second exhibit of the same hole: the design's central-enforcement list names "HostPlan mutations" as a writer, and "[base] Vocabulary enforced for every writer (Ibex, **HostPlan**, seed, admin, import): unknown roles fail typed" — yet §Non-Goals says role-managed membership writes are Ibex-mutation work and the HostPlan membership step is follow-on, implying a v1 HostPlan write to a declared table is refused wholesale (raw-write class), in which case the acceptance row asserts the wrong diagnostic class for HostPlan. The sweeper is acceptance-load-bearing ("[live] … the expiry **sweeper** materializes a timeout as an ordinary event-1 delete"), so this cannot be waved off as deferred protocol rigor — it is a design-section contradiction.
Resolution criterion: add one authoritative writer-class table for declared membership tables — Ibex handler (generated methods, `require`-gated), cron/system-authority handler (the exact spelling: e.g., a generated system-authority form or per-declaration sweep method that bypasses `require` only under resolved `system` authority and stamps `system` provenance), HostPlan (refused in v1, typed, with the acceptance row corrected to assert that refusal), seed/import/admin/`--as-membership` (tags), — and make the sweeper example use the pinned spelling. The [base] vocabulary and provenance rows must then name the correct diagnostic/tag per class.

**2. NON-BLOCKING — §Protocol obligations item 2 re-opens a choice the design and acceptance already pin.**
Evidence: §Rule helpers states "a subscription **denied** at establishment by `requireRole` is terminated (trusted denial → `delete`…)" and [live] (d) asserts "**denied-at-subscribe is terminated, not parked** — a later grant does not revive it." Obligation 2 then lists "the lifecycle of a denied live registration (**terminated vs. parked**)" as an implementing-spec question. If the parenthetical is meant only for the stale-deny/linearization case, it does not say so; as written, an implementer could read the terminated/parked choice as open, contradicting the acceptance row.
Resolution criterion: reword obligation 2 to inherit the pin — the deferred question is the linearization mechanics, witness, and what a *linearization-failed* deny does to the registration, not whether an ordinary denied-at-subscribe is terminated.

**3. NON-BLOCKING — the `authorityCreating` trigger, as composed with 0055, catches every bootstrap-bearing resource-creation mutation, which is overbroad and unremarked.**
Evidence: LLP 0055 §Grants pins "a function that **writes a declared LLP 0056 membership table** … is excluded from tier wildcards, requires an explicit `{fn, kind}` scope…". §Writes here makes the resource-creation mutation itself write the membership table (the bootstrap insert). So every "create an event/document/game" mutation becomes non-wildcard-grantable and forces `provenance` on the table — yet bootstrap structurally cannot seat an arbitrary principal (server-derived effective principal only, only when no row exists), so the self-seating escalation the marker exists to gate is unspellable through it. Neither document notices the interaction; the practical cost is consent-fatigue for the most common delegated operation.
Resolution criterion: state explicitly (here or in the 0055 composition obligation) whether bootstrap-only membership writes trigger the marker, with rationale — either an argued carve-out (bootstrap cannot name a principal argument) or an argued acceptance of the friction — and an acceptance row matching the choice.

**4. NON-BLOCKING — the denial-oracle acceptance pins byte-identical payloads but is silent on timing, unlike its sibling.**
Evidence: [base] requires "byte-identical status and body across hidden-resource and non-public-policy probes"; but an absent membership (index miss) and a wrong-role membership (row read plus comparison) plausibly differ in evaluation time, and the same probe distinguishes them. LLP 0055 pins "an equivalent timing envelope" for its non-enumerating pairing failures, so the corpus already treats timing as in-scope for oracle closure.
Resolution criterion: either add a timing-envelope clause to the existence-oracle negative, or name the timing side channel as an accepted residual with rationale.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — the optimistic-parity paragraph's "which the member usually has" is shaky under v1's own restrictions.**
Evidence: §What stays out of the session: "a helper-gated mutation evaluated optimistically needs the relevant membership rows in the local store — which the member usually has." But retained role-gated reads are a Non-Goal, retained reads under sessions await item 8, and the member-list preset is itself membership-gated (outside 0008's single-table subset) — so in v1 there is no blessed path by which those rows are in the local store. The deferral to 0004/0008 companion work is honest; the parenthetical undercuts it.
Resolution criterion: weaken the parenthetical to state that in v1 the check is effectively server-decides unless the app separately syncs the rows, and that the kit parity contract lands with the companion work.

## 4. Suggestions

- The cleanest resolution shape for Concern 1: let the declaration name its expiry column (`expiry: "expiresAt"`) and generate a per-declaration `sweepExpired()` method callable only under resolved `system` authority — vocabulary-checked, `system`-tagged provenance, event-1 by construction, and testable without an app hand-rolling the security-relevant delete. It stays inside 0053's no-deadline-scheduler posture because the app still supplies the cron cadence.

I verified the load-bearing implementation claims against the shipped code, and they all hold: the matcher gives captured dependencies precedence and can prove disjointness without consulting the static read summary (`src/live_matcher.rs:158-221` returns `Some(false)` when no captured dependency intersects); both the matcher and commit-time read-set validation ignore `CapturedReadKind::Permission` (`src/live_matcher.rs:190`, `src/storage.rs:3233-3234`), so pinning captures off that kind and pinning the empty-result query dependency are genuinely load-bearing, not decoration; `QueryPlan` is single-table with one auth variable (`src/manifest.rs:1143`, `AuthUserId` at `:1279`, `src/value_expr.rs:60`); `IndexManifest` has no uniqueness flag and `FieldManifest` has `optional` (`src/manifest.rs:243-264`); HostPlan's `Get` is an id-only point read, `Guard` carries an app-supplied code, and the generic write forms are exactly `Insert/Patch/Delete/DeleteWhere/InsertEach` (`src/manifest.rs:1199-1249`); the codegen taxonomy already classifies membership guards over non-id keys as outgrown-IR (`packages/snapback-codegen/define.mjs:398`); cron entries must be `Action` kind (`src/cron.rs:953-962`); and a failed Ibex handler drops its captured reads via `?`-propagation before commit (`src/runtime.rs:175`, `:230`), which is exactly why the RFC was right to stop claiming commit-time validation catches stale denies. The claimed contracts of LLP 0039 (roles pinned `[]`, `writeAuthorization: "handler"`, system-authority inheritance, `adminBypass` anonymous projection) and LLP 0055 (`authorityCreating` requiring `provenance: "full"`, the bootstrap carve-out mirrored at 0055's effect gate) all check out; the two sibling documents tell one coherent story from both sides.

The plan is buildable and the review loop has visibly earned its keep — the writer-class matrix, the creation-witness bootstrap, the race-proofed `lastHolder`, and the invariant denial payload are each corrections to real holes, and the pinned mechanisms ride machinery that verifiably exists. Two defects keep me from READY, both in §Writes, both design-level under the author's own ruling, and both narrow: the `by`-class semantics for `insert` are internally contradictory (the "self wins whenever it holds" precedence rule and the "`none` … (the open-join case)" gloss produce two conflicting spellings for open join, and no open-join fixture exists to force the question), and the `patch` transition grammar is column-blind while the declared expiry field is an authorization-bearing patchable column — so the document's own example pair ("a host may extend a guest's `expiresAt`"; "a member may edit their own notification preference" via `by: ["self"]`) composes into silent self-renewal of a time-bounded membership. Neither requires rearchitecting anything; both need a pinned answer before this document can claim §Writes is "settled here in one shape."

## 2. Strengths

- **The core positioning (§Summary, §Motivation).** "App data in a system-recognized shape" preserves queryability/joinability/exportability while buying compiled checks, explainable denials, and invalidation wiring. The rejection of session-claim roles is argued from the corpus's own hard-won ground, not taste.
- **The captured-dependency contract (§What exists, what this adds).** Pinning the representation for *every* outcome — success, empty, and row-field coarse fallback — against the verified `Permission`-kind trap and the verified captured-Query short-circuit is exactly the kind of design pin an RFC should make. The empty-result capture is correctly identified as load-bearing (a membership insert would otherwise be proved disjoint forever), and "coarse wake is a re-query cost, never a correctness loss" is the right asymmetry.
- **The writer-class matrix (§Writes).** Placing enforcement at one seam below both buffered Ibex writes and HostPlan execution — with HostPlan generic forms refused typed, a single bounded `system` sweep form, and `adminBypass` failing closed — closes the hole a `ctx.db`-only refusal would leave, and each row is justified from verified shipped behavior.
- **Bootstrap bound to a creation witness (§Writes).** "No rows exist" is correctly diagnosed as unsafe (emptied and imported-orphan resources), and the same-transaction, server-principal-only, single-use witness is a clean mechanism whose escalation-unspellability then does real work in LLP 0055's `authorityCreating` carve-out — verified as the same reasoning at 0055's effect gate.
- **`lastHolder` linearization (§Writes).** The race (two concurrent removals of the final two hosts, each seeing the other) is real under LLP 0004's ratified concurrency model, and the fix — a snapshot-bound range dependency over the `(resource, role)` index validated at commit — rides the same verified machinery as uniqueness and the stale-role check. No new mechanism, no serial-execution-only invariant.
- **The `incidental` floor (§Invalidation).** Closing the one-line false attestation that would have suppressed event 1 for every role *change* in an imperative-only app is a genuine structural improvement over hand-written `permissionBacking`, and it is exactly the corpus posture: refuse the false attestation structurally.
- **The invariant denial payload and removed public read (§Rule helpers).** `{ kind: "role" }` with the caller-state distinction moved to the gated doctor surface correctly identifies the visibility-bypass existence oracle, and the timing residual is named honestly as best-effort rather than asserted as testable.
- **Denied-at-subscribe vs. empty-at-subscribe (§Rule helpers).** A real, non-obvious asymmetry under 0053's terminal retirement, stated with concrete authoring guidance (filter when hidden-to-visible matters; gate when the query should not exist).
- **The blast radius, owned (§Invalidation).** Making membership churn a stream of event-1 coarse quarantines is 0053's deliberate trade, and this RFC neither hides it nor re-litigates it — it adds per-declaration measurement so narrowing proposals get evidence, and correctly identifies declaration-scoped narrowing as the cheap first target.
- **Validation Requirements.** Unusually sharp: hidden-to-visible proven with the coarse event factored out so a broken capture cannot hide behind the global resnapshot; bootstrap negatives against exactly the three unsafe-emptiness shapes; the denial-witness race in both directions; kick by delete *and* downgrade; `lastHolder` across every writer class including the sweep.
- **§Protocol obligations.** The deferred list is the right list for the ratified altitude — predicate AST/lowering/convergence, deny-linearization *mechanics* (with the semantic minimum correctly pinned in-document because Accepted 0053 requires it), ledger schema, witness mechanics, and instrumentation.

## 3. Concerns

1. **MATERIAL — the `by`-class assignment for `insert` is self-contradictory, and the open-join transition has two conflicting spellings.**
   Evidence: §Writes pins the classes as "disjoint and totally ordered," with class 1 `"self"` — "the effective principal **is** the target row's principal. This class wins whenever it holds" — and class 3 `"none"` — "the caller holds no membership in this declaration **(the open-join case)**." For an `insert`, the "target row" is the row being inserted, and `target: self|other` is explicitly "decided by the host comparing the effective principal to the target row's principal" (the example matrix uses `target: "other"` on an insert, so the argument principal *is* consulted for inserts). Under that reading, a non-member inserting their own row (open join) is class `"self"` — so the class-3 gloss is wrong, and open join is spelled `by: ["self"]`. Under the alternative reading suggested by "`insert` binds the resource being inserted into; `setRole` and `remove` re-read the **target row** and bind *its* resource and *its* principal" (insert binds no target principal for classing), open join is class `"none"` as glossed — and `by: ["self"]` inserts are unreachable. The two readings yield different manifest spellings for a transition every motivating app needs (open seats, public channels), each spelling being an unreachable-class manifest diagnostic under the other reading. The failure is fail-closed (join refused at manifest load or at runtime, never an escalation), but the central section that claims to be "settled here in one shape" admits two implementations that disagree. Compounding it: the [base] transition fixture list ("leave, decline, accept invite, invite, promote, kick") contains **no open-join row**, so acceptance would not force the question either.
   Resolution criterion: pin one sentence — whether the `"self"` class is evaluated against the argument principal on `insert` (then correct the class-3 gloss, since `"none"` is then "a non-member acting on another principal's row," not open join) or whether `insert` has no `"self"` class (then state it and keep the gloss) — give the open-join transition's canonical spelling in the example matrix, and add an open-join positive/negative to the [base] transition fixtures.

2. **MATERIAL — `patch` transitions are column-blind, so any `by: ["self"]` patch policy silently authorizes self-renewal of the declared expiry field.**
   Evidence: §Writes pins "`patch()` writes the declaration's **non-axis, non-role** columns (`expiresAt`, `joinedAt`, app columns). It is governed by its own declared `op: "patch"` transitions," and transitions carry only `(op, by, target, from, to)` — no field dimension. The document's own example pair — "a host may extend a guest's `expiresAt`"; "a member may edit their own notification preference" via `by: ["self"]` — cannot coexist safely: the moment an app declares the self-patch for preferences, that same transition authorizes the member to extend their **own** `expiresAt`, i.e. to self-renew a time-bounded membership. The RFC itself establishes that the expiry field is authorization-bearing (§Writes: "a `patch` touching any other column (`expiresAt`, say) **is** event 1, which is why the `incidental` floor exists"; the [live] row even fixtures `expiresAt` advancing the version and suspending a room). So invalidation fires correctly for a write that should have been *refused* — the system faithfully propagates an escalation. This is "a claim the mechanism cannot support": the closed-by-default posture ("any write that matches no declared transition is refused, typed") is undermined for the one non-role column the RFC itself designates as authorization state, and unlike the cross-declaration moderator cliff, this cliff is not named. There is also **no [base] fixture for patch policy at all** (the generic "matches no declared transition" row exists, but the fixture list omits patch entirely, so the collision would never surface in acceptance).
   Resolution criterion: any of — (a) add a field-scoping dimension to `op: "patch"` transitions (e.g. `fields: [...]`, closed, validated against the declaration); (b) carve the declared `expiry.field` out of `patch()` and give it its own transition op (an explicit `renew`, which also makes renewal policy legible in the transition log); or (c) at minimum, name the cliff in §Writes/§Non-Goals the way the moderator cliff is named, recommend the companion-table pattern for member-editable columns on declarations that declare `expiry`, and add a [base] fixture proving whichever behavior is pinned (including the negative: a self-patch policy's effect on the expiry field is the *declared* one).

3. **NON-BLOCKING — sweep semantics when a `lastHolder`-protected row is among the expired set are unpinned, and the idempotency claim does not cover it.**
   Evidence: §Writes pins "protection wins over expiry, so an expiring final host is *refused* with a typed diagnostic," and separately "the effect is idempotent by construction: a `downgradeTo` sweep also clears the expiry field … a `remove` sweep is trivially idempotent." A protected expired row is neither removed nor cleared, so it is re-encountered by every subsequent sweep run forever; and it is unstated whether one protected row fails the whole `sweepExpired()` transaction (wedging expiry processing for every other row of the declaration) or is skipped with a diagnostic while the rest proceed.
   Resolution criterion: one sentence pinning skip-with-diagnostic (per-row) vs. whole-sweep refusal, and extending the idempotency statement to own the perpetual re-encounter of protected rows (or clearing their expiry field with a distinct typed diagnostic).

4. **NON-BLOCKING — membership→resource referential integrity is enforced only on the resource-delete path; activation and import are not stated to validate it.**
   Evidence: §Writes pins the no-orphan rule as "the host refuses the resource delete while memberships remain," and motivates it as correctness ("a rule that gates on the membership alone … would still admit the holder"). But the activation scan validates only "vocabulary, uniqueness, nullability, and column types," and import is a privileged writer — so an imported corpus containing membership rows whose resource row is absent resurrects exactly the "authority outlives its object" state the delete refusal exists to prevent, via a path the refusal cannot see. Similarly, `lastHolder` guards *transitions*, so an imported state in which a resource has members but zero protected-role holders is stranded on arrival with no transition able to repair it except privileged writers.
   Resolution criterion: state whether activation/import validates the resource reference (fail-closed like the vocabulary scan) or name both residuals explicitly as operator-repair territory, and add the imported-dangling-resource case to the roster-hygiene open question.

5. **NON-BLOCKING — the null-effective-principal rule for generated write methods is pinned only implicitly, inside the `adminBypass` row.**
   Evidence: the writer-class matrix argues `adminBypass` fails closed because "it carries no user: no transition can match" — which presupposes a general rule (a null effective principal is assigned *no* `by` class, not `"none"`) that is never stated as such. An `optional-user` function invoking a generated method with a null principal needs the same rule; without it, a declared `by: ["none"], target: "other"` transition is arguably matchable by an anonymous caller. Rule helpers pin fail-closed-when-unauthenticated for reads; writes deserve the same explicit sentence.
   Resolution criterion: pin "a null effective principal matches no authorization class; generated methods refuse unauthenticated invocations typed" in §Writes and add the `optional-user`/null case to the [base] writer-matrix fixture.

6. **NON-BLOCKING — "no application-returning membership read" is enforced only at codegen/compile time, against the corpus's own two-gate posture.**
   Evidence: §Rule helpers pins the membership read as "an internal primitive with no application-returning form," and the [base] row asserts it "does not compile" and that "the generated surface contains no membership-read export" — compile-time evidence only. Accepted 0053 and LLP 0055 both insist the host gate is the sound half and static analysis is best-effort (aliases/computed access are fixtures). In v1 this is not a hole — handlers can already read the table via `ctx.db`, and handler output is the app's named trust domain — but the moment declarative read rules land, a JS-reachable internal op returning the bypassing fact becomes a boundary.
   Resolution criterion: pin that the `requireRole` composition is host-side (the captured read's value never crosses into handler JS), or add the host-gate negative alongside the compile-time one; a sentence in §Protocol obligations item 4 would suffice.

## 4. Suggestions

- **Name the HostPlan-creation cliff the way the moderator cliff is named.** Because every HostPlan write form is refused on membership tables and bootstrap is a membership write, a HostPlan `createEvent` mutation cannot seat its creator — resource-creation-with-bootstrap is Ibex-only in v1. §Non-Goals implies this ("Role-managed membership writes are Ibex-mutation work") but an adopting app authoring in the simple tier will hit it at authoring time; one sentence prevents that.
- **Novel connection worth making explicit:** the `provenance: "full"` transition log records `(op, from, to, principal, resource, commitVersion)` per authorization-relevant write — which is precisely the transactional old/new pair Accepted 0053 says the shipped `_snapback_changes` journal cannot supply for shadow-mode graduation ("inserts/updates record the postimage and deletes record no `row_json`"). For declared membership tables with full provenance, the preimage problem is solved by construction. Stating that the transition log doubles as the shadow-derivation evidence source would materially strengthen the "declaration-scoped narrowing lands here first" leaning and give `provenance: "full"` a second payoff beyond audit.
- **Consider `op: "renew"` as the resolution to Concern 2** rather than a `fields:` dimension: it keeps the transition grammar closed and small, makes renewal an explicit, logged, policy-declared act, and pairs naturally with the sweep (which already owns clearing the field).
- **Pin whether `by` may name a pending role.** The class-2 definition ("the caller's own role") plus the closed vocabulary (`roles` ∪ `pendingRoles`) implies an `"invited"` caller is class `"invited"` and `by: ["invited"]` is declarable. Probably harmless — but one sentence (allowed, or linted as suspicious) closes it.
- **Add LLP 0012 and LLP 0019 to the Related header** — both are load-bearing in §Companion obligations (diagnostic registrations; the F8 audit substrate) but absent from the metadata list.
- **Consider adopting 0053's changelog pattern.** The `Revised:` header is now a ~50-line narrative; the content is valuable but the header is the wrong container, and 0053 demonstrates the corpus's better shape (short header, dated §Changelog).
- The resource-delete refusal's typed diagnostic discloses "memberships remain" to a caller who may be barred from the member list by visibility policy; almost certainly acceptable (no count, no identities), but a half-sentence acknowledging it under the oracle discussion would make the never-echo audit complete.

## 5. Open questions

Beyond the RFC's own list (which is good and honest), the refinement questions I would add:

1. The `insert` class-assignment pin and the canonical open-join spelling (Concern 1) — this is the one that must be answered in-document.
2. Does `patch` policy get field granularity, a dedicated `renew` op, or a named cliff (Concern 2)?
3. Do activation and import validate membership→resource existence and `lastHolder`-satisfiability of the arriving state, or are those named operator-repair residuals (Concern 4)?
4. When a sweep encounters a protected expired row: skip-and-continue or refuse-whole-sweep (Concern 3)?
5. Must the dated 0053 input-profile amendment land *before* the helper surface is authored (the RFC says without it every helper-gated protected query is refused — is that ordering a landing-item gate, and whose)?
6. For the eventual declarative write rules: does the transition matrix become their compile target (the analyzable subset), so graduation is additive rather than parallel? The RFC pins semantics "so the matrix and the eventual rules agree" — stating the intended relationship (matrix as lowering target vs. matrix retired) would guide the 0004 companion design.
7. Should the transition log be pinned as the shadow-mode preimage source for declared memberships (see Suggestions), making the 0053 graduation dependency explicit rather than incidental?

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a strong, unusually honest RFC, and the design decision at its core is right. "Roles are app data in a system-recognized shape" is the correct middle answer to LLP 0011's open question: it keeps memberships queryable, joinable, and exportable while giving the system exactly enough structure to compile role checks into matcher-visible reads, refuse false `incidental` attestations structurally, and wire revocation into LLP 0053's machinery without the app author knowing that machinery exists. The Summary's argument for building this into Snapback rather than leaving it to app code — revocation correctness requires owning the sync layer — is the genuinely load-bearing claim, and the document delivers the mechanism to back it.

- **`lastHolder` linearization pinned (§Writes).** The aggregate-read race is named, and the fix rides the existing snapshot-bound range-dependency/commit-validation machinery rather than inventing a mechanism — consistent with what `validate_captured_read_set` actually does.
- **Creation/deletion witness symmetry (§Writes).** Bootstrap bound to an unforgeable same-transaction creation witness rather than emptiness (the emptied/imported-orphan cases are correctly identified as unsafe), and the teardown deletion witness resolving the `lastHolder`/delete deadlock is elegant and closed.
- **The writer-class matrix (§Writes).** The seam below every writer, HostPlan generic forms refused typed, the `system` sweep as the only system write (no `systemSetRole`), `adminBypass` fail-closed via 0039's anonymous projection — each row justified against verified shipped behavior.

## 3. Concerns

1. **MATERIAL — Referential integrity is unenforced on the live insert path, and the insert-vs-teardown race mints orphaned authority.** Evidence: §Declaring pins the invariant and its enforcement points — "Activation over existing data is fail-closed: … **and membership→resource referential integrity** … **Import validates the same predicate** — the resource-delete refusal (below) keeps live data clean … so orphaned authority cannot enter through a side door." The generated insert is not among them: its derived facts are the caller's class "computed over *that* resource" (a membership-index read) and the target principal — no resource-row existence read is required. The privileged-writer row lists "Vocabulary, uniqueness, nullability, immutability, and `lastHolder`" as still applying to seed/import/admin — referential integrity is absent, so a seed or admin edit can seat a membership on a nonexistent resource outright. Worse, under LLP 0004's ratified concurrency model the sanctioned teardown races an open-join insert: `clearForResource` + resource delete commits first; the concurrent `insert` (by: `"none"`, target: `"self"`) captured only the empty `(caller, resource)` membership range, which the teardown did not change, so commit-time validation passes and an orphan membership lands referencing a deleted resource — the state §Writes itself calls "a correctness matter rather than cosmetics: a rule that gates on the membership alone … would still admit the holder," and which resource-id non-reuse makes permanent. The roster-hygiene open question even hedges against "membership rows … whose **resource** row is deleted," a state the design elsewhere claims impossible. This is a claim the mechanism as pinned cannot support — the same race-defeasibility class the document fixed for `lastHolder`. Resolution criterion: pin that every membership insert (generated methods, seed, admin edits, `--as-membership`; import already validates) validates the referenced resource row's existence in the same transaction and captures it as a snapshot-bound dependency, so insert-vs-resource-delete linearizes at commit exactly like `lastHolder`; add the matching `[base]` rows (insert naming a nonexistent resource refused, typed; concurrent open-join insert vs. teardown yields exactly one winner); and reconcile the roster-hygiene open question to principal-side only.

2. **MATERIAL — The Validation Requirements omit the concurrent `lastHolder` race fixture the design exists to refuse.** Evidence: §Writes pins "Two concurrent `remove()`s of the final *two* hosts would each see the other present in their snapshot, both pass, and the resource would strand … The first removal commits and changes that range; the second fails commit-time read-set validation … Exactly one succeeds," and §Status records this race as "found independently by both families." Yet the `[base]` `lastHolder` row tests only serial refusals ("refuses the demotion **and** removal of the final holder for every writer class … A role revoked between check and commit fails commit-time read-set validation" — the check-vs-commit race for *role checks*, not the holder-set aggregate). The document's own pattern includes concurrency fixtures for the sibling races (concurrent same-pair inserts; concurrent bootstrap attempts; concurrent grant/revoke in the denial-witness row), so the omission is anomalous: a race-defeasible snapshot-read implementation of `lastHolder` would pass every current row. The brief's standard — validation must actually catch the failure modes that matter — makes this a document defect, not deferred mechanism (it is not in §Protocol obligations). Resolution criterion: add a `[base]` fixture asserting concurrent removals/demotions of the final two protected holders yield exactly one commit and one typed commit-time refusal, never a stranded resource.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The bootstrap revival defense rests on an id non-reuse invariant that nothing owns and the shipped system contradicts.**
*Evidence:* §Writes pins "Resource ids are unguessable and never reused, so a deleted resource's id cannot be re-bootstrapped by a later creation." That sentence is the entire defense against resurrection (creation-binding correctly handles the imported-orphan and emptied-resource cases; only non-reuse handles revival). But LLP 0008 §Mutation Replay *by contract* transmits client-captured `generatedIds`, and the shipped mechanism honors them: `PlanIdSource::next()` (`src/storage.rs`) returns `ledger.ids[cursor]` verbatim as the inserted row's `_id` for any mutation invoked with a client-supplied `ctx_ledger` on the ordinary surface, and `validate_ctx_answer_ledger` (`src/manifest.rs:1529`) validates only format (`REPLAY_OPAQUE_ID_PATTERN` — any 1–256-byte token string, not even UUID-shaped), count, timestamp range, and auth fingerprint — never freshness or non-reuse. Direct `_id` writes are refused (`SNAPBACK_SYSTEM_FIELD_WRITE`), but the ledger path is the designed optimistic-parity seam, not a bug awaiting a fix.
*Failure scenario:* app deletes event E (teardown clears the roster; app rows such as `messages.eventId = E` legitimately remain — the RFC governs membership referential integrity, not app-row references). An attacker invokes `createEvent` with a ledger naming E; the host inserts a resource row with `_id = E` (absent→inserted), mints the creation witness, and seats the attacker as `host` of "E". Every rule of the blessed shape `eventMembers.hasRole(row.eventId, "host")` now admits the attacker to the dead resource's residual rows. The RFC takes exactly this care for *declaration* ids ("once an id has been durably referenced… re-binding it… is a manifest error") while assuming it for free for *resource* ids.
*Resolution criterion:* any one of — (a) pin that inserts into tables referenced as a membership `resource` (or, narrower, witness-minting inserts) refuse client-ledgered/caller-influenced ids — server-fresh id only — with a validation row (a replayed creation naming a previously-used id never mints the witness); (b) pin a durable resource-id non-reuse check (tombstone) for tables bound by a membership declaration; or (c) rewrite the sentence as an explicit precondition with a named owner (a dated LLP 0004/0008 id-contract obligation in §Companion obligations) and a validation row, so the invariant is enforced or honestly residual rather than asserted.

**2. NON-BLOCKING — Resource-side enforcement and privileged-writer teardown are under-specified.**
*Evidence:* §Writes pins "the host refuses the resource delete while memberships remain, typed," but the writer-class matrix covers only *membership-table* writes, and the corresponding validation row ("deleting a resource row while memberships remain is refused typed") names no writer classes — unlike the insert-side row, which says "for every writer." Meanwhile `lastHolder` explicitly binds "seed, import, and admin writers," and the deletion witness exists only inside "the deleting transaction" of a mutation. Consequence: a privileged non-invocation writer (admin data edit, host-ops) can neither delete the resource (memberships remain) nor remove the final protected holder (`lastHolder`), and has no witness path — so a protected resource is un-teardown-able except through an app-authored mutation. That may be the intended fail-closed posture, but it is an operational cliff the document currently leaves to inference.
*Resolution criterion:* one paragraph and one validation clause: the resource-delete refusal enumerated per writer class (including HostPlan `Delete`/`DeleteWhere` on the resource table), and an explicit statement of the operator-plane teardown answer (app mutation only, or a named future host-ops path under the roster-hygiene open question).

**3. NON-BLOCKING — The `by`-class algebra is silent on pending roles.**
*Evidence:* §Writes defines class 2 as "a declared role… the caller's own role over the target row's resource," and the vocabulary union includes `pendingRoles`. Unstated: whether a caller holding only a *pending* role classifies as that role (and may therefore be named in `by`) or as `"none"`; and whether `by` may legally name a `pendingRole` at all. The difference is authorization-relevant — e.g., whether an `invited` holder can be granted authority over *other* rows via `by: ["invited"]`, and whether an invited caller matches an open-join `by: ["none"]` transition (moot for insert via uniqueness, but not for future ops).
*Resolution criterion:* one or two sentences pinning the classification of pending-role holders and the legality of pending roles in `by` (with a manifest diagnostic if refused).

**4. NON-BLOCKING — The `incidental` floor and field-bound writes do not name the declared `expiry.field` or the generated provenance columns.**
*Evidence:* §Invalidation's floor covers `principal`, `resource`, `role`, and provenance columns. An app could attest its own declared `expiry.field` (`expiresAt`) as `incidental`, suppressing event 1 for expiry extensions/shortenings. I believe this is *actually safe* in v1 — no rule may read the field (clock inputs refused), and the authorization effect materializes only through the sweep's remove, which always fires — but the document neither adds it to the floor nor argues its safety, while its own validation row treats `expiresAt` as the canonical "auxiliary authorization column." Similarly, §Writes' `patch`/`insert` `fields` lists are never explicitly barred from naming provenance columns (immutability implies refusal, but the manifest-load check is unstated).
*Resolution criterion:* either add the declared expiry field to the floor, or state the one-sentence argument for why attesting it is safe; and state that `fields` naming an axis or provenance column is a manifest error.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — Provenance bookkeeping is internally inconsistent.**
*Evidence:* the **Revised** header says "refined through ten dual-family review rounds," while §Status says "Refined through **eight dual-family review rounds**… all sixteen reviews are recorded" and "The current revision incorporates the round-8 findings." In a corpus that treats review provenance as load-bearing (LLP 0005), the round count, review count, and which-findings-are-incorporated statement should agree.
*Resolution criterion:* reconcile header and §Status to the true round/review counts and the actual last-incorporated round.

3. **NON-BLOCKING — the delete-side emptiness check is the third race-defeasible aggregate, and the only one whose capture is not explicitly pinned.** Evidence: §Writes pins the capture explicitly for `lastHolder` ("the holder-set read contributes a snapshot-bound range dependency") and for insert-side referential integrity ("reads the referenced resource row … and captures it"), each time *because* the natural reading was race-defeasible. 4. **NON-BLOCKING — the role-string grammar does not reserve the qualifier separator the document itself proposes.** Evidence: §Declaring a membership table reserves `"self"` and `"none"` and rejects control characters, but a role literally named `"appRoles:admin"` is declarable today, while §Non-Goals names `by: ["appRoles:admin"]` as the natural future spelling for cross-declaration authority. Resolution: reserve `:` (or whatever qualifier separator is chosen) in role identifiers now, in the same manifest-error clause as `"self"`/`"none"`.

5. **NON-BLOCKING — a declared `expiry` with no reachable sweep is silently inert.** Evidence: §Writes states the blessed shape (an app-declared cron action calling `ctx.runMutation` into the generated sweep) and owns the cadence gap "the way 0053 states its lease window" — but cadence-is-the-app's-choice does not cover *absence*: an app that declares `expiry: { field, onExpiry: "remove" }` and ships no cron entry gets memberships that never expire, which is precisely the silent authoring failure this corpus lints elsewhere (the manifest already validates cron kind, `SNAPBACK_CRON_ACTION_KIND_MISMATCH`, `src/cron.rs:954`). Resolution: a manifest or doctor diagnostic when a declaration opts into `expiry` and no cron entry reaches its generated sweep, or an explicit sentence stating that absence is a supported configuration and why.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The transition grammar's omitted-`from`/`to` semantics are unpinned, and the fail-open reading is available.**
Evidence: §Writes defines the matrix as "(`op` × caller class × self/other × `from`→`to`)" and describes `from`/`to` only as "the old role (from the captured target row) and the new role (from the argument)". Every example transition spells `to` explicitly on `insert`/`setRole`, but nothing in the document states whether `to` is *required* there, what an omitted `from` means on `setRole`/`remove` (any current role?), whether `patch` may carry `from` to scope which rows are patchable, or whether meaningless combinations (`to` on `remove`, `from` on `insert`) are manifest errors. M1 models all four as optional (`MembershipTransition`, src/manifest.rs:550-556) and shape-validates only role/field membership (storage.rs:3387-3466) — no per-op admissibility or requiredness diagnostics exist, and the `[base]` validation rows never test omission. Under an "omission = any" M2 reading, `{ op: "insert", by: ["none"], target: "self" }` (an author who forgot `to`) becomes "anyone may self-seat at any declared role, including `host`" — a security-policy authoring trap in the exact shape this document elsewhere refuses ("a blessed idiom is not enforcement"; the document pins `fields` omission fail-closed for `insert`/`patch` but is silent on `from`/`to`, an asymmetry that invites the permissive reading). This is design intent, not deferred protocol mechanics: it changes what a declared policy *means*.
Resolution criterion: the document pins per-op admissible and required transition fields and the omission semantics, fail-closed (e.g. `to` required on `insert`/`setRole`; `from` optional-meaning-any stated explicitly, or required; `to` on `remove` and `from`/`to` on `patch` either given meaning or made manifest errors), plus one `[base]` validation row covering the omitted-`to` insert.

**2. MINOR — The mandatory resource-row capture on every insert creates a false-conflict cost the document does not own.**
Evidence: §Writes pins "the host reads the referenced resource row in the same transaction and captures it as a snapshot-bound dependency". The only shipped presence-checking capture kinds are `Row` (version-checked: any update invalidates, storage.rs:4747-4772) and `MissingRow` (absence-checked). A `Row`-kind capture means every membership insert conflicts with any concurrent *update* to the resource row — not just its deletion. Join-heavy moments are exactly when hosts also edit the resource (lobby settings, event details), so open-join storms will take spurious commit-time conflicts proportional to edit rate, and I found no automatic mutation-retry machinery (retries in src/local.rs are explicit/manual). Correctness is unaffected — this is liveness/throughput — but a document this diligent about owning costs (blast radius, expiry/lastHolder) should own this one too, especially since the capture is mandated, not app-chosen. Notably, `MissingRow` validation already demonstrates a current-state (non-version) check (storage.rs:4774-4797), so an existence-only "row present" dependency — invalidating on delete, not on update — is a cheap, precise future refinement that preserves the insert-vs-teardown linearization.
Resolution criterion: a sentence in §Writes owning the update-vs-insert false-conflict cost (and the client retry expectation under LLP 0004), and either pinning that the resource capture is semantically existence-only (finer kind permitted when it exists) or naming the existence-scoped dependency as follow-on.

**3. MINOR — The global-roles first-holder story is missing: `scope: "app"` has no bootstrap path through the matrix, and the document never says so.**
Evidence: §Global roles and §Writes together imply it — the app singleton "has no creation event to anchor establishment on" (stated for `lastHolder`), so the creation witness can never mint for an app-scoped declaration, and any `by: ["none"]` self-seat transition would let *every* authenticated principal become `admin`, not just the first. The only viable seating paths for the first global admin are the privileged non-invocation writers (seed / admin edits / `--as-membership`), but no sentence states this, and `bootstrapRole` on a `scope: "app"` declaration — dead policy by construction — is not named a manifest diagnostic, though the document's own posture is "Declaring a transition whose `by` class cannot be reached is a manifest diagnostic" (§Writes).
Resolution criterion: §Global roles states the first-holder seating path explicitly (privileged writers; no witness exists for the singleton), and `bootstrapRole` on `scope: "app"` is pinned as a manifest diagnostic (or given a meaning).

**4. MINOR — The provenance fill rule and the import-preservation rule read as contradictory.**
Evidence: §Agents (lines 938-947): "Fill rules per writer class: … the tags `seed`, `import`, or `hostOps:<verb>` for the privileged non-invocation writers" is immediately followed by "**Import preserves** exported provenance values". Which rows get the `import` tag and which keep exported values is left to inference (presumably: preserve when the export carries values, tag when creating rows without provenance data), and whether imported rows produce `provenance: "full"` transition-log entries is unstated. Provenance is load-bearing for LLP 0055's `authorityCreating` audit story, so the ambiguity is worth a sentence.
Resolution criterion: one clarifying sentence distinguishing import-of-exported-provenance (preserve) from import-created rows (tag), and pinning whether import writes transition-log rows.

**5. MINOR — Holder eligibility is defined solely via LLP 0055 `deactivated_at`, so any future human-principal ending state silently reopens the stranding gap.**
Evidence: §Writes: "a holder whose principal is not permanently deactivated (LLP 0055 `deactivated_at`)". LLP 0055 owns *agent* lifecycle; the auth specs (0057-0060) are growing the human-account surface, and a later human account-closure state would not join this predicate — recreating, for humans, exactly the two-step schedule the eligible-holder refinement closed for agents. The roster-hygiene open question gestures nearby but is about sweeping rows, not about the eligibility predicate's extensibility.
Resolution criterion: eligibility is defined extensibly ("any corpus-defined permanently-ended principal state joins the ineligibility predicate; today that is exactly LLP 0055 `deactivated_at`"), or the open question explicitly names future human account closure as joining it.

**6. EDITORIAL — Internal bookkeeping inconsistencies in the header and §Status.**
Evidence: the header (line 8) says "refined through ten dual-family review rounds"; §Status says "thirteen dual-family review rounds … all twenty-two reviews" (thirteen rounds with both families each round would be twenty-six), and "the current revision incorporates the round-12 findings" alongside thirteen claimed rounds. Also `Revised: 2026-07-13` while the body records 2026-07-14 events (M1 via ENG-24907; the 0053 amendment via ENG-24906). For a document whose subject is precision about counts and witnesses, the metadata should agree with itself.
Resolution criterion: one consistent round/review count in header and §Status, and a `Revised` date no older than the newest dated statement in the body.

**7. EDITORIAL — "What exists, what this adds" is stale post-M1.**
Evidence: §What exists says "As shipped today: … `TableManifest` carries no membership metadata and `IndexManifest` no uniqueness flag", which commit b11a6fa (2026-07-14) made false; the correction arrives only via the Implementation-status paragraph below. Two clauses of the baseline should be re-dated ("as shipped before this RFC's implementation began") or moved to the landed column. Similarly, "confusable near-duplicates are linted" (§Declaring) understates the implementation, which refuses them as a typed error (`SNAPBACK_MEMBERSHIP_CONFUSABLE_ROLES`) — the safe direction, but this corpus uses "lint" vs. "typed error" deliberately.
Resolution criterion: baseline clauses re-dated and "linted" aligned with the enforced severity.

## 4. Suggestions

- **Pin the per-op transition grammar as a table** (op × {by, target, from, to, fields} × required/optional/forbidden). It resolves Concern 1 in the document's own idiom and gives M2 a checklist instead of a judgment call.
- **Name an existence-only captured-read kind** (the symmetric twin of `MissingRow`'s current-state check) as the intended future representation of the insert's resource dependency and possibly the delete's emptiness read — Concern 2's cost then has a designed exit.
- **Add a policy-reachability lint over the matrix.** The matrix is finite, so "which roles are reachable at all" is decidable: a role with no inbound transition, no `bootstrapRole`, and no privileged-writer intent is dead vocabulary, and `bootstrapRole` on `scope: "app"` is one instance of the same class. The document already commits to the narrower `by`-class-unreachable diagnostic; generalizing it is cheap and matches the corpus's authoring-failure posture (`SNAPBACK_CRON_ACTION_KIND_MISMATCH` precedent, which §Writes itself cites).
- **Restrict `lastHolder.protect` and `bootstrapRole` to non-pending roles.** A protected or bootstrap-seated *pending* role is almost certainly an authoring error (the creation witness would seat the creator as invited-but-not-a-member; `memberOf` would exclude the one guaranteed holder). M1 currently validates only vocabulary membership.
- **State the retry guidance for the two conflict shapes.** The `lastHolder` race loser and the insert-vs-resource-edit loser both surface as commit-time refusals, but one is terminal-after-retry (the re-run hits the real `lastHolder` refusal) and the other is purely transient. A sentence telling kit authors "retry conflicts; the policy refusal is what survives retry" turns the pinned linearization into client behavior.

## 5. Open questions

- What do omitted `from`/`to` mean, per op? (Concern 1 — should be answered in-document, not left to M2.)
- Should a blessed *product* path exist for seating the first app-global admin (the self-hosted "first signup becomes owner" pattern), or is privileged seeding the permanent answer? Worth adding to §Open Questions alongside multi-role.
- Does LLP 0004's client contract give membership joins acceptable liveness on hot resources without an existence-only capture kind, and should that kind land with M2 rather than later?
- When the auth specs grow human account closure, does that state join `lastHolder` eligibility automatically (Concern 5), and who owns asserting it from the other side, as LLP 0055 does for agents?
- Under `provenance: "full"`, do privileged non-invocation writes (seed/import/hostOps) append transition-log rows, or only invocation-gated and sweep writes? (The log's value as the `authorityCreating` audit substrate argues for "all writers".)

## 6. Readiness verdict

The proposal is a good idea, the plan is sound, the costs are owned, and the code-facing claims are accurate — this is one of the most verification-friendly RFCs in the corpus, and everything decisive I checked held up. But the central new contract — the declared transition matrix — has one unpinned dimension (omitted-`from`/`to` semantics, Concern 1) whose permissive reading is a self-seat escalation trap, and by this document's own "closed by default, a blessed idiom is not enforcement" standard that gap is a MATERIAL decision-quality concern, not polish. It is a one-paragraph fix plus one validation row. Concerns 2-5 are non-blocking; 6-7 are hygiene.

## Verbatim review

1. **Overall assessment**

LLP 0128 is a strong, honest Research document. Its fact base is in unusually good shape: I verified the target hash, read the document end to end, checked every load-bearing [confirmed] claim against the governing corpus (LLP 0004, 0007, 0012, 0046, 0080, 0103, 0104, 0115, 0125, 0126, 0127), and fetched all ten cited first-party external pages (SQLite whentouse, both PostgreSQL logical-replication pages, both FoundationDB pages, both CockroachDB pages, Cloudflare DO limits, Workers storage options, D1 read replication). The central claims — SQLite one-writer-per-file, 0007's per-app gap-free stream and DK-2 allocator mechanics, 0046's non-goal of horizontal write scaling, 0103's single environment coordinator, 0104's write-rate ceiling in the commercial contract, 0012's opaque `(stream identity, version)` cursor, logical decoding's re-delivery after slot rollback, DO single-threaded soft-limit scaling, and D1 bookmark-based read replication — all check out against their cited sources, several nearly verbatim. Provenance tagging is generally disciplined (the split tag at line 241, "[confirmed — LLP 0127; placement use is inferred]", is exemplary), the leaning is labeled as hypothesis, and the evidence program genuinely discriminates between families.

The document falls short of the bar in one coverage respect and a small number of material wording/provenance defects: the candidate-family list silently omits the multi-master/merge-replication family even though the document's own scope decomposition names geographic write latency as a first-class scale axis that no included family answers — and the corpus's existing rejection of that family (0007 DK-2) goes unrecorded; one [inferred] influence line reads as countermanding 0007's decided Postgres commit-fencing default; the motivating challenge is attributed to an unintroduced, unsourced "Philippe"; and the comparative frame drops four of the document's own seven decomposed scale dimensions. All fixes are local; none changes the document's architecture.

VERDICT: NOT READY

2. **Strengths**

- **Faithful sourcing under a demanding claim set.** §Findings 1–4: every external [confirmed] claim I checked matched its cited page, including subtle ones — the SQLite short-transaction concession ("SQLite will handle more write concurrency than many people suspect"), logical-decoding slot rollback ("in the case of a crash the slot might return to an earlier LSN, which will then cause recent changes to be sent again"), logical replication applying "in correct transactional order", CockroachDB's Parallel Commits over replicated write intents with SERIALIZABLE retries, and the DO limits page's "Each individual Object is inherently single-threaded" with the 1,000 rps soft limit.
- **The three-way decomposition (§Summary, Finding 1).** Separating application model, execution language, and physical authority topology is the correct analytical move, and Finding 1's "what is the write authority and transactional domain?" question correctly reframes the SQL-doesn't-scale challenge without strawmanning it.
- **Corpus fidelity on the hard internal claims.** Finding 2's rendering of 0007 DK-2 (IMMEDIATE reservation, commit fence, DO single-threaded allocation, and the named-but-undesigned intra-app sharding fork, 0007:256–265) and Finding 6's rendering of 0012 (0012:926–929, "must not compare versions across streams... or assume a stream survives intra-app sharding") are accurate to the letter.
- **No smuggled substrate decision.** The lean is stated as a hypothesis (§Summary blockquote), the comparative-frame ratings are explicitly "hypotheses for directing experiments, not benchmark results" (lines 385–386), Phase 2's default spike pair is qualified "absent contrary measurements" (line 472), and Phase 3 routes all decisions to a later RFC with the excellent "'Unlimited,' 'web scale,' and 'horizontally scalable' are not acceptance criteria" rule (lines 497–499).
- **Attributable measurement design.** Phase 1's requirement to attribute tail latency to reservation, execution, index work, version allocation, commit durability, publication, or retry (lines 465–468) correctly absorbs and extends LLP 0125's open question 5 (0125:630–633).
- **Non-strawmanned family entries.** Family F honestly includes "The design risks rebuilding a transactional database around the log" (lines 360–361); Family D honestly includes "distributed transactions do not make a single hot key scalable" (lines 319–320); Family E honestly enumerates the layer-author burden.

3. **Concerns**

**C1. BLOCKING-FOR-BAR — The family list omits multi-master/merge replication, leaving the document's own geographic-write axis unanswered and an existing corpus rejection unrecorded.** §Question and scope decomposes "geographic latency: distance to the write authority and readers" as a first-class scale dimension (line 74), and Family A concedes "geographic writes still travel to one authority" (lines 261–262) — a cost that in fact holds for every included family (B's single primary, C's per-cell authority, D's per-range leaseholder, E's per-key-range authority, F's per-partition log authority, G by construction). The one architecture family that directly attacks distant-write latency — multi-master / leaderless / CRDT-merge acceptance at multiple authorities — appears nowhere in §Candidate architecture families (lines 248–381), and the document never says why. The omission is not excused by contract incompatibility, because the document's own inclusion standard admits premise-failing families: Family F is included and then disqualified in place ("Current Snapback semantics do not yet establish that premise," lines 364–365). Worse, the corpus has already considered and rejected this family with a named revisit trigger — 0007-storage-portability.spec.md:266–273 ("**Rejected: HLC-style ordering**... **Revisit trigger:** a design that requires multi-master writes within one app stream (nothing planned does)") — and 0127-parity-and-local-first-synthesis.rfc.md:513 records "CRDT core and raw replica rejected" for the local-first program. A reader of this Research alone cannot see that the space was closed deliberately rather than overlooked. **Resolution:** add a short "Considered and excluded: multi-master/merge acceptance" entry (or an H-family stub) that (a) names the family and the geographic-write axis it uniquely targets, (b) records its incompatibility with 0007's gap-free "changes since N" visibility contract, (c) cites 0007 DK-2's rejection and revisit trigger and 0127's CRDT-core rejection, and (d) states explicitly that every included family leaves distant writes traveling to one authority per cell/range, so geographic write latency is answered only by placement (C/D locality) — not by write multi-homing — unless the contract changes.

**C2. MATERIAL — Finding 4's influence line reads as countermanding 0007 DK-2's decided Postgres default.** Lines 197–200: "implement and measure Postgres... and do not add a framework-global counter if the database commit log can eventually provide the required ordering." But the decided DK-2 graduation default *is* a framework-allocated counter — commit fencing via "per-app advisory lock or a single-row `UPDATE … RETURNING` version row" (0007:243–247), and 0046:74–76 states "The write-path mechanism is already decided — 0007 DK-2 pins commit fencing as the Postgres graduation default." The logical-decoding feed is 0007's *recorded escape hatch*, explicitly "deliberately not designed here... Measure first (M1-class evidence), then design" (0007:251–255). Read literally, the influence line advises against building the very 0046 target that this document's own Phase 1 measures (line 454). Family B's phrasing already carries the correct qualifier — "rather than funneling all commits through a framework counter *forever*" (lines 271–272) — but the influence line drops it. **Resolution:** reword lines 197–200 to match Family B and DK-2, e.g. "implement and measure Postgres with DK-2's commit fence as decided; do not harden the framework counter into a *permanent* contract if the commit log can eventually provide the required ordering (0007's recorded, undesigned escape hatch)."

**C3. MATERIAL — the motivating challenge is unsourced and "Philippe" is never introduced.** The document opens on the challenge «why build Snapback around SQL if SQL does not scale?» (lines 13–14) with no attribution, and line 146 then refers to "Philippe's concern" — the only occurrence of the name; he is never identified, and `/usr/bin/grep -rn -i philippe` across `llp/`, `issues/`, and `diaries/` finds no other occurrence in the corpus. The challenge is load-bearing (it frames the entire document and Finding 2's central influence claim), yet a reader — or this reviewer — cannot verify who raised it, when, in what words, or in what channel. Under the document's own Method section this is untagged provenance. **Resolution:** at the first quotation in §Summary, name the source (who Philippe is, date, and channel — feedback thread, review, conversation), record the concern verbatim or as a clearly marked paraphrase with an `[observed — <source>, <date>]` tag, and make line 146's reference resolve to that introduction.

**C4. MATERIAL — the comparative frame drops four of the document's own seven scale dimensions.** §Question and scope decomposes scale into seven axes (lines 71–78): write throughput, read throughput, data size, geographic latency, availability, tenant isolation, operability/cost. The frame's table (lines 388–396) rates the families on independent-key writes, hot-key writes, cross-partition atomicity, local parity, and Snapback-owned machinery — i.e., only the write axes plus two engineering axes. Read throughput, data size, geographic latency, and availability — axes on which the families genuinely differ (D's multi-region ranges and survivability vs. A's single cell; B/G's mature read replicas; E/D's storage horizontality) — have no row or column, so the frame cannot direct experiments along four of the dimensions the document itself says matter. A careful reader trips on the mismatch between the decomposition and the instrument. **Resolution:** either extend the table (or add a companion table) with data-size ceiling, geographic write/read latency, and availability/failover columns rated in the same hypothesis register, or add a sentence stating explicitly that the frame deliberately tables only the axes that discriminate the families' *authority topology*, with the remaining axes measured in Phase 1 rather than rated a priori — and say why tenant isolation needs no row (it doesn't discriminate: all families keep per-app isolation).

**C5. DETAIL — Status header/footer contradiction.** Line 4 says "**Status:** Review" while lines 592–594 say "## Status / Draft Research." One document, two statuses. **Resolution:** make the §Status body text agree with the header ("Review" per the capsule's post-Review-status content), or vice versa.

**C6. **C7. DETAIL — "CV-8's proposed sequence primitive" misattributes the proposal.** Lines 398–399 (and the checkpoint item at line 517) attribute the sequence primitive to CV-8. LLP 0115's CV-8 text proposes no primitive — it requires the sequence-counter limitation "either lifted by design or documented as refusals" (0115:481–483) — and 0127 is explicit that "the sequence-primitive-first ordering is *not* in this verified list — it is 0120's labeled lean (finding 4) and this RFC's proposal, riding open question 3" (0127:610–612; also 0127:719–721). **Resolution:** attribute it as "the sequence primitive proposed by LLP 0120 (option 5) and LLP 0127 Move 6, riding 0115's CV-8 tranche."

**C8.

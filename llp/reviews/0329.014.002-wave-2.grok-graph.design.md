### 1. Product contract

Joinery is a professional network for people who ship work with people they do not all share an employer with. A **member** has a profile, sends and accepts **connections**, belongs to **companies** for dated spells of employment, joins **projects**, and files **issues** that can **block** other issues, including across projects.

**Who uses it**

- **Members** — individuals. They own a handle, a profile, an adjacency list, and the issues they create or are assigned.
- **Project leads** — members with `owner` or `lead` on a project. They invite, assign, set issue network-disclosure, and import from GitHub.
- **Company admins** — members who claimed a company page. They publish a roster of members who opted in; they do not override the people graph.
- **The system principal** — expires invites, rebuilds hop-3 materializations, repairs DAG counters.

There is no paid “see past 3 hops” product. There is no InMail. Money is out of scope on purpose: the scarce resource is **who may learn that someone exists, that two people are connected, that an issue is blocked, and by whom**.

**Hop bounds (load-bearing, not settings)**

| Distance | What it unlocks |
|---|---|
| 0 (self) | Everything of yours |
| 1 (accepted connection) | Full profile, email if `shareEmailWithConnections`, mutuals, 1st-degree badge |
| 2 (common accepted neighbor) | Full profile **except** email; mutuals; `network` issues assigned to them, **title + status + blocker count**, not body |
| 3 (a path of length 3) | **Card only**: display name, headline, current company name, degree badge `3rd`. No work history, no mutuals list, no network-issue bodies or titles |
| ∞ (no path ≤ 3, or a block in either direction) | Same user-visible outcome as an unused handle: HTTP 200, body `{ visibility: "out_of_reach" }`, no identifying fields |

Distance is computed on **accepted** edges only. A pending invite does not shorten distance. A former connection does not shorten distance. A shared current company does not shorten distance. A shared project does **not** shorten distance for **profiles**; it does grant **issue** read inside that project (work wins inside a shared project; the people graph wins everywhere else).

**The 16 promises**

1. You can see your own profile and your own issues, including after you leave a project (those issues become a historical stub: title, status, your assignment interval).
2. A profile **card** is visible iff hop-distance ≤ 3, or the subject set `discovery = "world"`, or you share a current project (card only, unless a hop bound independently grants more).
3. A **full profile** (work-history intervals, recommendations, connection count as an integer) is visible iff hop-distance ≤ 2.
4. We display degree as `1st`, `2nd`, `3rd`, or `out of reach`. We never display `4th` or a numeric distance greater than 3.
5. **People you may know** are members at distance **exactly 2** whom you have not invited in the last 90 days, ranked by mutual-connection count then shared-company overlap, capped at 50. Clicking a PYMK card re-checks distance at view time.
6. **Mutual connections** of you and a subject you can already see are the intersection of the two accepted 1-hop lists. The subject may set `showMutuals = false`; you then see the string `Mutuals hidden`, never `0`.
7. Connection requests are consented: A cannot become 1st degree with B unless B accepts. Either party can end an accepted connection. Ending is immediate for 1st and 2nd degree.
8. **Block is absolute for profiles and silent for the blocked party.** If A blocks B, hop-distance(A,B) = hop-distance(B,A) = ∞ for profile authorization even if other paths exist. B does not receive a notification. Block does not remove either person from a shared project; the blocked member renders as `Hidden member` on issues.
9. After you **end a connection**, 1st- and 2nd-degree access is revoked in **that same action**. 3rd-degree cards that only existed because of that bridge become `out of reach` within **60 seconds**. We do not promise a zero-leak 3rd-degree window shorter than that; we do promise zero extra leak on full profiles (2-hop) the moment the edge is gone.
10. An issue is startable (`in_progress` is legal) only when every **transitive** blocker is `done` or `wontfix`. Direct and inherited blockers count. Diamonds count an ancestor **once**.
11. Adding a `blocked-by` edge that would create a cycle is refused with `CYCLE`, and the refusal includes one concrete directed path (at most 16 ids). The graph of issues is a DAG at every committed state.
12. Cross-project blockers are allowed. If you can read issue A but not its blocker B, you still see that A is blocked, you see a **structural** open-blocker count, and you see a stub `{ visible: false }` with no title, no assignee, no status, no project name.
13. Assigning a member to an issue whose `visibility = "network"` publishes that issue’s **title and status** into the assignee’s 2-hop neighborhood. The assignee is notified. A lead can set `visibility = "project"` and the neighborhood loses the issue at the next read (immediate).
14. **Offline:** already-opened cards, full profiles, and issues remain readable from the device cache. You cannot **newly** prove a 2-hop or 3-hop path while offline, so you cannot open a profile you have never opened. Writes queued offline (connection accept, new blocker, close, assign) replay on reconnect and may be refused; the UI then reverts.
15. **Multi-device:** the server’s committed graph is the source of truth. Devices carry `neighborhoodGeneration` per member they cache. A mismatch is a cache miss, not a leak: the device must refetch before showing a 3rd-degree card. 1st- and 2nd-degree checks are re-evaluated from accepted edges, not from the cache of cards.
16. **Concurrency:** two members may invite, accept, end, block, assign, close, and add blockers at the same time. The visible committed state is always a simple undirected people graph plus a DAG of issues. Lost invites and lost edges surface as `INVITE_NOT_FOUND`, `NOT_CONNECTED`, or `CYCLE`, never as a half-accepted edge or a cyclic blocker.

**Concurrency, offline, multi-device (what users actually feel)**

- Optimistic: pending invite chip, 1st-degree badge on accept, a new blocker chip, a closed issue. Not optimistic: 3rd-degree cards, PYMK rank, startability when any blocker is hidden, cycle-freeness.
- Two devices, two accepts of different people: both commit. Two devices, A→B and B→A blockers: the second replay fails `CYCLE`. Two devices, end-connection on one and a comment-on-their-issue on the other: the comment is refused on replay if it required 2-hop network visibility that no longer exists; a **project-member** comment still commits.
- Clock: invite expiry is a durable state change, not a read-time comparison that quietly changes authorization. Until the expiry job runs, a pending invite still accepts; after it runs, it does not. The job’s SLO is 1 hour.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **Distance is a fact about accepted edges right now.** hop-distance(V, S) is 0, 1, 2, 3, or ∞ as defined in §1, on the committed accepted-edge set, after applying blocks. A stored integer named `degree` is not this fact. *[secrecy] [time] [other people's data]*
2. **A former connection is not a connection.** The interval `[acceptedAt, endedAt)` may be shown to the two parties as “you were connected.” It does not authorize a card, a full profile, or a network issue. *[secrecy] [time]*
3. **Pending is not a hop.** An open invite does not create distance 1, does not appear in mutuals, and does not seed PYMK. *[secrecy]*
4. **Block dominates paths for profiles.** A block in either direction forces profile distance ∞, even when a length-1 path would otherwise exist (the ended connection) or when many length-2 paths exist. *[secrecy] [other people's data]*
5. **Block does not eject work.** Shared-project issue read continues; the blocked member’s profile is not reachable from it. *[other people's data]*
6. **Same string for missing and out-of-reach handles.** `/in/{handle}` never confirms existence to a viewer who is not already allowed a card. *[secrecy]*
7. **Search does not cover the unbound graph.** Typeahead and search return only: the viewer, their distance ≤ 3 set, `discovery = "world"` cards, and members of projects the viewer currently belongs to. A hit count is the count of **that** set, never of all members whose name matches. *[secrecy] [other people's data]*
8. **`connectionCount` is about the person, not about the viewer.** The integer on a full profile is the number of accepted edges of the subject. Counting only the subject’s edges the viewer can see is a lie. *[secrecy] [other people's data]*
9. **Mutual count is about the pair.** `|N1(V) ∩ N1(S)|` is exact for any V who is allowed to ask (distance ≤ 2, or distance 1). If `showMutuals` is false, we disclose neither the list nor the number. *[secrecy] [other people's data]*
10. **PYMK membership is distance exactly 2.** A 1st-degree, a 3rd-degree, a blocked member, a pending invitee, and yourself never appear. Ranking may lag 60 seconds; **membership in the list after an edge ends** must not survive more than 60 seconds. *[secrecy] [time]*
11. **The issue graph is a DAG in every committed state.** There is no directed path from an issue to itself along `blocked-by`. *[other people's data]*
12. **Startability is transitive.** I may move to `in_progress` only if every ancestor in the `blocked-by` closure has `status ∈ {done, wontfix}`. Direct-only checks are not enough. *[time] [other people's data]*
13. **Diamonds do not double-count.** Closure rows are unique on `(descendantIssueId, ancestorIssueId)`. Transitive open-blocker counts increment once per ancestor, not once per path. *[other people's data]*
14. **Hidden blockers still block.** `transitiveOpenBlockerCount` includes ancestors the viewer cannot read. The Start button uses that number, not the number of **visible** chips. *[secrecy] [other people's data]*
15. **A cycle refusal is not a probe of hidden work.** If the other path would go through issues the viewer cannot read, the message is `CYCLE` with **no path**. If every issue on one witnessing path is readable by the viewer, the path is included. *[secrecy]*
16. **Network visibility is 2-hop to the current assignee, at read time.** Changing `assigneeId` or `visibility` or the people graph changes who may read the title **on the next read**. We do not keep a stored ACL list of neighborhood readers. *[secrecy] [time] [other people's data]*
17. **Assignment does not require the assigner’s social hop to the assignee.** It requires current project membership of both, and `lead`/`owner` role to assign anyone; a plain `member` may assign only themselves. *[other people's data]*
18. **Policy and identity freeze onto events.** `issueEvents` and `connectionIntervals` are append-only. A later handle change, a later disconnect, a later project leave, does not rewrite who did what when. *[time] [other people's data]*
19. **Caps that make bounds real.** Accepted degree ≤ 5,000 per member; pending outgoing invites ≤ 100; pending incoming ≤ 500; direct blockers per issue ≤ 32; direct dependents per issue ≤ 64; longest dependency path ≤ 16; closure rows added by one new edge ≤ 512; PYMK list ≤ 50; project members ≤ 500; handle ≤ 40 chars `[a-z0-9-]`. Writes that would exceed a cap refuse with a named code, they do not truncate silently.
20. **Invite expiry is a write.** `pending` → `expired` is performed by a scheduled job. A read never treats `now > expiresAt` as authorization by itself. *[time]*

### 3. The traps, ranked

#### T1 — Hop-distance is a current-path fact (the active-versus-historical trap)

**Wrong version.** Store `neighborhood(observerId, subjectId, distance)` on accept, treat it as authorization, never take a generation. Or store `members.role = "connected"` as a boolean.

**Why it is wrong.** Distance is not a property of a pair. It is the length of a shortest **currently accepted** path. When Carol, the only common neighbor of Alice and Bob, ends her connection with Alice:

- Alice–Bob goes from 2 to 3 or to ∞ in one commit on the edge table.
- Alice’s **full** profile of Bob must vanish in that commit (I1, promise 9).
- Any `network` issue assigned to Bob must stop showing Alice its title in that commit (I16).
- Alice–Bob’s historical row in `connectionIntervals` still says they were never 1st-degree; a stale `distance = 2` row says they still are.

This is coterie’s membership-at-a-time, with a path-shaped membership: **the authorizing relationship is not a row on the subject, it is a path that can be destroyed by a third party who is not on the screen.** Carol does not open Alice’s profile of Bob. She ends her own connection. The ACL of a pair she is not in changes.

Employment intervals have the same shape: overlapping `companyMemberships` of Alice and Bob at Acme in 2021 is a PYMK signal and a “you worked together” badge. It is not distance 1 in 2026. A `currentCompanyId` foreign key cannot express that.

**Observation that exposes it.** Alice has Bob’s full profile open (live). Carol ends Alice. Alice’s client, without refresh, still shows Bob as `2nd` and still shows Bob’s `network` issue titles. A correct system invalidates that screen in the same commit as Carol’s end (for 2-hop) because the common-neighbor check now fails. A stored `distance = 2` does not fail.

#### T2 — 3-hop authorization has a fan-out that 1-row mutations cannot pay

**Wrong version.** On `acceptConnection(A,B)`, insert one edge and maybe two reciprocal adjacency rows. Compute 3-hop at read time with an unbounded BFS. Or, on each accept, transactionally rewrite every `(observer, subject)` pair whose shortest path used A or B.

**Why it is wrong.** 3-hop card authorization is a BFS of depth 3 over degrees up to 5,000. For two members of degree 200, a typical clustering coefficient of ~0.2, each accept changes on the order of 10³–10⁴ observer/subject pairs at distance 3. For a hub of degree 5,000 linked to a hub, it is 10⁵–10⁶ pairs. Doing that in the accept transaction makes accept unavailable. Doing none of it makes promise 2 a full-table BFS on every card open.

The trap is **using a 3-hop check as if it were a foreign-key check.** It is not. 1-hop is an edge-table point read. 2-hop is a bounded common-neighbor existence test (scan the smaller adjacency list, probe the edge, `take ≤ 5000`). 3-hop is a different computational class. A backend that only has “queries + mutations over rows” will either time out the read or write an unbounded set.

**Observation.** Accept a connection between two degree-2,000 fixtures. A third member who is 1-hop from the first and 2-hop from the second must gain a card. If accept returns in 20 ms and that card is still `out of reach` 10 minutes later, 3-hop is not an authorization function, it is a batch job pretending to be one. If accept takes 30 seconds and holds a transaction across 800,000 neighborhood upserts, the mutation is not a row write.

#### T3 — Visibility-filtered counts, ranks, and refusals (the silent-wrong-integer trap)

**Wrong version.** `connectionCount = count of accepted edges of S that V is allowed to read`. `openBlockerCount = count of blocker issues V can read`. PYMK rank uses `candidate.connectionCount` as scanned from V-visible rows. `/in/alice` returns 404 when the handle exists but is out of reach, 404 when it does not.

**Why it is wrong.**

- Almost none of S’s 1-hop are themselves within V’s 3-hop when S is 3rd-degree; a filtered count returns ~0–20 against a true 400. The integer is admitted and is a lie about the person.
- A is blocked by B (hidden) and C (visible). Filtered count returns 1, Start appears enabled, the write to `in_progress` then fails `STILL_BLOCKED`. Or worse, the write succeeds because the rule used the same filtered count.
- PYMK ranked by popularity: the candidates with the largest **visible-to-you** adjacency look like the most popular. They are the most **clustered with you**. The actual hub ranks as empty.
- Distinct refusals let V enumerate handles and learn which ones exist.

**Observation.** Fixture S with 400 connections, V at distance 2, V sharing 12 mutuals. Profile must show `connectionCount = 400` and `mutualCount = 12`. A visibility-composed `count(*)` on `connectionAccepted` returns 12 for both. For issues: A with two ancestors, one in a foreign project V cannot read. The page must show `openBlockers = 2` and one visible chip. A visibility-composed count shows 1.

#### T4 — Inherited blocked-by, diamonds, hidden ancestors, stored `isBlocked`

**Wrong version.** `issues.isBlocked` is a boolean flipped when a **direct** blocker opens or closes. Or: walk only direct blockers to decide startability. Or: recurse without a visited set.

**Why it is wrong.** A is blocked by B and C; B and C are both blocked by D. Close D: B and C become startable; A is **not**. A boolean on A flipped because “an ancestor closed” is wrong. A boolean on A that stays true until **all direct** blockers are done is right for A in this diamond **only if** B and C themselves stay not-done; it is wrong if someone stored `isBlocked` by copying D onto A at edge-add time and never re-derives.

Hidden ancestor: A (you can read) blocked-by B (you cannot). A boolean `isBlocked` you **can** read is a disclosure of B’s existence, which is allowed (I14), but a boolean you **cannot** read because it was derived from B under B’s visibility makes A look startable.

Cycles: a recursive walk that does not refuse A→A via a long path livelocks the close-and-unblock job.

**Observation.** Build the diamond. Close D. A’s `transitiveOpenBlockerCount` must remain 2 (B and C). Then close B. Count becomes 1. Then close C. Count becomes 0 and Start enables. Any stored boolean that enabled A when D closed fails this. Add a second path A→E→D before closing D; count must still drop by **one** when D closes, not two.

#### T5 — Concurrent edge writes invent cycles and double-promotions

**Wrong version.** Read the closure, check `not reaches(B,A)`, insert `A blocked-by B`. Repeat in another transaction for `B blocked-by A`. Both checks pass against the old snapshot; both inserts commit.

**Why it is wrong.** Cycle detection is not a unique index on `(issueId, blockerIssueId)`. The constraint is about a **path**. Two transactions can each insert a legal single edge that together are a 2-cycle. The same shape at distance 16 is worse: each sees a missing path of length 15.

People-graph analogue: A and B both accept each other’s simultaneously sent invites; you must end with one undirected edge, not two, and with `requestedBy` recorded as the earlier invite. A accepts while B withdraws: one winner, `INVITE_NOT_FOUND` for the other, no accepted edge.

**Observation.** Two clients, same millisecond, `addIssueDependency(A,B)` and `addIssueDependency(B,A)`. Exactly one commits. The other returns `CYCLE`. After both return, a traversal from A does not reach A. Repeat with a 3-cycle split across three clients (A→B, B→C, C→A).

#### T6 — Offline merge of ACL-granting writes

**Wrong version.** Device 1 (offline) accepts Carol. Device 2 (offline) opens Bob’s `network` issue because Carol is the 2-hop bridge, and queues a **title-revealing** local cache fill. Device 1 comes online first, then Carol ends Alice (the device-1 user) from her own client. Device 2 comes online, uploads “I displayed and locally starred this issue.”

**Why it is wrong.** The star/read-receipt is a write whose **authorization was true in a snapshot of a graph that is no longer the graph**. Replaying it as if the client’s proof of 2-hop still holds is acting on Bob’s work without a current path. The dual: two offline adds of inverse blockers, both look legal against an empty local closure.

Authority cousin, online: a project lead assigns Bob to a `network` issue titled “Stealth acquisition.” Bob’s 2-hop, who are not on the project, learn the title. Bob did not accept a **broadcast** to his neighborhood. That is acting on Bob’s adjacency as a distribution list. The product answer (promise 13) is: this is allowed, it is notified, and `visibility = "project"` is the out. A naive app does it without notification and without a way to retract except deleting the issue.

**Observation.** Queue an accept on device 1 and a network-issue cache fill on device 2 against the resulting 2-hop. Reverse the edge before device 2 syncs. Device 2’s sync must **not** create a server-side read-receipt on the issue; it may keep a local ghost until it refetches and then must drop it. For the assign-broadcast: assign Bob, confirm a 2-hop friend of Bob (not on the project) sees the title; set `visibility = "project"`; that friend refetches and the title is gone in that read.

### 4. Data model

Ids are 128-bit, client-mintable, opaque. Times are UTC milliseconds. Dates for employment are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings in the company’s stated timezone, compared lexicographically. There is no money column.

**Derived** means maintained by the operations in §5, not typed in by a client. **Append-only** means no update, no delete, for any principal. **Versioned** means updates allowed, last writer wins on the named fields, with `updatedAt` and `updatedBy`. **Time-bounded** means the row’s authority is an interval; ending the interval is a write, not a clock predicate.

#### `githubImports` (versioned; external-system cursor)

`id`, `projectId`, `installationId` text(40), `lastDeliveryId` text(64), `lastImportedAt` time, `state` `active` \| `paused`.

#### Relationships (plain)

- People graph: `connectionAccepted` bidirectional. Blocks subtract. Invites are not edges.
- Employment: `companyMemberships` intervals hang off `members` and `companies`.
- Work graph: `issues` belong to `projects`; `issueDependencies` is a directed edge; `issueClosure` is its transitive closure minus identity.
- Authorization composes: profile reads use people graph + blocks + `discovery`; issue reads use `projectMembers` **or** (`visibility = network` ∧ 2-hop to `assigneeId`); never `neighborhood3` for issue reads.

### 5. The hard operations

Shared helpers (not operations):

```
degree1(V,S) := exists connectionAccepted (V,S)
blocked(V,S) := exists blocks (V,S) OR exists blocks (S,V)
commonNeighbor(V,S) :=
  let small, large = orderByDegree(V,S)          -- acceptedDegree
  scan connectionAccepted.bySrc where srcId=small take 5000
  for each n: if n.dstId ≠ large and exists (n.dstId, large): return true
  return false
hopDistance(V,S) :=
  if V = S: 0
  if blocked(V,S): ∞
  if degree1(V,S): 1
  if commonNeighbor(V,S): 2
  if card3Hint(V,S): 3 else ∞
card3Hint(V,S) :=
  row = neighborhood3 (V,S)
  if row and row.observerGeneration = members[V].neighborhoodGeneration: true
  else if members[V].acceptedDegree < 500 and members[S].acceptedDegree < 500:
       onlineThreeHop(V,S)     -- see Op 5, budgeted
  else: false                  -- conservative: 3rd-degree flicker allowed; see promise 9
currentProjectMember(V,P) := exists projectMembers (P,V) with leftAt=0
```

`onlineThreeHop(V,S)`: for each `a` in N1(V) (`take 500`), if `hopDistance` treating `a` as viewer would be ≤ 2 to S (i.e. `degree1(a,S) or commonNeighbor(a,S)`), return true. Bound: 500 × 500 edge probes. Used only when both degrees < 500.

#### Op 1 — `requestConnection`

**Inputs:** `toMemberId`, `message`. **Caller:** member V.

**Authorization:** V ≠ to; not blocked either way; no current accepted edge; V’s pending outgoing < 100; to’s pending incoming < 500; no other pending (V,to). The target need not be in-neighborhood (you may invite a `world` card or a handle you already know). If the target is out of reach and `discovery ≠ world`, refuse `OUT_OF_REACH` with the same body as a missing handle — you cannot probe existence by inviting.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read `members[toMemberId]`. If missing, return the out-of-reach body.
2. If `blocked(V,to)`: same out-of-reach body (do not disclose the block).
3. If `degree1(V,to)`: refuse `ALREADY_CONNECTED`.
4. If pending invite (V,to): refuse `ALREADY_PENDING`.
5. If pending invite (to,V): **do not auto-accept**; refuse `THEY_INVITED_YOU` so the client calls Op 2.
6. Count pending outgoing of V (`take 101`). If ≥ 100: `PENDING_OUT_FULL`.
7. Count pending incoming of to (`take 501`). If ≥ 500: `PENDING_IN_FULL`.
8. Insert `connectionInvites` pending, `expiresAt = now + 30d`.
9. Enqueue email to `to.email` via the mail provider (external I/O **after** commit, at-least-once, keyed by invite id).

**Atomic:** 1–8. **Eventual:** the email. **Optimistic:** pending chip on V’s outgoing list. **Conflict:** unique pending (V,to) → `ALREADY_PENDING`.

#### Op 2 — `acceptConnection`

**Inputs:** `inviteId`. **Caller:** V = `invite.toMemberId`.

**Authorization:** invite exists, `state = pending`, `toMemberId = V`, not blocked, both acceptedDegrees ≤ 4999.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read invite. Else `INVITE_NOT_FOUND`.
2. If `state ≠ pending` or `toMemberId ≠ V`: `INVITE_NOT_FOUND` (same code; do not distinguish withdrawn/expired/someone else’s).
3. If `blocked(from, V)`: set invite `declined`, return `INVITE_NOT_FOUND`.
4. If `degree1(from, V)` already (double accept): set invite `accepted`, return success idempotently.
5. If `from.acceptedDegree ≥ 5000` or `V.acceptedDegree ≥ 5000`: `DEGREE_CAP`.
6. Insert `connectionAccepted` (from,V) and (V,from) with `sinceAt = now`.
7. Insert `connectionIntervals` (low, high, requestedBy = from, acceptedAt = now, endedAt = 0).
8. Set invite `accepted`, `respondedAt = now`.
9. Increment `from.acceptedDegree`, `V.acceptedDegree`; increment `from.neighborhoodGeneration`, `V.neighborhoodGeneration`.
10. Delete `pymkCandidates` (V, from) and (from, V) if present.
11. Enqueue `rebuildNeighborhood` for `{from, V}` with `generation` = the new generations (Op 13).
12. Append nothing to issues; 2-hop authorization is computed, not stored.

**Atomic:** 1–10. **Eventual:** Op 13 (3-hop cards and PYMK ranks, ≤ 60s). **Optimistic:** V sees from as `1st` immediately; V does **not** gain 3rd-degree cards from from’s 2-hop until Op 13. **Conflict:** unique `(src,dst)` on `connectionAccepted` → treat as step 4. Concurrent block: if block commits first, step 3; if accept commits first, Op 4’s block must run Op 3’s end path in the same transaction as the block.

#### Op 3 — `endConnection`

**Inputs:** `otherMemberId`. **Caller:** V, must be 1-hop with other.

**Authorization:** `degree1(V, other)`. Either party may end.

**Steps:**

1. If not `degree1`: `NOT_CONNECTED`.
2. Delete `connectionAccepted` (V,other) and (other,V).
3. Patch the open `connectionIntervals` row for the pair: `endedAt = now`, `endReason = "ended"`.
4. Decrement both `acceptedDegree`; increment both `neighborhoodGeneration`.
5. Delete `neighborhood3` rows where `(observerId ∈ {V,other} OR subjectId ∈ {V,other})` **is not feasible unbounded**. Instead: enqueue Op 13 for `{V, other}` **and** mark both generations bumped so any `neighborhood3` row with a stale generation fails `card3Hint`.
6. Enqueue Op 13.

**Atomic:** 1–4. Step 5’s generation bump is the **secrecy** mechanism for 3-hop: stale hints stop matching immediately; the rows are garbage-collected in Op 13. **2-hop secrecy is immediate** because `commonNeighbor` uses `connectionAccepted` in this commit. **Optimistic:** the other person becomes a non-clickable name on V’s device. **Conflict:** double-end: second sees `NOT_CONNECTED`.

#### Op 4 — `blockMember`

**Inputs:** `otherMemberId`. **Caller:** V.

**Authorization:** V ≠ other. Always allowed if other exists; if other does not exist, out-of-reach body.

**Steps:**

1. Upsert `blocks (V, other)`.
2. If `degree1(V, other)`: run Op 3 steps 2–4 with `endReason = "blocked"` in this same transaction.
3. Decline any pending invites in either direction (`take 2` is not enough; scan `byFromState` and `byToState` for this pair — at most a handful; `take 8`).
4. Increment `neighborhoodGeneration` on V (other’s 3-hop hint about V must fail; bump **other** too).
5. Enqueue Op 13 for both.

**Atomic:** 1–4. **Optimistic:** other disappears from V’s UI. Other’s UI does not show a block notice; on next profile open they get out-of-reach. **Conflict:** idempotent upsert.

#### Op 5 — `viewProfile`

**Inputs:** `handle`. **Caller:** V (or anonymous, which is treated as distance ∞ except `discovery = world` cards).

**Authorization:** I1–I6, I8. No extra role.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read `members` by handle. If missing: return `{ visibility: "out_of_reach" }`.
2. If S = V: return full profile including email, open interval, all recommendations received (`take 50`), `acceptedDegree`.
3. If `blocked(V,S)`: `{ visibility: "out_of_reach" }`.
4. Compute `d = hopDistance(V,S)` using the helper (1-hop and 2-hop **live**; 3-hop via hint + optional `onlineThreeHop`).
5. If `d = ∞` and `S.discovery = "world"`: return **world card** `{ visibility: "world", displayName, headline, currentCompanyName, degree: "out_of_reach" }` — world discloses existence by design; handle was already queried.
6. If `d = ∞` and not world: `{ visibility: "out_of_reach" }`.
7. If `d = 3`: **card** `{ visibility: "card", displayName, headline, currentCompanyName, degree: "3rd" }`. No `acceptedDegree`, no mutuals, no bio, no recommendations.
8. If `d = 2` or `1`: full profile. Include `acceptedDegree` from the **member row** (I8, not a visible-edge count). Include `bio`, work-history intervals (`take 20`), recommendations (`take 50`) with author cards filtered by a nested card check. Include email only if `d = 1` and `shareEmailWithConnections`.
9. If `d ≤ 2` and `S.showMutuals`: compute `mutualCount` as in Op 6 (structural) and `mutualsPreview` take 8. If not `showMutuals`: `mutuals: "hidden"`.
10. `degree` badge: `1st`/`2nd`/`3rd`.

**Atomic:** a single consistent snapshot of edges + blocks + member row. **Eventual:** none. **Optimistic:** cached last view, tagged with `neighborhoodGeneration`; if generation moved, show a spinner and refetch — do not show a stale full profile. **Conflict:** n/a (read). **K2:** step 8 must not count `connectionAccepted` under V’s read visibility.

#### Op 6 — `listMutualConnections`

**Inputs:** `subjectId`, `after` cursor, `take` ≤ 20. **Caller:** V.

**Authorization:** `hopDistance(V,S) ≤ 2`. If 3 or ∞: `OUT_OF_REACH` (same body as profile). If `S.showMutuals = false` and V ≠ S: return `{ hidden: true }` not an empty list.

**Steps:**

1. Authorize as above.
2. Let `small` be whichever of V, S has smaller `acceptedDegree`.
3. Scan `connectionAccepted` of `small` `take 5000`.
4. For each neighbor n ≠ the other party: `exists connectionAccepted (n, large)`. Collect matches, stable-sort by `n.displayName`.
5. Page `take` 20 from `after`.
6. `total` = size of the collected set (≤ 5000). This is the structural mutual count.

**Atomic:** snapshot. **Optimistic:** none. **Conflict:** n/a. **Bound:** 5,000 existence probes. **Wrong implementation:** scan S’s adjacency **as visible to V** and intersect — under-counts when S is 2-hop and V cannot read S’s far edges; those far edges are not mutuals, so this particular intersection happens to be correct **only because** mutuals are 1-hop of V and V can always read their own adjacency. The operation must still be specified as **intersect two adjacency lists from the edge table, unfiltered by profile visibility of the neighbors’ other edges**. Filter only: omit neighbors whom V has blocked (they would otherwise appear as mutuals with S).

#### Op 7 — `peopleYouMayKnow`

**Inputs:** none. **Caller:** V.

**Authorization:** self only.

**Steps:**

1. Read `pymkCandidates` for V, `order by score desc`, `take 50`.
2. Drop any row where `hopDistance(V, candidate)` ≠ 2 **at read time** (stale after accept/end/block).
3. Drop blocked.
4. Return remaining, with `mutualCount` as stored (may lag 60s) and a live `degree: "2nd"`.

**Atomic:** each hopDistance check is live; the rank is eventual. **Optimistic:** last list, then reconcile. **Conflict:** n/a. If `pymkGeneration` is behind `neighborhoodGeneration` by more than 60s, the client shows “refreshing” and Op 13 is considered late (ops SLO).

#### Op 8 — `addIssueDependency`

**Inputs:** `issueId` (dependent A), `blockerIssueId` (B). **Caller:** V.

**Authorization:** `currentProjectMember(V, A.projectId)` with role ∈ {owner, lead, member}. V must **read** B under Op 10’s rules (cannot depend on work you cannot see). Guests cannot add.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read A, B. Missing → `NOT_FOUND` (do not distinguish hidden vs missing for B if Op 10 would have returned out-of-reach: then `NOT_FOUND`).
2. If A.id = B.id: `SELF_EDGE`.
3. If unique (A,B) already: return success idempotently.
4. If A.directBlockerCount ≥ 32: `TOO_MANY_BLOCKERS`.
5. If B.directDependentCount ≥ 64: `TOO_MANY_DEPENDENTS`.
6. **Cycle check:** if A = B or `issueClosure` contains `(descendant=B, ancestor=A)`: `CYCLE`. If V can read every issue on one shortest witnessing path of depth ≤ 16 (walk ancestors of A that are descendants of B — actually: if closure (B,A) exists, path is B ↝ A; reconstruct by walking `issueDependencies` from B toward A with `take 16` on depth), include `path: [ids]`. If any reconstructed node fails Op 10 for V: `CYCLE` with **no** path (I15).
7. Let `AncB` = {B} ∪ ancestors of B from closure (`take 256`). Let `DescA` = {A} ∪ descendants of A (`take 256`).
8. If `|DescA| * |AncB|` > 512: `CLOSURE_CAP`.
9. If any pair would have `depth` > 16 (depth(A,B) = 1; depth(d,a) = depth(d,A)+1+depth(B,a)): `DEPTH_CAP`.
10. Insert `issueDependencies (A,B)`.
11. Insert missing `issueClosure` rows for every (d,a) in DescA × AncB; `depth` = shortest (min with existing).
12. Increment A.directBlockerCount, B.directDependentCount.
13. For each d in DescA: let `k` = number of newly added ancestors of d whose `status ∉ {done, wontfix}`. Increment `d.transitiveOpenBlockerCount` by k. (k is 0..|AncB|; compute from the inserted pairs, not from a visibility-filtered read.)
14. If A.status = `in_progress` and A.transitiveOpenBlockerCount > 0: set A.status = `open` (cannot stay in progress while blocked).
15. Append `issueEvents` `dep_added` on A.

**Atomic:** 1–15. **Optimistic:** blocker chip on A; Start disabled if the new blocker is **open**. If the client cannot read B’s status (should not happen — authorization required read of B), do not guess. **Conflict:** concurrent B→A: unique vs cycle, see T5; the losing transaction retries the cycle check and gets `CYCLE`. Concurrent two different blockers: both may proceed if caps allow; counts increment.

#### Op 9 — `closeIssue`

**Inputs:** `issueId`, `resolution` ∈ {`done`, `wontfix`}. **Caller:** V.

**Authorization:** current project member, role ∈ {owner, lead, member}; guests no. Assignee or lead/owner may close; a plain member may close only if they are `assigneeId` or `createdBy`.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read I. If status already in {done, wontfix} and equals resolution: idempotent success.
2. If `in_progress` or `open` or `backlog`: set `status = resolution`, `closedAt = now`.
3. Let `Desc` = descendants of I from `issueClosure.byAncestor` (`take 256`).
4. For each d in Desc ∪ {I’s dependents already in Desc}: **decrement** `d.transitiveOpenBlockerCount` by 1 (I was an open ancestor counted once). Clamp at 0 as a repair, but a negative is a product bug — log `COUNT_UNDERFLOW` and refuse the whole transaction rather than clamp silently in v1.
5. Append `issueEvents` `status`.
6. Do **not** auto-start descendants.

**Atomic:** 1–5. **Eventual:** none for counts. **Optimistic:** I shows done; visible descendants’ chips lose I; Start enables only if the client’s **local** remaining visible open count is 0 **and** the server’s `transitiveOpenBlockerCount` is 0 on refetch. **Never** enable Start from a local visible-only zero. **Conflict:** two concurrent closes: second is idempotent. Concurrent reopen (not in this list; inverse of this op) vs close: last write to `status` wins; counts must be recomputed from closure + statuses if `status` flips twice (reopen is a **full recount** of open ancestors `take 256`, not a blind increment, to keep T4 diamonds honest). Specify reopen here as the inverse: `status → open`, `closedAt = 0`, for each d in Desc increment by 1 if I is in that d’s ancestor set (always).

#### Op 10 — `viewIssue`

**Inputs:** `issueId`. **Caller:** V.

**Authorization** — readable iff any of:

1. `currentProjectMember(V, I.projectId)`, or
2. `I.visibility = "network"` AND `I.assigneeId ≠ 0` AND `hopDistance(V, I.assigneeId) ≤ 2` AND not `blocked(V, assignee)` (block already inside hopDistance), or
3. `I.createdBy = V` (historical stub: title, status, your events, no body if you are not (1)).

**Steps:**

1. Point-read I. If missing: `{ visibility: "out_of_reach" }` (same as profile missing).
2. Evaluate the three arms. If none: same out-of-reach body.
3. If arm 1: return full issue, body, direct blockers **each** passed through Op 10 recursively **only as a point-read of that blocker** (32 max). For each blocker: if readable, `{ visible: true, id, title, status, projectId }`; else `{ visible: false }`. Include `transitiveOpenBlockerCount` from the **issue row** (I14).
4. If arm 2 (network, not a project member): return `{ title, status, projectName, assigneeCard, transitiveOpenBlockerCount, directBlockers: all { visible: false } except those V can independently read }`. No body. No events.
5. If arm 3 only: stub `{ title, status, yourRole: "creator" }`.

**Atomic:** snapshot. **Optimistic:** last snapshot tagged with issue `updatedAt` and assignee’s `neighborhoodGeneration`. **K2:** the count on the page is the stored structural field, never `count(visible chips)`.

#### Op 11 — `expireConnectionInvites` (scheduled)

**Trigger:** every 15 minutes. **Principal:** system. **Input:** none (scan).

**Steps:**

1. Scan `connectionInvites.byExpires` where `state = pending` and `expiresAt ≤ now`, `take 500`.
2. For each: patch `state = expired`, `respondedAt = now`.
3. If `hasMore`, enqueue another run immediately.

**Atomic:** each row (or the batch of 500) in one transaction. **Not** a read-time authorization change: until this write, Op 2 still accepts. **SLO:** an invite is expired within 1 hour of `expiresAt`. **Optimistic:** n/a. **Conflict:** Op 2 vs this: if accept commits first, this finds `state ≠ pending` and skips.

#### Op 12 — `importGitHubDependencies` (external system)

**Inputs:** `projectId`, `githubDeliveryId`, payload of GitHub issue-dependency events: list of `{ dependentNumber, blockerNumber, action: add|remove }` with `take ≤ 100`. **Caller:** system, after verifying the GitHub app signature on the delivery; or a lead triggering “import now” which **fetches** GitHub’s REST `GET /repos/{repo}/issues/{n}/dependencies` with the stored installation token.

**Authorization:** `githubImports` row for the project is `active`. Human trigger: caller is project lead/owner. The GitHub token never goes to a client.

**Steps:**

1. Verify `githubDeliveryId` not already applied (unique on `githubImports.lastDeliveryId` / a small `githubDeliveryReceipts` table PK `deliveryId`). Duplicate delivery: success no-op.
2. For each number in the payload, point-read `issues.byProjectNumber`. Missing: **create** a local issue `title = "GitHub #N"`, `status = open`, `visibility = project`, `assigneeId = 0`, `createdBy = system`.
3. Build the **would-be** edge set: current project edges plus adds minus removes.
4. Cycle-detect **the batch** as one DAG: topological sort of the touched component (`take 512` nodes). If a cycle: refuse the **whole delivery** `CYCLE_IMPORT` with the GitHub numbers (leads can see their own project; path is in-project here). Do not apply a partial batch.
5. Apply each add via Op 8’s steps 3–15 under system principal, skipping the human read-of-B check (system can read all issues in this project; **cross-repo** blockers that are not in this project are created as local stubs in **this** project, never as silent edges to another Joinery project).
6. Apply each remove: delete `issueDependencies`, delete closure pairs that the removed edge uniquely provided (recompute closure for descendants of A ∩ ancestors of B: delete (d,a) if no remaining path; v1: **recompute closure for DescA**, bound 256 × 32, refuse `CLOSURE_REPAIR_CAP` if over).
7. Patch `lastDeliveryId`, `lastImportedAt`.

**Atomic:** 1–7. **Eventual:** GitHub’s own graph is the other system; we do not write back. **Optimistic:** none (system). **Conflict:** a human Op 8 in the same project concurrent with import: serializable; if the human created the cycle the import would create, import fails `CYCLE_IMPORT` and retries after a lead deletes the human edge.

#### Op 13 — `rebuildNeighborhood` (bulk / periodic)

**Inputs:** `memberId`, `expectedGeneration`. **Caller:** system. Also runs periodically every 10 minutes for members where `pymkGeneration < neighborhoodGeneration`.

**Authorization:** system only.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read member M. If `neighborhoodGeneration ≠ expectedGeneration` and this was an enqueue from Op 2/3/4, **exit** (a newer rebuild is queued). Periodic runs use the current generation.
2. Scan N1(M) `take 5000` from `connectionAccepted`.
3. **PYMK (exactly 2):** for each n in N1, scan N1(n) `take 500` (sample, not full — see scale). For each candidate c ≠ M and not in N1(M) and not blocked: increment a map `mutualCount[c]`. Keep a heap of top 50 by `score`. Write-replace `pymkCandidates` for M (delete missing, upsert 50). Set `pymkGeneration = neighborhoodGeneration`.
4. **Distance-3 hints:** for each n in N1(M), for each c in a `take 200` sample of N1(n), for each g in a `take 50` sample of N1(c): if g ∉ {M} ∪ N1(M) ∪ 2-hop-map: upsert `neighborhood3 (M, g)` with `observerGeneration = expectedGeneration`. Bound writes: **10,000** upserts per run; if the sample saturates, set `members.n3Partial = true` (a derived flag). Partial 3-hop is an **availability** miss (false `out of reach`), never a secrecy miss (we still bump generation on end, so **old** 3-hop rows do not authorize).
5. Garbage-collect `neighborhood3` rows for observer M with `observerGeneration < expectedGeneration` (`take 1000` per run, repeat).

**Atomic:** per member, steps 2–5 may be several transactions of 1,000 writes, each fencing on `expectedGeneration` still equal. **Eventual:** this **is** the eventual part of Ops 2–4. **Optimistic:** n/a. **Conflict:** a newer accept increments generation; this run exits and the new run starts.

Hub concession **inside** this op: sampling 2-hop/3-hop for members with degree ≥ 500 is why 3rd-degree is an SLO, not a same-commit fact. 1st and 2nd stay exact.

#### Op 14 — `assignIssue`

**Inputs:** `issueId`, `assigneeId` (0 = unassign). **Caller:** V.

**Authorization:** current project member. If `assigneeId ≠ 0`, assignee has current `projectMembers` row. Role: lead/owner may assign anyone on the project; member may set assignee to self or 0 from self; guest no.

**Steps:**

1. Point-read I and the project-membership rows.
2. If `assigneeId ≠ 0` and not current member of I.projectId: `NOT_PROJECT_MEMBER`.
3. Patch `I.assigneeId`.
4. Append `issueEvents` `assigned` / `unassigned` with **both** old and new ids.
5. No write to any neighborhood table. Network readers of the **old** assignee lose title on next Op 10 (I16). Network readers of the **new** assignee gain it on next Op 10.
6. If I.visibility = `network` and assignee ≠ 0: insert a notification row for the assignee `{ kind: "network_broadcast", issueId }` (table `notifications`: `id, memberId, kind, issueId, at, readAt`; readable only by `memberId`).

**Atomic:** 1–4, 6. **Eventual:** 2-hop friends discover the title on their next search/home; we do not push-fan-out the neighborhood. **Optimistic:** assignee avatar changes. Do not optimistically show the issue on the assigner’s friends’ home feeds. **Conflict:** last patch to `assigneeId` wins; events keep both, so history is not lost.

### 6. Scale and cost

**A successful specialist network, year 4** (order-of-magnitude, not a forecast):

| Thing | Size / rate |
|---|---|
| Members | 2 × 10⁶ |
| Accepted directed adjacency rows | ~3.2 × 10⁸ (avg degree 80; 2 rows per undirected edge) |
| Hub cap | 5,000; ~0.1% of members above degree 500 |
| Connection accepts | ~20/s avg, 200/s peak |
| Profile opens | ~5,000/s peak |
| PYMK home loads | ~1,000/s |
| Projects | 2 × 10⁵ |
| Issues | 2 × 10⁷ |
| Dependency edges | 1.5 × 10⁷ |
| Closure rows | ~6 × 10⁷ (mean ~3 ancestors) |
| Issue writes (create/assign/close/dep) | ~50/s avg, 400/s peak |
| GitHub import deliveries | ~5/s |
| Invite expiry job | 500-row batches, a few thousand pending expiries/hour |

**What gets expensive**

- **Op 5 at d=2** for two degree-5,000 members: common-neighbor may scan 5,000 keys. Must short-circuit on first hit (usual case in a clustered graph). Worst case 5,000 misses: a **negative** 2-hop (they are 3 or ∞). That worst case is also the 3-hop online path’s inner loop — this is why hubs are not allowed `onlineThreeHop`.
- **Op 2 + Op 13** at hub scale: a degree-2,000 accept dirties on the order of 10⁵ 3-hop pairs. Sampling keeps writes to 10,000 per rebuild but makes 3rd-degree **incomplete**. Full exact 3-hop materialization at this size is a dedicated graph store, not a row table.
- **Op 6** is the same 5,000-probe join as common-neighbor but **must not** short-circuit: it needs the full intersection. For two hubs, 5,000 probes every “see mutuals” click. Cache the count; page the list.
- **Op 7 / Op 13 PYMK:** exact 2-hop for a degree-200 member with neighbors of degree 200 is 40,000 candidates before unique. We sample `take 500` per neighbor, so PYMK is **not** the complete 2-hop set. Completeness of PYMK is the thing we concede (discovery, not authorization).
- **Op 8 / Op 9** on a fat node: 256 descendants × count updates is the designed max. A “epic” that everyone blocked-by would refuse `TOO_MANY_DEPENDENTS` at 64, which is the product telling you to split work.
- **Op 10 arm 2** (home feed of network issues): “issues assigned to anyone in my 2-hop” is **not** an index. 2-hop can be tens of thousands of member ids; issues-by-assignee for each is unusable. A feed requires a **sampled** 2-hop (same sample as PYMK) or a derived `networkIssueFeed` that we did **not** add, on purpose — v1 network visibility is **on open**, not a firehose. Building that firehose is a second fan-out, the same class as T2.
- **Live invalidation:** Carol ends Alice. Every open Op 5 of Alice onto Bob and every Op 10 of Alice onto Bob’s network issues must re-run. The read-set is “all pairs whose shortest paths used Carol,” which is not a row key. Practical invalidation: bump `neighborhoodGeneration` of Alice and of Carol; clients that tagged views with those ids refetch. Bob’s other viewers are unaffected.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

#### U1 — Variable-length path as an authorization predicate

`hopDistance(V,S) ≤ 2` is not `row.ownerId = viewer` and not `exists membership (org, viewer)`. It is a bounded but **data-dependent join** through the adjacency table, plus a block table that **subtracts** even when the join would hit.

A row-RLS backend will either (a) persist `distance` (T1, stale ACL), or (b) run recursive SQL in a policy (unbounded, unindexable, unsafe), or (c) only authorize 1-hop.

**Concession that is not a cheat:** drop 3rd-degree **cards** (keep 3rd-degree out of authorization entirely); keep exact 1-hop and exact 2-hop common-neighbor as the only profile ACL. PYMK can still **rank** 2-hop because PYMK is not a privilege.

**Cheat:** drop 2-hop full-profile and network-issue ACL, leaving only project membership and self. Then reachability is no longer load-bearing; the app is a project tracker with a friends list.

#### U2 — Same-commit DAG constraint, not a unique index

Cycle detection is reachability on the dependency graph. Unique `(issueId, blockerIssueId)` does not prevent A→B plus B→A in two transactions (T5). You need the cycle check and the closure write **in the same serializable transaction as the edge**, plus a bound so the write set is finite (512).

**Concession that is not a cheat:** forbid cross-project blockers, cap a project at 512 issues, recompute the whole project DAG on every edge write. Still a DAG check, still unusual, but the domain is a small dense graph.

**Cheat:** store direct blockers only, no transitive startability, no cycle check beyond “not self.” Then I11–I13 are gone and the axis is decoration.

#### U3 — Structural aggregates over rows the viewer must not see

`acceptedDegree`, `transitiveOpenBlockerCount`, `mutualCount` (when shown), PYMK `mutualCount`. Each is false if computed under caller visibility (T3). The backend must maintain counters in the writer’s transaction **without** applying the reader’s ACL, and must have a **declared** disclosure: which numbers leak existence (blocker count: yes; hidden blocker ids: no; connectionCount on a 3rd-degree card: **no**, which is why cards omit it).

**Concession that is not a cheat:** show `500+` buckets only to 1st-degree; hide blocker counts and disable Start whenever any stub `{visible:false}` is present (lose information, stay safe).

**Cheat:** compute the counts under RLS and show them as if they were global. That is a silently wrong integer about other people’s data.

#### U4 — Fan-out set writes on a single social action

Accept/end/block dirties 2-hop membership for PYMK and 3-hop hints for a set whose size is **not** a constant in the schema. Ordinary “patch this row, insert that row” cannot both (i) revoke 2-hop in the same commit and (ii) finish. The design splits: 2-hop is **computed**, 3-hop is **generation-fenced rebuild**. A backend needs either computed multi-hop in the read path, or a fenced bulk writer the client cannot call.

**Concession that is not a cheat:** the 60-second 3-hop SLO in promise 9, plus generation mismatch → deny card (secrecy over availability).

**Cheat:** 3-hop cards authorized from a nightly job with no generation fence (T1). Or accept() transactionally updates the full 3-hop of hubs (will not run at the sizes in §6, so the promise is fake).

#### U5 — Two graphs compose, and a third party can revoke

Issue title visibility is `projectMember OR (network AND 2-hop(viewer, assignee))`. The 2-hop can be destroyed by **Carol**, who is neither viewer nor assignee nor project member (T1). Offline, a device can hold a proof that was true. Replay must **re-check** path-shaped ACL, not trust the client’s retained read (T6).

A backend whose offline sync replays writes with the **original** read-set as proof will apply a star/comment/receipt that is no longer authorized. A backend that cannot invalidate “everything I showed you because you could reach Bob” when Carol disconnects will leak titles.

**Concession that is not a cheat:** network visibility is **on open** only (Op 10), no neighborhood firehose; notifications go only to the assignee (Op 14). Still path-shaped on every open.

**Cheat:** `visibility = network` means “logged-in world-readable.” Then hops do not authorize. Or: freeze network readers at assign time into an `issueReaders` table and never update it on disconnect — that is T1 as a feature.

**The cheat boundary in one sentence.** A product change is legitimate when the founder would still want it after a graph database became free (e.g. no 3rd-degree **cards**, no PYMK completeness for hubs, no network **feed**). It is a cheat when it is chosen so that authorization is once again a foreign key: 1-hop-only profiles **and** project-only issues **and** direct-only blockers.

### 8. Confidence

**Standard practice (would not surprise a LinkedIn/Jira/GitHub engineer):**

- Mutual-consent connections, silent blocks, 1st/2nd/3rd badges, PYMK as 2-hop ranked by mutuals, sealed “out of reach” for missing vs far handles.
- Employment as intervals, not a current-employer enum.
- Issue DAG with transitive blocked-by, cycle refuse, diamond-once closure, GitHub-style incoming dependency webhooks.
- Caps on degree, direct width, and depth so fan-out is finite.
- Invite expiry as a job that writes state.
- Generation numbers / cache epochs after graph mutation.

**Standard but easy to get wrong, and here required:**

- Common-neighbor for 2-hop rather than a stored degree.
- Structural counters vs visibility-filtered counts (waitlist-depth class).
- Serializable cycle check, not a unique index.
- Re-check path ACL on offline replay.

**Invented or tightened for this exercise (so the axis cannot be walked around):**

- Composing **network** issue titles with **live 2-hop to the current assignee**, revocable by a third party’s disconnect.
- Hidden blocker stubs that still increment a disclosed structural count.
- Cycle refusals that omit the path if any node on it is unreadable.
- Exact 2-hop in-commit secrecy vs 60-second generation-fenced 3-hop, with hub sampling that may false-negative cards but must not false-positive them after a generation bump.
- `CLOSURE_CAP 512` as a user-visible refuse, not an unbounded closure rewrite.
- No neighborhood firehose in v1, on purpose, so implementers cannot pretend a 2-hop index exists.

**Numbers:** average degree 80, 2 million members, 20 accepts/s, 5,000 profile opens/s, closure fan-out 256, degree cap 5,000 — realistic order-of-magnitude for a specialist network, not LinkedIn 2026 (10⁹ members). Hub sampling constants (500, 200, 50, 10,000 writes) are **design choices** to make Op 13 finish, not measured.

**What this design is for.** A later implementation in a candidate backend language should have to answer, in ordinary product flows, not in an appendix: *how do you bound a path, how do you refuse a cycle, how do you authorize on reachability, and how do you count things the viewer is not allowed to list.* If those four become foreign-key checks, the implementation has not implemented Joinery.

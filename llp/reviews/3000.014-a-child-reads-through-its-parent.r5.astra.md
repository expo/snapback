**The lifetime rule holds for authoritative server state under the specified checks.**

- **Deletes, jobs and same-mutation writes:** `insert parent; insert child; delete parent` refuses because the reverse probe sees the uncommitted child. Deleting the child first permits deletion. System authority bypasses ACLs, not the structural check.
- **Expiry and three levels:** Recursive minimum deadlines establish `deadline(C) ≤ deadline(P) ≤ deadline(G)`. Reads, existence probes and unique checks therefore cannot treat a descendant as live after its ancestor expires. Maintained views exclude expired contributions before physical removal; maintained columns refuse an effectively expiring source.
- **Replacement and sweep:** Same-id replacement remains blocked by physical children, including expired ones. Child-first sweeping removes their contributions before reuse. A replacement’s reads cannot traverse the previous parent’s unswept child population.
- **Upserts:** Existing-row patches preserve the immutable reference and captures. The insert arm takes the live-parent guard. Reserving capture work before branch selection closes the earlier budget-dependent refusal channel.
- **Seeds, imports and restores:** Ancestor-first resolution against final batch images preserves equality. Replacing captured parent inputs while children remain refuses. Restore preserves a coherent graph rather than silently recapturing it.
- **Activation:** Previous-schema expiry is evaluated before recapture, so an expired child cannot be revived under a later parent lifetime. Correspondence checks precede destructive projection; publication remains atomic.

This is **not a literal invariant of every device-stored image**. A horizon can omit a parent; separate logs can temporarily leave an orphan or mismatched pair; retained history can preserve one indefinitely. Revision 5 acknowledges those states and restricts the optimization accordingly. Finite deadlines use the advancing expiry clock; nonexpiring historical rows require the capture-match and retention rules. Parent authorization still does not establish child coverage.

One finding remains:

**Medium — §§4, 5 and 10: the private recorded-path acceptance case lacks a defined lowering and lifecycle.**

Consider membership-private `messages`, with immutable `chat`, and `receipts` declaring `read through .message`. Section 10 requires:

```quarry
view unread: count receipts by .message.chat
```

Ordinary recorded-path binding requires the target to be public, and core schema validation independently enforces that restriction. See [recorded-path binding](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/maintains.rs:65) and [schema validation](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/schema.rs:784).

The RFC distinguishes inherited captures from historical recorded fields, but never specifies that `.message.chat` can address the inherited capture. Their lifecycles also differ: historical attribution remains unchanged across rebuilds, while inherited captures can be recaptured during activation.

**Smallest change:** specify that a maintain path matching an inherited capture reuses that capture, including its type, rule-field identity and activation/rebuild lifecycle. Keep ordinary historical recorded paths under their existing contract. Without that sentence, a competent implementation can correctly implement inheritance yet reject the advertised Clique aggregate.

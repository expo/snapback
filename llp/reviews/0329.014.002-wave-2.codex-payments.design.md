### 1. Product contract

Relay is a USD stored-value network for consumers and businesses. Consumers fund wallets through an external card processor, transfer money to peers, and pay businesses. A business payment begins as a hold and becomes one or more captures. Businesses can refund captures and withdraw their wallet balance through processor-managed payouts. Users can dispute captured payments. Finance closes the internal books daily.

The first release supports USD only. Every cross-currency request is rejected; currency conversion is not silently approximated.

Relay makes these user-visible promises:

1. Every wallet shows four separate figures: `available`, `held`, `debt`, and `pending`; only `available` is spendable.
2. `net position = available + held + debt`, where debt is zero or negative, while pending card funding or payout is informational and not posted money.
3. A successful monetary response includes the resulting balances and a server cursor from the same commit; another device can use that cursor to distinguish stale state.
4. A purchase hold immediately removes its amount from available funds, may last from 15 minutes to seven days, and cannot be overspent.
5. A business may partially capture a hold several times; cumulative captures never exceed the authorized amount, and a final capture releases the remainder.
6. The business pays the capture fee locked at authorization: `fee(x) = min(x, 30¢ + ceil(2% × x))` on cumulative captured value, so each partial capture charges only the increase in cumulative fee.
7. Capture fees are non-refundable; refunds return purchase principal to the original payer wallet.
8. A posted peer transfer is immediate and final inside Relay: sender debit, recipient credit, and activity records succeed together, and reversal requires a new compensating transfer.
9. Peer transfers are free in this release; business payouts cost exactly `$1.00`, disclosed before submission.
10. Card funding becomes spendable only after the processor definitively reports a successful charge; submission or a network timeout remains pending.
11. Relay produces at most one wallet credit, charge, or payout for one business operation even if workers retry, responses are lost, and webhooks are duplicated or reordered.
12. An externally indeterminate operation remains pending and reserved until it is resolved by same-key retry, processor lookup, settlement reconciliation, or manual review.
13. A merchant refund changes balances only when accepted; two concurrent refunds cannot exceed the capture’s unrelieved principal.
14. Opening a dispute does not create spendable provisional money; a user-win decision posts final relief, while a merchant-win decision changes no balance.
15. An offline device may create a “queued on this device” intent but may not call it sent, paid, funded, or withdrawn; server acceptance is the first authoritative monetary event.
16. If two offline devices each try to spend the same cached funds, Relay orders the commands by server acceptance and may accept one and reject the other.
17. Incoming credits to a popular business may proceed in parallel, but conflicting debits from one wallet have one authoritative order; saturation produces explicit retryable backpressure, never relaxed balance checks.
18. Daily close does not stop ordinary payments, uses a sealed journal watermark, is restartable, and publishes the same result when rerun with the same inputs.
19. Late processor information never rewrites a published period silently; it posts as a later adjustment referencing the original event, or through an explicitly versioned restatement.
20. Wallet amounts, counterparties, disputes, and rejection reasons are visible only to authorized parties; a sender is not told a recipient’s balance, restriction, debt, or dispute status.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

1. **[Money]** Every amount is a positive integer number of cents at an API boundary, every aggregate uses an exact wider integer, and binary floating point is never used.
2. **[Money]** Every posted journal entry balances independently in USD, with the sum of its signed postings equal to zero.
3. **[Money, Time]** Posted journal entries and postings are never edited or deleted, and every correction is a linked reversal or compensating entry.
4. **[Money]** The journal is the source of truth, while balance rows, statements, dashboards, and close totals are reconstructible projections.
5. **[Money]** A wallet’s available balance cannot become negative unless an explicit debt account, risk limit, and restriction policy records the shortfall.
6. **[Money, Time]** Every operation that may consume one wallet’s available funds participates in a single authoritative order for that wallet.
7. **[Money]** A multi-wallet transfer posts every principal, fee, recovery, and control-account leg atomically or posts none of them.
8. **[Money, Time]** For every hold, `captured_minor + released_minor ≤ authorized_minor`, and its remaining held amount equals the difference.
9. **[Money, Time]** At any journal watermark, each wallet’s held balance equals the sum of the remaining amounts of its active purchase and payout holds.
10. **[Money, Time]** Capture, release, and expiry races have one winner, and no terminal hold can later be captured.
11. **[Money, Time]** Every capture uses the fee rule saved with its hold, rather than whichever fee rule is current when capture occurs.
12. **[Money, Other people’s data]** A peer transfer’s sender debit and recipient credit become visible together, while private source-wallet facts remain invisible to the recipient.
13. **[Money, Time]** The sum of refunds and final dispute relief against a capture never exceeds that capture’s posted principal.
14. **[Money, Time]** A refund, dispute decision, chargeback, or payout is a new monetary effect linked to its source operation and never a mutation of that operation’s history.
15. **[Money, Time]** A client operation identifier reused with the identical canonical request returns the original outcome, while reuse with different material fields is rejected.
16. **[Money, Time]** Before any processor request, Relay durably records its immutable payload digest, business identity, and stable processor idempotency key.
17. **[Money, Time]** Every retry of one external effect uses the identical key and material payload, and an ambiguous timeout is never converted into failure merely to permit a new key.
18. **[Money, Time]** Processor responses, webhooks, polling, and settlement files converge through one state machine and can create each local journal effect at most once.
19. **[Money, Time]** Money reserved for an indeterminate payout remains unavailable until definitive success, definitive failure, or a controlled exception resolution.
20. **[Money, Time]** A processor chargeback is recorded even when the wallet lacks funds, with the shortfall becoming explicit debt and the wallet becoming outgoing-restricted.
21. **[Secrecy, Time, Other people’s data]** Authority is checked at server acceptance using a grant active at that instant, so an offline draft or old login cannot preserve revoked authority.
22. **[Secrecy, Other people’s data]** Raw card numbers and security codes never enter Relay, processor tokens are encrypted, and dispute evidence is accessible only to the parties and assigned adjudicators.
23. **[Secrecy, Other people’s data]** Recipient lookup and refusal disclose no more than a confirmation name and a generic eligibility result.
24. **[Money, Time]** A daily close covers every journal entry after the preceding watermark and through its sealed watermark exactly once.
25. **[Money, Time]** A transaction that commits concurrently with close appears wholly before or wholly after the close boundary.
26. **[Money, Time]** Duplicate close workers, lost leases, or reruns cannot duplicate fees, postings, account totals, or published artifacts.
27. **[Money, Time]** An offline device without a server-issued, disjoint spending allowance has no authority to reserve or transfer shared wallet funds.
28. **[Money]** Replaying the journal through any published watermark produces the same account balances and zero platform-wide net posting total as the corresponding close.

### 3. The traps, ranked

1. **Treating “exactly once” as a local queue property.**  
   **Wrong version:** A charge times out, the job is marked failed, and a retry uses a new processor key.  
   **Why wrong:** The first request may already have charged the card; webhook deduplication cannot prevent a second external charge created under another key.  
   **Observation:** Kill the worker after the processor commits but before Relay records the response; after retry and a delayed webhook, the card has two charges although the wallet has one credit.

2. **Putting every balance behind one mutable hot row—or sharding without one debit authority.**  
   **Wrong version:** Every credit locks the merchant, fee, or clearing balance row, or concurrent debit workers independently check different balance shards.  
   **Why wrong:** The first design collapses throughput on a viral merchant or universal fee account, while the second permits write-skew overspending.  
   **Observation:** Send 2,000 captures per second to one merchant while submitting 200 simultaneous payouts from that wallet; either unrelated senders convoy or total accepted payouts exceed available funds.

3. **Merging offline money like ordinary application state.**  
   **Wrong version:** Each device subtracts from its cached balance, labels the transfer paid, and later resolves divergence with timestamps or last-write-wins.  
   **Why wrong:** Device clocks and local replicas cannot grant two claims on the same dollars, and no merge can retract money already represented as received by another person.  
   **Observation:** Two devices last saw `$100`, each queues an `$80` transfer, and both show success before reconnecting; the product has promised `$160` backed by `$100`.

4. **Using wall-clock dates or mutable current state for close.**  
   **Wrong version:** A job scans rows whose `created_at` says “yesterday” while new postings and late webhooks continue to arrive.  
   **Why wrong:** Pagination can omit or duplicate rows, device and processor times differ from commit time, and rerunning later changes the historical population.  
   **Observation:** A transfer commits across page boundaries while a 23:59 processor event arrives at 00:20; two runs of “yesterday’s” close produce different totals.

5. **Editing captures or maintaining independent refund and dispute counters.**  
   **Wrong version:** A refund changes the capture amount, while dispute resolution separately checks only the original capture.  
   **Why wrong:** History becomes unreconcilable and different relief paths can return the same principal twice.  
   **Observation:** Two merchant operators issue `$70` refunds against a `$100` capture while support awards a `$40` dispute; shallow per-row checks allow `$180` of relief.

6. **Confusing historical consent, current authority, and permissible disclosure.**  
   **Wrong version:** The employee who created a merchant hold remains able to capture forever, cached idempotent responses bypass current authorization, or precise recipient errors reveal why another wallet is restricted.  
   **Why wrong:** The customer authorized the business until hold expiry, not a particular former employee, while operation authority and information visibility must be evaluated independently at execution.  
   **Observation:** Revoke an operator on one device, then reconnect their offline device to capture an old hold and enumerate recipient handles; an incorrect system both moves money and leaks restriction state.

### 4. Data model

All timestamps are UTC instants. `money_minor` values are signed `decimal(38,0)` internally and checked to fit signed 64-bit API amounts. Identifiers are opaque 128-bit values. USD is stored explicitly even though it is the only enabled currency.

**Identity and authority**

- `users(user_id PK, status enum, created_at timestamp)`.
- `devices(device_id PK, user_id FK, public_key bytes, status enum, last_seen_at timestamp)`.
- `wallets(wallet_id PK, owner_kind enum[user,business], owner_id, currency char(3), status enum[active,outgoing_frozen,closed], created_at, debit_version int64)`.
- `wallet_grants(grant_id PK, wallet_id FK, user_id FK, permissions set, per_operation_limit_minor, valid_from, valid_until nullable, revoked_at nullable)`. Grants are time-bounded; historical rows remain.
- `payment_methods(method_id PK, user_id FK, provider, encrypted_provider_token, brand, last4, expiry_month, expiry_year, status)`. Raw PAN and CVC are forbidden.

**Operations and policies**

- `operations(operation_id PK, kind enum, actor_user_id, actor_grant_id nullable, source_wallet_id nullable, destination_wallet_id nullable, currency, principal_minor, fee_minor, client_operation_id, request_hash, device_id nullable, parent_operation_id nullable, business_state, external_state nullable, policy_version, created_at, accepted_at nullable, completed_at nullable, version)`, unique on `(actor_user_id, client_operation_id)`. Material request fields become immutable after acceptance.
- `operation_events(operation_id FK, event_no, state, reason_code, actor_id, server_time, metadata_digest, PK(operation_id,event_no))`. This is append-only and preserves every transition.
- `fee_rules(rule_version PK, operation_kind, fixed_minor, basis_points, valid_from, valid_until)`. Rules are versioned and time-bounded.

**Ledger and scalable balances**

- `ledger_accounts(account_id PK, owner_kind, owner_id, purpose enum[wallet_available,wallet_held,wallet_debt,fee_revenue,processor_clearing,payout_payable,dispute_loss,suspense], currency, segment_count, negative_floor_minor, status)`, unique on `(owner_kind,owner_id,purpose,currency)`.
- `journal_entries(entry_id PK, partition_no, commit_seq, operation_id FK, effect_key, kind, effective_at, recorded_at, reverses_entry_id nullable, adjustment_to_close_id nullable)`, unique on `effect_key` and on `(partition_no,commit_seq)`. Entries are append-only.
- `postings(entry_id FK, line_no, account_id FK, delta_minor, currency, PK(entry_id,line_no))`. Postings are append-only, and each entry’s deltas sum to zero.
- `balance_segments(account_id FK, segment_no, balance_minor, version, PK(account_id,segment_no))`. These are derived but updated atomically with postings; ordinary accounts use one segment, hot merchant accounts use 64, and universal fee or clearing accounts use 256.
- `wallet_obligations(obligation_id PK, wallet_id FK, source_kind, source_id, original_minor, collected_minor, status)`. Outstanding value is derived as `original_minor − collected_minor` and reconciles to the wallet-debt account.

A credit to a segmented account selects `hash(effect_key) mod segment_count` and need not lock the recipient’s debit fence. A debit locks the wallet’s debit fence and reads or locks all source segments in stable order. Thus hot incoming traffic fans out, while competing uses of shared spendable funds remain serialized. Any storage implementation lacking serializable validation must lock all source segments explicitly.

**Payment lifecycle**

- `holds(hold_id PK, operation_id FK, payer_wallet_id FK, merchant_wallet_id FK, authorized_minor, captured_minor, released_minor, status, fee_rule_version FK, expires_at, version)`. The counters are transactional caches derivable from capture and release effects.
- `captures(capture_id PK, operation_id FK, hold_id FK, ordinal, gross_minor, fee_minor, final_flag, journal_entry_id FK, created_at)`, unique on `(hold_id,ordinal)`.
- `capture_relief(capture_id PK/FK, final_relief_minor, pending_refund_minor, open_dispute_minor, version)`. These cached counters coordinate all relief paths.
- `refunds(refund_id PK, operation_id FK, capture_id FK, amount_minor, reason_code, status, journal_entry_id nullable, created_at)`.
- `disputes(dispute_id PK, operation_id FK, capture_id FK, claimant_wallet_id FK, amount_minor, status, outcome nullable, opened_at, evidence_due_at, decided_at nullable, decided_by nullable, approved_by nullable, version)`.
- `dispute_evidence(evidence_id PK, dispute_id FK, owner_user_id, encrypted_blob_ref, audience enum, created_at)`. Evidence is separately encrypted and access-controlled.
- `fundings(funding_id PK, operation_id FK, wallet_id FK, payment_method_id FK, amount_minor, processor_object_id nullable, status)`.
- `payouts(payout_id PK, operation_id FK, business_wallet_id FK, verified_destination_ref, net_minor, fee_minor, held_minor, processor_object_id nullable, status)`.

`available`, `held`, and `debt` come from their ledger accounts. `pending` comes from accepted operations without a terminal monetary effect and is not itself money.

**External effects**

- `external_actions(action_id PK, provider, effect_kind enum[card_charge,business_payout], operation_id FK, idempotency_key, request_hash, encrypted_request, status enum[ready,leased,sent_unknown,succeeded,failed_final,manual_review], lease_owner nullable, lease_until nullable, attempt_count, next_attempt_at, processor_object_id nullable)`, unique on `(provider,effect_kind,operation_id)`.
- `processor_objects(processor_object_id PK, provider, provider_object_id, merchant_reference, kind, amount_minor, currency, authoritative_state, last_provider_time)`, unique on `(provider,provider_object_id)`.
- `processor_events(provider, provider_event_id, body_hash, verified, received_at, provider_time, processed_at nullable, result_ref nullable, PK(provider,provider_event_id))`. Events are append-only.
- `reconciliation_exceptions(exception_id PK, provider, normalized_key, kind, expected_minor, observed_minor, status, created_at, resolved_at nullable, resolution_entry_id nullable)`, unique on `(provider,normalized_key,kind)`.

Relay retains operation and idempotency identities for seven years. Its processor contract must provide same-key deduplication for at least seven days and lookup by Relay’s merchant reference for at least 180 days; an unresolved action beyond those guarantees enters manual review rather than receiving a blind retry.

**Daily close**

- `close_runs(close_id PK, book_date date, timezone, version, state enum[sealing,running,verifying,published,failed], previous_close_id, started_at, published_at nullable, algorithm_version, digest)`, unique on `(book_date,version)`. The timezone is `UTC`.
- `close_watermarks(close_id FK, partition_no, start_seq, end_seq, PK(close_id,partition_no))`.
- `close_chunks(close_id FK, partition_no, chunk_no, from_seq, to_seq, posting_count, debit_total, credit_total, digest, state, PK(close_id,partition_no,chunk_no))`.
- `account_closes(close_id FK, account_id FK, opening_minor, movement_minor, closing_minor, digest, PK(close_id,account_id))`.

There are 128 journal partitions. `commit_seq` is a storage-issued commit-order token with the guarantee that no transaction can later become visible below a sealed partition watermark. A close watermark is therefore a consistent 128-element vector rather than a contended global sequence.

### 5. The hard operations

1. **Read wallet, balances, and statement.**  
   **Inputs:** wallet ID, optional statement cursor and snapshot watermark. **Authorization:** the caller must have a currently active `view` grant. **Steps:** take a consistent snapshot; sum the wallet’s available, held, and debt segments; derive pending amounts from visible operations; page postings bounded by the snapshot watermark; return balances, activity, and a new cursor. **Atomic:** the returned balance components and snapshot cursor must describe one state. **Eventually consistent:** notifications, search, processor-settlement labels, and analytics. **Optimistic UI:** none; an offline cache is labeled with its last cursor and sync time. **Conflict:** later commits appear only after the returned cursor and cannot alter a page already bounded by its watermark.

2. **Synchronize two devices’ offline queues.**  
   **Inputs:** authenticated device, last cursor, and an ordered batch of `{client_operation_id, request_hash, command, dependency}`. **Authorization:** recheck device status, login, wallet grant, limits, freezes, and destination eligibility for every command. **Steps:** process each command independently through its normal operation in that device’s local order; return stored results for duplicate IDs; reject hash mismatches; then return authoritative events after the old cursor. Commands from different devices are ordered only by server commit. **Atomic:** each money command, not the batch. **Eventually consistent:** cross-device push delivery. **Optimistic UI:** `queued—not sent`, then `submitting`, then the server state. **Conflict:** in the two-device `$100`/two-`$80` case, at most one commits and the other receives a durable insufficient-funds rejection visible on both devices.

3. **Authorize a purchase hold.**  
   **Inputs:** operation ID, payer wallet, merchant wallet, amount from `$0.01` through `$10,000`, expiry from 15 minutes through seven days. **Authorization:** the payer needs an active `spend` grant; the merchant must accept credits. **Steps:** deduplicate; lock the payer debit fence; read all available segments; validate currency, limits, status, and funds; save the current fee-rule version; post `payer available −amount` and `payer held +amount`; insert the hold and event. **Atomic:** sufficiency check, postings, hold, operation receipt, and returned balances. **Eventually consistent:** merchant notification and analytics. **Optimistic UI:** only “authorizing”; offline creation is not a hold. **Conflict:** concurrent debits serialize; affordable commands commit in server order and the rest fail without moving money.

4. **Capture a hold.**  
   **Inputs:** capture ID, hold ID, positive amount, and `final` flag. **Authorization:** a currently authorized operator of the hold’s designated business needs `capture` permission. **Steps:** lock the hold; reject expiry or excess capture; calculate `fee_this = fee(captured_before + amount) − fee(captured_before)` using the saved rule; route merchant net first toward merchant debt and then available funds; post `payer held −amount`, `fee revenue +fee_this`, `merchant debt +recovery`, and `merchant available +(amount−fee_this−recovery)`; if final, post the remaining held amount back to payer available; insert capture and update hold counters. **Atomic:** all postings, counters, capture, debt recovery, and operation outcome. **Eventually consistent:** receipts and notifications. **Optimistic UI:** “capture processing,” not received funds. **Conflict:** capture, final capture, and expiry lock the same hold; a loser re-reads and is rejected rather than silently receiving a smaller capture.

5. **Release or expire a hold.**  
   **Inputs:** hold ID, deterministic release ID, reason, and optional requested amount. **Authorization:** the merchant may void; the system may expire at `expires_at`; the payer cannot unilaterally revoke still-valid merchant consent. **Steps:** scan due holds in indexed batches of 1,000; for each, lock the hold; calculate `authorized−captured−released`; post that remainder from held to available; mark the hold released or expired. **Atomic:** each hold’s calculation, posting, state, and effect key. **Eventually consistent:** scheduling and notices. **Optimistic UI:** no restored availability before commit. **Conflict:** a capture that locked first consumes its amount; an expiry that locked first makes subsequent capture invalid, and duplicate expiry work replays harmlessly.

6. **Post a peer transfer.**  
   **Inputs:** operation ID, sender wallet, stable recipient wallet or handle, amount up to `$2,000`, and memo. **Authorization:** the sender needs current `spend` authority; recipient lookup returns only a confirmation name and generic eligibility. **Steps:** resolve the recipient; deduplicate; lock the sender debit fence; validate funds and daily limit; route recipient credit first to any debt; post sender available `−amount`, recipient debt `+recovery`, and recipient available `+(amount−recovery)`; store both activity records. **Atomic:** debit, credit, recovery, operation, and balances. **Eventually consistent:** push notification and memo search. **Optimistic UI:** “sending” online or “queued—not sent” offline. **Conflict:** sender debits serialize, while credits to a hot recipient select independent balance segments; a refusal never reveals the recipient’s balance or restriction reason.

7. **Fund a wallet through the card processor.**  
   **Inputs:** operation ID, wallet, owned processor token, and `$1`–`$5,000`. **Authorization:** the wallet owner must pass current funding and risk policy. **Steps:** atomically create `funding` and `external_action` with stable key and frozen payload; a worker leases it and calls the processor outside the transaction; every retry uses the same key; a verified response, webhook, or lookup updates the processor object; definitive success posts `wallet available +amount` and `processor clearing −amount` under one unique effect key; definitive failure posts nothing; timeout becomes `sent_unknown`. **Atomic:** intent plus action before the call, and processor evidence plus one local credit afterward; the network call cannot be atomic with either. **Eventually consistent:** dispatch, external settlement, and notices. **Optimistic UI:** pending funding is not spendable. **Conflict:** response, webhook, and polling may race, but unique processor-object and journal-effect keys converge on one credit.

8. **Refund a captured purchase.**  
   **Inputs:** refund ID, capture ID, amount, and reason. **Authorization:** a current merchant operator with `refund` permission, or assigned support, may refund only to the original payer. **Steps:** lock `capture_relief`; reject an amount above `captured−final_relief−pending_refund`; if a dispute is open, require resolution through that dispute; lock the merchant debit fence and require sufficient available funds; post merchant available `−amount` and payer available `+amount`; increment final relief; leave the original fee untouched. **Atomic:** capacity check, source sufficiency, postings, refund, and relief counters. **Eventually consistent:** notifications and formatted receipts. **Optimistic UI:** no refund before commit. **Conflict:** concurrent refunds serialize on relief and merchant funds; later requests fail with the exact remaining refundable amount shown only to the merchant.

9. **Open a dispute.**  
   **Inputs:** capture ID, amount, reason category, and optional encrypted evidence references. **Authorization:** only the original payer may dispute within 60 days of capture. **Steps:** lock capture relief; verify unrelieved principal; require at most one open dispute per capture; allocate `open_dispute_minor`; create the case with a 14-day evidence deadline; store evidence separately. **Atomic:** eligibility, allocation, and case creation. **Eventually consistent:** uploads, redaction, and notifications. **Optimistic UI:** “under review”; no provisional balance. **Conflict:** a refund that commits first reduces disputable value, while a dispute that commits first requires any merchant concession to use dispute resolution.

10. **Resolve a dispute.**  
    **Inputs:** dispute ID, outcome `user_win`, `merchant_win`, or `split`, awarded amount, evidence digest, and reason. **Authorization:** an assigned adjudicator may decide; awards above `$1,000` require a different second approver. **Steps:** lock dispute, capture relief, and merchant debit fence; validate award and remaining relief; for a user award, debit merchant available as far as possible, debit the merchant-debt account for the shortfall, and credit user available for the full award; for merchant win, post nothing; update relief and close the case. **Atomic:** approval, all postings, obligation, restrictions, relief, and outcome. **Eventually consistent:** evidence archival and notices. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Conflict:** duplicate decisions replay; a concurrent merchant debit may leave a larger debt but cannot reduce an already approved user award.

11. **Request and complete a business payout.**  
    **Inputs:** payout ID, business wallet, preverified processor destination, desired net amount, and expected `$1` fee. **Authorization:** a current `payout` grant is required, and a newly changed destination has a 24-hour cooling period. **Steps:** lock the business debit fence; reserve `net+$1` by posting available to held; atomically create the external action; dispatch with one stable key; on success post `held −(net+$1)`, `payout payable +net`, and `fee revenue +$1`; on definitive failure release the hold; on unknown leave it held. **Atomic:** reservation plus action, and later each terminal posting plus outcome. **Eventually consistent:** processor execution and bank arrival. **Optimistic UI:** pending payout with reserved funds. **Conflict:** payouts, refunds, and ordinary spending serialize on business availability; duplicate processor results cannot duplicate payout or fee.

12. **Apply a processor chargeback or reversal.**  
    **Inputs:** verified processor event, funding object, amount, and final outcome. **Authorization:** only authenticated processor evidence or reconciliation may invoke it. **Steps:** deduplicate the event and economic effect; lock the wallet; on a lost chargeback, remove available funds up to the amount, post the shortfall to wallet debt, create an obligation, and freeze outgoing operations; on later reversal, cancel the outstanding obligation and restore previously removed or collected value through a compensating entry. **Atomic:** event processing, postings, obligation counters, freeze, and operation state. **Eventually consistent:** appeal documents and notification. **Optimistic UI:** none. **Conflict:** out-of-order or contradictory terminal events are quarantined; duplicate events replay the prior result.

13. **Close the books for a day.**  
    **Inputs:** UTC book date, close version, and scheduler identity. **Authorization:** finance scheduler or a dual-controlled finance operator. **Steps:** at 00:05 UTC create one close; capture a consistent start/end watermark for all 128 partitions; process fixed ranges in resumable chunks; validate every entry balances; aggregate account movement; calculate closing from the prior close; verify the platform trial balance is zero and active-hold totals match held accounts; atomically publish account closes and a digest. **Atomic:** sealing the watermark, each deterministic chunk, and final verified publication; the whole scan need not be one transaction. **Eventually consistent:** parallel aggregation and archival. **Optimistic UI:** finance sees `closing` until verification. **Conflict:** foreground writes fall wholly before or after the watermark; duplicate workers converge by chunk key, and late events enter the next close as adjustments.

14. **Reconcile processor activity and settlement.**  
    **Inputs:** processor account, durable range cursor, API objects, settlement file rows, and unresolved actions. **Authorization:** trusted reconciliation workers; manual resolution requires a finance reason and second approval for monetary corrections. **Steps:** lease a bounded range; normalize records; match by provider object, Relay reference, effect kind, amount, and currency; send matches through the same funding, payout, or chargeback result path; create one exception for missing, duplicate, mismatched, or orphan records; advance the cursor only after durable processing. **Atomic:** each match or exception and its cursor checkpoint, not the entire file. **Eventually consistent:** scanning and human review. **Optimistic UI:** unresolved user operations remain pending. **Conflict:** leases and unique normalized keys make reprocessing safe; ambiguous matches stop for review rather than guessing.

### 6. Scale and cost

The target successful deployment has 5 million registered users, 2 million funded consumer wallets, 300,000 business wallets, and 500,000 daily active users.

It processes 10 million financial operations and 40–80 million postings per day. The expected sustained peak is 2,000 commands per second, with 8,000 per second retry or webhook bursts. A product launch may send 2,000 captures per second to one merchant for ten minutes, while fee and processor-clearing accounts participate in much of all traffic. Tests also submit 200 simultaneous debits against one wallet.

About 15% of operations involve the processor. Its assumed quota is 300 requests per second with a 1,000-request burst, and 0.05% of calls—roughly 750 per day—have an indeterminate result. Webhooks may be duplicated ten times, arrive before the synchronous response, or arrive 48 hours late.

Five million holds may expire daily, including 500,000 at a shared event boundary. Daily close must process 80 million postings within 45 minutes while adding less than 10% to foreground p99 latency. Uncontended internal transfers target p99 below 500 ms; a saturated source wallet may return retryable backpressure after two seconds.

The expensive work is:

- Serializing many debits from one wallet, which cannot be horizontally removed without preallocating spend rights.
- Summing or locking 64–256 balance segments when a hot account finally spends.
- Scanning and aggregating tens of millions of daily postings.
- Expiring large time buckets of holds while captures continue.
- Rebuilding long statements and seven-year audit histories.
- Reconciling processor objects, events, fees, settlement files, and bank totals.
- Retaining and indexing an eventual 100-billion-plus posting archive.

Uniformly distributed load would conceal the dominant costs; performance tests must use skewed recipient traffic and the actual universal control accounts.

| Requirement | Reason ordinary row operations struggle | Honest concession | Boundary where the concession is a cheat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic, balance-safe debits alongside high-fan-in credits | Locking both mutable wallet rows convoys on hot recipients; independent shards allow write skew | Use a measured central debit sequencer, explicit per-wallet backpressure, or delayed spendability for incoming credits | Claiming hot-account support from uniform traffic, or permitting temporarily negative authoritative balances |
| Effective-once external charges and payouts | No local transaction can include a remote processor call, and timeouts do not reveal whether it committed | Require processor idempotency plus lookup, expose `pending/unknown`, and escalate unresolved effects manually | Calling a queue “exactly once,” using a processor mock without ambiguous outcomes, or retrying under a new key |
| A nonblocking, deterministic close boundary | Wall-clock queries over live partitions omit or duplicate concurrent writes | Use a storage commit watermark or a brief partition-admission barrier, then perform bulk aggregation asynchronously | Freezing writes for the entire close or letting reruns select a different input population |
| Immutable accounting with versioned policy and late facts | Ordinary CRUD encourages edits to captures, balances, and historical reports | Support one currency, append compensating entries, and allow explicitly versioned restatements | Repairing discrepancies by changing or deleting old postings |
| Money synchronization across offline devices | Last-write-wins and common merge strategies cannot reconcile competing claims on shared funds | Treat offline actions as drafts, as Relay does; true offline payment requires capped, disjoint, server-preallocated device allowances | Displaying offline work as paid, trusting device timestamps, or letting two devices spend the same allowance |

A backend may choose a single ledger sequencer instead of segmented accounts, but it must publish and meet a finite hot-key ceiling. It may choose genuine offline spending only by subtracting device allocations from online availability before disconnection; calling an ordinary queued mutation “offline payment” would evade the requirement.

### 8. Confidence

The append-only balanced journal, atomic sufficiency checks, hold/capture bounds, compensating refunds, idempotent outbox/inbox protocol, explicit unknown external state, server-authoritative offline resolution, and watermarked daily close are standard payment-system practices with high confidence.

The exact fee schedule, limits, 60-day dispute window, no provisional spendable credit, UTC cutoff, two-second hot-wallet backpressure, 128 journal partitions, and 64/256 balance segments were chosen for this exercise. They are coherent product decisions, not industry requirements, and need load, accounting, risk, and processor-contract validation.

The least proven part is the segmented hot-account design and its performance at the stated skew. The impossibility boundaries are stronger than that particular design: shared funds require coordinated debit authority; a remote side effect requires processor-supported identity or authoritative lookup; and two uncoordinated offline devices cannot both promise final use of the same money.

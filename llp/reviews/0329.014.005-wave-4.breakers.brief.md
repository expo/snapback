# Brainstorm — application ideas that might BREAK the bounded-data-programs design

Read `llp/0329.014.004-bounded-data-programs-v1-design.rfc.md` (the design, v1.1) and
the one-paragraph summaries of the three waves in
`llp/0329.014.003-paper-archetypes-wave-3.research.md` §Wave-3 verdict. Twenty-nine
applications have been written in the language on paper and none needed a second
query class: messaging (Clique, coterie), booking, document workspace, sealed auction,
expense ledger, payments/exchange, semantic notes (vector/FTS), professional graph,
regulated health records, turn-based hidden-info game, collaborative editor, BI,
realtime 30 Hz game, Twitch-scale chat, dating, events with roles, real-estate
listings, eBay auction, prediction market, daily fantasy, brokerage, photo library,
calendar, Uber, DoorDash, fast-casual ordering, tournaments, Notion-style user
databases.

Your task, in ≤ 1,500 words: propose **8–12 application ideas that would most
plausibly BREAK the design** — not stretch a dimension it already has, but defeat a
*premise*: one server authority; deterministic programs over traced bounded reads;
rules the server can evaluate; a static certificate as the gate; the trace as the
offline closure; a TypeScript twin as the client; schedules/jobs/effects as the only
non-data-plane kinds; SQLite-first single-writer; a store-wide offline fence.

For each idea: (1) the app in one sentence; (2) the premise it attacks; (3) the
specific mechanism you predict fails (which dimension, kind, rule, or runtime
contract) and what "broken" would look like — a second query class, a guarantee that
cannot hold, an axis the schema cannot state, a scale regime where the certificate is
the wrong instrument; (4) why the twenty-nine did not already cover it; (5) the
cheapest falsification (one paper operation, or one measurement). Rank by probability
of actually breaking the design rather than ending at a declared boundary kind, and
say for each whether the honest outcome is more likely "new dimension", "new kind",
"product boundary", or "not this system". Be adversarial and concrete; no
repository modifications; your final message is the deliverable.

The likeliest fractures are authority placement, not missing relational operators. Wave 3 itself names multi-region, federation, and long-lived processes as untested; those dominate this ranking.

1. **Offline bearer cash — very high; not this system.**  
   **App:** Phones transfer anonymous digital cash device-to-device for days without connectivity. **Attack:** one server authority and the store-wide offline fence. **Failure:** the same coin can be spent to two isolated recipients; the last server trace is not closure over peer transactions, so uniqueness cannot hold while both recipients accept. Payments/exchange assumed online server settlement; queued prediction is not offline finality. **Falsify:** spend one coin concurrently to two disconnected devices and require both immediate acceptance and eventual no-double-spend.

2. **Disconnected industrial control mesh — very high; not this system.**  
   **App:** Mines, ships, or disaster teams coordinate safety interlocks and inventory peer-to-peer through multi-day outages. **Attack:** server authority, server-evaluable rules, the offline fence, and effects as the physical-world seam. **Failure:** after the fence expires, refusing a sensor-driven action violates safety/availability; accepting it makes a device authoritative over untraced physical state. The twenty-nine had offline UX and external inputs, never offline-critical actuation or peer settlement. **Falsify:** disconnect beyond `maxOfflineAge`, then demand a 10 ms sensor-to-actuator interlock plus later convergence.

3. **Server-blind encrypted legal workspace — high; not this system.**  
   **App:** A law firm searches and collaborates on end-to-end-encrypted evidence while enforcing content-derived privilege and legal-hold rules. **Attack:** rules the server can evaluate and trace-as-closure. **Failure:** a keyless server cannot authorize from plaintext, enforce plaintext uniqueness, build semantic indexes, or trace content dependencies; trusting the client destroys authority. The workspace, health, and semantic-note cases assumed server-visible facts; `seal` is a seam, not encrypted relational execution. **Falsify:** express “search documents mentioning X, but only where plaintext privilege tags permit this viewer” with the server holding no decryption key.

4. **Federated atomic escrow — high; not this system.**  
   **App:** A trade atomically swaps cash at one bank for title held by an independently governed registry. **Attack:** one authority and effects/outbox as the only external seam. **Failure:** no Snapback transaction, snapshot, or trace spans both authorities; `sent_unknown` and compensation cannot guarantee delivery-versus-payment atomicity. Brokerage and payments used one authoritative ledger and treated outsiders as effects. **Falsify:** crash after the bank irrevocably debits but before the registry transfers title, with neither participant accepting rollback or a trusted coordinator.

5. **Always-available multi-region inventory — high; new dimension.**  
   **App:** Emergency suppliers must accept reservations in every region during partitions while never overselling globally scarce stock. **Attack:** SQLite-first single-writer and one authoritative serialization point. **Failure:** two isolated regions can each sell the final unit; Postgres OCC does not solve partition availability. Booking tested centralized contention, not multiple writable authorities. **Falsify:** one unit, two partitioned regions, concurrent reservations; require both regional availability and global `sum ≤ 1`. The possible repair—preallocated escrow quotas—is a new **partitioned-authority** dimension and weakens the stated app.

6. **Private-device/shared-cloud personal agent — medium-high; new dimension.**  
   **App:** An assistant joins shared work records with local messages, location, calendar, and embeddings that may never leave the device. **Attack:** one authority, server-evaluable rules, trace closure, and the client as merely a twin. **Failure:** no authority owns a complete snapshot; a shared mutation derived from local facts either trusts a forgeable client assertion, uploads forbidden data, or creates a client-only query class. Photos, calendar, and semantic search used server-known data or declared substitutes, not authoritative joins against device-private relations. **Falsify:** authorize assignment only if an unuploaded calendar has a free slot, including offline replay.

7. **Interactive differential-privacy research lab — medium-high; new dimension.**  
   **App:** Researchers issue adaptive statistics over sensitive records under a formal cumulative privacy budget. **Attack:** static certificate as the gate and row-rule/disclosure policy. **Failure:** privacy is a hyperproperty of query history: two individually safe, authorized outputs can jointly reveal one person. A per-execution trace and cadence/min-group declaration cannot certify adaptive composition. BI exposed fixed declared aggregates, not adversarial query sequences. **Falsify:** admit counts with and without a target predicate; if both pass but differ by one, the policy failed. The repair is a stateful information-release-budget dimension.

8. **User-programmable durable automation — medium; new kind.**  
   **App:** A Zapier/Temporal-like product lets tenants define cyclic workflows, waits, retries, dynamic fan-out, and arbitrary connectors. **Attack:** static certificates, named programs, and schedules/jobs/effects as an exhaustive kind set. **Failure:** the executable graph is mutable data; individually bounded nodes can form an unbounded cascade whose reads, effects, and lifetime no single certificate bounds. Notion’s interpreter was pure, capped, and database-local. **Falsify:** install two tenant rules where A writes B and B writes A, with retrying effects, then derive a finite amplification certificate. This wants a durable-workflow kind with graph-level admission.

9. **Programmable spreadsheet with volatile formulas — medium; new kind.**  
   **App:** A collaborative Excel replacement supports `INDIRECT`, iterative circular formulas, external feeds, and user-defined functions offline. **Attack:** deterministic bounded programs, static read shape, trace closure, and TS-twin conformance. **Failure:** formulas stored as data select new ranges dynamically and may iterate until convergence; worst-case certification becomes “all cells/unbounded steps,” while external functions cannot replay identically. Notion capped its AST, views, and steps and excluded this open-ended behavior. **Falsify:** certify an `INDIRECT`-selected range inside an iterative cycle without whole-workbook retention or a fixed iteration cap.

10. **Massive temporal-event correlator — medium-low; product boundary.**  
    **App:** An IoT/security platform ingests millions of events per second and evaluates millions of mutable standing temporal patterns. **Attack:** SQLite single-writer and per-program certificates as sufficient admission control. **Failure:** every rule may be bounded while aggregate work—event rate × matching rules × window state—is unstable; the certificate is the wrong instrument for system capacity. Twitch tested outbound fan-out and BI exported history, not inbound firehose joins. **Falsify:** measure sustained 1M events/s against 100k patterns with bounded p99 and backlog. This honestly belongs in a streaming/dataflow product.

11. **Exact live route planner — medium-low; new kind.**  
    **App:** A transit planner returns provably shortest routes over a changing continental graph. **Attack:** bounded traced reads and static certificates. **Failure:** exact shortest-path proof requires data-dependent exploration with no application-independent `maxVisited`; a cap either refuses valid graphs or silently makes exactness false. Uber exercised nearest-driver geo/knn, not graph optimality. **Falsify:** use a graph family where Dijkstra must visit arbitrarily many nodes before proving the destination optimal. The likely result is a graph-solver read-site kind, effectively a third specialized query class.

## Verdict: SOUND WITH MAJOR ISSUES

The direction is right: one serial stream per store with pre-execution outside the lock, in-place members as the fallback and reference, serial in-batch validation, and triple qualification (replay oracle, overlay differential, validation adversary), all grounded in honest S0 measurements. But the core correctness argument does not hold as written. Validation completeness is asserted "by construction" over a boundary that misses real outcome-influencing reads and writes; non-store inputs escape the model entirely; and batch-failure recovery assumes per-path receipts the parent RFC explicitly defers. All five findings below are repairable inside this direction, but F1–F2 break the "serializable in revision order" claim (§3.5) and must be closed before K2/K3.

## Fundamental and major findings

### F1. Validation completeness "by construction" fails on the real read/write surface — FUNDAMENTAL — read-sets RFC §2, §3, §6.1; committer RFC §3.4, §3.5, §4.2

§2 claims recording at `Rows`/`PartitionRows`/family operations "is complete by construction for those calls," and §6.1 qualifies it with a census of *trait methods*. But outcome-influencing traffic that never crosses those traits is invisible to both the read sets and the census:

- **Receipts.** `_ops` reads and writes are raw `conn()` SQL, not `Rows` calls ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:503), [store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:742), [store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:747), [store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:1301)). Neither §3 table has a receipt entry. Two concurrent submissions of the same op id can both pre-execute seeing "no receipt," both pass validation (nothing to intersect), and both commit — violating "receipts recorded once" (committer §3.5). The RFC never says where the receipt check runs in the new pipeline (admission? snapshot S? serially at batch admission?). Each position has different soundness consequences and none is specified.
- **Internal members' framing.** Job chunks, which the committer lists as pre-executable Mutations (§3.2), frame execution in direct `_jobs`/`_meta` SQL outside any `Runner` ([job.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/job.rs:250), [job.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/job.rs:298), [job.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/job.rs:440)). Effect claim/settle paths read and conditionally update `_outbox`/`_effect_presence` in direct SQL ([effects.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/effects.rs:218), [effects.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/effects.rs:256)), as does timeline work ([timeline/work.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/timeline/work.rs:246)). A `Rows` overlay cannot intercept `tx.conn()` statements, so these members cannot pre-execute as designed — yet §4.1 eligibility excludes only administrative ops, taint, budget, and unqualified families, not "speaks raw SQL."
- **Two snapshots, one S.** The session digest "resolves in the admission transaction" ([mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:180)), separate from the execution snapshot S (§4.2). Admission-anchored authority reads are therefore older than S, but §4 validates every entry against `(S, current]`. A revocation landing between admission and S is missed. (Exact admission call-site UNVERIFIED; the two-transaction structure is on the RFCs' own account plus the cited comment and the double replay check at [mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:920) and [mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:937).)
- **Trait surface wider than the table.** `Rows` has ~30 methods, many with adapter-dependent defaults that can steer execution: `holds_assets`, `time_audience_published`, `change_footprint`, `provisional_*`, `retained_*`, `acquired_boundary` ([rows.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/rows.rs:209)), with `scan_selected`/`scan_metered` as distinct methods from `scan` ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:1163), [store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:1173)). §3 maps none of the capability/defaulted reads. "Coarsening is always safe" does not help reads nobody records.

Recommended direction: replace the trait-method census with a census of *every outcome-influencing read/write site*, direct SQL included; default all engine-internal members to in-place until each is decomposed (serial framing at commit + pre-executable program) onto the parent's `StoreTx` — making that extraction an explicit predecessor of K3/K4; give read-set entries individual snapshot lower bounds (or re-resolve admission inputs inside S); and specify the authoritative receipt-check position (serial at batch admission is the natural one).

### F2. Presence, entropy, and server-minted ids are outside the model — MAJOR — read-sets RFC §2; committer RFC §3.5, §4.2; overlay RFC §6

§2 fixes only `now`, schema generation, and principal as non-store inputs. Three more flow into outcomes:

- **Presence.** `connected[p]` reads the in-process `Live` registry via `Context.presence` ([mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:965)), branches execution with predictions ([runner.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:249), [runner.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:538)), and gates effect delivery ([effects.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/effects.rs:266)). Presence changes never commit, so they can never appear in a footprint — validation *cannot* catch a presence flip between snapshot and commit, and the committed outcome equals no serial execution. The RFCs never mention presence, although the schema statically knows which operations read it ([serve.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1198)).
- **Entropy and minter.** `context.entropy` is copied in and evolved ([mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:968), [mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:1017)); `ids::mint` draws from `getrandom` ([ids.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/ids.rs:18)). §3.5's recorded admission inputs (operation, arguments, principal, `now`, placeholder resolutions) omit all three, so the replay oracle cannot reproduce byte-for-byte outcomes for any program touching them — the oracle will either false-fire or, more likely, the generator will quietly avoid these programs and the oracle will cover less than claimed.

Recommended direction: treat all three exactly like `now` — freeze at admission/snapshot as explicit inputs (overlay answers `connected[p]` from the frozen map; ids pre-drawn at admission), record them for replay, and mark operations with presence sites (the [serve.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1198) list) as needing frozen presence or in-place execution. Uniqueness of minted ids across concurrent pre-executions via the shared `Arc<Mutex<…>>` minter should be stated, not left implicit.

### F3. "Same answers ⇒ same charges" is false where charging is physical — MAJOR — overlay RFC §2, §4, §5; read-sets RFC §6

Overlay §2 forbids the overlay from charging ("must not evaluate expressions, run rules, charge the meter") and then asserts charges follow answers automatically. But substantial charging lives inside the `Rows` implementations: "a store must charge before decoding/cloning" ([rows.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/rows.rs:319)); `scan_metered` runs a byte-level word-heap preflight with page-size guards and an activation-head fast path ([store.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:1173)). An in-memory merge over decoded `Value`s does not execute any of that. This is soundness-relevant, not cosmetic: mutations run `Cut::Abort` ([mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:1000)), so a charge skew flips success↔hard-error at a site validation cannot see; and reported `metered` is part of the outcome ([mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:1010)), so any skew breaks §6.4's "same outcome as the reference" and replay byte-equality. The adversary's "scan cut by the meter" case tests conflict detection, not charge equality.

Recommended direction: promote charge replication to an explicit overlay obligation — share the adapters' preflight/charge code, compute from canonical bytes for buffered rows — extend the §5 family rollout to cover charge schedules, and add a fail-safe rule: metered scans over shadowed keys fall back to in-place until their schedule is replicated. Keep the planned differential at every forced cut position and gate K2 on it.

### F4. Placeholders in keys defeat the taint rule through store-side ordering — MAJOR — committer RFC §4.1, §5

§5 permits placeholders in keys and claims resolution "before validation" suffices because "footprints are always concrete." That handles footprints but not scan fidelity: the provisional encoding is unspecified, and nothing requires the map provisional→resolved to preserve interleaving with concrete keys. A scan whose `[lo, hi']` bound was recorded in provisional order can return a different row sequence after resolution — a divergence no validation step sees, since the member's own resolution is not a conflict. The taint rule (§4.1; parent §6.3 item 5) covers only program-observed flows (branch, comparison, string op, read-back, row round-trip); index ordering is done by the store, invisibly to the program. (Outbox `ORDER BY seq, id` delivery is safe — ids `effect-<seq>-<n>` ([runner.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:686)) resolve monotonically within a member — but generic secondary indexes over placeholder-bearing columns are not.)

Recommended direction: `check`, which has the schema, must reject from pre-execution any program where a placeholder can reach an indexed component, unless the provisional encoding is proven order-preserving under resolution; add placeholder-keyed concurrent emitters to the promised qualification. Soundness of the static classifier for Quarry's dynamic features is currently assumed — state the covered language subset (UNVERIFIED against `check`'s implementation, which I did not locate).

### F5. Batch-failure recovery spends receipts the parent has not established — MAJOR (sequencing) — committer RFC §3.4, §9; parent §5.2

§3.4: "Each member's receipt makes that retry exactly-once (parent RFC §5.2)." But parent §5.2 says the per-path retry table "is a P1a deliverable, not an assertion," and names the ambiguous-COMMIT window (seconds under sync replication, W9) that makes batch retry the dangerous case: a batch whose COMMIT raced termination, then "rerun each member alone," double-applies every member kind without a recorded key. Sweeps, steps, asset views, time-audience pins, activation rebuild, the Following launch, and the smoke witness (§2.2/§3.2) show no idempotency keys anywhere in the three RFCs. K0 routes *all* of §2.2 through the stream with batch reruns now, while the keys arrive in P1a later.

Recommended direction: make P1a's per-path table (or an explicit per-member-kind key inventory) a predecessor of K0's batching; until then, either keep single-member transactions for unkeyed kinds or restrict batches to keyed members.

## What the documents get right that should not change

- One stream per store with in-place members as fallback plus the conflict budget and decaying per-key score (§4.3): hot keys degrade to the reference instead of retry-storming, and administration keeps a path. Do not let later revisions "simplify" to pure OCC.
- Serial validation *within* the batch, folding accepted members into the maps (read-sets §4; parent §6.3 item 3): this closes the two-readers-one-counter hole. Keep it exactly.
- The internal revision covering non-head writes (parent §6.3 item 4, preserved here): revocation-after-snapshot is the classic miss, and the design names it.
- Gating K2–K5 on S1a's execution-stage sizing while landing K0/K1 regardless (§9): correct sequencing, given S0 measured only the commit-stage upper bound (15,744/s at N=64, 0.5 ms) and left execution unmeasured — the README's own caveat.
- The triple qualification (replay oracle + differential + adversary) and the honesty about what S0 did not measure (per-member statements, 20 µs flush, proxy overhead). Fix the inputs the oracle records (F2) rather than weakening byte-equality.

## Small notes (optional, at most five)

1. The 15,744/s headline assumes all-pre-executed batches; Direct operations (mints, claims, steps, settles) each cost 1–2 round trips *inside* the serial section (§3.2, open question §11.1). For worker-heavy workloads the binding limit is the mixed-batch rate, not the bound — extend one S1a cell to mixed batches before citing the headline for capacity.
2. Admission-fixed `now` plus "rerun if below the committed pin" (§6) risks rerun churn when pins advance fast relative to queue waits; and window-too-old reruns (§4.4, default 2 s) should explicitly count toward the §4.3 conflict budget, or slow programs livelock between re-execution and the window instead of falling back to in-place.
3. Per-member post-commit fan-out for batches is undescribed: `committed` Condvar, change answers, long waits, and what enqueues the next timeline-delivery member ([serve.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:100), [serve.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1314), [serve.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1426)). State the per-member wakeup hook in revision order.
4. No disposition for ephemeral operations (`mutate_ephemeral` bypasses the durable path at [mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:926); `Layered` journals undo for ephemeral rows): whether ephemeral ops enter the stream, which lock they take once `FairMutex<Engine>` is gone, and how the overlay composes with `Layered` — UNVERIFIED beyond the cited sites, but the RFCs should say.
5. Postgres-specific charge and snapshot details (PG `Rows` charge schedules, `REPEATABLE READ` revision visibility for step 3 of §4.2) are UNVERIFIED — I confirmed only the shared shape (`Runner::new(&tx, …)` at [postgres/mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/mod.rs:295), receipt parity at [postgres/mod.rs](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/mod.rs:224)). F1–F3 apply to both adapters only insofar as both funnel through `Rows`; per-adapter verification belongs in K2/K4 qualification.

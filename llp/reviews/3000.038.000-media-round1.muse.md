MATERIAL issues:

1. Upload route underspecified. §3 defines `POST /assets?intent=<id>` but never states how it selects media vs `as=file` vs audio. Parent requires `as=file`; this RFC adds audio sniff types with no verifier, bound, or allow-list definition. Admission cannot check the column bound without this.
2. Bounds untestable. §2 requires individual and total unresolved byte bounds but gives no numbers, no pre/post-hex accounting, and no IndexedDB transaction-size analysis. Proof obligations cannot pass without them.
3. Shared id conflates domains. One id names upload intent and mutation execution, but changed-input comparison scope is ambiguous when non-asset placing args differ, and collision with ordinary mutation ids plus `E_ASSET_INTENT_REUSE` vs `E_WRITE_ID_REUSE` precedence is undefined.
4. No refusal taxonomy. `E_ASSET_INTENT_REUSE`, `E_ASSET_INTENT_EXPIRED`, `E_ASSET_TYPE/TOO_LARGE`, quota, viewer/store mismatch are never mapped to `blocked` retryable vs manual vs terminal `failed`, so `retryUpload`/`discardUpload` semantics and discard-vs-dispatch races are unenforceable.
5. Head-of-line blocking unstated. A held upload dependency at FIFO head blocks unrelated mutations; the RFC neither acknowledges nor justifies this.
6. Adapter bypass. Allowing Exact to use raw `admit_upload`/`upload_ready` with its own transport recreates a second outbox and could send placement via the online tier around `begin_send` fencing. One surface or Exact-side proof is required.
7. Missing durability edges. No Web-Locks-absent refusal, no `uploading` crash-reset rule, no terminal-eviction rule for `uploads()` vs write-history authority, no orphan-transfer atomicity for large byte values, and no guide fence preventing offline use of immediate `upload`.

## MATERIAL findings

1. **The baseline overstates recovery as an operable capability, and no phase clearly closes the gap.** `src/instance_fork.rs` contains and tests `recover(parent, destination)`, but the only caller is its unit corpus; `snapback fork create/list/diff/drop/reap` and startup do not invoke it, and there is no `fork recover` or registry-scanning reconciliation command. A crash can therefore leave an intent/staging state that ordinary list/reap paths cannot repair. The publication protocol is fail-closed and crash-safe against unsafe boot, but product-accessible local-evidence recovery is not implemented. Phase 1 promises crash/partial-publication recovery guidance and Phase 2 assumes interrupted runs leave recoverable forks, yet neither explicitly adds the missing recovery surface. The Plan must distinguish the tested recovery primitive from product recovery and assign automatic reconciliation or an exact repair/reap command before those exits can pass.

2. **The operative phase order contradicts the Plan's own stated order and lets broad consumers precede their safety/resource gates.** The Summary says to close missing isolation and diff evidence before integrating fork-per-worktree, suite, and agent workflows. The actual critical path puts Phase 2 integration before Phases 3 and 4. Phase 2 then claims parallel consumers cannot trample credentials or cleanup ownership and deploys fork-per-agent automation before Phase 3 completes the credential matrix, parent-after-fork isolation, lifecycle races, fork-count/byte/diff-work limits, and exhaustion behavior. It also captures diff receipts before Phase 4 makes them trustworthy. The existing F0 corpus is sufficient for constrained non-production dogfood, but the Plan never limits Phase 2 that way. Either split Phase 2 into controlled dogfood versus general agent/suite integration, with the latter gated by Phase 3 and review-artifact use gated by Phase 4, or reorder the phases.

3. **Phase 4 cannot meet its "exact diff" exit because it omits three prerequisites already owned by LLP 0164/0165.** LLP 0164 requires an immutable retained base or first-write preimages for an exact net diff; the Plan names neither production substrate. The current derived-fork receipt is an attributed post-image sequence, not an exact net base-to-head diff. LLP 0165 also requires the generated writer-authority/SQLite-authorizer choke point for journal completeness; oracle fixtures alone do not prove that all declared writes enter the journal. Finally, the current receipt refuses after retention advances past the fork point, but the Plan does not choose a finite history lease, immutable-base retention, or another budgeted availability rule. Phase 3's exhaustion tests do not supply that contract. Phase 4 must explicitly consume the LLP 0164 substrate choice, the LLP 0165 completeness gate, and a finite retention/base policy—or narrow its claim to enumerated-fixture inventory rather than exact diff.

4. **Publication is not gated on the governing documents and acceptance evidence that currently prohibit publication.** Phase 0 considers only whether LLP 0158 should move from Draft to Review. LLP 0158's same-`appId` derived-deployment amendment is explicitly effective only on its acceptance, so Phase 6 cannot publish that identity model while LLP 0158 remains Draft or merely Review. LLP 0164 and LLP 0165 remain Draft, and the named acceptance-tail ticket requires their real review/acceptance plus every applicable LLP 0162 row—including row 13—to pass before publication. Phase 3 covers only rows 5–8, Phase 4 incompletely covers row 9/row 4, and Phase 6's timing measurements do not require row 13's paired baseline, author-ratified bounds, or applicable correctness gates. Open question 6 cannot remain unresolved at the publication gate. Phase 6 needs explicit owner-controlled status and row-passage prerequisites.

5. **Phase 6 does not identify a valid publication authority for this kind of local operating capability.** LLP 0113 governs a closed `TargetCapabilityId` qualification/claim-family mechanism centered on qualified target capabilities; it is not, by inference, the generic publication mechanism for a source/package CLI workflow or a Guide capability-index row. LLP 0134 explicitly says local/package publication requires a source-owned carrier in LLP 0110, the governing LLP, or another focused design, and a new hosted family requires a dated LLP 0113 amendment. The Plan does not decide whether `operating.instance-forks` becomes a runtime `TargetCapabilityId`, remains an LLP 0061 Guide capability, or gains a new source/package attestation. Consequently "define … under LLP 0113" is neither implementable nor authority-safe as written. Phase 0 must select and record the exact publication class and owner; Phase 6 must consume that mechanism rather than invent it implicitly.

6. **Phase 5 reopens an LLP 0158 asset decision and has an internally inconsistent exit.** LLP 0158 OQ5 already decides the local posture: refusal now, fork-private same-cut copying as the additive local path, and a fork-aware shared store with GC barriers in F2. Phase 5 instead reopens a local choice between private copying and an "immutable/content-addressed sharing model," even though LLP 0158 records that current physical asset keys are opaque rather than content-addressed and assigns shared-store architecture to F2/LLP 0044 work. Its exit then requires "same-cut private custody," which does not describe the sharing branch it just allowed. Keep Phase 5 to private-tree implementation or continued refusal; move shared custody to Phase 8 under an LLP 0158/0044 amendment. If the earlier decision is to be reconsidered, that owner-controlled amendment must precede the Plan's choice.

7. **Phase 8 assigns general fork and containment research to an authority LLP 0099 does not hold.** LLP 0099 is a container-VM migration-executor profile binding LLP 0081 migration semantics to LLP 0072's adapter. It expressly adds no new semantics, and its provider snapshots are supplemental to verified streaming migration backup. It does not own general instance-fork volume materialization or the `ProcessContained` threat tier. Those remain LLP 0158 F2 semantics, with deployment/adapter work under LLP 0072 or a new focused hosted-fork profile. Phase 8 may reuse LLP 0099 evidence and infrastructure, but "assess … under LLP 0099" misdraws the authority boundary and would let a migration profile acquire fork semantics by implication.

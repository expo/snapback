## Problems the revision introduced or still has

**MAJOR 1. The always-on keyed preflight charges the common first-party path one flight for properties a conditional read would give at no cost on the miss path, and the RFC does not argue the trade.** §2 says first-party clients send operation ids, so the keyed first attempt is the common mutation, and §4 prices it at 9 or 10 under (a) and 12 under (b) against the headline 8 and 11. The extra flight is the preflight snapshot, opened on every keyed request before the queue. The same contracts hold without it on the miss path: put the fenced re-check ahead of the in-memory program lookup and decode inside the durable transaction (it already sits in the setup flight, and decode is in-memory, so this costs nothing), let a metadata-bearing refusal open the snapshot on demand as §3.1 already does for unkeyed requests, and on a fence failure for a keyed request read the receipt once unfenced before mapping to retryable. That gives keyed first attempt 8 under (a), replay hit 4 instead of 2, and ownership-loss replay two extra flights on a rare path. There is a real argument for the revision's choice: it keeps replay bursts off the writer's serial section entirely, and it mirrors K3's S-time read plus serial re-check, so in-place keyed execution is structurally the same as pre-execution. But the RFC states neither the cost nor that rationale, and §1 still presents 8 and 11 as "unkeyed mutations" without saying the common path pays more. Either adopt the conditional form or add the rationale and put the keyed figure beside the headline.

No other fundamental or major problem. The in-place ordering remains sound: the fenced re-check is a later command than the head lock under both mechanisms, the receipt INSERT and bind UPDATE at `engine/mod.rs:1082–1083` precede COMMIT under that lock, and the preflight's authority facts, receipt and head come from one snapshot.

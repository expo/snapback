# Ward — zero-trust clinical records portal with live external oracles

Archetype: zero-trust access control. Axis under test: authorization whose inputs are not rows — freshness, oracles at commit, and the gap between "checked" and "committed".

### 1. Product contract

**What it is.** Ward is the clinical records portal of a six-hospital network (14,000 staff). It is the only way staff read or write a patient chart. Every read and every write is allowed only if, *at the instant the data moves*, four external authorities agree and the local policy holds:

| Oracle | Attests | Scope of the fact |
|---|---|---|
| IdP (identity provider) | session still valid; MFA level and `auth_time` | one session (sessions are device-bound) |
| DPS (device posture service) | device compliant (encryption, patch level, MDM enrolled, screen lock) | one device |
| RE (risk engine) | score 0–100 for *this request*, model version, feature hash | one request |
| MDM location heartbeat | lat/lng/accuracy or network-zone, every 60 s | one device |
| Scheduling system | who covers which service/unit, with shift start and end | one principal, an interval |

Local, row-based inputs: roles (interval-bounded), care relationships (interval-bounded), patient restricted-category flags, geofence and time-of-day policy tables, and a revocation ledger fed by IdP push, DPS non-compliance events, and SecOps.

**Who uses it.** Clinicians (attending, resident, nurse, pharmacist); HIM clerks (records); privacy officers (review break-glass and disclosures); SecOps (oracles, policy, forced states, forensic audit); the system (sync, sweeper, reconciler), which is a principal with its own decision rows.

**Operation classes.** Every request is one of: `view.summary` (demographics, allergies, active problems), `view.chart` (full chart), `view.restricted` (psych / HIV / substance / VIP / employee-patient entries), `write.note`, `write.order` (medication and lab orders; controlled substances are a stricter sub-case), `write.admin` (roles, policy, forced oracle states), `export` (print, download, bulk). Freshness budgets and risk thresholds are per class and published:

| class | session age ≤ | MFA age ≤ | posture age ≤ | risk age ≤ | location age ≤ | risk step-up ≥ | risk deny ≥ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| view.summary | 300 s | 12 h | 4 h | 120 s | 600 s | 70 | 85 |
| view.chart | 60 s | 8 h | 1 h | 60 s (open) / 300 s (renew) | 300 s | 50 | 85 |
| view.restricted | 10 s | 15 min | 15 min | 10 s | 60 s | 30 | 85 |
| write.note | 30 s | 4 h | 1 h | 30 s | 300 s | 50 | 85 |
| write.order | 10 s | 15 min | 15 min | 10 s | 60 s | 30 (bound step-up if controlled) | 85 |
| write.admin | 5 s | 5 min | 5 min | 5 s | 60 s | 20, always bound step-up | 85 |
| export | 5 s | 5 min | 5 min | 5 s | 60 s | 20, always bound step-up | 85 |

**Promises.**

1. Every read of patient data and every write is a *decision* with a durable record of exactly which attestations were used, how old each was at commit, which policy version applied, and which oracles were degraded — recorded in the same transaction as the read lease or the write.
2. Freshness is measured against the commit instant, not the instant you clicked. A "yes" that is too old by the time the write lands is a "no".
3. Revoke a session, a device, or a person anywhere, and within 5 s every open view on it closes and no further write commits. The audit shows, for every access, whether it fell in the window between the revocation's effective time and the moment Ward learned of it.
4. Step-up is asked only when needed and returns you to what you were doing. For controlled-substance orders, admin changes, and exports, the approval screen shows exactly what will be committed, and that approval is good for that one commit only.
5. An open chart is live only while its lease is renewed. You are warned 30 s before it would close, and when it closes you see why, in coarse terms ("re-authenticate", "device no longer compliant", "coverage ended").
6. Break-glass is always available to a clinician with a valid session and a compliant device: one step-up, one reason, four hours, one patient, always reviewed by a privacy officer within 72 h, never silent.
7. When an authority is unreachable, you can still do what the published degraded policy allows; you see a "degraded" indicator; those decisions are re-checked when the authority returns.
8. A refusal shows a code from a published table and a reference number — never the score, the threshold, or whether a restricted record exists. Probing for existence is possible only through break-glass, which is audited and reviewed.
9. Counts are honest: "my patients" is what you may see; the ward census a charge nurse sees is the true census.
10. Each device has its own session, MFA freshness, posture, and location. Stepping up on your phone does not freshen your laptop.
11. Offline: a device keeps what it was already shown until the lease it was shown under expires (the client wipes it; the server knows the exposure window). Nothing can be written offline. Drafts are kept on the device and committed under a fresh decision when online; authored and committed times are both recorded.
12. Two people never overwrite each other. Chart entries are append-only with amendments; amending an entry that has since been amended is refused and the latest is shown.
13. The audit is itself protected: privacy officers see who, what, when, why, and outcome; SecOps additionally see device, location, score, and features; patients receive an accounting of disclosures through a privacy officer.
14. SecOps can force any oracle to "down" and force "deny" for any (oracle, class) pair instantly; doing so is itself an audited admin decision.
15. Policy changes are versioned, take effect at a stated instant, and never re-label a past decision.
16. Delegated coverage is interval-bounded, cannot outlive the delegator's own authority, and is visible to both parties.
17. Nothing about anyone's authority is a boolean. It is an interval, and "did they have it at instant T" is answerable for any T in the six-year retention window — including "as Ward knew it at instant T2".

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: **[T]** time, **[S]** secrecy, **[P]** other people's data. There is no money in this product; the cost analogue is oracle calls.

1. No patient data leaves the server without a decision row committed in the same transaction as the lease that authorizes it, and no patient-data write commits without a decision row in the same transaction. **[P][T]**
2. For every attestation a decision uses, `observed_at + budget(class, kind) > committed_at`. Ages are computed against the commit instant. **[T]**
3. A decision is denied if any revocation targeting its session, device, or principal has `effective_at ≤ committed_at` and had been received before the commit. **[T]**
4. A revocation received *after* a decision committed, whose `effective_at` is *before* that commit, never alters the decision row. It appends a reconciliation row marking the decision `contradicted` and opens a review task. **[T][P]**
5. A live view delivers no update at instant t unless an unclosed lease exists with `granted_at ≤ t < expires_at` and invariant 3 holds at t. **[T][P]**
6. A lease's `expires_at` is the minimum over its attestations of `observed_at + budget`; for `view.restricted` it never exceeds 15 min from grant. **[T]**
7. MFA freshness, posture, and location are facts about one device. A session is bound to one device. No attestation is ever read across devices. **[S]**
8. A bound step-up approval authorizes exactly one commit, whose content hash equals the hash shown at approval, and is consumed atomically with that commit. **[T][S]**
9. Break-glass never bypasses session validity, device posture, the risk hard-deny, or step-up at grant. It bypasses only care relationship, geofence, and time-of-day. Every delivery under it is individually recorded. **[P]**
10. Every break-glass grant has an open review task until a privacy officer closes it with an outcome. **[P]**
11. A delegation's interval is contained in the delegator's own authority interval as it stood when the delegation was made; a later shortening of the delegator's authority shortens the delegation. **[T]**
12. A denial is recorded durably even though the refused operation is not performed. **[P]**
13. A user-facing refusal carries only a published code and a reference; score, threshold, features, and the existence of a restricted or absent record are never in any response to the subject, and the deny path does the same work regardless of which reason applied. **[S]**
14. Every degraded decision names the degraded oracles and the degraded-policy row used, and is later marked `confirmed`, `contradicted`, or `unreconcilable`. **[T]**
15. Oracle health transitions are hysteretic and recorded; a forced state records who forced it and when. **[T]**
16. Policy rows are versioned with validity intervals; a decision records its version; no later version re-labels it. **[T]**
17. Roles, relationships, delegations, and grants are intervals. The question "has role R" is never evaluated without an instant. **[T]**
18. Attestation payloads (device identity, location, score, features) are visible to SecOps only; privacy officers see decisions without them. **[S][P]**
19. A restricted-category entry is never delivered under a `view.chart` lease. **[P]**
20. The server never accepts a write whose decision was not made at its own commit; an offline draft commits under a new decision. **[T]**
21. Delivery after lease expiry is a detectable, recorded event, not a silent one. **[P]**

### 3. The traps, ranked

**T1 — "Checked" is a past instant; "committed" is a future one (temporal + concurrency).** *Wrong version:* the handler calls four oracles, receives four yeses, writes the row, logs `session_valid: true`. *Why wrong:* each yes is a fact about the instant the oracle answered (T1…T4); the write lands at T5. Between them a revocation can land, a budget can expire, a policy version can roll over, and the handler's `now()` — taken at entry — is not the commit instant. A backend that retries a conflicted transaction without re-evaluating makes it worse: the retry reuses attestations gathered before the *first* attempt. *Observation:* SecOps revokes at 14:03:07.200; Ward receives it at 07.210; the log shows a `write.note` at 07.450 marked "session valid". Both statements are "true" in the naive system. *Correct:* gather attestations outside the transaction; inside it, compute each age against the commit instant, check the revocation ledger, and check the policy version — then commit either the write plus an `allow` decision or a `deny` decision alone. The same trap in another costume: bound step-up. The user approves "morphine 4 mg"; the client retries with "morphine 40 mg"; the naive backend, seeing fresh MFA, commits. The commit must verify the approved content hash against the committed content and consume the approval.

**T2 — Revocation is bitemporal (temporal).** *Wrong version:* when the IdP push arrives, set `session.valid = false`; the audit reads the current row. *Why wrong:* the IdP revoked at 07.200; the push arrived at 09.100. Accesses committed in `[07.200, 09.100)` were authorized under a session the authority had already revoked — Ward simply did not know yet. "Was this access authorized" now has two answers: *as decided* (yes, on the evidence Ward had) and *as known now* (no). A single boolean can express neither, and the security team's first question after an incident is "what did we do after the IdP revoked". *Observation:* a note committed at 08.000 is "allowed, session valid" forever; no query can find it. *Correct:* revocations carry `effective_at` (the authority's clock) and `received_at` (Ward's); decisions are never edited; reconciliation rows are appended; the audit supports both "as-of T" and "as-known-at T2". This is the archetype's active-versus-historical membership: a session is an interval whose end may be learned after it has passed.

**T3 — A live view is a stream of disclosures, each needing its own authorization (concurrency + temporal).** *Wrong version:* authorize once at subscribe; re-run the query whenever rows it reads change. *Why wrong:* the view's read set is the chart. Session validity, MFA age, posture, and location are not in it, so when they lapse nothing fires and updates keep flowing. Expiry is a *time* event with no write behind it, so a change-driven system never notices it. And renewal races: the lease expires at 12:00:00.000; an update computed at 11:59:59.950 is delivered at 12:00:00.100. *Observation:* revoke a device; its open chart keeps updating for minutes; nightly, `last_delivered_at > expires_at` rows exist. *Correct:* a lease row with an `expires_at`; every delivery checks the lease and the revocation ledger at delivery time; revocation closes leases and pushes a close; a sweeper closes expired leases within 5 s; restricted and break-glass leases record every delivery.

**T4 — An unreachable oracle is a third state with a policy, not a yes or a no (availability + temporal).** *Wrong version:* timeout → deny. Or timeout → allow. Or timeout → retry → use whatever cache exists, unmarked. *Why wrong:* deny turns a 20-minute IdP outage into a hospital-wide outage; allow turns it into a hospital-wide hole; the silent cache is the worst, because the decisions are indistinguishable from healthy ones. An oracle at 50 % failure produces a random mix with no recorded transition. *Observation:* after a 20-minute risk-engine outage, count the decisions that mention it. Naive answer: zero. *Correct:* per-(oracle, class) degraded policy with explicit cache ages and actions; hysteretic health state; SecOps forced states; a `degraded_oracles` marker on the decision; reconciliation when the oracle returns.

**T5 — Break-glass and delegation are authorities with intervals, not bypasses (authority).** *Wrong version:* `if break_glass: skip checks`; delegation copies the delegator's role onto the delegate; a grant "revokes" by flipping a flag. *Why wrong:* skip-everything lets a stolen session read anything with a reason string; a copied role outlives the shift it was copied from; a flipped flag leaves open leases streaming. *Observation:* the attending's coverage ends at 19:00; at 18:50 they delegate "until 07:00"; at 22:00 the resident reads under it and the naive system allows. Or: privacy officer revokes a grant; the chart stays open. *Correct:* break-glass is a stricter decision rule (step-up at grant, posture required, hard-deny respected, per-delivery audit, mandatory review); delegation is interval-contained; revoking any authority closes leases. The system's own jobs (sync, sweeper, reconciler) act under a named system principal with their own decision rows.

**T6 — Refusals, counts, and the audit leak; attestations have scopes (visibility + multi-device merge).** *Wrong version:* the error says why; "patient not found" differs from "restricted" in code or latency; "my patients" count includes restricted patients via a second code path; the audit table is readable by privacy officers with location columns; MFA freshness is a column on `principal`. *Why wrong:* a stolen session probes 50 MRNs and learns which are VIP; the score and threshold teach an attacker how to stay under it; a privacy officer learns where a nurse was at 03:00; a step-up on the phone freshens the laptop — the thing MFA proves ("the person holding *this* device") is not what was proven. *Observation:* differential timing between absent and restricted MRNs; a laptop write succeeding 2 s after a phone step-up. *Correct:* sealed codes; a uniform deny path that does the same oracle work for absent, restricted, and unrelated patients; existence learnable only via audited break-glass; "my" counts on the visible domain, census counts on the true domain under a declared permission; two audit projections; every attestation keyed by the scope its oracle actually attests. Multi-device here is not a merge of data — it is the refusal to merge attestations.

### 4. Data model

Types are neutral: `id` (opaque), `ts` (UTC instant, microsecond), `str`, `int`, `bool`, `json`, `set<enum>`. Append-only tables are marked **AO**; interval-bounded tables carry `valid_from`/`valid_to` (`valid_to` null = open).

**Principals, devices, sessions**

- `principal` — `id`, `employee_id str unique`, `display_name`, `status enum{active,suspended,terminated}`, `terminated_at ts?`, `home_facility_id`.
- `role_assignment` (interval, **AO** by closing `valid_to`) — `id`, `principal_id`, `role enum{attending,resident,nurse,pharmacist,him_clerk,privacy_officer,secops,admin,system}`, `facility_id`, `service_id?`, `valid_from`, `valid_to?`, `granted_by`, `decision_id`.
- `device` — `id`, `mdm_device_id unique`, `owner_principal_id`, `platform`, `enrolled_at`, `retired_at?`.
- `session` — `id`, `idp_session_id`, `principal_id`, `device_id` (one device), `issued_at`, `not_after` (from token), `terminated_at?`, `termination_reason enum{logout,revoked,expired,device_revoked,principal_revoked}`. Derived: `last_introspected_at` = max `observed_at` of session attestations.
- `revocation` (**AO**) — `id`, `kind enum{session,device,principal}`, `target_id`, `effective_at ts` (authority's clock), `received_at ts` (Ward's clock), `source enum{idp_push,dps_event,secops,hr_feed}`, `source_event_id`, `decision_id?`. The two timestamps are the bitemporal core.

**Attestations (AO, time-bounded)**

- `attestation` — `id`, `kind enum{session,mfa,posture,risk,location,schedule}`, `scope_kind enum{session,device,principal,request}`, `scope_id`, `observed_at ts` (oracle's clock), `received_at ts`, `hard_valid_until ts` (`observed_at` + the largest budget any class allows for this kind; per-class budgets are tighter), `payload json` — session: `{valid, idp_exp}`; mfa: `{level, method, bound_intent_id?, consumed_at?}`; posture: `{compliant, failures[]}`; risk: `{score, model_version, features_hash}`; location: `{lat, lng, accuracy_m, source enum{mdm,ip_zone}}`; `oracle_call_id`, `from_cache bool`, `superseded_by?`. Risk attestations have `scope_kind=request`, `scope_id=intent_id` or the decision's pre-allocated id.
- `oracle_call` (**AO**, 90-day retention) — `id`, `oracle`, `requested_at`, `responded_at?`, `status enum{ok,timeout,error}`, `latency_ms`, `request_id`.

**Patients and clinical data (protected)**

- `patient` — `id`, `mrn str unique`, `name`, `dob`, `facility_id`, `restricted_flags set<enum{psych,hiv,substance,vip,employee}>`, `deceased_at?`.
- `encounter` — `id`, `patient_id`, `facility_id`, `unit_id`, `admitted_at`, `discharged_at?`.
- `care_relationship` (interval, **AO** by closing) — `id`, `patient_id`, `principal_id`, `kind enum{attending,consulting,nursing,covering,delegated,breakglass}`, `valid_from`, `valid_to?`, `source enum{adt,schedule_sync,delegation,breakglass}`, `source_ref`. Derived from the ADT feed, schedule sync, delegations, and grants.
- `chart_entry` (**AO**) — `id`, `patient_id`, `encounter_id`, `kind enum{note,result,order,allergy,problem}`, `category enum{general,psych,hiv,substance}`, `body`, `authored_by`, `authored_at` (client), `committed_at` (server), `decision_id`, `amends_entry_id?`, `superseded_by?` (the only mutable field), `signed bool`.
- `order` — `id`, `patient_id`, `chart_entry_id`, `ordered_by`, `item`, `dose`, `controlled bool`, `status`, `intent_id`, `decision_id`, `committed_at`.

**Policy (versioned)**

- `op_class_policy` — `op_class`, `version int`, `valid_from`, `valid_to?`, `budget_session_s`, `budget_mfa_s`, `budget_posture_s`, `budget_risk_open_s`, `budget_risk_renew_s`, `budget_location_s`, `risk_stepup`, `risk_deny`, `bound_stepup enum{never,controlled_only,always}`, `breakglass_allowed bool`, `geofence_policy_id`, `tod_policy_id`, `decision_id`. Values in §1.
- `geofence_policy` — `id`, `zones json` (facility polygons + radius), `ip_zones json`, `unknown_location enum{allow_marked,deny}`.
- `tod_policy` — `id`, `windows json` (per role, per weekday, timezone).
- `degraded_policy` (versioned like `op_class_policy`) — `oracle`, `op_class`, `action enum{deny,use_cached,allow_marked,step_up}`, `cache_max_age_s?`. Initial matrix:

| oracle down | view.summary | view.chart | view.restricted | write.note | write.order | write.admin / export |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IdP | use_cached 1800 s (and token `not_after` unexpired) | use_cached 1800 s | deny | use_cached 600 s | deny | deny |
| DPS | use_cached 86400 s | use_cached 86400 s | deny | use_cached 14400 s | step_up | deny |
| RE | allow_marked | allow_marked | step_up | allow_marked | step_up | deny |
| location | allow_marked if `ip_zone` inside a facility | same | deny | same as chart | deny | deny |
| schedule (sync age > 2 h) | allow_marked on last roster | same | deny | same | deny | n/a |
| schedule (sync age > 24 h) | deny (break-glass only) | deny | deny | deny | deny | n/a |

New logins during an IdP outage: deny.

- `oracle_health` — `oracle`, `state enum{healthy,degraded,down}`, `since`, `last_success_at`, `consecutive_failures int`, `forced_state enum{none,down}`, `forced_by?`, `forced_at?`, `forced_decision_id?`. Derived from `oracle_call` windows.
- `oracle_health_event` (**AO**) — `oracle`, `from_state`, `to_state`, `at`, `cause enum{auto,forced,unforced}`, `decision_id?`.

**Decisions and audit (AO)**

- `decision` — `id`, `requested_at`, `committed_at` (the transaction's instant; set for denials too), `principal_id`, `session_id`, `device_id`, `op_class`, `effective_op_class` (after restricted escalation), `resource_kind enum{patient,entry,policy,principal,probe}`, `resource_id`, `probed_mrn_hash?`, `intent_id?`, `outcome enum{allow,deny,step_up_required}`, `deny_reason enum{no_session,revoked,session_stale,mfa_stale,posture_noncompliant,posture_stale,risk_deny,risk_stepup,geofence,tod,no_relationship,restricted,not_found,policy_changed,intent_mismatch,degraded_deny}` (internal), `user_code enum{DENY_AUTH,STEP_UP,DEGRADED,OK}`, `reference str`, `policy_version`, `degraded_policy_version?`, `attestations_used json[]` — each `{attestation_id, kind, scope_kind, observed_at, age_at_commit_ms, from_cache}`, `degraded_oracles set`, `breakglass_grant_id?`, `delegation_id?`, `lease_id?`, `actor enum{user,system_job}`, `job_name?`.
- `decision_reconciliation` (**AO**) — `decision_id`, `status enum{confirmed,contradicted,unreconcilable}`, `checked_at`, `oracle`, `evidence json`, `review_task_id?`. Derived view: a decision's current reconciliation status = latest row, else `pending` if `degraded_oracles` non-empty or a later revocation overlaps, else `n/a`.
- `view_lease` — `id`, `decision_id`, `session_id`, `device_id`, `principal_id`, `patient_id`, `op_class`, `granted_at`, `expires_at` (invariant 6), `renewed_from_lease_id?`, `closed_at?`, `close_reason enum{renewed,expired,revoked,renewal_denied,client_closed,grant_revoked,server_shutdown}?`, `deliveries int`, `last_delivered_at?`.
- `delivery` (**AO**; restricted and break-glass leases only) — `lease_id`, `seq int`, `delivered_at`, `entry_id`.
- `write_intent` — `id`, `principal_id`, `session_id`, `device_id`, `op_class`, `patient_id?`, `content json`, `content_hash`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (`created_at` + 600 s), `consumed_by_decision_id?`.
- `stepup_challenge` — `id`, `session_id`, `device_id`, `triggered_by_decision_id`, `bound_intent_id?`, `method enum{webauthn,push,totp}`, `nonce`, `issued_at`, `expires_at` (+120 s), `completed_at?`, `result enum{ok,failed,expired}?`, `attestation_id?`.
- `breakglass_grant` — `id`, `principal_id`, `patient_id`, `reason_code enum{emergency_treatment,patient_unable_to_consent,covering_unscheduled,disaster_mode}`, `reason_text str(≥20)`, `granted_at`, `expires_at` (+4 h), `stepup_attestation_id`, `decision_id`, `care_relationship_id`, `review_task_id`, `revoked_at?`, `revoked_by?`.
- `delegation` — `id`, `from_principal_id`, `to_principal_id`, `scope_kind enum{patient,service}`, `scope_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `reason`, `decision_id`, `care_relationship_ids json[]`, `revoked_at?`.
- `review_task` — `id`, `kind enum{breakglass,contradicted,delivery_after_expiry,degraded_batch,disclosure_request}`, `subject_id`, `assigned_role enum{privacy_officer,secops}`, `created_at`, `due_at`, `closed_at?`, `outcome enum{justified,unjustified,referred,no_action}?`, `closed_by?`, `decision_id?`.

**Audit projections.** `decision_for_privacy` = decision minus `attestations_used` payload detail (keeps kinds and ages) minus `probed_mrn_hash`; `decision_for_secops` = full row joined to attestations. Patients' accounting of disclosures = decisions with `outcome=allow` on their `patient_id`, showing principal name, role, facility, date, and break-glass flag.

### 5. The hard operations

Conventions: `now()` in pre-checks is the handler's clock; `T_COMMIT` is the transaction's instant, visible inside the transaction, monotone with commit order. Oracle calls have a 1,500 ms timeout and are never made inside a transaction. `budget(K)` reads the policy version active at the relevant instant.

**5.0 `authorize(op_class, resource, session, device, intent?)` — the core primitive every operation calls.**
Inputs: class, target (patient / entry / policy / MRN string), session id, device id, optional intent. Authorization: this *is* the authorization; the rules below are the whole of it.

Phase A (outside any transaction; may be stale by commit):
1. `t = now()`. Load `session`; if missing or `terminated_at` set or `not_after ≤ t` → `deny(no_session)`; still run steps 4–6 for timing uniformity, then go to Phase B with a deny.
2. Resolve target. If MRN-shaped and no `patient` row → mark `not_found`, continue with a phantom patient (no flags). Load `restricted_flags`; if any flag's category is present in the requested view and `op_class ∈ {view.chart}` → `effective_op_class = view.restricted`. Load policy version active at `t`.
3. Row inputs at instant `t`: `role_assignment` with `valid_from ≤ t < valid_to`; `care_relationship` for (principal, patient) active at `t` (any source), else an active `delegation` covering the patient's service, else an unrevoked `breakglass_grant` with `expires_at > t`. None → `no_relationship` (unless class is `write.admin`, which uses roles only).
4. For each kind K in {session, mfa, posture, location}: find the newest unsuperseded attestation for the correct scope (session → session; mfa → session; posture → device; location → device) with `observed_at ≥ t − budget(K)`. If none: call oracle K. On success insert `attestation` (`from_cache=false`). On timeout/error: consult `oracle_health` and `degraded_policy(K, effective_op_class)`: `deny` → record `degraded_deny`; `use_cached` → reuse the newest attestation if `observed_at ≥ t − cache_max_age_s`, marking `from_cache=true`, else `degraded_deny`; `allow_marked` → proceed with K in `degraded_oracles`; `step_up` → outcome becomes `step_up_required` unless an mfa attestation with `observed_at ≥ t − 60 s` exists for this session.
5. Risk: call RE with request features (class, patient flags, device, location zone, hour, recent-denial count). Score `≥ risk_deny` → `risk_deny`. `≥ risk_stepup` → `step_up_required` unless a generic mfa attestation for this session has `observed_at ≥ t − 60 s`. If policy `bound_stepup` applies (controlled order, admin, export) → require an mfa attestation with `scope_kind=request, scope_id=intent.id, consumed_at=null` regardless of score. RE down → per matrix.
6. Geofence: location attestation against `geofence_policy`; unknown → per policy. Time-of-day: role windows at `t`. MFA level: `payload.level ≥ 2` required for restricted/order/admin/export.
7. Compute `expires_at = min_K(observed_at_K + budget(K))` for the used attestations (renewal budget for risk if this is a renewal).

Phase B (one transaction; this is the TOCTOU guard):
8. Read `T_COMMIT`. Re-load the policy version active at `T_COMMIT`; if it differs from step 2's → `deny(policy_changed)`.
9. For every attestation used: assert `observed_at + budget(K) > T_COMMIT` (else `session_stale`/`mfa_stale`/`posture_stale`/…). Assert session `not_after > T_COMMIT` and `terminated_at` null.
10. Assert no `revocation` with `(kind, target_id) ∈ {(session, s), (device, d), (principal, p)}` and `effective_at ≤ T_COMMIT`. Rows present are, by construction, received.
11. Re-assert the row inputs of step 3 at `T_COMMIT` (relationship, role, grant, delegation intervals).
12. If an intent is bound: assert `intent.expires_at > T_COMMIT`, `intent.consumed_by_decision_id` null, `hash(content_to_commit) = intent.content_hash`, and the bound mfa attestation's `consumed_at` null; set both consumed.
13. Insert `decision` with `committed_at = T_COMMIT`, `attestations_used[].age_at_commit_ms = T_COMMIT − observed_at`, `degraded_oracles`, `policy_version`, `outcome`, `deny_reason`, `user_code`, `reference`. If `outcome = allow`: perform the caller's write or insert the `view_lease`. If deny: perform nothing else. **The deny decision commits.**
14. Return `{outcome, user_code, reference, lease?, challenge?}`; for `step_up_required`, insert a `stepup_challenge` (bound to the intent if any) in the same transaction.

Atomic: steps 8–14. Eventually consistent: the oracle facts themselves (that is the point). Optimistic UI: none; the client shows "checking" then the result. Concurrent conflict: two decisions on the same session serialize on the revocation read; a revocation committed between A and B is seen by B and not A — A's window is closed by 5.4.

**5.1 Establish session (spans IdP and DPS).** Inputs: IdP callback token, MDM device identity. Steps: verify token signature and audience; assert `device.owner_principal_id` = token subject and `retired_at` null; call IdP introspection → session attestation; insert mfa attestation from `auth_time`/`amr`; call DPS → posture attestation (down → per matrix; a non-compliant device gets a session that can only reach `view.summary` under `use_cached`); insert `session` bound to the device; decision row `op_class=session.establish` (class with budgets 0, i.e., must be live). Atomic: session + decision. Conflict: an existing unterminated session for the same (principal, device) is terminated with reason `logout` first.

**5.2 Open live chart.** Inputs: `patient_id`, `mode ∈ {summary, chart, restricted}`. Steps: `authorize(view.<mode>, patient, session, device)`; on allow the transaction inserted `view_lease{expires_at}`; subscribe the client to `chart_entry` where `patient_id` and `category` permitted by the lease's class; deliver the current chart as delivery seq 1. Delivery of each later change: (a) load lease; (b) assert `closed_at` null and `expires_at > t_deliver`; (c) assert no revocation for its session/device/principal with `effective_at ≤ t_deliver`; (d) restricted category entries only under `view.restricted`; (e) deliver; (f) update `deliveries`, `last_delivered_at`, and for restricted/break-glass leases insert `delivery`. Steps (a)–(f) run atomically per delivery. Optimistic UI: cached content with a staleness badge until the lease is confirmed. Conflict: a revocation and a delivery race — whichever commits first wins; the delivery-side check sees any revocation committed earlier; the leak detector (5.12) catches the residue.

**5.3 Renew lease.** Inputs: `lease_id`. Client calls 30 s before `expires_at`. Steps: `authorize(lease.op_class, patient, session, device)` with `renew=true` (risk budget 300 s, and the RE is re-called only if the previous risk attestation is older than that); on allow: new lease with `renewed_from_lease_id`, old lease `closed(renewed)`, subscription re-pointed — atomically; on deny or step-up: old lease is left to expire, client shows the coarse reason and, for step-up, the challenge. Conflict: renewal and sweeper race on the old lease; the sweeper only closes leases with `expires_at ≤ now` and `closed_at` null, so a renewed lease is untouched.

**5.4 Revocation ingest (spans IdP push / DPS event / SecOps).** Inputs: kind, target, `effective_at` from the source, source event id. Steps, one transaction: insert `revocation{received_at = T_COMMIT}` (idempotent on `source_event_id`); terminate matching sessions; close every open lease whose session/device/principal matches with `close_reason=revoked`; push "view closed" to those clients; then enqueue reconciliation for every decision with `outcome=allow` on that target with `committed_at ∈ [effective_at, T_COMMIT)` — each gets `decision_reconciliation{contradicted, evidence: revocation_id}` and a `review_task(contradicted, secops, due 24 h)`. The insert + terminate + close are atomic; the reconciliation rows may follow within 60 s. Conflict: a decision committing concurrently either sees the revocation (deny) or commits before it and is caught by the window scan.

**5.5 Write clinical note.** Inputs: patient, encounter, body, `authored_at` (client), `amends_entry_id?`. Steps: insert `write_intent` (content = body + refs); `authorize(write.note, patient, session, device, intent)`; inside Phase B on allow: if `amends_entry_id` is set and its `superseded_by` is non-null, this is a data conflict, not an authorization refusal — the transaction still commits the `allow` decision (authority was present; nothing was written) and returns `CONFLICT` with the latest entry id; otherwise insert `chart_entry{committed_at = T_COMMIT, decision_id}` and set the amended entry's `superseded_by`. Optimistic UI: the note appears in a "pending" style; never as committed. Conflict: two amendments of the same entry → second is refused. Two notes from one author on two devices → two entries (no merge; each under its own device's decision).

**5.6 Place controlled-substance order (bound step-up).** Inputs: patient, item, dose, route. Steps: insert `write_intent{content_hash}`; `authorize(write.order, …, intent)` → `step_up_required` with a challenge whose `bound_intent_id = intent.id` and whose nonce covers `content_hash`; client performs WebAuthn; 5.7 completes; client retries `authorize` with the same intent; Phase B step 12 verifies hash and consumes; insert `order` + `chart_entry{kind=order}`. Optimistic UI: the approval sheet shows the exact order; the order list shows "awaiting approval". Conflict: a second retry after consumption → `deny(intent_mismatch)`; an edited dose → hash mismatch → deny, new intent required.

**5.7 Complete step-up (spans IdP / WebAuthn).** Inputs: `challenge_id`, assertion. Steps: assert `expires_at > now`, `completed_at` null; verify assertion against the device's registered credential and the nonce; insert mfa attestation `{level: 2, method, bound_intent_id?}` with `scope = session` (generic) or `scope = request/intent` (bound); mark challenge `ok`; decision row `op_class=stepup.complete`. Atomic. A generic step-up freshens only this (session, device). Conflict: two completions of one challenge → the second fails on `completed_at`.

**5.8 Break-glass.** Inputs: `patient_id` or MRN, `reason_code`, `reason_text`. Authorization: role ∈ {attending, resident, nurse, pharmacist} active now; session valid; posture compliant (or DPS `use_cached`); risk < `risk_deny`; bound step-up on an intent whose content is `{patient, reason_code, reason_text}`. Steps: insert intent; `authorize(breakglass.grant, patient, …, intent)` — a class with restricted-tier budgets, `breakglass_allowed` irrelevant, relationship check skipped; on step-up, 5.7; retry; Phase B on allow: insert `breakglass_grant{expires_at = T_COMMIT + 4 h}`, `care_relationship{kind=breakglass, valid_from=T_COMMIT, valid_to=expires_at}`, `review_task{breakglass, privacy_officer, due +72 h}`, notification to the patient's current attending; if the MRN does not exist, the grant is still recorded and the subsequent open returns `DENY_AUTH` — existence is learned only here. Then the user opens the chart via 5.2, which finds the relationship; all deliveries under it are per-row recorded. `revoke_breakglass(grant)` by privacy officer or SecOps: set `revoked_at`, close the relationship (`valid_to = T_COMMIT`), close leases (`grant_revoked`), atomically. Not allowed for `write.admin` or `export`.

**5.9 Delegate coverage.** Inputs: delegate principal, scope (patient or service), `[from, to]` ≤ 24 h, reason. Authorization: delegator has an active relationship of kind attending/covering for the scope at `from`; delegate has an active clinical role at `from`. Steps: `authorize(write.admin-lite …)`; in Phase B compute `to' = min(to, delegator relationship valid_to, delegate role valid_to)`; insert `delegation{valid_to = to'}` and `care_relationship{kind=delegated, source=delegation}` rows; notify both. When a later event shortens the delegator's authority (5.10 closing a row), the sync also closes delegations that depended on it. Conflict: overlapping delegations to the same delegate are allowed (union); revocation of a delegation closes its relationships and leases.

**5.10 Schedule sync (bulk, periodic, spans the scheduling system).** Every 15 min under the `system` principal: pull the roster for the next 48 h; each roster line has `principal, unit/service, start, end`. Steps, one transaction per facility: for each line without a matching `care_relationship(source=schedule_sync, source_ref)` insert one with `valid_from=start, valid_to=end`; for each existing sync row whose line disappeared, close it at `max(T_COMMIT, start)`; for each row whose `end` changed, update `valid_to`; close delegations whose delegator row was closed; close leases held under a relationship that is now closed with `valid_to ≤ T_COMMIT`. Record `oracle_health(schedule).last_success_at`. Relationship checks between syncs use the row's own `valid_to`, so a shift that ended at 07:00 is closed at 07:00 even if the sync runs at 07:12; only *unscheduled* swaps are learned late, and those show up as `contradicted` when the diff closes a row with `valid_to` in the past (reconciliation rows for decisions in `[new valid_to, T_COMMIT)`).

**5.11 Reconcile degraded decisions and oracle health (background).** Health: every 10 s compute per oracle over the last 60 s of `oracle_call`: ≥ 3 consecutive failures or p50 latency > 2,000 ms → `degraded`; no success for 300 s or ≥ 30 consecutive failures → `down`; recovery requires 10 consecutive successes over ≥ 60 s; each transition is an `oracle_health_event`; `forced_state=down` overrides. Reconcile: on transition to `healthy`, for each decision with that oracle in `degraded_oracles` and no reconciliation row, ask the oracle's history endpoint about the instant `committed_at` (IdP: session state at T; DPS: posture at T; RE: not replayable → `unreconcilable`); insert `decision_reconciliation`; batch 500 per transaction; `contradicted` opens a `review_task(secops, due 24 h)`; if ≥ 1,000 decisions are unreconcilable in one outage, open one `degraded_batch` task instead of thousands.

**5.12 Lease sweeper and leak detector (scheduled).** Every 5 s: close leases with `expires_at ≤ now` and `closed_at` null (`expired`), push close to clients — 200 per transaction. Also expire `write_intent` and `stepup_challenge` rows past `expires_at`. Nightly: select leases with `last_delivered_at > expires_at` or `delivery.delivered_at > lease.expires_at` or deliveries after a revocation's `effective_at` on the same target; each opens `review_task(delivery_after_expiry, secops)`. This job exists because invariant 21 is not provable from code review.

**5.13 Audit read.** Inputs: filter (patient, principal, window, outcome, degraded, reconciliation status, break-glass). Authorization: privacy officer → `decision_for_privacy` projection for their facilities; SecOps → `decision_for_secops` network-wide; a patient's disclosure accounting → via a privacy officer's `review_task(disclosure_request)`. Every audit read is itself a decision (`op_class=audit.read`, restricted-tier budgets) — reading the audit is reading other people's data. Two required query shapes: *as-of* ("decisions on patient P in [a, b] with current reconciliation status") and *as-known-at* ("decisions on P in [a, b], considering only revocations and reconciliation rows with `received_at`/`checked_at ≤ T2`"). Eventually consistent: reads lag commits by up to 60 s on a replica; the UI shows the replica instant.

**5.14 Policy change (write.admin).** Inputs: new `op_class_policy` or `degraded_policy` row, `valid_from ≥ now + 60 s`, or a forced oracle state. Authorization: role `secops` or `admin`; always bound step-up on the intent whose content is the full new row; two distinct SecOps principals must approve (the second approval is a second bound step-up on the same intent within 10 min). Steps: insert the new version; set the prior version's `valid_to = valid_from`; forced state → `oracle_health.forced_state`, event row. In-flight leases keep their version until renewal; a shortened budget makes the next renewal compute a shorter `expires_at`. Never re-labels past decisions.

### 6. Scale and cost

- Principals 14,000; devices 22,000; peak concurrent sessions 6,000 (day shift); open leases at peak ≈ 4,000 (about two per active clinician); patients 2.1 M, encounters 9 M, chart entries 400 M (growing 60 M/yr); care relationships 3 M active, 40 M historical; role assignments 60 k.
- Decisions: peak 250/s (opens, renewals, writes, audit reads), average 80/s → 2.5 B/yr; retention 6 years → 15 B rows, partitioned by month, indexed on `(patient_id, committed_at)`, `(principal_id, committed_at)`, `(session_id)`, and `(committed_at)` for window scans. Attestations ≈ 1.2× decisions, 1-year retention. `oracle_call` 90 days.
- Oracle traffic at peak: IdP introspection ≈ 6,000 sessions / 60 s ≈ 100/s plus push; DPS ≈ 22,000 devices / 1 h ≈ 6/s; MDM location heartbeat 22,000 / 60 s ≈ 370/s ingested, not called; RE ≈ 250/s at open/write, ≈ 13/s for renewals under the 300 s renewal budget (with a 60 s renewal budget it would be 67/s — the renewal split exists for this reason).
- Fan-out is small: a chart change touches ≤ 10 leases; a device revocation closes ≤ 5 leases; a principal revocation ≤ 20. A *policy* change that shortens budgets causes a renewal wave bounded by lease count spread over the old budget.
- Expensive: (a) the window scan in 5.4 for a *principal* revocation after a long-lived session — `committed_at ∈ [effective_at, now)` can be hours if the IdP push was delayed; it is an index range scan on `(principal_id, committed_at)`, fine, but the review tasks it generates are the human cost; (b) reconciliation after a 20-minute RE outage: 250/s × 1,200 s = 300 k decisions, all `unreconcilable` → one batch task; after an IdP outage of the same length, 300 k history calls at ≈ 50/s take ~100 min; (c) the nightly leak detector over ~7 M leases/day — a join on `last_delivered_at > expires_at` with a partial index on open-then-closed leases; (d) six-year "as-known-at" audit queries — a join across decisions, revocations, and reconciliation with a `received_at ≤ T2` filter; keep `received_at` indexed; (e) restricted-lease `delivery` rows: ~2 % of leases, ~50 deliveries each → 7 M rows/day, cheap individually, kept 6 years.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **The commit instant must be a first-class value inside the transaction, and freshness is a predicate over it.** Invariant 2 compares `observed_at + budget` against the instant the write becomes visible, not the instant the handler started. Ordinary backends give the handler a clock at entry, or no stable transaction time at all, and retry conflicted transactions with the same entry-time inputs. *Concession:* use the handler's entry time plus a fixed skew allowance (say 500 ms) and record both, and forbid retry without re-running Phase B. *Cheat boundary:* when the allowance exceeds the smallest budget in play (5 s for admin/export is fine; a 10 s revocation-to-close promise is not), or when a retried transaction reuses attestations gathered before the first attempt.

2. **Authorization inputs that are not in the read set, and time events with no write behind them, must tear down live reads.** A live chart depends on attestations, revocations, and the passage of time, none of which its query reads. *Concession:* make the lease a row the view reads, have every revocation write touch the lease rows it affects, and run a sweeper that writes `closed_at` when `expires_at` passes — turning time and oracle facts into writes the reactive machinery can see. *Cheat boundary:* if a lapse merely *recomputes* the view and the recomputation still delivers, or if delivery does not itself check `expires_at > t_deliver`, the residue between sweeper ticks is delivered data under no authority. A sweeper latency above 5 s breaks promise 3; a delivery that trusts the lease as of subscription time is a leak, not a concession.

3. **Bitemporal audit.** "Was this authorized" must be answerable as-decided and as-known-now, and as-known-at any T2, from append-only rows plus late-arriving facts with two timestamps each. Ordinary backends have one timeline (current state) and an `updated_at`. *Concession:* keep `revocation.received_at` and `decision_reconciliation.checked_at` and answer as-known-at by filtering on them. *Cheat boundary:* mutating `decision.outcome` in place, or storing only `effective_at` — either destroys one of the two timelines and the incident question becomes unanswerable.

4. **Two-phase decisions: oracles outside, validation inside, with a policy-governed third state.** The oracle call cannot sit inside the transaction (1.5 s holds, timeouts aborting writes), and cannot be trusted from before it (T1). The backend must let an operation gather facts, then open a transaction that re-validates their ages and the revocation ledger and commits *either the write or the denial*. *Concession:* none that preserves the axis. *Cheat boundary:* "call the oracle in the mutation and retry on timeout" is the silent-fallback class — a decision that cannot say which oracle was degraded is indistinguishable from a healthy one, and the reconciliation queue is empty when it should hold 300 k rows.

5. **Denials are durable writes; refusals are sealed; the audit row has two projections.** The refused operation must not happen while the decision row must — the opposite of "throw to roll back". The user sees a code; SecOps sees payloads; privacy officers see neither payloads nor probed MRNs; and the deny path must cost the same for absent, restricted, and unrelated patients. *Concession:* write the denial in its own transaction before raising. *Cheat boundary:* if the denial write can fail and the operation still proceeds, or if an *allowed* write and its decision are in different transactions (a crash between them leaves an unaudited write or a phantom decision), or if the response for a missing MRN skips the oracle calls and returns 40 ms faster.

### 8. Confidence

*Standard practice:* continuous evaluation and per-request policy (NIST SP 800-207, BeyondCorp); OAuth token introspection with push revocation (CAEP / Shared Signals); device posture from an MDM; per-request risk scoring with step-up; WebAuthn with transaction binding (PSD2 dynamic linking, "what you see is what you sign"); break-glass with mandatory review and the HIPAA accounting of disclosures; bitemporal (valid-time / transaction-time) modeling; hysteretic health states. The trap set T1–T6 is the well-known failure catalogue of these systems, not an invention.

*Invented for the exercise:* the specific budgets, thresholds, and degraded matrix (plausible, uncalibrated); the lease as the unit of continuous authorization with a 300 s renewal budget for risk; the 60 s generic step-up grace; the four-hour break-glass with 72-hour review; the `decision_reconciliation` vocabulary; the nightly leak detector; existence learnable only via break-glass; the two-approver policy change.

*Uncertain:* whether real IdPs and posture services expose "state at instant T" history (many do not; reconciliation then degrades to comparing against the revocation feed, which this design already does in 5.4); whether a risk engine can ever be replayed (assumed not); whether 22,000 devices' minute-level location heartbeats are acceptable to the workforce (a real deployment might coarsen to network zone, which weakens the geofence to a facility-level fact and should be recorded as such in the attestation `source`).

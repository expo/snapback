Verdict: NOT READY — one High retention hole in the commit-boundary accounting §3 claims as closed, plus a Medium disclosure-adjacent gap carried by ticket.

1. High — `account_row_change` rebirth path drops revocations. [partition.rs](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-ccheever-projects-snapback/fdf7fb80-9b69-48f2-b659-1b99e033e6e3/scratchpad/review-r3/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:210) registers `born_rows` for *any* change with `old.is_none()`, including a re-insert of an id deleted earlier in the same commit; the born branch (:251) then takes precedence over the `touched_rows` boundary branch (:262), withdraws the delete's `del`, and logs only a `put` to the final set. Interleaving: task owned by Alice in team1; one commit deletes it and recreates the same id owned by Bob in team2 (Alice holds team1, not team2). Correct accounting per §3 is `put` to team2/`p:bob` plus `del` to team1/`p:alice`; the code emits only the `put`. Alice's (and team1's) devices keep a stale readable-looking row, and no revocation ever names it — falsifying the §4.5 "r deleted" row and the §3 claim that pass-through partitions receive "neither its image nor its id" (here the reverse: a boundary partition receives *neither*, when it is owed a `del`). Genuine births and born-then-deleted are handled correctly; only delete-then-reinsert with a changed partition set breaks. Fix: only insert `born_rows` when `touched_rows` lacks the key, so a rebirth falls through to boundary accounting (`put` to final, `del` to start−final). Reaching test: owner-or-team world op "delete task + recreate same id in other team, one commit", asserting the stream carries the `del` to old-partition holders and drained devices converge. This also makes the §10 disposition of round-1 GPT-2 ("adopted") incomplete: the transient-update case is fixed, the transient-rebirth case is not.

## Overall judgment

The core diagnosis in [1018.008](llp/1018.008-elegance-after-the-corrections.research.md) is right: Snapback 2 is elegant where one established fact is reused, and rough where the app must reconstruct it. The drafts are unusually honest about boundaries, evidence gates, and non-overlap with LLP1019/1020/1021.

But as a batch they are too much mechanism for a system near its 85,000 gzip-byte ceiling with 59 current links, a 1500-line cap, and three active landings. Each RFC says "no new gate/registry/loader," yet collectively they add: background recovery ownership, compiler slicing + certification + metadata, a new Quarry word + lineage + pricing, build-time module closure + identity, multi-page transport batching, and a new policy-identity domain. The cure in at least three cases is worse than the rough edge.

Adopt the discipline, not the full scope: do the docs fix, empty grants, admission-time recovery only, and measured dedup. Defer or shelve compiler-derived presentation, `collect`, full closure assembly, batched acquisition, and hash narrowing until post-1019/1020 baselines and real demand exist.

Definite bugs vs hypotheses: the only definite bugs established by the drafts are prose contradictions in orientation docs and two deliberate restrictions (`validateClosure` rejects imports; empty grants rejected). Grant exhaustion after 23 sends, broad invalidation conflicts, and cold-sync costs are observed behaviors, not bugs — the current contracts handle them conservatively correctly. Everything about payoff, dominant cost, slicing soundness, IR lineage survival, and renewal frequency is hypothesis.

## Per-document concerns

### 1018.008 parent — High: unbounded aggregate cost

§5 correctly lists constraints, but never sums bytes, certifier work, or maintenance across .000–.004 plus the §7 invalidation experiment. Near-ceiling, that sum is the decision variable.

§5 order — .000, .001, then .004 — prioritizes the highest-authority-risk work (.000 recovery, .001 slicing) before the lowest-risk measurement (.004 §2–§3). Reverse it.

Resolution: require each RFC to name bytes removed and net runtime delta against the unchanged 85k roots, and to rebase measurements on landed 1019/1020 before claiming benefit. Success metric in §5 — count full app including shared bindings/observers/recovery — is right; keep it, but publish a baseline count now since this turn ran no benchmark.

### 1018.008.000 recovery — High: background renewal and retry bound

The split in §3 — capacity acquisition vs byte-identical replay — is correct, and invariants in §4 (no resubmission of rejected/non-durable work, no fence/epoch change, explicit `offline()` always wins, writer-only renewal) are necessary.

Material issues:

- §3/§6 background renewal: "schedule one background renewal" on low-watermark crossing plus "register demand when handle prepared" means render/observation can trigger I/O, contradicting "reading `can` itself never starts I/O." More important, §6 admits renewal might repeat expensive cold resume work. Invisible sends must not become expensive sends. Severity: High. Resolution: land admission-time join-renewal plus recovery drain first; gate background/prefetch on proof of a cheap validator path, not a timer around `online()`.
- Bounded retry: "at most three attempts per transition" with "ordinary frames/rerenders do not reset" leaves `transition` undefined. Auth flap or offline→reachable flap can create unbounded transitions over time. Severity: High. Resolution: define transition as monotonic transport/auth epoch, add jitter and a global cap per epoch, reuse existing transport deadlines, and log attempts. Manual `online()` as new opportunity is fine.
- Demand `1+maxNewIds` tied to generation: correct direction — client cannot supply arithmetic — but "at most one subsequent demand-specific request" can still spin if the certificate flips repeatedly. Severity: Medium. Resolution: pin generation for the call; on mismatch, refuse with sourced bound rather than chaining.
- Security ordering: terminal recovery before compatibility checks but after live auth must hold for auto-drain. §3 says reacquire fence/coverage and run under current account, but acceptance must explicitly assert no auto-drain under stale epoch and no watermark advance on malformed later state. Severity: High, resolvable with named controls already listed in §5.

Simplify: drop background renewal; keep join-before-admission + coalesced resume/drain + explicit offline cut. That is half the code and almost all the ergonomics.

### 1018.008.001 presentation — Critical: authorization; High: cost

Current `withHistory` behavior is correct: whole-query truth, separate receipts, no per-field `unheld`. Do not weaken it.

- §3 slicing: "preserve every authored `require`/refusal/control dependency" including count-dependent guards is stated, but sufficiency is explicitly unverified — §5 requires independent review. Until that proof exists this is hypothesis, not design. Severity: Critical. Resolution: no implementation without that review; any guard-dropping variant is a different authority design outside NOT-DOING.
- Cost vs deletion: deriving a closed slice, lowering ahead of time, Rust-certifying it, binding primary/companion metadata, plus generation-pinned lifecycle and shared-subscription integration (§3–§4) to delete `table/rows/select/order/belongs/withHistory` from one chat query is disproportionate near ceiling. A common query "must not acquire an unconditional second subscription" is promised, but even conditional evaluation doubles work on exactly the partial path. Severity: High. Resolution: limit first slice to proven descriptor defaults (table/selector/order) from existing certifier facts; retain explicit `withHistory`. Prototype only on final 1019 shape plus one non-chat aggregate, and close if no material line-count reduction.

Definite vs hypothesis: friction is definite; that safe slicing removes it without new disclosure is hypothesis.

### 1018.008.002 `collect` — High: no demand; Critical if built: lineage

§1 evidence gate is the best part: do not build without a surviving use case after 1019, do not re-implement recipient log. Honor it by shelving now.

If built, §3 lineage requirement — capped-source stays capped through aliases, fields, helpers, branches, list construction; capped→fan-out still refuses — is security-critical and currently hypothetical: §2 says "must verify existing IR supports lineage before committing." Twin parity, pricing of nested projections, and `bound`/`quarry` diagnostics add compiler/certifier/evaluator work plus a frozen-word-inventory change against the 1500-line cap. Severity: Critical for soundness, High for scope.

Resolution: leave Draft/shelved. If demand appears, prefer returning the page and projecting client-side over a new cursor-less list type. The `collect` spelling is better than conflating pages with lists (§6), but that choice only matters after demand.

### 1018.008.003 closed effects — Medium, splittable

The boundary in §2 — relative-only, realpath, no bundler/runtime resolver/npm promise, no evaluation in `check`, refuse cycles/dynamic/mutable captures/escaped paths — is the right minimal shape and respects "no new runtime loader."

Split it:

- Empty `grants: []` with runtime `fetch` still refusing (§4) is a definite, low-risk deletion of the guide's dummy-grant workaround. Land separately.
- Full closure assembly — TS graph walk, renaming/hygiene, deterministic normalization, identity including helper source, per-job isolation proof with LLP 1003 host (§2–§3§5) — is still substantial compiler work for unproven reuse. §5 itself says defer if not two consumers. Severity: Medium. Resolution: require two real effects deduplicated, reduce to function-declarations + JSON constants per §6 fallback, and prove second-job isolation after local mutation + timer plus pending-continuation helper edit. No private source/maps into public metadata.

### 1018.008.004 acquisition — Medium, gate §4

§1–§2 measurement discipline is exemplary: frozen fixture, post-1019/1020 re-baseline, separate transport/authority/persistence/materialization/evaluation/memory, no asymptotic claims from one 127 MB post-GC run or one p95 on contended BLACK.

- §3 dedup candidates are hypotheses, not claims — correct. Canonical row-image sharing must never merge selection/observation evidence; coalesced invalidation must preserve clock/refusal/reset/terminal distinctions. Severity: Medium, resolvable with paired baseline/candidate runs.
- §4 multi-page batching — several ≤50 envelopes per 1 MiB response, per-page receipts, aggregate turn allowance, `commitMany` atomicity, generation/authority mid-response handling, interplay with 1019 acks and no-gap tail — is a protocol change. With 207 commits and 613 evaluations for 201 responses, evaluation/materialization likely dominates round trips. Batching before §3 attribution risks complexity for <20% gain. Severity: High for complexity. Resolution: do §2+§3 only; gate §4 on proof transport dominates after §3, with single-protocol end state and existing single-page oracle.

§5 retention stance is correct: measure by category, no silent eviction/hold-sets/delta-resume under a perf RFC.

### 1018.002 invalidation — Critical if narrowed; likely descope

§6 audit honesty — retain broad binding, disconnected-table candidate does not solve witnessed `messages.category` two-write conflict — plus §4 ordering (live session → stable terminal lookup → compat checks) and §5 preservation matrix are the right safety posture. Static vs dynamic separation and twin agreement are necessary.

But payoff is narrow: only disconnected-table edits benefit initially, with coordinated native/twin/certifier/wire-domain cost and old envelopes staying conservative. The witnessed subject-table case needs field-level reasoning deliberately excluded. Severity: Critical for missed revocation if exclusion proof is wrong; High for negative cost/benefit.

Resolution: run §7 isolated counterfactual only after 1020, with relevant-edit controls and cold-restart/FIFO/cycle/derived-index coverage. Proceed only on demonstrated unchanged-bytes replay plus intact refusals and independent auth review of every excluded dependency. Otherwise keep broad binding and close — §7 already permits this. Do not let this become a general invalidation framework. Cheaper alternative is better conflict UX for re-issue, since new intents already commit.

## What to simplify or delete

- Delete background/prefetch renewal from .000; keep admission join + drain.
- Reduce .001 to descriptor defaults; delete slice synthesis until proof + 1019 shape stable.
- Shelve .002; no grammar change without surviving demand.
- Split .003: land `grants: []` now; defer assembly until two consumers.
- Delete .004 §4 batching until §3 attribution proves transport dominates.
- Keep 1018.002 §7 as experiment only; default to closing with broad binding retained.
- Do the §6 orientation prose reconciliation in one small change after 1019–1021 land; no generator/registry/freshness policy.

## Smaller alternatives

Admission-time renewal instead of watermark prefetch; linted `defineItems` conventions instead of compiler slicing; client map over held pages instead of `collect`; shared JSON/request-builder constants instead of closure assembly; evaluation coalescing and index reuse instead of transport batching; explicit conflict re-issue UX instead of hash narrowing. Each removes app lines without new authority machinery.

## Suggested order

1. Orientation fix after active landings.
2. Empty grants.
3. Recovery: admission-time join + bounded auto-drain, no background.
4. Offline: paired measurement + §3 dedup only.
5. Re-evaluate with real post-1019/1020 numbers before considering cheap-path background renewal, descriptor defaults, batching, closure assembly, or the invalidation witness. Three fix rounds then descope per constraints; .002 stays shelved.

## Remaining defects and repair regressions

### A. High — Rule-probe cuts still bypass “lookup is unknown”

There are two paths through the unchanged raw point reader:

- A positive `read <- exists members[…]` probe exhausts its budget and returns false. The point reader restores its meter mark, erasing that cut, then returns null. The new expression check sees an uncapped meter and accepts false/absence, potentially with `complete: true`.
- With `read <- not exists bans[…]`, the cut probe’s false becomes true. The point reader returns the row, and the new `Get` check only calls `require_uncapped()` for **null** values. A row that the completed rule would hide can therefore still be returned, marked incomplete.

The relevant chain is [read.rs:45](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:45), [rules.rs:222](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/rules.rs:222), [read.rs:316](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:316), and [runner.rs:492](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:492).

For the second case, compare a banned viewer’s hidden row against an absent row: the hidden world returns its body; the absent world returns null. Paired worlds do **not** remain identical.

**Coverage:** Missing. The new fixture uses owner-equality read rules; its [probe-cut test](snapback4/crates/snapback4-sim/tests/common/point_read_cut.rs:79) exhausts the predicate lookup’s initial charge, before testing these authorization paths. These raw-reader holes predate the repair, but prevent its engine-wide claim.

**Needed:** Preserve an indeterminate authorization verdict without breaking hidden/absent normalization; cover positive and negated read-rule probes.

### B. High — Confirmed replay can disappear before its settlement notification

After A’s admission aborts, its exposure record may be its only remaining record. Ordinary replay receives `sent`, then [deletes that record](snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:937). Before emitting `onWrite`, it [awaits synchronization](snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:958).

If that synchronization’s `apply` persistence fails, the rejection reaches the flush catch. Notification is skipped. The next flush has neither durable A nor exposed A, so it cannot recover the notification. A’s caller received `failed/E_EXIT_ADMISSION`; A applied; no later settlement arrives.

**Coverage:** Missing. The [new failure-interleaving tests](snapback4/packages/snapback4/test/navigation-model.test.mjs:423) fail admission persistence, then allow subsequent saves. They do not fail acquisition persistence after confirmed replay.

**Needed:** Retain or deliver the authoritative settlement independently of subsequent synchronization.

### C. Medium — A permanent, unrecorded refusal retries forever and strands successors

Exposed writes now require `failed` **and** `replayed: true` before a refusal releases the queue. But unknown operations and invalid arguments return without recording a receipt: [serve.rs:851](snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:851), [serve.rs:864](snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:864).

For example, expose A after its operation’s argument limit has changed. Every retry of its captured arguments returns the same unrecorded `E_INPUT`. The [terminal check](snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:930) never succeeds, retries continue at up to five-second intervals, and valid B remains blocked—even across same-page reopen.

This preserves ordering, but introduces permanent queue blockage without terminal settlement or an explicit stopped state.

**Coverage:** The [unrecorded-refusal regression](snapback4/packages/snapback4/test/navigation-model.test.mjs:289) demonstrates the hold, then changes `allowReplay` to true. It does not construct an actual permanent server rejection.

**Needed:** A recorded terminal refusal/cancellation mechanism, or an explicit recoverable blocked outcome; silently dropping A would reopen the original ordering defect.

### D. Medium — A heap cut after another cut now becomes a refusal

The revised [unwind condition](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:170) requires the thrown bound’s kind to equal the **first** recorded cut.

A plain scan over two 4-KB rows with `examined = 1` and `heap_bytes = 100` first records an examined cut, then [charges the returned row’s heap](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:501). The heap error now escapes as `E_BOUND`; previously it unwound to null/incomplete/no continuation.

**Coverage:** Missing. Existing [heap-cut tests](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/tests/meter.rs:24) make heap the first cut.

**Needed:** Preserve the previous heap-unwind behavior alongside the new unknown-lookup unwind.

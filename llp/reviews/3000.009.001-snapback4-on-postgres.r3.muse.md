## New fundamental and major findings

### N1. Failover keep-decision rests on trust, not evidence — MAJOR — [child A §5.4](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:330)

The three-part guarantee's legs are not runtime-decidable. Leg 2 is verified as "non-empty `synchronous_standby_names`, or the provider's documented quorum" — a config check, not a check that a sync standby was actually connected and caught up at each renewal (`pg_stat_replication` is never mentioned). Leg 3, "the provider fences the old primary before promoting," is pure trust: the new owner cannot establish for *this* promotion that the old primary is fenced, and if the trust is misplaced (wrong SKU, provider bug, partition misread as failover), the old owner keeps renewing its lease on the old primary while the new owner serves the new one — two writers, one `store_id`, divergent histories, with no mechanism in the documents that would notice. The first-class list (RDS Multi-AZ, Cloud SQL HA) itself comes from a documentation-only pass the experiment labels UNVERIFIED ([fencing README](snapback4/experiments/postgres-fencing/README.md:402)). There is also an incoherent corner: if the serving role cannot read the timeline at all, promotion is undetectable, so "same-incarnation failover is unavailable" cannot mean "refuse after promotion" — it must mean refuse to adopt.

Recommended direction: make refuse-after-timeline-change the default and require positive evidence to keep serving (e.g., per-provider failover qualification witnessed live, or old-primary-fenced evidence via provider API), rather than documented trust. Make timeline plus system-identifier readability a P1 preflight requirement — refuse adoption where unreadable — since without it every reconnect is indistinguishable from promotion. Check sync-standby *liveness* at renewal, not just the setting.

### N2. Optimistic validation is incomplete across committers and silent on monotone writes — MAJOR — [child A §6.3–§6.4](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:441)

Point 4 assigns the internal revision and key footprints to "every durable write" *under (a)* — but (0) runs concurrently as the hot-key fallback, so (0)'s writes must advance the revision and record footprints too, or (a) silently misses conflicts with exactly the hottest keys. That participation, plus where the revision counter lives and what it costs the fast path, is unstated. Second, time-audience pins are durable fenced writes taken on read paths constantly ([child A](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:228)), yet nothing says how they validate: if a pin's footprint conflicts like any write, every two concurrent pinning mutations conflict and (a) serializes on the clock row — collapsing to (0)-like throughput on the common case and contradicting the sizing case; if pins commute as monotone max-advances (the correct answer, since `now` is assigned at admission, not store-read), the documents must say they are conflict-exempt with batch-max application, and §6.4's rerun rule restated for batch order. S1a's clock-row contention cell will then measure an unspecified design.

Recommended direction: state that all committers — reference, (0), (a) — advance the internal revision with footprints in one shared key space; exempt monotone pins from conflict detection with max-merge at batch commit; and write the S1a cell against that specified design, not an implementation choice.

### N3. Ambiguous-commit retry safety is asserted for every path and witnessed for none — MAJOR — [child A §5.2](llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:291)

"Every write path is retry-safe across owners through its receipt or idempotency key" is a universal claim over mints, claims, steps, sweeps, pins, views, and activation — but the prototype proved *ordering* (old commits land before takeover, W9), not retry safety, and no P1b witness covers commit-racing-termination followed by same-op retry. The sharpest case is session mint: an ambiguous mint followed by client retry plausibly creates two live sessions, one unknown to anyone — dangling credentials, not just litter — and nothing in §5.2 or §10 names the per-path story (UNVERIFIED against code; shell unavailable). A property this broad, load-bearing for the whole fencing argument, cannot rest on assertion.

Recommended direction: add a per-path retry table (what the idempotency key is, what a retry observes in each of the three outcomes — rolled back, committed-acked, committed-unacked) and extend W9 into a P1b witness: force a commit to race termination under a sync wait, retry the same operation under the new owner, and assert a single visible effect, for mint, claim, and receipt paths at minimum.

## What the author wants from you

**Look for fundamental, foundational, or directional errors — not details that would be worked out in implementation anyway.** Examples of what counts:

- A decision that is wrong, or that rests on a false premise about the code, Postgres, SQLite, Supabase, or Convex.
- A missing part of the problem that would change the design if it were included (for example, a durable state, a coordination point, or a failure mode that breaks one of the stated invariants).
- A mechanism that cannot achieve what it claims (for example, a migration step that cannot be made seamless or correct as described, a commit protocol that violates exact snapshots or contiguous watermarks, a fencing scheme with a hole, a scale test that cannot answer its own question or whose controls are unfair in a way that biases the conclusion).
- A sequencing or scoping error that would waste a large amount of work or foreclose a needed option.
- A better overall direction that the documents missed or dismissed for a wrong reason.

Do **not** spend effort on wording, naming, formatting, minor missing details, specific constants, or implementation choices the documents explicitly leave to the increments — unless one of them hides a directional problem. If you have small notes, put at most five in a short final section.

Verify claims against the code where you can; say UNVERIFIED where you cannot. Cite files and lines.

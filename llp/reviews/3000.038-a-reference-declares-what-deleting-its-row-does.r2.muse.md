## 2. New problems

- **N0 — High — §2.2: traversal hops through a set-null column are not excluded.** Scenario: `tasks.project ? on delete set null` with `read <- exists members[.project.org, viewer]`; nulling `.project` moves the row's audience though no rule "keys or equates on" the bare column — the probe key is the chain `.project.org`. Same for another table's `sync to members[.message.conversation]` and other tables' write rules using `ReferenceChain` through the column. Fix: define "reads/traverses" as any probe key, equality, hop, or sync-audience path *containing* the column, in any table's read, write, or sync rules.
- **N1 — High — §2.2 vs §4.1: the field-owned claim overstates the rule.** §4.1 says a child holding other principals' private field-owned values "cannot be a cascade or set-null target at all", but §2.2 refuses only columns "not readable by its audience". A collaborator's `notes written by .author`, readable by all members, is still destroyed by cascade. Fix: either refuse cascade/set-null into any table with field-owned columns regardless of readability, or downgrade §4.1 and add the warning to the §4.3 rule of thumb.
- **N2 — High — §3 step 2, §4.2, §5.1: the physical draw does not bound expired-backlog visits.** Caps bound *live* rows; the closure/index scans still physically visit expired unswept rows (cf. the cap path at `write.rs:549–556`, which fetches rows to test expiry). Either steps 3–6 scan through `GuardedRows` — and a 20k-expired-backlog group fails with `AuthorizationBudget` where an empty group succeeds, i.e. hidden-count-dependent refusal — or they scan unguarded, in which case fixed `R(P)` bounds nothing physical. "One seek skips an expired backlog" (§2.3) is asserted, never specified as a seek-start/storage guarantee or priced. Fix: state the skip guarantee (`[col, expire, id]` seeks start at the live suffix) and include backlog visits in `probe(r)`, or count expired visits against the reservation.
- **N3 — High — §2.2, §5.1: timeline member deletes carry unpriced work outside the "mapped table" refusal.** Deleting a row of a timeline's posts/reposts/edges table runs `timeline_remove_id`, tombstones, purge enqueues and delivery (`timeline.rs:108,140`) — data-dependent work with no term in `reservation()` and no `unit(C)` entry. If "mapped" means only mapped application tables (§6.6 usage), posts/reposts/edges/authors remain eligible cascade targets with unbounded-amplified unpriced work — GPT 5's counterexample survives in new clothes. Fix: refuse cascade/set-null into *any* timeline-member table (posts, reposts, edges, authors), or add the transition term to `unit(C)`.
- **N4 — Medium — §2.2: a null-exempt cap proves nothing even when the query pins non-null.** `cap 500 by category` still bounds every non-null group, so a loop over one pinned category is bounded — but the RFC discards the bound wholesale. Sound, but refuses valid programs. Fix: exempt caps still prove bounds for groups with non-null evidence (the same non-null evidence the enforcement path already requires).
- **N5 — Medium — §5.1: no stored `R(P)`; three hosts recompute seven-dimension arithmetic.** `unit(C)` (index-entry counts, `words` token bound, heap ceiling) plus the recursive `R(C)` sum will drift across server/compiler/devices. Fix: either store `R(P)` per table in the IR under the `reference-actions` capability (Muse 6's fix), or mandate cross-host golden tests on `R(P)` across the difftest seeds.
- **N6 — Medium — §5.1: heap is summed along the chain but specified as a max.** `R(P) = Σ …` over every meter kind including heap over-counts transient heap (rows processed one at a time). Conservative = safe, but produces spurious `E_CASCADE_BOUND` refusals on deep chains at `check` time. Fix: heap takes the max over the chain, sums everywhere else.
- **N7 — Low — §3 steps 4–6: refusal precedence and removal edge-set are unspecified.** A delete blocked by both a `restrict` occupancy and a `read through` child always reports `E_REFERENCED` (step 4 runs first) — deterministic, but `test expect` authors and the wire table need it stated. "Order of the reference graph among tables" should say the union of cascade and `read through` edges. Fix: two sentences.
- **N8 — Low — §2.2: "grouped by a cap together with other columns" is ambiguous** (multi-column `by (a, col)` vs. co-grouping). Fix: say a set-null column's cap must be `cap N by <column>` with that single column.

## 3. Authorization / disclosure blockers

N2 (hidden-count-dependent `AuthorizationBudget`) and N1 (overstated authority confinement) block implementation as written; N0 leaves a real audience-changing hole. The `restrict`-bit contract itself (one bit per reachable reference, schema-order reporting, transitive carry declared and `check`-named) is sound. `keep`, the read gate, and device queue-without-proof are sound.

## Verdict: NOT READY

Revision 1 closes the large majority of round 1 — closure-first delete, schema-fixed trace, `keep`, scoped postcondition, device witness discipline are all sound. It is not ready because the reservation's two completeness gaps (N2 physical visits, N3 timeline transitions), the set-null traversal hole (N0), and the field-owned overstatement (N1) each break a load-bearing claim (boundedness, authority confinement). The smallest path to ready: refuse cascade/set-null into all timeline-member tables, specify and price the expired-backlog skip, extend the set-null exclusion to hops/traversals in every table, and align §4.1 with §2.2.

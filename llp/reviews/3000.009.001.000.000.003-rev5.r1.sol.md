   Suppose revision 101 takes 20 ms while 102–132 finish in 5 ms. The spike can acknowledge those later requests and reuse their keys. With W4’s 32 recurring client keys, this changes subsequent dispatch and queueing, beyond adding publication-processing time.

Require the ≤6.0 ms experiment gate on that scheduling behavior before proceeding with R1–R3.

2. **MINOR — IN-DELTA — §§3.2, 4.4, 6.1, 11: reconsider two durable identity records per singleton.**

   Each successful singleton writes both an attempt record and a public commit record; a void similarly needs both. At N=1, much of their identity and resolution purpose overlaps. This adds durable work precisely where restoration has only 0.4 ms of proposed margin.

   **Suggested fix:** measure enriching `_commits` with revision, owner-attempt and resolution fields, inserting it first and using its unique sequence key for the commit/void race. Preserve the distinct evidence, receipt and log retention contracts. This is a potentially cheaper representation, not evidence that it reaches 4,525/s.

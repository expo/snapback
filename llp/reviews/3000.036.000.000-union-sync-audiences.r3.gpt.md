NOT READY

1. **High — §3/§9: the epoch fallback authorizes pre-epoch deletes.**  
   Alice never belonged to team T under the old audience. A Bob-owned row X in T is deleted. The group arm is removed, Alice joins T, and the union is restored. Alice then requests `stream: true, from: 0` under the current generation.

   `partition.rs:857–859` filters membership history by epoch, but `intervals` falls back to Alice’s **current** membership and opens a span at `watermark + 1` (`871–876`). `stream` uses that start without an epoch floor (`926–934`). It therefore reads the old `g:tasks:T` delete; R1 finds X absent and sends `del X` (`1072–1080`). Alice could read X at no commit boundary: D2 fails. The HTTP endpoint permits this request (`snapback4/src/serve_sync.rs:14–27,69–106`).

   **Change:** bound group-log authority by the audience epoch, or require a fresh snapshot for a watermark preceding it. Handle superseded principal-partition membership events consistently. Extend the epoch test with an old deleted row and streams from pre-epoch watermarks; its current snapshot-derived watermark (`snapback4-sim/tests/union.rs:1221–1224`) cannot reach this failure. The round-1 epoch disposition is incomplete.

2. **High — §5.2: two arms can share an index, losing one arm’s boundary and rows.**  
   Use `.owner = viewer or exists staff[viewer]` with `sync last 1 by .owner, .at`. The roster and owner arms both plan `[owner, at, id]`: the union planner removes repeated prefix columns and reuses identical index requests (`snapback4-lang/src/bind/intent.rs:59–75,323–332`). Neither binder nor core refuses the collision.

   `Sync::arm_by_window` returns the first matching arm (`snapback4-core/src/schema.rs:470–471`), which is the roster. Consequently, `device/boundary.rs:60–62` rejects the owner’s one-element acquisition group against the roster’s zero-element width.

   Let Alice be staff, with an acquired Alice-owned row at time 1 and the roster floor at a Bob-owned row. Insert a new Alice-owned row at time 3. Its key is below the roster floor because `alice < bob`; its owner boundary is missing. `Boundaries::accept` rejects it (`104–124`), and both ordinary application and snapshot catch-up discard it (`device/deferred.rs:234`; `device/snapshot.rs:93`). After draining, Alice lacks a row inside her acquired owner window.

   **Change:** identify boundaries by the applicable arm as well as index, or refuse colliding arm windows in both binder and core until supported. Add a windowed staff world using this order, checking fresh markers and subsequent owner inserts. The existing staff world has no windows.

3. **High — §3/§10: a same-commit renewal reopens the acquiring commit.**  
   This uses supported single-group sync, which the new global acquiring-commit change also affects. Mallory initially lacks group G. At time 10, one commit inserts her membership with deadline 100, renews it to 200, and deletes G’s row X. Serve at time 20.

   Accounting preserves both joins (`snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:358–405`). `intervals` makes the first join’s zero-width span end in the second join (`883–888`). `continuous_renewal` accepts that edge (`1030–1032`), so `stream` classifies the second span as continuous and starts it **at** the acquiring sequence (`929–933`). X’s delete is streamed even though Mallory could read it at neither boundary. Join expansion cannot restore a deleted row or undo the id disclosure.

   **Change:** preserve the acquisition’s exclusive commit boundary through every renewal within that commit. Including a renewal’s commit requires authority at the preceding commit boundary. Add `join → renew → delete/move` compound tests to the expiring single-group world, at both page sizes. The round-2 acquiring-commit disposition is incomplete.

4. **Low — §2.1/§10: printed unions still do not always compile.**  
   Give `members` two principal columns, `who` and `inviter`, and compile `sync to .owner or members[.team] by .who`. `Audience::arm` omits the recipient selector (`snapback4-core/src/schema.rs:439–443`), and union printing uses that spelling (`481`). The printed line lacks `by .who`; rebinding refuses the ambiguous recipient (`snapback4-lang/src/bind/schema.rs:413–424`).

   **Change:** print the group arm’s recipient selector, including `sync by .who` for an empty-prefix roster. Add round-trip tests with multiple principal columns. The round-2 printing fix covers window spelling but does not establish the stated “every printed line compiles” guarantee.

I found the remaining principal mechanisms sound on inspection:

- Per-arm logging deduplicates principal partitions. Commit-boundary row accounting removes transient deliveries; delete/reinsert retains the original revocations, while born-and-deleted rows withdraw their deliveries.
- Ordinary puts, snapshots, join expansion and R1 apply serve-time read checks and projection.
- R2 checks scope width and recipient type, uses committed or separately staged images, advances past spared rows, and walks a plain index. Both application paths preserve column-arm rows.
- For distinct arm indexes, boundary reads stop at the selected floor and defer when coverage is insufficient. No-window authorization absence requires a wholly covered arm.
- The §8 exclusions are enforced across core validation and binding. Aggregate source and target backstops and the PostgreSQL union refusal are present. The capability carrier makes older decoders fail closed before silently ignoring `also`.

The corresponding §10 dispositions are sound, subject to the incomplete fixes above. Window retention should be understood through **acquired floors**: the implementation deliberately does not backfill a current top-N window after deletion, despite §5.1’s broader wording.

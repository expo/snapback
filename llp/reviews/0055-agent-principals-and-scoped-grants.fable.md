# Review: LLP 0055 — Agent principals and scoped grants

Reviewer: independent (Claude family), LLP 0005 loop. Read in full: LLP 0055 (target), 0011, 0039, 0053, 0024, 0042, 0019, 0056, 0000 (auth/agent sections), 0017/0010 (cited claims); implementation spot-checks in `src/server.rs`, `src/admin.rs`, `src/admin_secret_read.rs`, `src/host_ops.rs`, `src/ephemeral.rs`, `src/storage.rs`, `src/manifest.rs`.

## 1. Overall assessment

This is the right document to exist and mostly the right design. It answers the gap LLP 0019 F2 names as the largest auth hole, and its central moves are correct: one principal id space with `kind` as an immutable, queryable fact (avoiding the fork of every `userId`-keyed surface); `userId` = *effective* principal so app code, RLS, and memberships written for humans work unmodified under delegation; a closed, analyzable scope grammar that can only narrow; consent-minted delegated grants enumerable and revocable without touching the user's own session; and an unusually honest attestation posture (declared kind is honor-system; the credentialed grant chain is what's strong). The three-way disentangling in the Summary — operator plane, application plane, attestation — is genuinely clarifying, and the degenerate-case unification (a human session is actor = effective, grant = session) is elegant. The document is fluent in the corpus's established patterns: prefixed checksummed secrets stored hashed, staged manifest policy defaulting closed, `mintSession`-mirrored gating, the protected `_auth_*` catalog posture, 0039's throttle honesty. I verified its claims about reality: `servicePrincipal` appears nowhere in `src/`; the `_auth_*` catalog, provider, and session bearers are unbuilt while the route-policy machinery (`PolicyAuthMode::Sessions`, `EffectPolicy::ImpersonationCapability` composing with `AdminWrite`, `AdminSecretRead` leases, legacy body-auth rejection) exists — the RFC's "reserved"/"depends on" claims match the code.

It is not ready, for reasons that are fixable without changing the architecture. The most serious: §Revocation restates LLP 0053's Accepted contract and gets it wrong in precisely the direction 0053's own history records having corrected once already — it calls grant revocation a "permission-backing write" (event-1 vocabulary for a table the RFC's own §Catalog makes matcher-invisible) and claims it behaves "exactly as a session revocation does," when 0053 explicitly carves session revocation *out* of the authorization-event universe as an authority transition. Second, the new credential classes (`sbac_`, `sbat_`) arrive with no route/credential matrix: no mint-time actor-binding predicate, no statement of which surfaces a scoped token may address, no connection-binding or deadline story, no LLP 0031 registration — and 0039 demonstrates at length that this is exactly where auth holes live. Third, the boundary between grant scopes and the operator plane is implicit while the Summary's framing ("deploy, inspect… the operator-plane `servicePrincipal`") invites the wrong reading of what un-reserving authorizes. Fourth, the lifecycle matrix applies 0039's restore reasoning to short-lived tokens but not to the long-lived credentials and grants this document introduces, which are the actual resurrection vector.

None of these undermines the design's spine; each has a bounded resolution. But for the document whose explicit job is to be the thing LLP 0024/0042 un-reserve against, the missing pins are load-bearing, and the 0053 contradiction is disqualifying on its own under this corpus's precedence rule.

## 2. Strengths

- **§Summary** — the operator-plane / application-plane / attestation disentangling is the document's best idea; "a plain human session is the degenerate case" unifies the model instead of bolting agents on.
- **§Principal kinds** — the one-id-space argument (versus a separate principals table forking profiles, memberships, RLS variables, audit) is correct and well-stated; `kind` immutability quietly makes subscribe-time auth-variable resolution sound forever under 0011's rule model.
- **§Resolved authority** — `userId` as the effective principal is the right load-bearing choice and is argued as such; `actor` absent for direct sessions gives "was this actually the person?" a single, checkable answer.
- **§Registration** — abuse posture as staged app policy defaulting `disabled`, with 0039's throttle honesty note carried forward rather than re-litigated.
- **§Credentials and tokens** — clean two-tier mirror of the session posture (`sbac_`/`sbat_` + CRC, hashed at rest, credential never on app-data requests); sender-binding named as trajectory, not claimed.
- **§Grants** — closed scope grammar with honest coarse tiers; one-directional narrowing with RLS still running against the effective principal; revocation deliberately decoupled from the user's own session.
- **§Attestation, honestly** — exemplary honesty; the distinction between honor-system declared kind and server-issued grant-chain facts is exactly right.
- **§Non-Goals** — the delegation-depth cap and the explicit "nothing here is a precondition of" 0039's frozen slice are the right fences.
- **§Catalog and lifecycle** — correct protected-catalog posture (outside manifest, never journaled/synced, version bumped).
- **§Validation Requirements** — contains the sharpest delegation proof available: "a row the user cannot see, the delegated agent cannot see."

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — §Revocation contradicts Accepted LLP 0053, in the exact way the corpus already corrected once.**
Evidence: the target says grant revocation and agent deactivation "are **permission-backing writes** — they advance the authorization version and project reauthorization obligations … exactly as a session revocation does." LLP 0053 §The authorization-event universe (Accepted): "**Not on this list, and deliberately so: session revocation.** Logout, session expiry, and admin *session* revocation are **authority transitions**, not authorization events … They are LLP 0039's, they run through its `AuthCoordinator`, and they **delete** the client's retained data outright." LLP 0039's changelog records fixing precisely this conflation ("conflating an authority transition (identity ends → delete) with an authorization event (permission narrows → quarantine)"). Additionally, "permission-backing write" is 0053's event 1 — a matcher-observed write to a *declared app table* — while the target's own §Catalog pins `_auth_grants` outside the app manifest, never journaled, never synced: a grant write structurally cannot be event 1. And 0053's event universe is "enumerated, not derived" and closed, so adding grant revocation to it is a dated amendment to an Accepted document, which the target does not name. Design cost, too: v1 has exactly one *global* epoch, so routing every user-initiated `DELETE /auth/grants` through it quarantines every retained-boot client in the app per revocation — the coarse cost 0053 exists to avoid.
Resolution criterion: rewrite the section choosing the category explicitly. Preferred: grant revocation and agent deactivation are **authority transitions** — extend 0039's connection binding and two-phase revocation with a grant projection (pending-revoked markers, drain-side fencing, deadline sweep), with `delete` as the agent-client outcome. Alternatively, an explicit dated 0053 amendment adding them to the event-4 out-of-band family, with the global-epoch cost weighed. Either way, remove "exactly as a session revocation does."

**2. MATERIAL — the new credential classes have no surface/binding matrix.**
Evidence: (a) `POST /auth/agents/token { grantId }` "authenticated with the credential" never states the predicate that the grant's `actor_user_id` must equal the authenticated credential's principal, and no Validation row covers cross-credential minting — the classic confused-deputy mint. (b) Nothing pins which surfaces accept an `sbat_` bearer: only the app-data invocation gate, or also `/auth/grants` (a delegated agent enumerating/revoking the user's grants), invitation minting, registration, `/connection/open`? 0039's credential-precedence contract ("presented-but-invalid is an error, never a fall-through"; "`Authorization` is reserved for session bearers") is exactly where this class of hole lives, and three credential prefixes now share one header. (c) The Validation section assumes grant-backed *live* subscriptions exist ("grant revocation mid-connection"), but 0039's `POST /connection/open` binds only `anonymous` or `session {…}` — the `servicePrincipal` bound-authority variant and its deadline (min of token expiry and grant expiry, which is also how clock expiry of a grant converges for live surfaces without an epoch event) are unspecified. (d) None of the four-plus new routes has an LLP 0031 route-registry row (path policy, credential resolution, effect policy, response sensitivity) — the corpus's mechanism for making this checkable rather than remembered; 0031 is not even in Related.
Resolution criterion: add a 0039-style route/credential table covering every new route and credential class; pin the mint-time actor-match predicate and add its negative to Validation; pin scoped-token-valid surfaces (recommended: invocation gate and `/connection/open` only; grant/invitation management requires a real session or, for the agent's own token minting, the credential); pin the bound-authority variant and deadline; name the 0031 revision as a companion obligation.

**3. MATERIAL — extends 0053-pinned exact `ctx.auth` projections without an amendment, and leaves `kind`'s presence ambiguous for plain sessions.**
Evidence: 0053 §Protected-query admission (Accepted) pins that sessions "project **exactly**" `{userId, sessionId, isAuthenticated:true, provider, roles:[]}`. The target's `AuthContext` adds `kind?` ("kind of the effective principal") and `actor?` without saying whether an ordinary human session carries `kind: "human"` — if yes, that alters an Accepted exact enumeration absent a dated amendment; if no, the field is asymmetric and the doc should say so. The new `servicePrincipal` projection shape (e.g. `sessionId: null` with `isAuthenticated: true`) must also join 0053's sealed enumeration and protected-query profile, and `SNAPBACK_AUTH_GRANT_SCOPE_DENIED` joins the trusted-denial producer set that 0011's addendum enumerates ("Declarative RLS and room reauthorization are the other trusted producers enumerated by LLP 0053").
Resolution criterion: pin `kind`'s presence per authority mode; name the dated 0053 (and 0011) amendments for the sealed projections and the trusted-producer/diagnostic registrations.

**4. MATERIAL — the operator-plane boundary is implicit where the Summary implies the opposite.**
Evidence: Summary item 1 frames `servicePrincipal` as "an agent operating Snapback itself — deploy, inspect, run functions under explicit authority," but §Grants' scope grammar covers only generated functions (`fn:*`), and LLP 0024 attaches the authority tuple to reset, manifest loads, and mutating acceptance as well — so after un-reserving, "which host-ops operations accept `servicePrincipal`?" is a question 0024's own table forces and this document never answers. Failure scenario: an implementer honors a `fn:mutation:*` grant for `app.reset` or `schema.load` on the theory that this RFC blessed operator use, bypassing `AdminWrite`/`ImpersonationCapability` carriers.
Resolution criterion: state explicitly that v1 grants authorize generated-function invocation only (`function.run`/`function.explain` and the app-data projections); lifecycle/provisioning/reset verbs continue to refuse `servicePrincipal` and remain on operator-plane carriers; adjust the Summary framing to match.

**5. MATERIAL — declared restore revokes the wrong artifact class.**
Evidence: §Catalog and lifecycle: "`--restored-from-backup` revokes restored agent tokens alongside sessions." Tokens live ≤ 24h; the durable resurrection vector is the long-lived `sbac_` credential and the grants — a credential or delegated grant revoked *after* the backup returns live indefinitely on restore. For sessions, 0039 closes exactly this by revoking every restored session and then honestly naming the undeclared-restore residual; the target's "revocable" headline is weaker than 0039's parallel for its own new artifact class.
Resolution criterion: declared restore also revokes (or forces rotation of) restored agent credentials and revokes restored grants — or the residual is named explicitly with its acceptance rationale, 0039-style.

**6. NON-BLOCKING — no companion-obligations list.**
Evidence: 0039 §Landing order names every owning-document amendment; this RFC changes 0024 and 0042 (un-reserve), needs a dated 0011 addendum, 0012 diagnostic-family registration (`GRANT_SCOPE_DENIED`, registration/invitation codes), the 0004 generated spelling for `auth.kind`, and the 0031 revision (concern 2), yet names none as obligations — only "blocks un-reserving" in §Status.
Resolution criterion: add a companion-obligations paragraph enumerating them.

**7. NON-BLOCKING — `auth.kind` does not constrain delegated agents, but the flagship example implies it does.**
Evidence: `kind` is "kind of the effective principal," so a delegated agent posts as `kind: "human"`; the §Principal kinds example ("agents may read but not post in this channel") gates only autonomous agents, and 0056 inherits the same pattern ("humans only may be `host`" is satisfiable by a delegated agent). App authors will read the example as an anti-agent gate and be wrong.
Resolution criterion: state the limitation explicitly and decide whether rules get an actor-visible variable (`auth.isDelegated` / actor kind) or delegation is deliberately indistinguishable at the rules layer.

**8. NON-BLOCKING — token validity model internally tensioned; token/credential storage unenumerated.**
Evidence: §Credentials justifies the 24h hard max as "a token the server cannot reach outlives revocation by at most the same window," while §Revocation says "tokens are validated per invocation, so finite request/response work fails closed immediately on a revoked grant" — if per-invocation validation consults the grant, server-reachable revocation is immediate and the TTL is a theft-window bound, not a revocation bound (the lease analogy holds only for the retained/offline case). Also, only `_auth_grants` gets a field list; credential hashes and token records are implied by the lifecycle claims but never appear in the catalog enumeration.
Resolution criterion: pin opaque, hashed, per-invocation-validated tokens; recast the TTL rationale; enumerate the credential/token tables 0039-style.

**9. NON-BLOCKING — invited-mode minting authority under-specified.**
Evidence: "a single-use invitation minted by an authenticated human user" lets *any* app user create agent principals with no narrowing hook (the route is built-in, so app rules cannot gate it), and "mirroring `auth.mintSession`'s gating" leaves ambiguous whether the host-ops path inherits mintSession's dev/test/demo-only, refused-on-`prod` profile — which would make user-minted invitations the only production path.
Resolution criterion: pin who may mint (and whether apps can restrict it) and the host-ops path's profile matrix.

**10. NON-BLOCKING — the audit claim has no named carrier for plain HTTP app-data.**
Evidence: §Audit: "Every invocation envelope records the LLP 0019 F2 tuple … uniformly across HTTP, host-ops/CLI, and MCP." Host-ops/MCP have the 0024 envelope (`authority.actorPrincipal`/`effectivePrincipal` already exist in shape); ordinary HTTP app-data responses carry no such envelope, and 0039 pins "an auth event/audit trail beyond `identity_id` provenance" as a non-goal — so there is nothing defined to record into, and "recorded" (emitted vs retained) is unresolved (0024 says "emit **or retain**").
Resolution criterion: name the carrier and retention per projection, or scope the claim to the envelopes that exist plus a named follow-on.

## 4. Suggestions

- Add a landing decomposition keyed to 0039's activation matrix: finite request/response agent auth can land after item 5; grant-backed live surfaces only after item 6; delegated retained reads (lease-bounded) only after item 8. Also state plainly that `auth.agents.mode` is meaningful only under `auth.mode: "sessions"` (under dev mode, `/auth/*` is a typed 404).
- Scope grammar: state the action posture — `fn:query:*`/`fn:mutation:*` cover neither actions nor their egress; either add `fn:action:*` or pin that actions are reachable only via explicit `fn:<functionId>`.
- Pin the consent rendezvous minimally: the grant is created by the effective user's session naming actor and scopes; "the agent requests scopes" can be app-level UX or a device-code-shaped flow (RFC 8628) — the current wording implies a protocol without owning one.
- Say what an older session-aware binary does with a bumped `auth_catalog_version` (fail closed vs. serve-without-agents); the catalog is versioned precisely for this and one sentence closes it.
- Credential rotation vs. `_auth_identities` subject stability: subject = credential id, so rotation must keep the id and swap the secret, or the identity row churns — pin it.
- Export/import: imported agent users carry identities but no credentials, so imported grants are inert until re-credentialed; name the re-credentialing path or drop grants from export.
- Validation additions: a grant-cannot-widen negative (scope names a function whose RLS denies the effective user → still denied); the cross-credential mint negative; scoped-token-on-management-route negatives; a mint-rate throttle.
- Give `GET /auth/grants` a `lastUsedAt` per grant — cheap, and it is most of what makes user-facing revocation UX trustworthy.
- Non-standard idea worth a line in Open Questions: macaroon-style attenuation as the eventual answer to delegation chains — an agent could mint a *strictly narrower* offline attenuation of its own token without a server round trip, which composes with (rather than fights) the one-directional-narrowing invariant, and pairs naturally with the DPoP trajectory.
- Consider caps mirroring the 20-session cap: max live grants per user, max tokens per grant.

## 5. Open questions

The RFC's own list is good (attestation, sender-binding, scope growth, `open`-mode timing, delegation chains, `_auth_accounts`). Add:

- Is grant revocation an authority transition or an authorization event? (Concern 1's decision — it determines which machinery gets extended and what the client-side disposition is.)
- Should the rules layer be able to distinguish a delegated invocation from the user themself, or is indistinguishability the product position? (Concern 7; 0056 inherits the answer.)
- Who may mint invitations in `invited` mode, and what is the production posture of the host-ops mint path? (Concern 9.)
- Do agent principals ever need sessions (e.g. a future console-visible agent), or is credential+token the permanent shape?
- Which host-ops operations, exactly, will accept `servicePrincipal` when 0024 is amended? (Concern 4 forces this to be answered somewhere.)
- Are per-user grant counts and per-grant token counts bounded, and by what?

## 6. Readiness verdict

The architecture is sound, the corpus positioning is accurate (verified against 0019/0024/0042/0017 text and against `src/` — the reserved/built claims all check out), and the honesty posture is exemplary. But the document restates LLP 0053's Accepted contract incorrectly at its most security-relevant section (revocation), leaves the new credential classes without the surface/binding matrix this corpus has repeatedly shown is where auth holes live, leaves the operator-plane boundary implicit where its own Summary invites over-reading, extends 0053-pinned exact projections without naming the amendment, and under-protects its own long-lived credential class on declared restore. Under the precedence rule — where 0011/0039/0053 pin a contract, those documents win — concern 1 alone blocks; concerns 2–5 are each independently material and each cheaply resolvable without architectural change.

# Review: LLP 0055 — Agent principals and scoped grants (round 2)

**Reviewed revision:** sha256 `e5f377b47f96b2fa4eb4a8ce2ccbf61ce722205e85dfff110b7163db4788edae` (Draft, 2026-07-13). Read in full against LLP 0011, 0039, 0053 (Accepted), 0024, 0042, 0019, 0056, 0031, 0017, 0012, 0000, and the current `src/` implementation.

---

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a strong RFC whose central architecture I believe is right, and right in ways that were easy to get wrong. The three-way disentanglement in the Summary — operator plane vs. app principal vs. attestation — is the document's best move: it prevents "agents log in" from quietly becoming "agents hold admin authority," and it lets the honest attestation story (declared kind is honor-system; credentialed facts are strong) coexist with real security claims. Agents-as-users with an immutable `kind` column is the correct call and is argued correctly (a separate principals table would fork every `userId`-keyed surface). The effective-principal-as-`userId` projection with an `actor` block is the right delegation shape: existing app code, RLS, and memberships work unmodified, and the code that must distinguish can. Immutable grants are the load-bearing simplification — they make every grant lifecycle change an LLP 0039-style authority transition, which keeps LLP 0053's closed event universe closed and its trusted-denial enumeration unamended. I checked that seam carefully: the classification of grant/credential ends as authority transitions (delete, no epoch) genuinely follows 0053's own carve-out, and the RFC correctly routes the *effective user's* permission churn through ordinary 0053 machinery. The scope grammar (consent-time wildcard expansion for delegated grants; actions never wildcard-grantable; `fn:<id>` binding kind and failing closed on drift) shows real consent-integrity thinking. Composition with LLP 0056 is coherent in both directions, including the corrected `auth.kind` vs. `auth.isDelegated` example.

I verified the load-bearing factual claims. The `sbat_` admin token (`src/admin.rs:450`), `sbcc_` (`src/admin.rs:267`), and the `sbrk_`/`sbrl_`/`sbrp_`/`sbrm_` family (`src/admin_secret_read.rs`) exist as claimed, so the round-1 `sbat_` collision fix was real and the registered-prefix rule is well-founded; `FunctionDefinition.id` is `Option<String>` (`src/manifest.rs:564`), confirming the stable-function-id landing prerequisite; the host-ops envelope already carries the 0024 authority block with `actorPrincipal`/`effectivePrincipal`/`scope` (`src/host_ops.rs:112–124`); `ImpersonationCapability` exists as an effect policy (`src/server.rs:1423`); the 0024/0042 "reserved until LLP 0011 pins scoped tokens" quotes are verbatim; the 0019 F2 tuple and F8 attribution are accurate. One factual claim is false (`sbs_` is pinned by 0039 but minted nowhere in the repo — see Concerns).

What keeps this from READY is not the architecture but a cluster of gaps on the *ending* side of the authority lifecycle — ironic for a document whose stated purpose is authority that is "scoped, expiring, revocable." The RFC defines minting surfaces in meticulous detail and defines no revocation or deactivation surface at all: "operator revoke" and "agent deactivation" are named events with no verb, route, gating, or profile, and on the production profile there is no defined path to kill a rogue autonomous agent's grant or credential. Second, two token-authority-ending events the document itself creates (cap-eviction token revocation, rotation's generation bump) are absent from the live-connection projection contract. Third, the plane-boundary section contradicts itself and its own routes table about whether host-ops `function.run` can carry `servicePrincipal`. Fourth, a downgrade-protection claim rests on older-binary behavior that no document pins. All four are bounded edits; none requires redesign.

## 2. Strengths

- **The plane disentanglement** (Summary; §Plane boundary): operator plane explicitly not extended; `servicePrincipal` explicitly not an operator credential; the "an implementer must not honor `fn:mutation:*` for `app.reset`" sentence is exactly the right kind of pre-emptive pin, and it matches 0056's same wall from the other side.
- **Agents are users** (§Principal kinds): one id space, `kind` as a queryable immutable fact, with the alternative honestly weighed. Immutability also makes `auth.kind` subscribe-time-safe under 0011's rule model.
- **Delegation projection** (§Resolved authority): `userId` = effective principal is the load-bearing choice, stated as such; the `auth.kind` vs. `auth.actorKind`/`auth.isDelegated` distinction is called out as load-bearing and 0056 inherits it exactly; the sealed-`ctx.auth` obligation is correctly routed through a dated 0053 amendment rather than restated.
- **Immutable grants → clean invalidation classification** (§Grants; §Ending authority): revoke-and-remint eliminates the "permissions changed while authority lives" middle state; the authority-transition classification genuinely follows 0053's carve-out and leaves its closed event universe and trusted-denial enumeration untouched; `SNAPBACK_AUTH_GRANT_SCOPE_DENIED` deliberately not a `PERMISSION_DENIED` producer is exactly right.
- **Consent integrity** (§Grants): delegated wildcards expanded to the concrete function list at consent time so a later deployment cannot silently widen a user's approval; actions grantable only by explicit id; `fn:<id>` fails closed on kind drift.
- **Structurally enforced depth-1 delegation**: approval requires a live session of the effective user, and agents have no session path, so agent-to-agent chains are impossible by construction rather than by rule.
- **Prefix registry with cross-class dispatch** (§Credentials): the earlier `sbat_` collision was real (verified in code); structural class dispatch before resolver selection with a typed mismatch, never fall-through, is the right posture and the collision sweep is testable.
- **Honest attestation** (§Attestation, honestly): the declared-kind honor system is stated without pretense, and the credentialed-facts claim ("agent X acting for user Y under grant Z" is server-issued) is precisely scoped.
- **Honest audit narrowing** (§Audit): claims only the envelopes that exist (verified in `src/host_ops.rs`), names F8 as the follow-on, and pins the durable log as a precondition for hosted mutation beyond trusted-local so the gap "cannot be quietly shipped past."
- **Lifecycle/restore rigor** (§Catalog and lifecycle): declared restore revoking credentials *and grants* with the correct rationale (the long-lived credential is the resurrection vector); grants excluded from export with the dangling-authority argument.
- **Landing decomposition** (§Landing decomposition) keyed correctly to the 0039/0053 shared activation matrix, with finite work after item 5 and grant-backed live after item 6.
- **Validation Requirements** are unusually good: cross-class negatives, the prefix sweep, wrong-actor mint non-enumeration, kind-semantics byte-for-byte projection checks, delegated-wildcard-vs-autonomous divergence, queue/replay fencing, and the catalog-version downgrade row.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — No revocation or deactivation surface exists for credentials, autonomous grants, or agent principals; production has no defined kill path for a rogue autonomous agent.**
Evidence: §Credentials names "Revocation-class events (operator revoke, restore, deactivation)" — but no operator-revoke verb, deactivation verb, or route is defined anywhere in the document; §Routes lists only mint-side surfaces (register/token/rotate/grant-requests/invitation-mint) plus session-only `DELETE /auth/grants/{id}`. §Ending authority's contract ("Grant revocation, grant expiry, credential revocation, and agent deactivation all end an authority") and the Validation rows ("Credential revocation and agent deactivation cascade the same way"; "user deactivation does [end grants]") therefore test events with no defined trigger — and 0039's Non-goals explicitly exclude built-in user deletion, so "deactivating or deleting the *user*" is likewise unowned. Failure scenario: on `prod`, a compromised autonomous agent credential is unrevocable as specified — `DELETE /auth/grants` requires a session and an autonomous grant has no session-holding owner (agents cannot log in); every host-ops verb this RFC defines is dev/test/demo-only; the operator plane has no hosted carrier. The credential lives until grant expiry (which has no pinned bound — see Concern 6) with rotation available only to the credential holder, i.e., the attacker.
Resolution criterion: define the revocation/deactivation surfaces — e.g., host-ops `auth.revokeAgentCredential` / `auth.revokeGrant` / `auth.deactivateAgent` gated `AdminWrite` *without* `ImpersonationCapability` (destroying authority grants no identity and is fail-safe in direction, so the impersonation gate is not needed and prod availability becomes possible where an AdminWrite carrier exists), state their profile availability explicitly including the prod story, and add matching validation rows. Alternatively, state plainly that production revocation of autonomous authority is impossible in v1 and gate `auth.agents` on non-prod — but say which.

**2. MATERIAL — Token revocation (cap eviction) and rotation's generation bump end token authority but are absent from the live-connection projection contract.**
Evidence: §Ending authority projects pending-revoked onto bound connections only for "Grant revocation, credential revocation, and agent deactivation." §Credentials pins "live tokens per grant (default 10; minting the 11th revokes the oldest — the session-cap pattern)" and "Rotation … bumps `credentialGeneration`," and the connection deadline is `min(token expires_at, grant expires_at)` — a revoked token's `expires_at` is unchanged, so revocation does not shorten the deadline. In 0039, the borrowed session-cap pattern's eviction *is* a revocation-class event that projects through the two-phase protocol; here the pattern's security half went missing. Failure scenario: a thief holding a scoped token opens a live connection; the agent rotates (its only self-service response — see Concern 1) or the token is cap-evicted; per-invocation validation kills finite work, but the thief's live stream continues to the deadline — up to 24 hours for a max-TTL token — while the document elsewhere insists "the TTL is a theft-window bound, not the revocation mechanism." Separately, the replay fence refuses a "rotated-away credential generation" while nothing states whether a live connection bound under that generation is fenced, so an implementer must guess between killing live streams on hygiene rotation (availability surprise, unstated) and serving under ended authority (security gap, unstated).
Resolution criterion: extend the pending-revoked projection list to token revocation and rotation (or explicitly state the bounded residual for each and why it is accepted), and add validation rows: cap-eviction mid-connection, and rotation mid-connection, each with the pinned outcome.

**3. MATERIAL — The plane boundary contradicts itself and the routes table on whether host-ops `function.run` accepts `servicePrincipal`.**
Evidence, three sites: (a) §Plane boundary authorizes "the `function.run` / `function.explain` projections over generated app functions (CLI/MCP included)" — and per 0024 (ENG-24561), CLI `snapback run` maps to host-ops `function.run`, whose route policy is `AdminRead`/`AdminWrite`; (b) the same paragraph says "everything gated `AdminRead`/`AdminWrite`/… **refuse `servicePrincipal`**"; (c) §Routes pins the app-data invocation gate and `POST /connection/open` as "the only two surfaces scoped tokens address" — yet the §Validation audit row asserts "the F2 tuple is present and identical in shape across **host-ops**, CLI, and MCP invocations of the same function under `servicePrincipal`," which requires a host-ops invocation to run under `servicePrincipal` at all. Failure scenario: an implementer wires host-ops `function.run` to accept `--service-principal <token-ref>` (0024's reserved flag), creating a third scoped-token surface on an admin-gated, loopback-tokenless-adjacent route; or conversely refuses the CLI/MCP projections that §Plane boundary promises.
Resolution criterion: one pin stating the transport — e.g., "CLI/MCP `servicePrincipal` invocations are carried over the app-data invocation gate with the scoped token as the sole credential; the admin-gated host-ops `function.run` operation itself never resolves a scoped token" — and reconcile the audit validation row's "host-ops" wording with it (envelope shape parity can still be required of the gate-carried invocation).

**4. MATERIAL — The catalog-version downgrade claim asserts older-binary behavior that no document pins, and the fix is missing from the companion obligations.**
Evidence: §Catalog and lifecycle claims "An older **session-aware** binary that opens a database whose `auth_catalog_version` exceeds what it understands fails closed at open with a typed diagnostic (the version marker exists precisely for this)." LLP 0039 pins only that the marker exists ("what the collision check trusts and future migrations key on"); it nowhere requires a session-aware binary to *refuse at open* a higher version, and 0039's collision check runs at the sessions transition, not on every open. Nothing of item 3 is built yet (no `_auth_users` in `src/`), so the check can still be injected — but only if some document owns it, and §Companion obligations' dated-0039-amendment list (Authorization widening, connection-binding variant, restore list, descriptor axes) omits it. This is precisely the "a new field cannot bind a receiver that predates it" class 0039 spends pages on. Failure scenario: item 3 ships without the `>`-version refusal; agents later land; a rollback to the item-3 binary serves the database "without agents," silently stranding live grants — the exact hazard this section names.
Resolution criterion: add to the dated 0039 amendments: "session-aware binaries refuse at open any `auth_catalog_version` above their understanding, from the first catalog-creating binary onward," and soften "exists precisely for this" to match the actual provenance.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — "The repository already mints `sbs_`" is false.**
Evidence: `sbs_` appears nowhere in `src/`, `tests/`, or `packages/` (verified by repo-wide search excluding vendor); it is a 0039-pinned, unbuilt prefix. `sbat_`, `sbcc_`, and the `sbrk_`/`sbrl_`/`sbrp_`/`sbrm_` family are real (and note `sbrp_`/`sbrm_` are a nonce and a MAC, not bearer credentials — the cross-class sweep should classify them accordingly). Resolution: reword to "already mints or pins," and mark which prefixes are bearer-class.

**6. NON-BLOCKING — Grant lifetime policy is absent despite the Goals promising "TTL'd" grants.**
Evidence: `_auth_grants.expires_at` exists and the token mint clamps to it, but nothing states whether `expires_at` is required, who chooses it (app policy? user at consent? requester?), or whether unbounded grants are permitted; every other credential class in this document and 0053 carries an explicit default and hard maximum. Resolution: pin requiredness and defaults (or explicitly bless indefinite grants with rationale), including what the consent UI shows.

**7. NON-BLOCKING — `created_by` tag vocabulary cannot label the production autonomous-grant path.**
Evidence: §Grants enumerates `session:<id>`, `hostOps:<verb>`, `startup` — a template grant applied at registration/re-credentialing (the stated production path) is created by none of these. Resolution: add a `registration`/`invitation:<id>` tag.

**8. NON-BLOCKING — The grant cap is ambiguous on which principal it counts and what happens at the cap.**
Evidence: "live grants per user (default 50, counting autonomous and delegated together)" — autonomous grants have only an actor, implying an actor-side count, in which case one agent can serve at most 50 delegators (a real product ceiling worth stating); and the behavior at cap (consent approval refused? template application refused? typed code?) is unstated while the token cap gets explicit eviction semantics. Resolution: name the counted side, the at-cap behavior, and its diagnostic.

**9. NON-BLOCKING — `invitedBy: "users"` has no per-user minting cap or throttle.**
Evidence: §Registration caps pending grant requests per agent and throttles the register route, but any authenticated user may mint invitations with no named budget — a mass-principal-creation lever on the only production minting path. Resolution: a per-user invitation budget/throttle constant alongside the other v1 knobs.

**10. NON-BLOCKING — The new auth variables' interaction with `Retainable ⇒ userId-only` is unclassified.**
Evidence: 0039 pins retainability to declared `userId`-only authority dependence; `auth.kind` is `userId`-derived (immutable) and compatible, but `auth.actorKind`/`auth.isDelegated` are not functions of `userId` (the same effective user reaches a result via session or delegation). Unreachable in v1 (service principals are online-only), but the classification should be pinned before item 8 makes it load-bearing. Resolution: one sentence in the 0039 amendment list classifying the three variables for retainability.

**11. NON-BLOCKING — The `{ grantRequestId }` mint path lacks the actor-equality pin and validation row.**
Evidence: the mint predicate is stated for `{ grantId }` ("grant.actor_user_id equals the credential's principal"); the poll path's non-enumeration list covers denied/expired/nonexistent but not a *foreign* request, and Validation's wrong-actor row covers grants only. Resolution: extend the predicate and the wrong-actor validation row to grant requests; also add the session-side pending-request enumeration route to the routes table (approval is listed; discovery is not).

## 4. Suggestions

- State the default-configuration consequence plainly: `invited` + `invitedBy: "operators"` (the defaults) makes agent onboarding on `prod` impossible, since the operator verb is profile-refused there. An app enabling agents should be forced to notice this at manifest time, not at deploy time.
- Give agents a self-service kill switch: a credential-authenticated `revoke-self` (credential suicide) on the credential routes. Rotation is the wrong theft response when the thief may hold the current secret; self-revocation needs no operator and composes with re-credentialing.
- Consider a delegator-side "revoke all grants to actor X" operation — the realistic emergency UX is "cut this agent off," not per-grant deletion.
- Register the poll's "typed still-pending signal" as a named code in the LLP 0012 obligation alongside the three named diagnostics.
- Give `sbagt_` the same `+ CRC` treatment `sbac_` gets (currently only the credential prefix is stated as checksummed).
- Make the session projection's `kind` "the user's kind" rather than the literal `"human"`, even though no agent session path exists in v1 — it costs nothing and won't rot if a provider ever authenticates an agent-kind user.
- State that grant-backed live surfaces after item 6 require the agent client to negotiate `authInvalidationProtocol: 1` like any session client, and name who owns the agent-client clearing coordinator (the browser kit's `AuthCoordinator` is not the natural home for a CLI/daemon agent client; grant-scoped — not client-wide — delete needs an owner).
- Have the 0024/0042 amendment text itself carry the §Audit precondition (durable log before hosted mutation beyond trusted-local), so the gate travels with the un-reservation rather than living only here.
- Define how `auth.kind`/`auth.actorKind`/`auth.isDelegated` resolve under `devImpersonation` (today: unstated; the sealed dev projection has no `kind`). Local sessions mode is a workable test path, but a cheap dev-authority answer (default human/false, optional `--as-agent`) would keep kind-gated rules testable in the cheapest loop.
- Non-standard idea: make the token-mint response carry the grant's resolved scope list and expiries (a self-describing "grant receipt"). Agents planning multi-step work can self-check capability without a query surface, and it dovetails with the macaroon-attenuation trajectory already in Open Questions.

## 5. Open questions

- Who may revoke an autonomous grant or agent credential in production, through what carrier, and does that answer arrive with this RFC or with the hosted-operator-principal document? (Concern 1 forces this; the RFC currently has no answer at any profile above dev/test/demo.)
- Is rotation intended to be revocation-strength for outstanding tokens and live connections (generation bump fences them) or hygiene-strength (they run to deadline)? The choice has opposite availability/security consequences and the document must own one. (Concern 2.)
- Should grant `expires_at` be mandatory with a default/hard-max, and who chooses it at consent? (Concern 6.)
- When agent invitations need real delivery (not out-of-band handoff), does the 0045 outbox carry them, and does the invitation secret inherit the capture-class/durable-intent constraint 0039 pins for auth codes?
- How does a delegator *discover* a pending grant request (push vs. poll), and is that surface part of the 0031 registration this RFC obligates?
- Does the F2 audit tuple gain a field distinguishing autonomous from delegated at the envelope level, or is `effectivePrincipal: null` the encoding?

## 6. Readiness verdict

The architecture is sound, corpus-fluent, and in several places genuinely elegant — the plane disentanglement, immutable grants riding 0053's authority-transition carve-out, consent-time wildcard expansion, and structurally-enforced depth-1 delegation would all survive contact with implementation. Factual claims check out against code and the governing documents with one wording-level exception. But four MATERIAL items remain: the missing revocation/deactivation surfaces (a real hole in a document whose purpose is revocable authority, with no production kill path at all), the token-revocation/rotation live-connection gap, the three-site plane-boundary contradiction about host-ops `function.run`, and the unowned catalog-version downgrade pin. All are resolvable with bounded edits and none reopens the design, but each is a correctness, safety, or coherence defect an implementer would have to guess through.

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a good proposal and, in most of its architecture, an excellent one. The question LLP 0019 F2 posed — machine identity with scoped capability tokens, distinguishable in audit — is answered with the right decomposition: the §Summary disentangling of *operator plane* / *app principal* / *attestation* is the single best framing move in the document, and it prevents the classic failure where "agent auth" quietly becomes a second operator credential. The three load-bearing structural choices are all correct and well-argued: agents as `_auth_users` rows with a `kind` column (one id space, so profile/membership/RLS surfaces need no fork — and the retainability analysis of `auth.kind` vs `auth.actorKind`/`auth.isDelegated` is exactly the kind of second-order care 0039/0053 demand); the effective principal as `ctx.auth.userId` with an `actor` block (delegated agents run through unmodified app code, and "was this the person themself" is checkable); and **grants immutable in v1**, which is what makes the authority-transition classification sound — because a grant can only end, never narrow, the RFC genuinely stays out of LLP 0053's authorization-event universe, and its reading of 0053's carve-out ("one ends an identity, the other narrows a permission") is verified correct against 0053's actual text.

Corpus consistency is very high. I verified the claims that most often rot: the prefix registry matches code (`sbat_` at `src/admin.rs:450`, `sbcc_` at `src/admin.rs:267`, the `sbrk_`/`sbrl_`/`sbrp_`/`sbrm_` family in `src/admin_secret_read.rs` with `sbrp_`/`sbrm_` correctly described as nonce/MAC; `sbs_` and `servicePrincipal` absent from `src/` exactly as "pinned, not minted"/"reserved" claim; the registered set is mutually prefix-free); `FunctionDefinition.id` is indeed `Option<String>` (`src/manifest.rs:564`), so the stable-id prerequisite is real; `auth.mintSession` gating is quoted accurately from 0039; the sealed-`ctx.auth` obligation correctly routes through a dated 0053 amendment rather than pretending extra keys are possible; the landing decomposition matches 0039's activation matrix item-for-item; and the 0056 composition (kind conjunction, delegated memberships, sibling profile amendment) is coherent from both sides.

What keeps this from READY is not the architecture but three contained defects. First, the route census — presented as the complete new surface, with pinned consequences derived from it — is missing surfaces its own prose requires: the user-session invitation mint path (`invitedBy: "users"`), any invitation revocation, and any producer of the consent state machine's `denied` state. This matters because the document's own round-2 theme was "authority-ending surfaces defined... closing the missing production kill path," and it explicitly claims "Nothing in this document's ending contract lacks a caller" — yet invitations are a 7-day, principal-creating spendable secret with no kill switch, on the one profile (prod, `invitedBy: "users"`) where they are the only onboarding path. Second, the queued-replay settlement tuple includes `tokenId`, which — as literally pinned — strands queued work on routine token expiry and cap eviction, contradicting the corpus's own hard-won carrier-vs-authority distinction (0039 deliberately made queued mutations survive bearer rotation, and separately fixed the per-boot-id variant of exactly this mistake). Third, the servicePrincipal audit story is internally inconsistent: all three projections ride envelope-less app-data routes, §Audit honestly narrows plain-HTTP attribution to denials/dry-runs, yet the Goals and a validation row claim the F2 tuple is "present and identical in shape across the CLI, HTTP, and MCP projections" with no specified server-derived carrier for the success path.

All three are fixable with bounded text changes; none reopens the architecture. Do we have a good plan? Yes — the landing decomposition, companion-obligation list, and validation grid are unusually disciplined, and the hosted gate traveling inside the 0024/0042 amendment text is exactly how to keep scope honesty from being quietly shipped past. With the concerns below resolved, this document would be ready to review-exit.

## 2. Strengths

- **§Summary — the three-way disentangling.** Operator plane (explicitly not extended), app principal (autonomous vs delegated), attestation (honor-system declared kind vs strong credentialed facts). This framing prevents scope creep in both directions and is restated as enforceable pins in §Plane boundary.
- **§Principal kinds — agents as users.** One id space with `kind` as a queryable fact; the alternative's cost (forking every `userId`-keyed surface) is stated; the `auth.kind`/`auth.actorKind`/`auth.isDelegated` semantics are exact, the "person themself" conjunction is right (and 0056 consumes it verbatim), and the retainability classification (`kind` is a pure function of immutable `userId`; the other two are not) correctly anticipates item 8.
- **§Resolved authority — effective principal as `userId`.** App code, RLS, and membership checks written for humans work unmodified for delegated agents; the sealed-projection constraint from 0053 is respected via a named dated amendment rather than hand-waved; projections are given byte-exactly.
- **§Grants — immutability.** "Created, then revoked or expired — never edited" collapses the surviving-authority-with-narrower-permissions case, which is what makes §Ending authority's transition classification genuinely consistent with 0053's carve-out rather than a convenient relabeling. Verified against 0053's own text and its event-4 boundary.
- **§Grants — delegated wildcard expansion at consent.** Tier scopes are expanded to concrete `{fn, kind}` at consent so a later deployment cannot silently widen what a user approved; actions are never wildcard-grantable; manifest activation re-validates scope bases and fails closed on semantic drift. This is the best-designed part of the scope model.
- **§Registration — the creation/recovery invitation class split.** Structural (enforced at mint and consumption), closing the recredentialing-takeover path; the prod honesty ("no grant-preserving recovery in v1... users re-consent") is the right trade stated plainly.
- **§Routes / §Ending authority — the production kill path.** Operator revocation/deactivation verbs on every profile with `AdminWrite` and deliberately no impersonation gate, with the correct rationale (destroying authority grants no identity and fails safe) — consistent with the corpus's existing posture that destructive-but-safe operations (e.g. `app.reset`) sit at `AdminWrite`.
- **§Credentials — prefix hygiene and class dispatch.** The registered, collision-checked, prefix-free namespace matches shipped code; the earlier `sbat_` collision is fixed; `sbs_` is correctly described as pinned-not-minted; class dispatch before resolver selection with a typed mismatch (never fall-through) extends 0039's credential-precedence discipline cleanly.
- **§Attestation, honestly.** Declared kind is honor-system and the document says exactly what the declaration buys anyway. This section will age well.
- **§Landing decomposition.** Correctly keyed to 0039's activation matrix (finite after 5, live after 6, queued after 7, retained item-8/never), with the kit refusing offline enqueue before item 7 — matching 0039's item-5→7 posture for sessions.
- **§Companion obligations.** The full amendment fan-out (0024/0042 with the hosted gate carried in the amendment text, 0053 sealed enumeration, 0039 dated amendments, 0011, 0031, 0012, 0004, 0056) is enumerated rather than implied; this is how the corpus's document-precedence rules stay true.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The route census is missing three surfaces its own prose requires (§Registration, §Grants, §Routes).**
Evidence: (a) §Registration pins invitations "minted by any authenticated human user's session when `invitedBy: "users"` — ... the only production-profile minting path," with a per-user budget "plus a route budget" — but §Routes ("The new surface") contains no user-session invitation-mint route; only the operator verb `auth.mintAgentInvitation` (dev/test/demo, prod-refused) exists. As written, the only production onboarding path has no surface. (b) No invitation revocation or enumeration surface exists anywhere: a user-minted creation invitation is a 7-day, principal-creating bearer secret (potentially conferring the app's template autonomous scopes, including `fn:mutation:*`), and once leaked it cannot be killed before consumption — despite §Ending authority's claim that "Nothing in this document's ending contract lacks a caller," and despite the same revision having thought about invitation lifecycle for declared restore. It also permanently occupies the 10-per-user budget until TTL. (c) §Consent flow step 4 distinguishes "denial or expiry," and `_auth_grant_requests` carries `denied_at` — but no route or verb produces denial (§Routes has `approve` only), so the state machine contains an unreachable terminal state.
Resolution criterion: add the user-session invitation mint route to §Routes with its credential class and budget; add invitation enumeration/revocation surfaces (user: own invitations; operator: `AdminWrite`, all profiles — same fails-safe rationale as the other kill verbs) with validation rows; add a request-denial surface (or explicitly define denial as expiry-only and delete `denied_at` and the "denial" wording).

**2. MATERIAL — `tokenId` in the queued-replay settlement tuple strands queues on routine token churn (§Resolved authority "Replay, queues, and stores bind the full authority"; Validation §Ending authority "Queue/replay fencing").**
Evidence: "work recorded under `(actorUserId, credentialId, credentialGeneration, grantId, tokenId)` settles only under that same tuple." Tokens default to a 1-hour TTL (24h max) and are cap-evicted (minting the 11th revokes the oldest). Under the literal reading, any queue that outlives its token — the normal case for the offline windows item 7 exists to serve — can never settle and strands as discard-or-re-record; cap eviction strands queues even while online. This conflates the transient *carrier* with the durable *authority* (the grant + credential generation), the exact distinction 0039 built: session refresh deliberately preserves queued mutations ("same session id, same generation, same `authorityInstanceId` — so healthy connections and queued mutations survive"), and 0039 separately corrected the dev-mode per-boot-id variant of this mistake because it "would strand the client's queued mutations on every `cargo run`." The text is also internally ambiguous: the "never under" negatives enumerate a direct session, another grant, another credential, and a rotated generation — but not another token — suggesting the equality axis may not have been meant to include `tokenId` at all. (Contrast: including `tokenId` in the *connection* binding tuple is correct — a connection is opened with a specific token and should die with it.)
Resolution criterion: pin the settlement-equality axis explicitly and deliberately — either `(actorUserId, credentialId, credentialGeneration, grantId)` with `tokenId` recorded for audit but not compared, or a stated justification for token-scoped settlement that reconciles with the 1-hour TTL, cap eviction, and 0039's precedent — and make the validation row match.

**3. MATERIAL — The success-path audit carrier for servicePrincipal app-data invocation is unspecified, and the Goals/validation claims contradict §Audit (§Goals; §Plane boundary; §Audit; Validation §Audit and projections).**
Evidence: §Plane boundary pins that CLI and MCP servicePrincipal invocations "ride the same gate... the MCP server presents the agent's token to the app-data routes," and that host-ops `function.run` (the surface that carries LLP 0024's envelope and its authority block) "never resolves a scoped token." §Audit then states "Plain HTTP app-data responses carry no envelope today... for those requests v1 promises attribution in denial diagnostics and dry-run facts, not a per-request envelope." Yet §Goals claims the tuple is "recorded uniformly across the CLI, HTTP, and MCP projections," and the validation row demands "The F2 tuple is present and identical in shape across the CLI, HTTP, and MCP projections of the same app-data function under `servicePrincipal`." Since all three projections traverse the same envelope-less app-data routes, no specified server-derived surface carries the tuple on success; any envelope a CLI or MCP wrapper emits would be client-synthesized, making its authority block an assertion rather than the server-attested fact §Attestation relies on ("which no honor-system flag can fake because the grant chain is server-issued"). As written the validation row is unimplementable or would be satisfied by untrusted packaging.
Resolution criterion: pin one answer — either (a) a server-derived attribution fact on servicePrincipal app-data responses (a typed response block/header carrying actor/effective/grant, named as new surface with its response-sensitivity classification), or (b) narrow the goal and the validation row to what §Audit actually promises (denial diagnostics and dry-run facts, identical in shape across projections) until LLP 0019 F8 lands — and in either case state who constructs the CLI/MCP envelopes and from which server-attested source their authority block is copied.

**4. NON-BLOCKING — Grant-scoped `delete` is cited to "LLP 0053's authority-transition rule," but 0053's rule is client-wide (§Ending authority).**
Evidence: 0053 pins "An authority transition under LLP 0039 always uses client-wide `delete`," and its carve-out enumerates logout/session-expiry/admin-session-revocation. This RFC extends the transition class to grant/credential/token endings — correctly — but applies "client-side `delete` disposition for the agent client's affected state" and later "grant-scoped `delete` rather than the browser's client-wide story," attributing the scoping to 0053. The scoped variant is sensible for multi-grant clients (up to 50 live grants per actor) and fail-closed per grant, but it is a 0055-defined narrowing of a rule neither 0039 nor 0053 states, currently housed only in unnamed "agent-SDK work."
Resolution criterion: state explicitly that grant-scoped delete for the new transition class is defined by this RFC (or by the item-6 agent-SDK document), not inherited from 0053, and either add it to a dated amendment list or name its owning document.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — The 0053 amendment's coverage of dev impersonation and the auth-variable IR forms is under-specified (§Principal kinds; §Companion obligations).**
Evidence: the amendment names "the two `servicePrincipal` projections and `kind` on the session projection," but §Principal kinds also assigns dev-impersonation defaults (`kind = "human"`, etc.), and 0053's sealed dev-impersonation projection (`{userId, sessionId:null, isAuthenticated:true, roles:[...]}`) is byte-exact — whether `ctx.auth.kind` exists under `devImpersonation` is unstated, and the validation row demands byte-for-byte fixture matches. Separately, `auth.kind`/`auth.actorKind`/`auth.isDelegated` as *rule expressions* are new IR expression kinds, which 0053's profile denies by default until classified; "the protected-query input profile covers them" is ambiguous about whether it covers the ValueExpr forms (today only `auth.userId` exists in the IR). 0056's sibling amendment spells its expression-kind classification explicitly; this RFC should match that precision.
Resolution criterion: the amendment bullet enumerates every projection it touches (including the dev-impersonation answer) and explicitly classifies the three auth-variable expression kinds.

**6. NON-BLOCKING — No redaction or response-sensitivity treatment for the three new secret classes (§Credentials; §Routes; Validation).**
Evidence: 0039 pins credential responses as no-store (`/auth/exchange`, `/auth/refresh`) and requires planted-sentinel redaction tests ("tokens, codes, and keyring material never appear in logs, diagnostics, or details, hashed or plain"). This RFC mints three new secret classes (`sbac_`, `sbagt_`, `sbagi_`) returned by register/token/rotate/recovery responses, but §Routes marks no response as credential-sensitive/no-store and the validation grid has a prefix/resolver sweep but no redaction sentinels for the new classes across logs, diagnostics, denial details, and the gated inspection twin.
Resolution criterion: mark the credential-bearing responses in §Routes (or delegate explicitly to the 0031 revision's response-sensitivity axis) and add planted-sentinel redaction validation rows for all three classes.

**7. NON-BLOCKING — The mint route collapses credential-state failures into `GRANT_INVALID`, hiding facts from the party that already holds the credential (§Grants "Mint predicate").**
Evidence: "Any failure is the one non-enumerating `SNAPBACK_AUTH_GRANT_INVALID`" — including a revoked or rotated-away credential. Telling the *presenter* of a credential that it is dead leaks nothing (they hold it), and the corpus deliberately discriminates such states for client recovery (0039's `AUTH_REQUIRED` vs `SESSION_EXPIRED` vs `CREDENTIAL_SUPERSEDED`). The RFC itself discloses "revoked credential" as a reason kind at the invocation gate, so the mint-route collapse is inconsistent polarity and gives an agent wrong recovery guidance (retry vs re-register).
Resolution criterion: split credential-liveness failures at the mint route into a typed credential-state error (non-enumerating with respect to *grants* only), or record why the collapse is intended.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — Production autonomous-grant re-application is internally contradictory; recovered/imported autonomous agents are permanently grantless on `prod`.**
Evidence: §Recovery: "applying it is a separate, separately-audited operator step (`auth.mintAgentGrant`) or an app-policy decision" and "Recovery is also the answer to … **production autonomous-scope evolution** (recover, then apply the current template as an explicit step)." But §Grants and the routes table pin `auth.mintAgentGrant` as "`AdminWrite` + `ImpersonationCapability`, dev/test/demo, **refused on `prod`**." No other autonomous-grant creator exists post-registration (the template applies only at registration; "app-policy decision" names no route), so on `prod` the promised explicit application step cannot be executed. This also silently breaks §Catalog's import story — "imported principals recover on any profile" restores the credential but never the autonomous authority — and Validation's "imported principals recover on any profile" row would pass while the agent remains unusable.
Resolution criterion: make the three statements consistent. Either (a) allow `auth.mintAgentGrant` on `prod` behind the same dual carrier as `auth.mintAgentRecovery` — the justification is identical (it is the continuity path's second half, and it is authority-creating in exactly the class the impersonation carrier exists to gate) — updating the routes table, §Grants, and Validation accordingly; or (b) define a concrete prod-available application surface (e.g., a recovery-consumption option or an app-declared route) and pin its gating; or (c) retract the production autonomous-scope-evolution claim and state the limitation plainly in §Recovery, §Catalog, and Validation.

**2. MATERIAL — `auth.mintAgentRecovery` on `prod` contradicts LLP 0039's pinned prod-impersonation posture, and the dated 0039 amendment list omits the carve-out.**
Evidence: LLP 0039's auth-mode matrix pins the sessions×prod cell as "sessions enforced; **no impersonation**," and its Acceptance pins "every impersonation effect is refused on `prod`." LLP 0055's routes table pins `auth.mintAgentRecovery` as "`AdminWrite` + `ImpersonationCapability` … **recovery class; all profiles**," and §Recovery argues the exception ("available on every profile including `prod` — unlike `mintSession`"). The argument is sound, but §Companion obligations' dated 0039 amendments list ("`Authorization` widened…; connection binding…; restore's revocation list…; `--allow-auth-downgrade`…; the item-7 descriptor…; the auth-variable retainability classification; and the catalog-version refuse-at-open rule") carries no amendment to the prod cell or the acceptance sentence. Under the corpus rule this review is charged to enforce — where this RFC restates a contract 0039 pins, 0039 wins — a conforming implementation refuses the verb on `prod`, and the round-4 recovery design collapses on the profile it was built for. The document's own standard elsewhere ("the 0024/0042 amendment text carries both gates so they travel") shows exactly what is missing here.
Resolution criterion: add to the dated LLP 0039 amendments an explicit item amending the sessions×prod impersonation posture — a named carve-out for the recovery-class verb (and whatever concern 1's resolution adds) with its rationale — so 0039's matrix and acceptance sentence are updated rather than contradicted; mirror it in the 0031 registry-row obligation.

**3. MATERIAL — the `authorityCreating` wildcard exclusion rests on statically deciding "writes a declared membership table," which the corpus's own accepted standard says is unsound; the dynamic host-gate twin is unpinned.**
Evidence: §Grants: "a function that writes a declared LLP 0056 membership table (or is marked `authorityCreating` in the manifest) is **excluded from tier wildcards**." Membership writes flow through 0056's generated per-declaration methods (raw writes refused), which are runtime host-mediated operations reachable through aliasing, computed access, or data-dependent branches — exactly the constructs Accepted LLP 0053 refuses to trust static scanning for ("load-time analysis rejects a statically visible call; **the host gate rejects a dynamically reached call**"; acceptance: "aliases and computed property access are tested, not accepted as a static-scanner limitation"). Failure scenario: an Ibex mutation reaches `eventMembers.insert` through an alias; codegen's derivation misses it; the function is unmarked and included in a delegated mutation-tier expansion at consent; the agent invokes it, the effective user's role satisfies the generated method's `require` check, and the agent seats its own actor as `host` — the durable privilege surviving revocation that this marker exists to prevent. Neither §Grants, Validation ("unreachable via tier wildcards" would be tested only with statically visible fixtures), nor §Protocol obligations names the runtime twin.
Resolution criterion: pin the two-gate rule at design altitude — the membership-write host operation refuses, typed, when invoked under a `servicePrincipal` authority whose grant covers the invoking function only via a tier scope rather than an explicit `authorityCreating` consent (equivalently: an undeclared/unmarked function that dynamically reaches a membership write fails the host gate) — and add the dynamically-reached negative (alias/computed-access fixture) to Validation Requirements or, at minimum, to §Protocol obligations as a named obligation.

**4. NON-BLOCKING — "a function gaining the marker terminally invalidates every tier scope that previously covered it" is ambiguous between two readings that differ materially in blast radius.**
Evidence: §Grants (consent-binding paragraph). For delegated grants, tier scopes were expanded at consent, so "the tier scope covering it" is one concrete `{fn, kind}` entry; for autonomous grants the tier wildcard is a single live scope record, and grants are immutable ("narrowing means revoke-and-remint"), so the literal reading kills the **entire** mutation-tier wildcard of every autonomous grant when any one function gains the marker, while the narrower reading (exclude the function from wildcard coverage) is an in-place narrowing the immutability rule forbids. Both readings are fail-closed, but implementers and app operators need to know which availability cliff they are on.
Resolution criterion: one disambiguating sentence choosing a reading (the whole-entry terminal invalidation is the one consistent with grant immutability; if chosen, state the autonomous-wildcard consequence plainly) plus a matching Validation clause.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — two lifecycle constants the design depends on are unpinned where every comparable knob is pinned.**
Evidence: §Credentials pins the tombstoned-family horizon only as "a pinned horizon (the credential's tombstone window, mirroring `_auth_session_tokens` retention to absolute expiry)" — but agent credentials, unlike sessions, have **no absolute expiry** to anchor that window, so the mirror does not define a value; and pairing *requests* (as distinct from the 10-minute code) have sweep-on-expiry behavior with no stated TTL. "Scope entries and bytes per grant: bounded" likewise carries no numbers beside the otherwise-numeric caps list.
Resolution criterion: pin v1 constants (or explicit derivations) for the family-hash horizon, the pending-request TTL, and the scope-entry/byte bounds, alongside the existing caps.

**6. NON-BLOCKING — the browser-reachable permanent-destruction residual is unnamed, against the corpus's named-residuals posture.**
Evidence: routes table: `auth.revokeGrant`/`auth.revokeAgentCredential`/`auth.deactivateAgent` are "`AdminWrite`, **no impersonation gate** … all profiles," and LLP 0017 pins that a browser-held console credential satisfies ordinary `AdminWrite`; §Principal kinds pins deactivation as "**permanent in v1** (no reactivate verb)" and §Recovery pins "a **deactivated** agent cannot be recovered." Composition: an app-origin XSS holding the console credential can permanently and unrecoverably destroy every agent principal, on `prod` included — a strictly stronger destructive primitive than anything previously on the browser-reachable plane (reset at least leaves re-provisioning possible). The fail-safe direction is right and consistent with 0039's posture; the omission is that the residual is not stated the way 0039 states its standing residuals.
Resolution criterion: name it (one sentence in §Ending authority or a residuals note), and/or answer whether permanence plus recovery-blocking is worth a softer terminal shape (see Suggestions).

**7. NON-BLOCKING — "Rotation … cannot lock the owner out" overclaims; the lost-rotate-response path ends in service lockout with operator recovery as the only exit.**
Evidence: §Credentials. If the rotate response is lost, the owner holds only a tombstoned family secret; `revoke-self` is available but is destroy-only, so the honest statement is "cannot lose the ability to destroy the family," not "cannot be locked out." LLP 0039 states the analogous lost-refresh-response case explicitly ("the session is unrecoverable by design — re-authentication is the v1 answer"); this document should mirror that honesty, noting that for agents "re-authentication" means operator recovery (impersonation carrier, and on `prod` — see concern 2).

## 4. Suggestions

- **Close the hosted-management-routes question as refused-in-v1.** Leaving registration/mint/consent conditionally open on hosted targets lets durable consent accumulate under a "can never be invoked" regime and spring alive when hosted invocation is later un-refused. Refusing them now is one row; alternatively, pin that the future un-refusing amendment terminally revokes (or forces re-consent for) grants created before hosted invocation existed.
- **State the invited-mode lost-registration-response consequence**: the single-use `sbagi_` is consumed, the secret is gone, and the remedy is a fresh invitation (budget permitting) — one sentence, mirroring the lost-mint-response treatment.
- **Specify the disposition of the superseded `_auth_identities` row after recovery** (new subject row, same principal): removed, or retained as historical identity data? Cosmetic, but export and uniqueness readers will ask.
- **Add two Validation rows**: (a) declaring `auth.agents.*` under `dev` mode is a manifest-load error (pinned in §Registration, untested); (b) a per-route throttle sweep for the new route family, since "carries its own throttle budget" is otherwise only spot-tested.
- **Name the agent-fleet custody consequence**: one-live-credential plus revocation-strength rotation means replicated agent instances sharing a credential must coordinate rotation out of band (there is no grace-window/shared-custody contract as there is for human bearers, deliberately). One sentence prevents an implementer from inventing a grace window that reopens the theft path.
- **Name the deadline-convergence cost for agent live surfaces**: with a 1-hour default token TTL and `deadline = min(token, grant)`, agent connections die and mandatorily resnapshot hourly. Deliberate and safe, but worth stating so it is a choice rather than a discovery.
- **Consider a consent-time credential fingerprint**: display names are self-declared and `registration_provenance` is not attestation; showing a short fingerprint of the agent's credential id at the consent moment (which the agent can display out of band, SAS-style) would cheaply harden the "which agent is asking" gap and the pairing-code-interception case without solving attestation.
- **Consider softening deactivation's permanence** without weakening fail-safety: a terminal-after-sweep shape (deactivate immediately ends all authority; the no-recovery property becomes irreversible after a pinned window) would shrink the concern-6 residual while keeping the kill path browser-fast.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The "observationally identical" soundness claim is false, and the dual run's "direct projection" is a third sealed value the document never defines.**
*Evidence:* §Resolved authority pins the composition rule against "the effective user's _direct_ projection," then argues: "Absent the declaration, the sealed projection simply has no actor keys … There is then nothing to alias, compute, or reflect: **the two projections are observationally identical, so single evaluation is sound by construction** rather than by analysis." But the document's own sealed projections differ beyond actor keys: the delegated projection is `{ …, sessionId: null, provider: "agent-credential", … }` while the direct human-session projection carries a real `sessionId` and the user's login provider. An *undeclared* handler can therefore observe delegation via `ctx.auth.provider === "agent-credential"` or `ctx.auth.sessionId === null` — the acceptance fixture's own `Object.keys`/spread operations observe these values — and take a wider branch for delegates; single evaluation delivers that result with no intersection. Under 0039 v1 (`writeAuthorization: "handler"`, function-level checks as half of 0011's model) this is a reachable widening channel that the section's headline ("Non-widening is **host-enforced**, not merely intended") claims to foreclose independent of app discipline. Separately: for *declared* functions, the direct-projection run needs an exact sealed value (what `sessionId`? what `provider`? there is no live session), and the dated 0053 amendment enumerates only "the two `servicePrincipal` projections," `kind`, and the anonymous answer — not this third projection, in a regime where 0053 makes exact key sets load-bearing.
*Resolution criterion:* Define the direct projection's exact sealed value and add it to the 0053 amendment enumeration. Either (a) define it as the delegated projection minus actor keys — which makes the identity claim true by definition but requires restating the composition rule honestly (the guarantee is relative to a synthetic session-less projection; `sessionId`/`provider` remain delegation-observable channels outside the host-enforced non-widening guarantee, named as a residual) — or (b) normalize/gate those fields so the identity claim holds as written. Add a provider/sessionId-branching handler fixture to the adversarial acceptance either way.

**2. MATERIAL — The dual-evaluation execution mechanics for opaque mutations is load-bearing, genuinely subtle, and missing from §Protocol obligations.**
*Evidence:* §Resolved authority pins: "For **mutations**, every authorization decision (write rules, `requireRole`, and the trusted denial) must pass under **both** projections, and the handler's writes come from the single delegated execution." For the closed rule IR this is well-defined (re-bind variables, re-evaluate). For an opaque Ibex handler it is not: a handler that *branches* on an actor key can make different `deny()` calls, reach different `requireRole` sites, or produce different writes under the two projections, so "every authorization decision passes under both" requires either a dry direct-projection execution (mutation dry-run does not exist — §Audit says so) or a defined decision-set comparison with a divergence rule; neither is specified. The same gap covers what "the intersection of the delegated run with the direct-user run" means for query results that are not row sets (aggregates, computed values). §Protocol obligations — the document's explicit device so deferred rigor "cannot be quietly lost" — records five items (transition inventory, settlement, consent, fence, hosted admission) and omits this one, which is of at least the settlement predicate's subtlety and guards the central delegation guarantee.
*Resolution criterion:* Add a sixth protocol obligation pinning the dual-evaluation execution contract: the execution model for the direct-projection evaluation of mutations (dry re-execution vs. decision re-check), the fail-closed rule on any divergence between the two runs, side-effect discipline of the direct run (including nested `ctx.runMutation`), the meaning of "intersection" for non-row-set query results, and the cost posture (declared actor-reading invocations execute twice).

**3. NON-BLOCKING — Undeclared actor-narrowing silently degrades to an effective-user check; the generated `isDirectHuman` must force the declaration.**
*Evidence:* §Principal kinds ships `isDirectHuman(ctx)` (`ctx.auth.kind === "human" && ctx.auth.actor == null`) "so applications do not hand-roll this security-sensitive conjunction wrong." But in an *undeclared* function the sealed projection carries no actor keys, so `ctx.auth.actor == null` is true for a delegated agent and the conjunction **passes delegates** — fail-open for the narrowing intent (bounded: the delegate still acts only within the delegator's authority and its grant scopes, so the non-widening guarantee holds; it is the app's *extra* narrowing below the user's own authority that silently fails). The document never states that the generated helper implies/forces `readsActor: true`, and hand-rolled computed-access spellings (`ctx.auth["ac"+"tor"]`) in an undeclared function get `undefined` and pass — the same static-scanning limitation 0053 refuses to trust, here producing silent non-narrowing rather than refusal.
*Resolution criterion:* One pin in §Principal kinds or the 0004 alignment: the generated `isDirectHuman` (and any generated actor accessor) carries the `readsActor` declaration by construction; plus one sentence naming the residual — an undeclared hand-rolled actor check observes no actor keys and therefore does not narrow, bounded by the delegator's authority.

**4. NON-BLOCKING — The prod-residual claims need scoping for durable authority staged under non-prod boots.**
*Evidence:* §Recovery gates invitation *consumption* across the profile-reboot seam precisely because "`ServerProfile` is resolved per boot," but credentials established and grants minted under a dev/test/demo boot (registration, `auth.mintAgentGrant`) persist and operate after a reboot into `prod` — no sweep, no re-attestation. That is consistent with the invariant's letter (no authority-creating *act executes* on `prod`), but the residual narrative overstates: "**on `prod` an agent's autonomous _grant_ is fixed at registration** … no operator can mint it another one" is true only while the instance stays `prod`; an operator with reboot control can revoke-and-remint under a demo boot and return to `prod`. The corpus's standard is naming such seams (this document does exactly that for invitations).
*Resolution criterion:* One sentence in §Recovery or the profile-invariant paragraph: durable agent authority created under non-prod boots survives into `prod` by design (operator custody of the box is already total — keyring, downgrade, restore), and the "fixed at registration" / "scope evolution unavailable" claims are scoped to a continuously-`prod` instance.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — Two catalog records omit load-bearing fields §Recovery depends on.**
*Evidence:* §Recovery's atomic consumption "bumps the principal's **recovery generation**," and pairing requests are voided by comparison against it — but the `_auth_users` column list (§Principal kinds) has no recovery-generation field, and `_auth_grants`' sibling records give field lists for four of the five new tables while `_auth_pairing_requests` gets only "the consent records (below)" with no field list (the consent flow section names no fields either). For a document whose stated posture is "field lists and invariants, not DDL," the two records that §Recovery's atomicity and the recovery-generation comparison actually ride are the ones missing their invariant-bearing fields.
*Resolution criterion:* Name where the recovery generation lives (presumably `_auth_users`) and give `_auth_pairing_requests` the same field-list treatment as its four siblings (at minimum: id, agent/actor id, requested scopes, recovery generation at creation, state, `expires_at`, consumed/denied markers, code linkage).

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a strong, unusually careful RFC that answers a real and long-blocked corpus need (LLP 0019 F2; the `servicePrincipal` reservation in LLP 0024 §Authority Tuple and LLP 0042 §Authority). Judged at the ratified RFC altitude, the design is coherent, the seams to LLP 0011/0039/0053 are drawn against those documents' *actual* text rather than a remembered version of it, and the sibling composition with LLP 0056 is genuinely reciprocal. Two design moves stand out as first-rate. The **CIBA-shaped user-minted pairing code** (§Grants) dissolves the identity-oracle seam and the handle-distribution problem *structurally* rather than by mitigation — unsolicited consent requests cannot exist, and an invalid code is a single non-enumerating failure. And the **ownership insight** (author ruling #2, §Re-credentialing) is the correct resolution of the prod-continuity problem: an owner re-issuing their own agent's credential is app policy, exactly parallel to `invitedBy: "users"` creation which prod already allows, so it needs no amendment to LLP 0039's "every impersonation effect refused on prod" invariant. I verified the load-bearing code claim behind the round-that-fixed-it: `app.reset` is indeed `NonProd` in `src/admin.rs:163`, so the RFC's current reliance on `AgentDestroyCapability` (not the false `app.reset`-parity argument) is sound; and `EffectPolicy::ImpersonationCapability` exists in `src/server.rs`, `sbat_`/`sbcc_`/`sct_`/`sbr*_` prefixes exist as claimed, and the new prefix set is mutually prefix-free.

These are exactly the places a careless RFC contradicts the parent; this one does not.

There is, however, **one MATERIAL safety hole in the pinned revision**: the `authorityCreating`-consent path lets a stolen session mint durable authority that survives the compromise-response cascade, directly violating the document's own centrally-stated security property. It is a real, traceable exploit, and the pinned Validation Requirements do not catch it. The remaining concerns are precision/coordination issues that do not block at RFC altitude.

## 3. Concerns

**Concern 1 — MATERIAL (safety). Approving an `authorityCreating` consent needs no step-up, so a stolen session can mint durable authority that outlives the compromise cascade — violating the document's own pinned property.**

Evidence, in the pinned revision:
- §Re-credentialing pins the property: *"A stolen session must not be convertible into authority that outlives its revocation."*
- Step-up is required only on routes that "mint a durable secret" and the irreversible delete, and the RFC explicitly exempts consent: *"(Ordinary consent approval does **not** require step-up — it grants an agent access but mints no secret, and the cascade below covers it.)"*
- But §Grants (`authorityCreating`) states a delegated agent's grant can seat *"a **durable** LLP 0056 membership — including for its own actor principal — which **survives grant revocation**."* And the cascade (§Re-credentialing, `revoke-all`) explicitly says *"Principals, profile rows, and memberships survive."*

Exploit (all steps available to a stolen-bearer holder with any agent credential of their own): (1) from the stolen victim session, mint a pairing code; (2) present it from an **attacker-owned** agent (registered under the attacker's own account — agent creation's step-up protects the *victim's* invitation route, not the attacker's separate agent); (3) approve an `authorityCreating` scope from the victim bearer — **no step-up in the pinned revision**; (4) the attacker's agent, acting as the victim, seats a membership for its own actor principal on the victim's resource (an ordinary "host invites other" transition in 0056's matrix). Victim runs `revoke-all`: it kills victim sessions, victim-*owned* agents, and victim-*issued* grants. The seated membership survives (memberships explicitly survive), and the attacker's agent's credential is untouched (not victim-owned). The attacker's agent now autonomously holds authority over the victim's resource. Authority outlived the revocation.

The pinned Validation Requirements reinforce the gap: the "stolen session cannot be upgraded into durable authority" row tests step-up refusal only at `/auth/agent-invitations`, `/auth/agents/{id}/credential`, and `DELETE /auth/agents/{id}` — it does **not** test the `authorityCreating`-consent-survives-cascade path, and it asserts *"after the … cascade, every credential, token, and delegated grant … is dead"* while a seated membership is none of those.

Resolution criterion: require a fresh, operation-bound step-up proof when the approval covers any `authorityCreating` scope (because that consent's consequence — a durable membership — is the one thing the cascade cannot revoke), and add a Validation Requirement exercising the full stolen-bearer → authorityCreating-consent → seated-membership → revoke-all → membership-persists trace. *This is exactly the change the successor revision `9c9713…` makes (its §Re-credentialing now reads "But approving a scope marked `authorityCreating` **does** [require step-up]… a membership the delegate then seats is durable app data the cascade cannot revoke," and the VR is updated to match). Independent convergence on the fix corroborates the finding; the pinned artifact under review is not ready as pinned.*

**Concern 2 — NON-BLOCKING (decision-quality / precision). The write-side non-widening claim is categorically overstated relative to the pinned mechanism.**

§Resolved authority claims, for mutations, *"every write and every effect must be authorized under both [projections]"* and *"Delegation can refuse a write the user could make, but never permit one the user could not,"* and globally *"nothing can fail open by being hidden."* But the same section pins that *"The handler body executes once, under the delegated projection,"* and LLP 0039 has **no declarative write-side row rules** (0039 Non-goals; `writeAuthorization: "handler"`). A handler that branches on `ctx.auth.actor`/`isDelegated`/`provider === "agent-credential"` to make a *different write decision* when delegated runs only once, under the delegated projection, so its divergent write is not re-evaluated under the direct projection. The literal claim ("never permit a write the user could not") is therefore false for delegation-conditional imperative writes.

The *security outcome* is nonetheless sound — writes commit under the effective user's identity (so no authority beyond U is gained), reads are intersected at the data layer (the real visibility-widening vector, correctly closed), and the one dangerous durable-privilege write is host-gated regardless of handler logic. So this is precision, not a hole. Resolution criterion: reconcile the prose with 0039's `writeAuthorization:"handler"` posture — state the guarantee as "reads intersected; writes commit as the effective user; durable-privilege writes host-gated at the effect site," and scope "authorized under both" to the trusted authorization layer (declarative rules / `requireRole` / the trusted denial), acknowledging that delegation-conditional handler *logic* is the app's trust domain exactly as it is for any 0039 handler. (Protocol obligation 5 defers the *mechanics*; this is about not over-claiming the *semantics* the mechanics can deliver.)

**Concern 3 — NON-BLOCKING (coherence). The production security spine depends on step-up, whose prod viability rides an unnamed delivery dependency and a new challenge type.**

Author ruling #2 makes production continuity the document's spine, and §Re-credentialing makes step-up load-bearing for it: step-up is *"a freshly verified LLP 0039 email-code challenge, scoped to this operation and this agent."* On prod, a real email-code challenge needs real delivery, which LLP 0039 states is unbuilt and gated on LLP 0045 (0039 §Summary: self-serve "additionally needs LLP 0045's real delivery seam"). The RFC names 0045 only in an open question about invitation/pairing delivery, not as a prerequisite for step-up. It is defensible that this is *inherited* rather than new (prod login already needs delivery, and step-up reuses that flow), but step-up additionally needs an **operation-and-agent-bound** challenge, which 0039's address-scoped challenge is not. Resolution criterion: name, in §Companion obligations, that the step-up amendment (a) inherits 0039's real-delivery dependency for prod and (b) introduces an operation-bound challenge variant beyond today's address-scoped one, so a reader does not treat step-up as free on prod.

**Concern 4 — NON-BLOCKING (coordination). The security posture rests on several substantial *new* LLP 0039 surfaces proposed as amendments to a document the author has explicitly FROZEN.**

§Companion obligations lands step-up authentication (a new provider flow), `POST /auth/sessions/revoke-all` (a new user-facing gesture), and `AgentDestroyCapability` (a new effect capability) as "dated LLP 0039 amendments." These are owned and buildable — not the unowned-dependency antipattern — but LLP 0039 is Draft/FROZEN after its own dual-family gate, and the central security property here is only true once these unbuilt, un-gated surfaces exist. Resolution criterion: no design change required, but state explicitly that 0055's security readiness is *coupled* to landing these 0039 amendments and that they have not themselves been through 0039's review gate, so a reader does not conflate "pinned here" with "settled in 0039."

**1. MATERIAL — The document pins two contradictory evaluation rules for undeclared delegated invocations, and its own soundness argument is refuted by its own fixture.**

- **Thief-rotates-N analysis (§Credentials and tokens).** The family-wide `revoke-self` with time-only retention and a durable rotation throttle is a genuinely worked adversarial design; the recorded failure history (single-predecessor beaten by two rotations; count-based horizon beaten by five) shows the loop earning its keep.
- **Plane boundary and the corrected MCP matrix (§Plane boundary).** The explicit "devUser is not displaced" correction, the hosted wholesale refusal including agent-management routes, and the effect-site gating of durable invitations (mint-in-demo → reboot-into-prod → consume refused) close the laundering paths a reviewer would otherwise have to hunt for.
- **Validation Requirements.** Among the best in the corpus: both halves of settlement, the ABA basis-generation negative, the sessionId-detection fixture justifying dual evaluation, widening-shape proofs (disjunctive/negated/actor-only), the unmarked-wrapper host-gate proof, prefix sweep across the full registered namespace, and profile-invariant proofs at mint *and* effect site.
- **Status-section candor.** Verified-in-code corrections (e.g. the `app.reset`-comparison falsified against `src/admin.rs`) and the recorded defect history give a reviewer real purchase.

## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The stolen-session security property rests on two surfaces that exist nowhere and are obligated nowhere.**
Evidence: §Re-credentialing pins the property ("a stolen session must not be convertible into authority that outlives its revocation") and delivers it via (a) step-up — "a freshly verified LLP 0039 email-code challenge within a short window" bound to the operation — and (b) the "revoke all my sessions" cascade, which "additionally revokes the user's owned agents' credentials and tokens, and every delegated grant that user has issued." LLP 0039 defines neither: its provider flow is request-code/exchange (exchange *mints a session*; there is no operation-bound challenge-proof flow), and its session surfaces are `logout` (one session), cap eviction, and admin revocation — no user-facing revoke-all gesture (grep of 0039/0053/0011 confirms). §Companion obligations' dated-0039-amendment list (AgentDestroyCapability, `Authorization` widening, connection-binding variant, restore list, downgrade, item-7 axis, retainability, catalog refuse-at-open) contains neither mechanism; §Protocol obligations doesn't carry them either; and §Routes has no row for the cascade trigger despite §Ending authority's own rule that "every ending has a trigger surface (§Routes)." The threading is also already inconsistent: §Re-credentialing and VR line ~1218 require step-up at **both** `POST /auth/agent-invitations` and `POST /auth/agents/{id}/credential`, but the §Routes row for `/auth/agent-invitations` (line 908) lists only "session bearer" while the `{id}/credential` row (line 922) says "+ step-up auth." Note the cascade is load-bearing beyond re-credentialing: it is the only thing that eventually kills a delegated grant a thief mints to their own agent via pairing (pairing-code mint and consent approval are deliberately not step-up-gated).
Resolution criterion: add both mechanisms to the dated LLP 0039 amendment list (a user-facing revoke-all-sessions surface with the agent cascade hook, and the operation-bound fresh-challenge step-up flow — or an explicit ruling that step-up is spelled as session-freshness over existing machinery), add the cascade's trigger row to §Routes, and reconcile the `/auth/agent-invitations` row with §Re-credentialing.

**2. MATERIAL — Owner deactivation is browser-reachable permanent destruction on `prod`, un-gated and unnamed.**
Evidence: §Routes line 923: `DELETE /auth/agents/{id}` — "session bearer of the owner … (all profiles)." Deactivation is "permanent in v1 (no reactivate verb)" (§Principal kinds), "a deactivated agent cannot be recovered by either path" (§Re-credentialing), and it terminally voids pending requests and recovery invitations and ends all grants — including third parties' consents. So a stolen bearer (the exact XSS-exfiltratable credential the corpus names as the unowned posture) can permanently and unrecoverably destroy every agent the user owns, on `prod`, with damage that survives the compromise cascade and session revocation. This contradicts the document's own design logic in both directions: step-up was added to the *minting* routes precisely because "a stolen bearer alone must not reach" durable consequences, and the operator twin `auth.deactivateAgent` is `NonProd` *because* identity-terminal verbs are irreversible (line 927, citing the `app.reset` precedent). The header's "browser-reachable permanent-destruction residual named" refers to the dev-profile console-credential case; the stolen-owner-bearer case on `prod` is nowhere named.
Resolution criterion: any one of — require step-up on owner deactivation (symmetric with the minting routes; cheapest); make owner deactivation reversible for a bounded window; or explicitly name and accept the stolen-bearer permanent-destruction residual with an argument, and adjust the VR to assert whichever gate is chosen.

**3. NON-BLOCKING — Duplicate, contradictory route rows for `auth.revokeAgentInvitation`.**
Evidence: line 912 lists it as "`AdminWrite` | all profiles" with no `prod` qualifier; line 926 (grouped with `revokeGrant`/`revokeAgentCredential`) and the "profile invariant, mechanically" paragraph require **`AgentDestroyCapability`** on `prod`. A reader cannot tell whether killing an unconsumed invitation on `prod` needs the non-browser carrier.
Resolution criterion: delete or qualify the line-912 row so the table states one rule.

**4. NON-BLOCKING — "On `prod` every destruction is irreversible (no re-creation)" overstates, and the adversarial-owner loop is unnamed.**
Evidence: §Routes line 926 justifies `AgentDestroyCapability` with irreversibility, but for **owned** agents operator destruction is fully reversible by the owner (`{id}/credential`, all profiles, re-applies the template). Consequently VR's "revoking credential + grants under the carrier fully stops a rogue prod agent" holds only until an adversarial owner re-issues; with `auth.deactivateAgent` `NonProd` and no user deletion in the corpus, the operator plane has no permanent answer to a malicious owner on `prod` — a direct, presumably intended consequence of the invariant, but this document's method is to name such residuals.
Resolution criterion: qualify the irreversibility rationale to "irreversible *by the operator plane* / for unowned agents," narrow the VR claim, and name the adversarial-owner residual (pointing at the inherited no-user-deletion gap).

**5. NON-BLOCKING — The production narrative silently inherits 0039's delivery gate, and the `prod` VR rows need an authentication bootstrap.**
Evidence: 0039's capture mailbox is `AdminWrite` + `ImpersonationCapability` and "every impersonation effect is refused on `prod`"; `auth.mintSession` is likewise prod-refused. With capture-only delivery, no session can be established *on a `prod` profile* at all until LLP 0045's real delivery seam lands — so every §Motivation/§Re-credentialing production story, and every VR row of the form "the owner re-issues from an ordinary session on `prod`," transitively gates on 0045 delivery (or on sessions minted under another profile surviving a restart into `prod`). 0055 cites 0045 only in an open question about invitation/pairing delivery.
Resolution criterion: one sentence naming the inherited delivery gate for production use, and a stated session-bootstrap recipe for the `prod`-profile acceptance rows.

**6. NON-BLOCKING — The export enumeration omits `owner_user_id`, which the import story depends on.**
Evidence: §Catalog's export list names `display_name`, `operator_contact`, `registration_provenance`, `deactivated_at` — not ownership — while §Re-credentialing and VR pin "imported agents land on `prod` re-credentialable **if owned**," which requires ownership to survive export/import (and the owner row to exist in the target).
Resolution criterion: add `owner_user_id` to the export enumeration and state the owner-row co-import assumption.

**7. NON-BLOCKING — `_auth_scope_bases` is a sixth table with no record definition and an implied exemption from GC.**
Evidence: §Catalog counts six tables; §Grants' "Protected catalog records" defines five and says "All five sweep on expiry+slack," leaving the scope-basis table's shape and its (necessarily) never-sweeping lifecycle to be inferred from the terminal-invalidation prose and VR ("a durable tombstone that survived the removal … across restart and declared restore").
Resolution criterion: give `_auth_scope_bases` a field list/invariant entry beside the other five, stating explicitly that it never sweeps.

**8. NON-BLOCKING — Registration/metadata drift in the newest revision.**
Evidence: (a) `SNAPBACK_AUTH_AUTHORITY_CREATING_UNCONSENTED` (defined §Grants, asserted in VR) is missing from §Companion obligations' LLP 0012 code list; (b) the step-up freshness window is "a short window" where every other lifetime in the document is a pinned v1 constant; (c) the Revised header narrates rounds 1–5 while §Status reports eight rounds and round-8 findings incorporated — the header no longer summarizes the actual revision history it claims to.
Resolution criterion: add the code to the 0012 list, pin (or explicitly defer-to-constants) the step-up window, and bring the Revised header up to date.

## 4. Suggestions

- **Agents-first sender-binding.** DPoP is named as a trajectory for human bearers, where key custody is hard; agent credentials are held by programmatic clients where a keypair is trivial. Consider making sender-bound `sbac_`/`sbagt_` the *first* DPoP landing rather than inheriting the human-bearer timeline — it would materially shrink the stolen-token story for the class of credential this RFC creates.
- **Return `consent_digest` in the grant receipt** so the agent side can log exactly what it was granted — a symmetric audit record that costs nothing and helps agent operators debug scope-denied failures.
- **Emit a diagnostic on rotation-throttle hits.** A credential rotating at the ceiling is a strong theft signal; surfacing it (to the owner surface and operator inspection) turns the throttle from a storage bound into an alarm.
- **State the owner-capture consequence of ownership.** The owner-re-issue power means the invitation-minting user permanently holds capture power over the agent identity and its memberships (relevant when a vendor operates the agent). This is within the trust model, but one sentence in §Principal kinds would make the ownership-transfer open question concrete.
- Consider whether the token cap's evict-oldest (vs. the grant cap's refuse) deserves one sentence of rationale — the asymmetry is right (tokens are carriers, consent is not) but currently implicit in the "consent is never silently evicted" phrasing.

## 1. Overall assessment

This is a genuinely strong RFC, and it answers the question LLP 0019 F2 posed at the right altitude. The three-way decomposition in §Summary — operator plane (not extended), app-plane agent principals (autonomous and delegated), and attestation (honestly punted) — is the correct factoring, and the document's central structural choices are right: agents as `_auth_users` rows keyed by the same principal id (so LLP 0056 memberships, profiles, and RLS variables work unchanged); the actor/effective/grant tuple as the universal audit shape with the plain human session as its degenerate case; grants immutable so every lifecycle change is an authority transition rather than a "permissions changed under live authority" middle state; and the carrier axis (`tokenId`, connection) cleanly separated from the durable authority axis so token churn neither strands queues nor cross-fences sibling tokens.

The two rulings the document rests on both hold up under scrutiny. The altitude ruling is applied consistently: the §Protocol obligations section is a real deferral list, not a fig leaf — everything I checked that is missing at protocol grain (settlement algorithm, pairing-code timing envelopes, dual-evaluation mechanics, hosted admission capability) is on that list. The ownership ruling is the document's best idea: deriving "on `prod` the operator plane may destroy agent authority but never create or assume it" directly from LLP 0039's frozen "every impersonation effect is refused on `prod`" — and then routing production continuity through *owners* acting from ordinary sessions — resolves what earlier rounds framed as an impossible tension, without amending the frozen matrix. I verified the classification is faithful to 0039's own text (its impersonation-effect matrix and `auth.mintSession` gating), and the owner-path argument ("no more privileged than rotating an API key they hold") survives the obvious attacks because re-credentialing terminally revokes all grants (so third-party consent is never silently captured) and step-up closes the stolen-bearer upgrade.

I verified the load-bearing implementation claims against source, and they are accurate: `sbat_`/`sbcc_` mint in `src/admin.rs`, `sct_` in `src/server.rs`, the `sbrk_`/`sbrl_`/`sbrp_`/`sbrm_` family in `src/admin_secret_read.rs` (with `sbrp_`/`sbrm_` correctly described as nonce and MAC), `sbs_` pinned in 0039 but absent from source, `EffectPolicy::ImpersonationCapability` present in the route registry with its AdminWrite composition invariants, and `app.reset`/`function.run` gated `NonProd` in `src/admin.rs` — the exact fact the Status section says a prior residual argument was corrected against. ## 3. Concerns

**1. MATERIAL — The `prod` owner paths depend on a challenge-delivery seam the frozen slice does not have, and the dependency is unnamed.**
Evidence: §Re-credentialing pins step-up as "a freshly verified LLP 0039 email-code challenge," required at `POST /auth/agent-invitations`, `POST /auth/agents/{id}/credential`, `DELETE /auth/agents/{id}`, and `authorityCreating` consent — i.e., every owned-agent creation/recovery/destruction act on `prod`. Under LLP 0039, v1 delivery is capture-only, and reading the capture mailbox is an impersonation effect: 0039 §Acceptance — "every impersonation effect is refused on `prod`," with "mailbox access" first in the non-substitutability matrix; `auth.mintSession` is likewise `prod`-refused. So on a `prod` profile no fresh challenge can ever be verified — step-up, and even ordinary login, are unreachable until LLP 0045's real delivery seam (or an equivalent operator-provisioned adapter) exists. Consequently §Re-credentialing's "available on every profile including `prod`" and the Validation bullet "the owner re-issues it from an ordinary session on `prod` (with step-up)" describe a flow that cannot execute under the slice as it stands; the RFC's only 0045 mention (Open Questions) concerns invitation/pairing delivery, not this. 0039 itself names the delivery gate for self-serve signup; this document must do the same for its central claim — by its own stated standard.
Resolution: add the named gate (prod step-up/login requires the LLP 0045 delivery handoff or a designated prod delivery adapter; until then the prod owner paths are pinned-but-inert exactly like the hosted cells), and scope the prod validation fixtures accordingly (test delivery seam, or sessions established pre-boot with the step-up fixtures explicitly gated on the seam).

**2. MATERIAL — The ownership retrofit left contradictions in the normative routes/verbs layer.**
Three instances, all security-relevant, all surviving into the working-tree revision:
(a) **`auth.revokeAgentInvitation` appears twice with different `prod` requirements** — once as its own row ("`AdminWrite` | all profiles; kills any unconsumed invitation of either class", line ~960) and once in the kill-path row ("`AdminWrite`; on `prod`, + `AgentDestroyCapability`", line ~976, matching the §profile-invariant paragraph and Validation). An implementer reading only the first row ships a browser-reachable destruction verb on `prod`.
(b) **The `POST /auth/agent-invitations` row omits step-up** ("session bearer | … budgeted", line ~956), while §Re-credentialing and Validation both pin step-up for exactly that route ("a valid bearer without step-up is refused at `/auth/agent-invitations` …"). The table is the surface implementers consult.
(c) **The irreversibility rationale is now false.** The kill-path row says "On `prod` every destruction is irreversible (no re-creation)" and §Ending authority says `prod` "forbids the re-creation" — but §Re-credentialing makes owner re-creation available on every profile, and revoked delegated grants are re-creatable by fresh consent on `prod`. Irreversibility now holds only for *unowned* agents. The rule (non-browser carrier on `prod`) remains correct — those verbs command destruction across **every** principal, which no app-origin XSS should reach, and mass grant destruction is mass consent loss even when individually recoverable — but the stated justification is stale pre-ownership reasoning, and a future editor reasoning from it could wrongly relax the gate for owned agents.
Resolution: merge/delete the duplicate row; add "+ step-up auth" to the invitation-mint row; reword the destruction rationale (irreversible for unowned agents; cross-principal blast radius otherwise).

**3. NON-BLOCKING — The pinned stolen-session property overclaims for ordinary grants.**
Evidence: the block-quote "A stolen session must not be convertible into authority that outlives its revocation" (§Re-credentialing). An ordinary delegated grant the thief approves deliberately survives logout and per-session revocation ("ordinary logout … leaves grants alone") and dies only via the revoke-all cascade or explicit grant revocation; the mechanisms deliver the property only against durable *secrets* and durable *app data*, plus grant-borne authority *after the cascade runs* — which the document itself states precisely two paragraphs later ("after the cascade, nothing an attacker minted from the stolen session still authorizes anything"). The design choice (no step-up on ordinary consent; cascade as remedy) is deliberate and defensible; the headline property should match it.
Resolution: reword the block-quote to bind the property to the compromise response ("…that survives the compromise-response cascade") or explicitly carve out cascade-revocable grants.

**4. MATERIAL in the pinned revision; already resolved in the working tree — 0055/0056 composition gap on `lastHolder` × deactivation.**
Evidence: LLP 0056 §`lastHolder` load-bearingly asserts "LLP 0055 therefore **refuses deactivation** in exactly that case (naming the resources; seat a replacement first)" — one of the three legs keeping its invariant non-vacuous against a permanently inactive sole holder. The pinned revision of 0055 contained no such refusal (deactivation "closes every door … has no reactivation path," unconditionally), so the sibling attributed a rule to this document that it did not carry. The working-tree revision (`5e5b6b34…`) adds §"Deactivation is refused while it would strand a resource" plus the matching validation bullet, which closes the gap correctly and with the right justification (same fail-closed shape as 0056's resource-delete refusal).
Resolution: land the working-tree paragraph (done); nothing further beyond keeping the two documents' wording aligned.

**5. NON-BLOCKING — The double-authorization write claim should name its handler-code residual.**
Evidence: §Resolved authority enumerates the authorization surfaces ("declarative rules, `requireRole`, write rules, the trusted denial") and claims "every write and every effect must be authorized under both" projections. Under 0039's ratified `writeAuthorization: "handler"` narrowing, v1 write authorization *is* handler code — which executes once, under the delegated projection, and can see `sessionId`, `provider`, and `actor` by design. A handler whose own write gate is delegation-sensitive (e.g., more permissive when `sessionId == null`) is outside anything the host can doubly evaluate; the read intersection bounds reads but not a handler-authorized write. Protocol obligation 5 defers the *mechanics* but the residual itself is a design-level fact worth one honest sentence, since the document is otherwise scrupulous about naming residuals.
Resolution: scope the write claim to host-mediated authorization surfaces and name the handler-internal residual (pointing at `isDirectHuman` and the 0039 narrowing), or explain why the intersection makes it moot.

(a) "replayable once" (§Re-credentialing) vs. Validation's "single-use" for step-up proofs — the former literally reads as usable twice; (b) "planted-sentinel redaction acceptance for all four new classes" (§Credentials) vs. Validation's "all five new secret classes — including the pairing code"; (c) §Catalog's logical-export list omits `_auth_scope_bases` while Validation requires exporting it; (d) `_auth_pairing_requests` is the only protected catalog record with no field list, and the recovery generation introduced in round 5 has no named storage home (presumably a principal-row or credential-row field).

# LLP 3000.013 design review — Astra (gpt-6-astra), reasoning effort max

**Date:** 2026-09-23. **Revision reviewed:** initial (6d9f3e001). **Mode:** blind, read-only, over the checkout. **Brief:** reproduced below the verdict.

## Verbatim review

The RFC needs changes at the authorization and background-delivery boundaries. I read it in full and checked the named code, prior LLPs, recipe, and guide cards. No files changed.

1. **Blocker — §3.3 and §4: structural recognition does not establish permission to materialize.**

   The recognized shape permits private posts or private edges. Suppose Alice’s posts are owner-only and Bob follows Alice. Background delivery copies the event into Bob’s inbox; the stated inbox predicate passes. Even if `posts[.post]` subsequently returns null, the event exposes existence, actor, timestamp, and ordering. Publishing a removal tombstone can disclose the same private event.

   The [current admission check](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/timeline.rs:89) requires explicitly public, fully synced sources and tightly constrained mutations.

   **Smallest fix:** retain those disclosure and immutability prerequisites, except restrictions explicitly replaced here. Other shapes should remain ordinary authorized planned reads. Private materialization needs a separate per-recipient authorization and revocation design.

2. **High — §2.1 and §5.1: streaming scalars need the existing scan-admission restrictions.**

   Calling a scan `count` or `exists` does not make it a safe point probe. [LLP 3000.007 §21.9](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/llp/3000.007-relaxed-quarry-and-explicit-residency.rfc.md:6074) records the counterexample: a permitted blind update moves a hidden row into the caller’s prefix while making it expired; a subsequent aborting scan reveals its presence or retained fields through whether the meter refuses. A snapshot query reveals nothing in either world.

   The unrestricted wording could reopen this through `count(R)` in a mutation.

   **Smallest fix:** specify that streaming consumers preserve the underlying scan’s structural admission, authorization, expiry, and coverage checks. Keep expiring mutation scans refused; preserve the existing distinctions between queries, mutations, and jobs.

3. **High — §2.2 and §2.3: positional probes cannot generally synthesize their own meaning.**

   Consider `members(roomId, principal, invitedBy)`, with two principal columns and no relevant declared index. `exists members[.roomId, viewer]` does not identify whether `viewer` means `principal` or `invitedBy`. Guessing can grant access to an inviter instead of a member. Multiple unique keys of the same arity create the corresponding ambiguity for §2.2.

   Today, [probe binding](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/schema.rs:454) selects among declared indexes and refuses ambiguity.

   **Smallest fix:** resolve logical target columns before synthesizing physical indexes. Require named equalities unless an existing logical key determines the mapping unambiguously. Synthesized indexes must not enter the candidate set and change another read’s meaning. Give generated indexes identities derived from their complete canonical descriptors, including computed-key parameters, with collision handling independent of declaration order.

4. **High — §3.1: source IDs are not globally unique.**

   The [store’s primary key is `(table, id)`](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:162). Explicit IDs can therefore coincide across `posts` and `reposts`. With equal timestamps and equal IDs, the proposed cursor position identifies two distinct events. The [existing merge deduplicates by ID alone](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:1087), so one disappears; merely disabling that deduplication still lets a cursor skip the other.

   **Smallest fix:** define view identity and the final ordering key using source identity, such as `(source table, source ID)`. Specify whether overlapping branches coalesce a source row or produce distinct branch rows; the latter also needs branch identity. Carry that complete identity through deduplication and cursor translation.

5. **High — §2.1: a lower time bound does not define a finite bucket window.**

   The example `.at > now - 1d` includes future-dated posts. A post dated tomorrow with the highest `likeCount` must win, but lanes ending at the current hour omit it. Keeping the predicate as a residual filter cannot recover an omitted bucket. Likewise, `.at < now - D` describes an unbounded historical range.

   The [current Trending card](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/feeds.md:580) includes the missing `.at <= now`.

   **Smallest fix:** require proven finite lower and upper bounds before choosing finite bucket lanes. Enumerate every intersecting bucket and retain exact endpoint predicates. Otherwise use a different plan. Add the upper bound to the example rather than silently changing its meaning.

6. **High — §4, transition table: delivery and backfill overlap, but idempotent admission is unspecified.**

   A post commits its delivery record. Before that record runs, Bob follows its author and receives the post among the newest 50. The delivery worker subsequently encounters Bob and inserts the same copy. The [current copy operation](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/timeline.rs:209) uses an ordinary insert, so this becomes a uniqueness refusal that can park delivery to other recipients.

   Only the post transition explicitly includes a durable work record; a follow must also survive a crash after its first 50 copies commit.

   **Smallest fix:** enqueue every required delivery, backfill, and cleanup task atomically with its source transition. Make copy admission idempotent by authoritative event/recipient identity, and commit copies and task progress together. Reopen must resume that durable work.

7. **High — §4: delayed work needs edge and event incarnations.**

   Two independent resurrection/deletion races remain:

   - Bob unfollows Alice, then refollows before old cleanup finishes. The old cleanup deletes the refollow’s valid copies. Checking the current edge only in the read filter cannot repair missing rows.
   - An event is removed and its tombstone is purged while an earlier delivery task remains pending. That task later recreates an inbox copy. Reusing a deleted repost’s source ID makes old and new work harder to distinguish.

   The [current tombstone guard](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/timeline.rs:220) explicitly relies on purging all copies together with the tombstone.

   **Smallest fix:** bind tasks and copies to edge/event incarnations. Cleanup may affect only its retired incarnation; delivery must revalidate the current source and relationship in each committing chunk. Retire tombstones only after stale work can no longer publish that incarnation.

8. **High — §3.3 and §4: delivery lag has no completeness or cursor contract.**

   A foreign event at time 95 is already committed but undelivered. Bob reads an inbox page ending at time 90 and receives its cursor. Delivery then inserts the event at 95. Continuing after 90 never returns it—even if the source tables and follow graph never changed during pagination.

   A device can also be fully caught up with the partition log while background materialization remains incomplete. Its sync watermark cannot establish completeness of the declared logical view.

   **Smallest fix:** track reader delivery/backfill progress, propagate it to devices, and define how it affects `complete`, refresh, and continuation. Conservatively withhold a cursor that could cross an undelivered key. Alternatively, explicitly define this as pagination over delivered inbox contents and withdraw equivalence to the complete named query.

9. **Medium — §4: the filter costs a probe per candidate, not per returned row.**

   After unfollowing a prolific author, 9,000 stale copies may precede every still-relevant event. Before cleanup catches up, a request for 20 rows can exhaust the examined/probe meters and return none. Tombstone checks add further work. This recreates the sparse-filter problem that motivated the redesign.

   **Smallest fix:** correct the cost contract and explicitly support progress across rejected inbox candidates, with truthful incomplete results. If filled pages at the previous cost envelope remain a requirement, add a serving mechanism that excludes inactive author ranges without inspecting every stale copy; background cleanup alone provides no such guarantee.

10. **High — §5.1: streaming result retention does not establish the claimed heap improvement.**

    The cited [digest investigation](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/llp/3000.011.001.000-digests-beyond-one-chunk.rfc.md:157) already dropped decoded rows in small batches. Its 5,000-candidate fixture still incurred **22,940,000 bytes** of decoded-row heap accounting, exceeding 16 MiB before full interpreter work. The existing scan accounting accumulates charges; dropping the result vector alone does not erase them.

    **Smallest fix:** specify the bounded decoding and allocation-lifetime mechanism. Any released heap allowance must cover allocations actually destroyed, while preserving charges for retained dependencies, buffers, lane state, and other work. Keep all meters and scalar incompleteness semantics. Make the busy-chat claim conditional on passing the retained recipient-heavy fixture.

11. **High — §5.2: ordinary predicate reads are not automatically resumable sweeps.**

    A job ordered by mutable `.priority` processes one chunk. Another write moves an unvisited row behind its cursor; that row is permanently skipped. Moving a processed row forward can repeat its work. Adaptive K does not fix either case. The existing [stable-chunk check](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/program.rs:989) refuses mutable index components.

    The existing executor also replays setup for each chunk, so ordinary loop-carried locals cannot silently acquire persistent-loop semantics.

    **Smallest fix:** restrict automatic chunking to the existing stable sweep class; persist evaluated domain bounds and progress with writes. State which locals survive commits. Meter retries must restart the same uncommitted work with fresh transient state; ordinary rule, constraint, and generation refusals must still park explicitly.

12. **Medium — §5.3: effect deduplication does not replace recipient completion markers.**

    The [current digest recipe](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/jobs.rs:240) records completion even for zero-unread recipients. With conditional `emit … once`, a zero-result run records nothing. A replacement run after a late backdated message can now send a notification for that already-completed recipient/period.

    Replay introduces another required distinction: [effect recording](/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/effects.rs:81) can suppress the payload entirely, so the durable once receipt cannot depend solely on an outbox row.

    **Smallest fix:** retain completion markers until an engine-owned once scope can record successful no-effect completion. Specify atomic receipt/effect admission, suppression behavior, and receipt retention independently of effect payload lifecycle.

What is sound: authorization before residual filtering and projection is the correct order. Derived aggregate readability is safe under the stated narrow rule when “uniformly readable” means the existing `Public | Allow`, and otherwise the unchanged rule depends only on bare grouping keys. Existing maintained-column disclosure remains an intentional declared channel.

The existing merge machinery also provides useful foundations for `in` lanes: duplicate suppression and withholding continuation after incomplete discovery. Preserve its non-uniform-read, missing-device-coverage, opening-cut, and drain-cut distinctions from LLP 3000.007 §20.22. Program `exists` must retain ordinary read authorization; raw rule probes are not interchangeable with it.

**Overall verdict: NOT READY.**

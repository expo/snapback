## Required behavior

P1: expiration is a server-authored delete trigger. Every scheduled sweep, born-expired row, and same-id expired replacement must apply the declared cascade, set-null, and restrict actions atomically. A logically expired root is unreadable even when restrict or retained-history physical children keep its image. Restrict may defer that root but cannot starve later due roots, including repeated calls with `limit = 1`, multiple blockers, and cursor wrap. Any late error after prospective cascade/set-null work must roll back the whole transaction. Memory, SQLite, and Postgres paths must agree; the Postgres changes have compile coverage but no live Postgres runtime in this packet.

P2: maintained aggregate ids are lowercase 64-character SHA-256 hex over a versioned, domain-separated canonical logical group tuple at every check/read/write/rebuild site. Empty logical tuple is the identity input for ungrouped views while the physical `$global = true` discriminator remains a target field. Existing stores without the marker rebuild reconstructible maintained rows and publish marker plus a new generation in the same activation transaction; failure must roll everything back. A real store written by frozen 0.4.28 must reopen and preserve its aggregate value under the digest id. Schemas require `maintained-id-sha256-v1`; an old device must refuse rather than silently read a legacy address. Adoption estimates and fast-open paths must include the migration.

Lean evidence must state logical lifetime, sweep action/progress, fixed aggregate-id bound, and atomic adoption invariants without claiming to prove SHA-256. The differential generator must retain and actually execute supported expiry sweep cases; maintained-view evaluation remains explicitly outside the full Lean oracle and gets a separate generated invariant. Simulator and both device stores must exercise the relevant families.

## High-risk questions

1. Can a restricted expired root remain logically visible, or can its physical retention repeatedly block sweep progress?
2. Does the continuation cursor ever skip a due row permanently, loop on blockers, or publish after rollback? Check empty pages and wrap.
3. Can cascade/set-null mutate rows before a swallowed `E_REFERENCED` or later error, or can born-expired/same-id replacement bypass actions?
4. Do action witnesses correctly ignore expired children while retaining future/unopened physical children where required?
5. Do all aggregate identity paths use the digest, and is marker/generation publication transactional with rebuild and adoption failure?
6. Does the legacy wire escape stamp the new capability only for the intended one-time compatibility lane, while negotiated old devices refuse?
7. Is ungrouped identity based on `[]`, with `$global` retained only as the physical discriminator?
8. Do the formal and generator tests execute supported cases rather than count named unsupported cases as passes?

## Overall assessment

The action→mutation execution model is the RFC's strongest decision and is genuinely well-suited to Cloudflare. But the scheduler/target half is not yet CF-viable as written. Three things block it: the calendar-vs-interval → CF-primitive mapping is left as "and/or" rather than a rule; the run-ledger and atomic "claim a due run" have no CF-consistent backing store named; and — the headline gap — the design names the scale-to-zero tension but treats it as inherent and proposes no demand-gating, even though its own flagship cadence (All Aboard's 15–30s, below CF's hard 1-minute Cron Trigger floor) forces an always-armed Durable Object alarm that bills continuously with zero viewers. These are sharpening fixes, not a redesign, but they should land before the RFC leaves Draft because they also shape the local seam.

## Strengths

- **"Execution: the action class"** — egress-outside-the-transaction, commit-via-mutation is exactly right for CF, not merely a local nicety: you cannot hold a DO storage transaction (or a snapshot read-set) open across a `fetch`, so "the action fetches, then calls `ctx.runMutation`" is the *only* safe shape on Workers/DOs. Reusing `notify_live_subscriptions` means realtime fan-out falls out of the same path on every target.
- **"Delivery, overlap, and missed ticks"** + **"Failure and retry"** — at-least-once + idempotency keys + no-overlap-by-default map cleanly onto verified DO-alarm semantics (at-least-once guaranteed, single alarm per object, single-threaded execution). The public contract is pinned to the strong primitive rather than to the weak one.
- **"Schedule / cadence"** — treating minimum granularity as a per-target capability *fact* with a structured diagnostic (not silent coarsening) is the correct posture for CF's 1-minute floor.
- **"Cron job definition"** — source-authored, statically compiled jobs (no runtime-defined cron) align with Cron Triggers being deploy-time static config; a static-extraction-into-wrangler path is feasible. The RFC's choice to *not* support runtime-armed jobs is a good fit for CF (only DO alarms could do dynamic arming anyway).
- **"Scale-to-zero tension (stated honestly)"** — it at least names the collision instead of hiding it. The fix is to go one concrete step further (below).

## Concerns

### [High] The scale-to-zero collision is named but not mitigated, and the RFC's own flagship cadence sits below CF's floor.
"Schedule / cadence" says All Aboard polls every 15–60s and the `defineCron` example hardcodes `everyMs: 30_000`; "Multi-target deployment" notes Cron Triggers have a one-minute floor. Connect those dots against the verified facts: 15s and 30s are *below* CF's hard 1-minute Cron Trigger minimum (no sub-minute support exists), so on Cloudflare All Aboard's poll *cannot* be a Cron Trigger and is forced onto a Durable Object alarm. A DO alarm re-armed every 15–30s wakes the object 2–4×/minute forever, defeats WebSocket Hibernation, and bills duration+requests 24/7 *even when no client is watching trains*. "Scale-to-zero tension" frames this as inherent ("a job that polls every 15s keeps the app warm — it never idles"), but it is not inherent: the CF-idiomatic mitigation is demand-gating — the realtime DO arms the poll alarm only while it has ≥1 live subscriber, cancels it (`deleteAlarm`) when the last subscriber disconnects so the object hibernates, and fires an immediate catch-up poll when a subscriber re-arms it (which is just the existing "run once on recovery" path). For a "next train, pushed live" product whose data only matters when someone is looking, this restores true idle→zero and costs one paragraph to state. As written, the RFC's primary example on the primary second target violates the primary product goal of both LLP 0000 and LLP 0014 ("an idle deployed app should stop doing steady compute work").
**Resolved by:** Add a "demand-driven polling" subsection making clear that on scale-to-zero targets a frequent-interval job SHOULD be gated to live-subscriber presence (arm-on-subscribe / cancel-on-empty / catch-up-on-rearm), and state plainly that an always-armed sub-minute alarm bills continuously and only an always-on VM makes that free. Reframe "it never idles" as "it never idles *unless polling is demand-gated*."

### [High] The calendar/interval → CF-primitive mapping is "and/or," not a rule, and a Cron Trigger is only a wakeup, not a complete solution.
"Multi-target deployment" says "map interval/calendar jobs to Cron Triggers … and/or Durable Object alarms." That mush will produce a wrong implementation. The sharp rule, grounded in verified CF mechanics: (a) calendar/`cron:` expressions and whole-minute intervals expressible as a 5-field cron string → Cron Trigger; (b) sub-minute intervals, *and* ≥1-min intervals that aren't cron-expressible (e.g. `everyMs: 90_000` — every 90s can't be written as a cron field) → DO alarm only; (c) a Cron Trigger fires at wall-clock minute boundaries, which is *not* the same as `everyMs` fixed-delay-since-completion semantics — only DO alarms preserve interval-since-last-run. Two further hard limits the section omits: CF caps Cron Triggers at a small number per Worker (documented as 3; historically cited as 5 — verify), so an app with several calendar jobs cannot get one trigger each and must consolidate behind a single `* * * * *` dispatcher; and a Cron Trigger's `scheduled()` Worker is stateless and runs once globally — to honor the run ledger, no-overlap, and the atomic claim it must hand off to a Durable Object anyway. So the real architecture is: *wakeup source* (Cron Trigger OR DO alarm OR demand event) vs. *runner/ledger* (always a DO). The RFC currently treats Cron Triggers and DO alarms as two parallel full solutions.
**Resolved by:** Replace the "and/or" bullet with a decision table (cadence/expressibility → primitive), state the per-Worker Cron Trigger cap and the consolidation-behind-one-trigger consequence, note `everyMs` vs wall-clock-cron semantic difference, and state that on CF the ledger/claim/no-overlap always live in a DO regardless of which primitive supplies the wakeup.

### [Medium] The run ledger and atomic "claim a due run" have no CF-consistent backing store named.
"Scheduler state and run ledger" specifies a SQLite transaction locally and then "Durable Object ownership / storage *or another profile-specific primitive named by LLP 0014*." That punt hides the one place the local model may not translate. The atomic claim needs strong consistency + serialization: DO storage gives it for free (single-threaded + transactional — the clean mapping the RFC should *name*, not gesture at); D1 can only approximate it via a single conditional `UPDATE … WHERE inflight=0` CAS, with read-replica lag and no interactive transactions as documented caveats; KV cannot back it at all (eventually consistent, last-write-wins, no CAS). If the ledger lives in D1 (Profile A) but the claim needs serialization, you still need a DO in front. The RFC's local-SQLite-transaction model translates *only* to the DO-storage case.
**Resolved by:** State that the claim requires DO-class single-threaded serialization (DO storage recommended; D1 conditional-UPDATE CAS as a caveated alternative; KV explicitly ruled out), and that the run ledger's durable home is DO storage or D1 — chosen by the LLP 0014 profile — never KV.

### [Medium] "Opt into retry with backoff" has no native Cron-Trigger backing.
"Failure and retry" offers backoff retry as an opt-in. Verified: Cloudflare Cron Triggers do *not* retry a failed `scheduled()` invocation — the next execution is simply the next scheduled time. Native retry-with-backoff exists only on DO alarms (2s-start exponential, up to 6 retries) and Queues. So a calendar job that wants backoff cannot get it from a Cron Trigger; it must be routed to a DO alarm or a Queue. The RFC presents retry as a free per-job knob when on CF it silently changes which primitive the job must use.
**Resolved by:** In "Failure and retry," note that backoff retry is a primitive capability (DO alarm / Queue), not available on bare Cron Triggers, and that selecting retry on a calendar job forces it off the Cron-Trigger path on CF (or onto a Queue) — report a capability fact accordingly.

### [Medium] DO-alarm auto-retry re-runs the *entire* action, and the idempotency key is mis-specified.
"Execution: the action class" warns that a first `runMutation` may commit and fan out before a later failure, and proposes the key `(jobName, scheduledAt, runId)`. On CF this is sharper than the RFC realizes: a DO alarm that throws is auto-retried up to 6×, re-executing the *whole* alarm handler — re-fetch + re-apply *including the already-committed first mutation*. For idempotency to hold across those retries the key must be stable across attempts, so it must be `(jobName, scheduledAt)` — *not* include `runId` or `attempt` if those vary per retry (the RFC lists both in `ctx.cron` as distinct values). As written, keying on `runId` would defeat dedup on exactly the retry path CF will exercise.
**Resolved by:** Fix the idempotency key to `(jobName, scheduledAt)` (with `runId`/`attempt` as observability only), and add a sentence that on CF the DO-alarm retry granularity is the whole action, which is *why* "one durable mutation per run when practical" is load-bearing, not just tidy.

### [Low] The action→mutation path crosses DO boundaries on CF, and the subrequest/CPU budget is untraced.
"Execution: the action class" reads as in-process. On CF, the action (in a scheduler/realtime DO or cron Worker) calling `ctx.runMutation` likely crosses into the data-owner DO, then into the realtime owner — each hop a cross-DO RPC against the per-invocation subrequest budget, all under the verified 30s default CPU limit (raisable via `limits.cpu_ms`). Fine at Caltrain scale (tens of trains, one batched mutation), but the multi-hop path and its limits aren't acknowledged.
**Resolved by:** Add one line noting that on CF the action→mutation→fan-out path may cross DO boundaries (RPC + subrequest budget, 30s CPU/invocation) and that this reinforces batching writes into a single mutation per run; defer the exact routing to LLP 0014.

### [Low] The frozen-clock determinism bar is a local-only guarantee.
"Determinism and testability" makes a frozen-clock + seed + manual-tick reproducibility the acceptance bar. CF has no equivalent: `wrangler dev --test-scheduled` fires the `scheduled()` handler on demand but does not inject/freeze `Date.now()`, and DO alarm timing isn't frozen. So determinism is reproducible on the local target but not on CF as a platform guarantee.
**Resolved by:** State that the deterministic-tick guarantee is a local/Ibex capability, that the CF profile must report whether it can reproduce it (likely a capability gap or a Snapback-injected clock shim), and don't imply the bar transfers to CF for free.

## Suggestions

- The `enabled` flag (in "Cron job definition" / "Scheduler state and run ledger") can be honored at runtime for DO-alarm jobs (just don't re-arm) but *not* for a Cron-Trigger job — a Cron Trigger keeps firing until wrangler is redeployed or the management API is called, so `enabled:false` on a calendar job means "the trigger still fires and the handler early-outs," which still bills a (small) invocation. Worth one sentence so the flag's semantics aren't assumed uniform across primitives.
- Lean into the alignment: a `cron: "0 3 * * *"` string *is* a valid CF Cron Trigger expression (same 5-field syntax), so calendar jobs extract into `[triggers] crons` verbatim. Calling this out makes the static-extraction story concrete and shows the `cron:` form is the CF-friendly one while `everyMs` is the form that usually needs a DO alarm.
- "Implementation plan" step 6 defers all hosted work to LLP 0014, which is reasonable, but steps 1–3 (manifest, action kind, local scheduler) should be designed *knowing* the CF split above — e.g. normalize schedules into a `{ wakeup: cron-expr | interval-ms, needsDurableRunner: bool }` shape now so the local ledger row already carries what the CF adapter needs, rather than retrofitting.

## Open questions

- Verify the exact per-Worker Cron Trigger cap (search returned "3"; Cloudflare docs have historically said 5 — confirm against the current Limits page) and the per-account total, since it bounds how many distinct calendar jobs an app can map 1:1 before consolidation is mandatory.
- Verify the CPU/wall ceiling specifically for the `scheduled()` (cron) handler and for Queue consumers — DO alarm/HTTP is confirmed 30s default; I did not confirm the sometimes-cited 15-minute ceiling for cron/queue handlers, and an egress+multi-mutation action's worst case should be checked against whichever applies.
- For demand-gated polling: what is the acceptable staleness on first-subscribe-after-idle, and does the catch-up poll reuse the "missed ticks → run once on recovery" path or a separate trigger?
- Which CF profile (A: D1 store + DO coordinator vs B: DO app shard) owns the run ledger, and does that choice force the D1-CAS claim or the DO-storage claim? This couples directly to LLP 0014's still-open "first profile" question.
- Should the RFC commit now to "DO alarm is the canonical CF scheduler primitive and Cron Trigger is a wakeup that hands off to a DO," or keep both first-class? The former simplifies the single-runner story considerably.

## Recommended next step

**revise-and-stay-Draft.** The execution model is sound and CF-friendly; the gaps are in the scheduler/target seam and are concentrated, concrete, and fixable in-place (a demand-gating subsection, a cadence→primitive decision table, a named ledger/claim store, and a corrected idempotency key). None requires a Cloudflare prototype to resolve on paper, but all four materially change what the *local* scheduler should record and how the seam is shaped, so they belong in 0028 before it advances — and before All Aboard Phase 2 builds against it. With the GPT-5 general pass and this Claude Cloudflare pass both applied, the Draft has had a real multi-family loop; the remaining gate is the empirical local proof (run ledger, manual tick, no-overlap, deterministic recorded-feed run, action→mutation→realtime), not another review.

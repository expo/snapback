# Breakers — Claude Fable 5 (orchestrator), written before seeing codex or grok

Ranked by probability of breaking a *premise* rather than ending at a boundary kind.

1. **End-to-end-encrypted messaging with multi-device (Signal/WhatsApp).** Premise
   attacked: *rules the server can evaluate* and *the server is the authority over
   content*. The server holds ciphertext it cannot read; group membership is
   server-authoritative but *decryptability* is client-authoritative (sender keys,
   rotation on remove). Mechanism: every rule over content (search, mentions,
   "unread with keyword"), every maintained aggregate over content, and every
   structural fact about content is impossible on the server; the trace/receipt
   model still works for ciphertext rows and metadata, but **authority the server
   cannot evaluate** is a dimension the design does not have. Why uncovered: all
   twenty-nine assumed the server can read what it authorizes. Falsification:
   write "remove a member and rotate keys atomically with the membership write"
   as one program; the key material the program must produce is client-only.
   Likely outcome: **new dimension** (content-opaque partitions with
   client-evaluated authority) — or a product boundary.
2. **Offline-first field operations (disaster relief, ships, military) —
   devices offline for weeks, device-owned records, human conflict resolution.**
   Premise: *one server authority* and *a store-wide offline fence*. Mechanism:
   the device is the authority for rows it owns until sync; `maxOfflineAge`
   cannot be 3 weeks under the protected posture; conflicts are resolved by a
   person, not by OCC/LWW/merge modes. Uncovered: every app assumed the server
   settles and the device predicts. Falsification: a device-owned `patients`
   partition with a 21-day disconnect and a server-side edit to the same row.
   Likely: **new dimension — authority placement** (home authority per
   partition: server, device, region, foreign deployment).
3. **Multi-region active-active (a global marketplace or bank with regional
   homes).** Premise: *single-writer, one acceptance order*. Mechanism:
   `serialBy` and `commitTime` authority are per region; cross-region keys
   need a home; the trace/OCC set must be evaluated against the key's home.
   Uncovered: everything was one SQLite. Falsification: two regions accepting
   bids on one item with `serialBy(itemId)`. Likely: **the same authority-
   placement dimension** as #2, or "not this system" for v1.
4. **Federation (Mastodon/ActivityPub, email).** Premise: *principals and rows
   belong to one deployment*. Mechanism: remote-authored rows the local engine
   cannot certify or re-execute; rules over foreign principals; moderation
   across trust domains; retention of foreign content. Uncovered: no app had a
   second deployment. Falsification: a local timeline query over a mix of local
   and remote posts with a local block rule. Likely: **boundary kind**
   (`federation` = effects + local mirrors with declared provenance) unless
   remote rows are made first-class, which is #2's dimension again.
5. **Spreadsheet (Google Sheets).** Premise: *bounded fan-out* and *no clock or
   randomness in the data plane*. Mechanism: a formula DAG of millions of cells
   where one edit recomputes an unbounded dependent set (deeper than Notion's
   rollups); `NOW()`/`RAND()` are product features inside the computation
   graph; collaborative cell edits are CRDT-shaped. Uncovered: Notion capped
   relations; the editor covered text, not a dependency graph. Falsification:
   an edit to A1 with 100k dependents in one commit. Likely: **product
   boundary** (recompute is a job with visible staleness; volatile cells tick
   on a schedule) — but the "reactive computation graph as data" is the
   closest thing to a second *evaluation* class the family has seen.
6. **Platform with customer-supplied programs on shared tables (Shopify apps,
   Salesforce).** Premise: *one trusted author of rules*. Mechanism: tenants
   add programs and *rules* to tables the platform owns; rule composition
   (conjunction? whose wins?), per-tenant certificates and quotas as
   fairness, capability grants between tenants, a tenant's rule reading another
   tenant's rows. Uncovered: every app had one author. The language is safe for
   untrusted authors by construction — the gap is **rule ownership and
   composition**, a dimension. Falsification: two apps installing conflicting
   `read` rules on `orders`.
7. **Non-custodial wallet / signature-authorized writes (a chain-like ledger).**
   Premise: *row rules are the only authorization*. Mechanism: authority is a
   signature the server verifies but did not grant; nonces/replay; the server
   cannot forge or predict; prediction of another party's signed action is
   meaningless. Uncovered: bearer capabilities were server-minted. Likely:
   **kind** (signature-verified principal) — or #1's dimension.
8. **Version control hosting (git).** Premise: *bounded traversal*. Mechanism:
   commit DAGs of millions; merge-base, blame, log are unbounded walks;
   per-branch permissions are reachability rules. Uncovered: `dag` was capped
   at hundreds. Likely: **product boundary** (objects outside the plane, metadata
   inside; walks are jobs) — "not this system" for the graph itself.
9. **Real-time bidding / ad exchange (100k QPS, 100 ms).** Premise:
   *SQLite-first single writer*. Mechanism: scale regime where per-op
   certificates are fine and total throughput is impossible; budget pacing as
   sum-caps at extreme rate. Likely: **not this system** — a deployment
   boundary, not a language finding.
10. **Long-lived human workflows (insurance claims, hiring) with versioned
    definitions upgraded in flight.** Premise: *schema succession is
    atomic*. Mechanism: millions of instances of a workflow definition that
    changes while they run; compensation across many external systems over
    months. Likely: **jobs + versioning** — covered in spirit by Notion's
    definitions-as-data; medium-low probability of breaking.

What "broken" would mean for the design as a whole: any of #1–#4 forcing
**authority placement** (who is authoritative for which rows, and can it
move) would be a fourteenth dimension the design cannot currently state; #5
would be the first genuine second *evaluation* class; #6 is a missing
dimension (rule ownership) that a platform product cannot do without.

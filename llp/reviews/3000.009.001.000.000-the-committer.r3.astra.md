## Findings

### N1. Commit time still does not establish authority freshness — MAJOR — Committer §§3.6, 6

[§3.6, lines 261–268](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:261) checks session expiry against `at`, but [§6, lines 456–460](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:456) computes `at` entirely from previously sampled times.

For example, A pre-executes at `T=1000`, its session expires at `1050`, and an older long member holds the writer until `1100`. If the preceding pin is at most `1000`, A receives `at=1000`; its boundary predicate passes although its session has expired. This fits comfortably inside the two-second validation window. Without `not before`, retaining request time produces the same problem.

The reference samples fresh `shared.now()` while resolving write authority behind the writer, then checks `expires_at <= now`: [serve_identity.rs:58](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve_identity.rs:58), [authority.rs:207](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:207).

**Required correction:** validate credential and claim deadlines against a fresh authority-clock sample at serial batch admission. Keep that check distinct from application time where the reference does.

### N2. The time boundary and taint rules omit eligible time-dependent control flow — MAJOR — Committer §§4.1, 6

[§6, lines 461–474](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:461) supplies lifetime/credential boundary sources and excludes comparisons of `now` against stored data. The general [taint rule](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:290) covers sequences and allocations, not all `now`-derived control flow.

Consider `require hourOfDay(now) != 0 else CLOSED`, followed by an ordinary insert. A executes just before midnight; an unrelated member overtakes it and advances the pin to midnight. No examined row expires or opens, and the session remains valid, so all specified boundary checks pass. Serial execution at `at` refuses, whereas the buffered execution succeeds. The comparison is against a literal, and no time-derived value need reach a row, deadline or index. Calendar operations and this conditional behavior are established by [guide/time.md:70](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/time.md:70) and [runner.rs:612](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:612).

**Required correction:** extend conservative taint handling to all time-derived control, read-address and error-producing computations, or explicitly derive and validate their additional boundaries. Returned values and stored receipts also need resolution: `return now` is outside the listed placeholder sinks. Defining B as “anything … changes meaning” needs a complete mechanism or an explicit eligibility restriction.

### N3. Parent-local monotonicity does not justify the general indexed-placeholder exception — MAJOR — Committer §§4.1, 4.5

The [order argument at lines 293–298](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:293) works for the parent-prefixed sequence index. Durable counters preserve that argument across deletions and gaps. It does not establish safety for other indexes containing the ordinal.

A concrete counterexample:

1. At S, parent P’s counter is 9. Target table M has `unique ordinal`, with another parent’s existing row at ordinal 11.
2. A pre-executes an insert into M under P, provisionally allocating 10. Its uniqueness lookup finds no collision.
3. B inserts into a different table sharing P’s counter and commits allocation 10. A then resolves to 11.

The existing conflicting row never changed, and B touched neither M nor its indexes. Suppressing the counter dependency therefore permits validation to pass—even if the lookup span is rewritten to 11. Serial execution would refuse for uniqueness.

Counters are shared by parent across tables, as [sequences.rs:203](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/sequences.rs:203) shows; uniqueness checks use every unique index at [write.rs:621](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/write.rs:621). No authored code needs to read the allocated number.

**Required correction:** prove substitution preserves every affected index lookup, selection and constraint—not just `$sequence` ordering—or keep those operations in place. Checking only post-S footprints cannot validate a lookup whose address changed onto pre-existing data.

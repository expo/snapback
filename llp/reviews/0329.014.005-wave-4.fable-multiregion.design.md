# Multi-region active-active inventory — design (wave 2, phase A)

Archetype: an emergency-supplies marketplace, "Relay". Axis under test: single-writer authority, one acceptance order per key, and what "home" means for a row when two authorities are live.

### 1. Product contract

**What it is.** Relay is a marketplace for globally scarce emergency supplies (ventilators, dialysis consumables, satellite phones, water-purification units). Suppliers list stock; hospitals, relief organisations, businesses and individuals reserve it; regional centres ship it. It runs in six regions (`us-east`, `us-west`, `eu`, `sg`, `jp`, `br`). Every region serves its own users at all times, including while it cannot reach the other five. The interesting SKUs have tens of units worldwide and thousands of claimants at once.

**Who uses it.** *Customers* in four verified priority classes (P0 life-safety institutions, P1 relief organisations, P2 businesses, P3 individuals). *Suppliers* (list SKUs, restock, set escrow policy). *Regional operators* (fulfilment, returns, the region's legal book, incident controls). *Auditors* (read closed books).

**Vocabulary users see.** A *reservation* is always in exactly one of: **Held** (queued; the position is a lower bound while any region is out of sync), **Confirmed** (one accountable authority has assigned the units; you will be charged), **Cancelled**, **Expired**, **Released** (payment failed), **Revoked** (a declared incident took the units back; front of queue and compensated), **Returned**.

**Promises.**

1. **Reserve anywhere, always.** From any region, at any time — including while that region is cut off — a reservation request is accepted as Held within one region-local round trip.
2. **Never oversold.** Confirmed units never exceed on-hand units for a SKU, in any network state. If the last unit is reserved in two cut-off regions at once, exactly one becomes Confirmed; the other stays Held and is told so.
3. **Confirmed means confirmed.** The app never shows Confirmed unless one authority has assigned the units under an authority term that is still acknowledged. Confirmed is undone only by the customer or by a *declared incident* (promise 12).
4. **Local stock is certain.** Units pre-allocated (escrowed) to your region are confirmed by your region alone; no other region can spend them or block them.
5. **Honest numbers.** Availability is shown as "*N certain in your region*" and "*M worldwide as of hh:mm:ss*", never as one bare number; during a partition the worldwide figure carries a visible staleness notice.
6. **Deterministic, clock-free queueing.** Held reservations are served in a published order: priority class, then fair interleaving across regions, then the order the region accepted them. No timestamp ever decides who gets a unit.
7. **Positions worsen only from resync, never from favouritism.** The position shown is exact when every region is current, otherwise a lower bound labelled with the stale regions. Nobody who arrived later in your region is ever served before you.
8. **Bounded reconciliation.** Within 60 s of a partition healing at ordinary volumes, every Held reservation has been adjudicated against the remaining stock; during a surge, progress is visible and no reservation is skipped.
9. **Cancellation always succeeds immediately** from the customer's point of view, from any device; stock is released exactly once; refunds follow the release (normally seconds; across a partition, at heal).
10. **Returns release stock where they arrive**, reverse the sale in the original legal book, and refund the original payment.
11. **Regional legal books.** Every sale is booked in the book of the delivery jurisdiction *as of confirmation*; a day, once closed, never changes; late-arriving events are booked on the day they arrive with their event date attached.
12. **Incidents are declared, not silent.** Operators may move a pool's authority away from an unreachable region only with a majority of regions agreeing. Published consequence: confirmations the old region issued after the cut-over are honoured if stock remains, otherwise Revoked with front-of-queue placement and a credit.
13. **Charged only on confirmation**, never for a Held reservation; a failed capture releases the units.
14. **Move freely.** A customer may move their account between regions; history stays booked where it was; reservations keep working during the move; profile edits are *refused with a reason* during the move window, never silently deferred.
15. **Multi-device, never device-authoritative.** The same account on several devices, possibly in different regions, sees the same reservations, each labelled with an "as of" freshness; actions on one device reach the others within replication lag (normally < 1 s; across a partition, at heal). A request composed offline is "Queued on this device", not Held, until a region accepts it.
16. **Suppliers see one truth** for each SKU — on-hand, assigned, escrow per region, queue depth — with per-region freshness, and restock in their home region.
17. **Everything is traceable**: every stock change is an acceptance record `(pool, term, seq)` from exactly one authority.

**Concurrency, offline and multi-device expectations.** A session is pinned to one *serving region* for its lifetime (nearest healthy at start), so every request has one origin and the device reads its own writes; two devices sending the same request produce one reservation (device-generated id). Races for the last local unit are serialised by the region (the loser is Held, not refused); races for the last global unit are promise 2; the cancel-versus-confirm race across a partition is T1.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: **[M]** money, **[S]** secrecy, **[T]** time, **[O]** other people's data / fairness.

1. For every pool at every moment, assigned units ≤ on-hand units. **[M]**
2. Every unit belongs to exactly one pool at a time; a transfer moves units through an *in-transit* state in which no pool may assign them. **[M]**
3. Each pool has exactly one authority per term; only that authority may assign or release units of the pool; a record carrying a lower term than the highest term a replica has acknowledged for the pool is never applied as an acceptance. **[O]**
4. Within one pool and term, acceptances form a gapless, duplicate-free sequence; two records with the same `(pool, term, seq)` and different content are a *fork*, an incident resolved in favour of the record the majority of replicas already hold.
5. A reservation has exactly one origin region, which is the only writer of its request state (creation, cancellation, expiry, payment); every other region's action on it is a message the origin applies in its own order. **[O]**
6. A reservation's visible status is a pure function of its request state and its accepted assignment; every interleaving of the origin's records and the pool authorities' records converges to the same status. **[T]**
7. The app renders Confirmed only when the origin has *accepted* an assignment record from a pool authority under a term that is not demoted.
8. A reservation accepts at most one assignment; every other assignment it receives is released by the assigning pool exactly once, and units are released exactly once per assignment. **[M]**
9. Every pool serves its queue in the published order — incident priority, priority class, rank within origin, rotated region index — computed from records only, never from clocks. **[O]**
10. A displayed queue position is exact only when the displaying region is the pool's authority with a valid lease and every origin log is current; otherwise it is a lower bound labelled with the stale regions. **[T]**
11. Escrow units of a region are assigned only by that region; the home region cannot spend them, recall them unilaterally, or block them. **[O]**
12. A region that cannot renew its lease with a majority of regions stops assigning from the global pools it holds within 10 s; its escrow pools are unaffected. **[T]**
13. Authority changes always create a new term with a recorded base; acceptances the old authority issued after the base are demoted to review and, if not honoured, the customer is compensated per published policy. **[M][O]**
14. Sales are booked in the book of the delivery jurisdiction as it was at confirmation; a later move of the customer or of the SKU's home never rewrites a past entry. **[M][T]**
15. A closed book day is immutable; an event whose time falls in a closed day is booked on the open day with `asOfDay` set and a late-arrival flag. **[M][T]**
16. Payment is captured at most once per reservation, only after confirmation, with the reservation id as the idempotency key; a capture that fails releases the assignment. **[M]**
17. Returns credit the receiving region's escrow pool, reverse the original book entry, and refund the original payment. **[M]**
18. Customers see only their own reservations, their own book entries, and banded public availability; exact stock, escrow allocations and regional books are visible only to operators, auditors and (for their SKUs) suppliers. **[S]**
19. A per-customer cap on a SKU is enforced exactly at adjudication and only as a lower bound at acceptance; excess reservations are skipped in queue order, never silently deleted. **[O]**

### 3. The traps, ranked

**T1 — One row, one region (authority + merge).** *Wrong version:* `reservation` has `status` and `region`; the origin writes it, the home writes it, replication merges by last-writer-wins. *Why wrong:* the reservation has two legitimate writers in ordinary use — the origin (create, cancel, expire, payment) and the pool authority (assignment). During a partition the customer cancels at `eu` while `us-east` assigns the last unit. LWW keeps one write: if the cancel wins, `us-east`'s `assigned` counter still holds the unit until a repair job notices (the unit is dark); if the assignment wins, the cancellation is silently lost and the customer is charged. *Observation:* after heal, Σ`assigned` over pools ≠ the count of Confirmed reservations; or a customer with a "Cancelled" screenshot has a captured payment. *Right version:* two rows with disjoint single writers (`holdRequest` at the origin, `holdAssignment` at the pool authority) and a status function under which *cancelled + assigned* means "release once, charge never" (§4).

**T2 — "Home" is a column, but authority is a term (authority + temporal).** *Wrong version:* `sku.homeRegion = 'us-east'`; a region assigns if it is the home. *Why wrong:* home changes — planned re-homing, forced transfer while `us-east` is unreachable, a `us-east` restored from a pre-hand-off backup — and each time the old home keeps issuing assignments that replicas accept because the column says it may. Two authorities are live and nothing in the data says whose records count. *Observation:* after fail-over and heal the acceptance log holds `(P, 7, 501)` from `us-east` and `(P, 8, 1)` from `eu` for the same last unit, and both customers render Confirmed. *Right version:* authority is `(pool, term)`; every acceptance carries `(term, seq)`; replicas reject lower terms; a new term records its `base`; old-term records past the base are demotion candidates with a published outcome (O10).

**T3 — Decrement locally, merge later (concurrency).** *Wrong version:* every region holds a replica of `available`, decrements it if ≥ 1, confirms, and lets replication merge. *Why wrong:* with a mergeable counter both decrements apply and the counter reads −1 with two confirmations; with LWW one decrement is lost and the counter reads 0 with two confirmations. Either way promise 2 is broken and the breakage is *invisible in the counter*. *Observation:* on-hand 1, Confirmed 2, no error anywhere. *Right version:* a region confirms only from a pool it is the sole authority for; everything else is Held and adjudicated by the pool's authority in one order.

**T4 — Counts over a domain that is not all there (visibility + temporal).** *Wrong version:* position = `count(holds where sku = S and key < mine)` over local rows; availability = local `available`; the per-customer cap = `count(holds where customer = C)` locally. *Why wrong:* during a partition the local rows are a subset: the customer is told "#1" and "1 available", is Held, and at heal is #4 because three already-accepted `us-east` reservations interleave ahead; the cap is exceeded by placing in two regions. *Observation:* a position that worsens with no customer action and no incident; a customer at twice the cap. *Right version:* every count is computed with the replication frontier attached and rendered as a lower bound naming the stale regions (O5); the cap is enforced exactly at adjudication (O2 step 5). Secrecy corollary: exact "certain here" reveals a region's escrow, so customers see bands ("1–3", "4–9", "10+"); refusals still leak that a unit exists for a higher class, which the product accepts and states.

**T5 — The book is looked up at read time (temporal + money).** *Wrong version:* `bookRegion = customer.region` resolved when the report runs, and `bookDay = date(eventAt)`. *Why wrong:* a customer moving `eu → us-east` shrinks the EU book retroactively; a heal delivering yesterday's confirmations appends them into a closed day and the sealed total no longer matches the filed report. *Observation:* an auditor re-runs the month and gets a different number. *Right version:* `bookRegion` is captured on the reservation at creation from the delivery jurisdiction; book entries are append-only with `bookDay = open day at arrival`, `asOfDay = event day`, and a closed day refuses appends — including appends that arrive through replication (O11).

**T6 — Expiring what you cannot see, and trusting your own clock (time + authority).** *Wrong version:* a Held reservation expires 15 minutes after creation "because the authority has not answered"; a lease holder decides it still holds the lease by its own clock alone. *Why wrong:* the first turns every partition into mass expiry of reservations the authority would have confirmed, plus a race when the assignment arrives after the expiry. The second is the two-authorities case again: with 5 s of skew the old authority keeps assigning after the majority has fenced it. *Observation:* thousands of Expired reservations timestamped inside the partition; old-term assignments after the base. *Right version:* expiry follows the payment authorisation and the customer's deadline, decided by the origin in its own log order (a late assignment is rejected and released — O13); the lease needs majority attestation to renew, self-fences at expiry, and forced transfer waits 30 s beyond that (O10); the residue clock skew still allows is what demotion is for.

### 4. Data model

Types are neutral: `id` (opaque string), `int`, `money` (integer minor units + currency), `ts` (wall clock, informational only), `enum`. **Every table with an authority column has exactly one writer per term.** Four kinds of key each have one acceptance order:

| Key | Log | Single writer |
|---|---|---|
| `poolId` | `poolLog` | the pool's authority at the pool's current term |
| `originRegion` | `originLog` | that region |
| `bookRegion` | `bookEntry` | that region |
| `customerId` | `customerLog` | the account's home region at its current term |

**`region`** — `regionId`, `name`, `rosterIndex int` (0–5, fixed), `jurisdictions [string]`, `status enum(active|evacuating|retired)`.

**`regionLease`** — `regionId`, `leaseTerm int`, `renewedAt ts`, `expiresAt ts` (holder's clock), `attestations [regionId]`, `selfFenced bool`. Local to the holder; time-bounded.

**`sku`** — `skuId`, `supplierId`, `name`, `physical bool`, `homeRegion` (informational; the truth is `pool.term/authorityRegion`), `perCustomerCap int`, `priceByJurisdiction {jurisdiction: {money, taxRate}}`, `escrowPolicy {regionId: {cap int, horizonHours int}}`, `autoFailover bool`.

**`pool`** — `poolId`, `skuId`, `kind enum(global|escrow)`, `region` (escrow only), `term int`, `authorityRegion`, `base {term, seq}` (where this term started), `onHand int`, `assigned int`, `lastSeq int`. *Derived:* `available = onHand − assigned`. The row is a fold of `poolLog`. Time-bounded: `(term, authorityRegion)` is an interval.

**`poolLog`** (append-only; key `(poolId, term, seq)`) — `kind enum(assign|release|credit|transferOut|transferIn|transferRefused|handoffPrepare|handoffAccept|allocationEvent|demote|honorDemoted)`, `holdId?`, `qty int`, `ref` (transferId / returnId / restockId / incidentId), `authorityRegion`, `authorityClock ts`, `contentHash`. Forks are detected on `contentHash`.

**`holdRequest`** (projection of `originLog`; single writer = `originRegion`) — `holdId` (device-generated, idempotency key), `originRegion`, `originSeq int`, `customerId`, `deviceId`, `skuId`, `qty int`, `priorityClass enum(P0..P3)` *captured at creation*, `incidentPriority bool`, `supersedes holdId?`, `deliveryJurisdiction`, `bookRegion` *captured at creation*, `quote {money, taxRate}` *captured at creation*, `authState enum(authorized|deferred|invoice)`, `paymentAuthId?`, `authExpiresAt ts`, `expiresAt ts`, `requestState enum(open|confirmedUncaptured|confirmedCaptured|cancelled|expired|paymentFailed|demoted|returned)`, `acceptedAssignment {poolId, term, seq}?`, `capturedAt`, `refundState enum(none|pending|done)`.

**`originLog`** (append-only; key `(originRegion, seq)`) — `kind enum(create|createLocalAssign|acceptAssignment|rejectAssignment|cancel|cancelIntent|expire|captured|paymentFailed|refunded|returnReceived|recallRequest|demotionCandidate)`, `holdId`, `payload`. Messages to other writers (`cancelIntent`, `recallRequest`, `returnReceived`, `demotionCandidate`) travel here too; the addressee applies them in *its* order.

**`holdAssignment`** (projection of `poolLog`; single writer = the pool's authority) — `holdId`, `poolId`, `term`, `seq`, `qty`, `assignmentState enum(assigned|released|demoted|honored)`, `releasedRef {term, seq}?`.

**Status function** (derived, never stored; I6):

| `requestState` | accepted assignment | shown as |
|---|---|---|
| open | none | Held (position, lower-bound label) |
| confirmedUncaptured / confirmedCaptured | yes | Confirmed |
| cancelled | any | Cancelled (each assigning pool releases on applying `cancel`) |
| expired / paymentFailed | any | Expired / Released (same release rule) |
| demoted | demoted | Revoked (a superseding `holdRequest` with `incidentPriority` exists) |
| returned | yes | Returned |

**`poolQueue`** (materialised at the authority; an estimate elsewhere) — `poolId`, `holdId`, `originRegion`, `originSeq`, `priorityClass`, `incidentPriority`, `rankWithinOrigin int` (derived), `eligible bool`. Maintained incrementally as records are applied.

**`queueSnapshot`** — `poolId`, `term`, `eventSeq`, `positions [{holdId, position}]`, `remaining int`. Written by the authority after each allocation event; regions serve positions from it plus locally-known newer holds (a lower bound).

**`transfer`** — `transferId`, `fromPoolId`, `toPoolId`, `qty`, `state enum(debited|credited|refused)`, `outRecord {term, seq}`, `inRecord {term, seq}?`, `initiator enum(rebalancer|operator|supplier|partitionReserve|recall)`. Units are in transit between `debited` and `credited`.

**`bookEntry`** (append-only; key `(bookRegion, entrySeq)`) — `kind enum(sale|reversal|adjustment|compensation)`, `holdId`, `amount money`, `taxRate`, `eventAt ts`, `eventRef {poolId, term, seq}`, `bookedAt ts`, `bookDay date`, `asOfDay date`, `lateArrival bool`. **`bookDayClose`** — `bookRegion`, `bookDay`, `closedAt`, `entryRange {first, last}`, `totals {kind, currency → money}`, `hash`, `prevHash`.

**`customer`** (projection of `customerLog`; writer = `homeRegion` at `homeTerm`) — `customerId`, `homeRegion`, `homeTerm int`, `verifiedPriorityClass`, `addresses`, `paymentMethods`, `invoiceTerms bool`, `suspended bool`. **`customerLog`** (append-only; key `(homeRegion, seq)`) — profile changes, `movePrepare(toRegion, cutoff)`, `moveAccept(homeTerm+1, base)`.

**`replicationFrontier`** (local, never replicated) — `sourceRegion`, `lastSeq int` (each region multiplexes every log it writes into one ordered stream; per-pool frontiers derive from it), `lastAt ts`, `stale bool` (over 5 s elapsed on the observer's own clock since `lastAt` — never a comparison of two clocks).

**`incident`** — `incidentId`, `kind enum(forcedTransfer|fork)`, `poolIds`, `fromRegion`, `toRegion`, `attestations [regionId]`, `base`, `state`. **`compensation`** — `holdId`, `incidentId`, `amount money`, `state enum(pending|booked)`.

**`demandSample`** — `skuId`, `regionId`, `window enum(6h|24h)`, `holds int`. Derived from origin logs every 15 minutes.

**Append-only:** `poolLog`, `originLog`, `customerLog`, `bookEntry`, `bookDayClose`, `incident`. **Versioned by term:** `pool`, `customer`. **Time-bounded:** `regionLease`, `pool.(term, authorityRegion)`, `customer.(homeRegion, homeTerm)`, `holdRequest.expiresAt`, `holdRequest.authExpiresAt`, `transfer` while `debited`, `bookDay` open/closed, `holdRequest.priorityClass` (as of creation; a later upgrade applies only to new reservations — a stated concession). **Client-only:** the device's offline queue of unsent requests (never a server table).

### 5. The hard operations

Conventions: "at R" means the steps and transaction are local to region R; "applies" means a region processes a replicated record, in the source's order, through the same handler as a local record. Replication is a mesh (records are forwarded transitively over any path), so "partition" means no path at all.

**O1. Place a reservation** (customer, any region).
*Inputs:* session `(customerId, originRegion R, deviceId)`, `holdId` (device-generated), `skuId`, `qty`, delivery address, `paymentMethodId`, optional `deadline`.
*Authorization:* authenticated, not suspended; `qty ≤ sku.perCustomerCap` counting this customer's open holds for the SKU in R's view (a lower bound; exact enforcement is O2).
*Steps:*
1. If `holdRequest[holdId]` exists at R, return its current view (idempotent).
2. Resolve `jurisdiction` from the address, `bookRegion` from the jurisdiction map, and `quote` and `priorityClass` from `sku` and `customer` as replicated at R.
3. Payment: customers on invoice terms → `authState = invoice`. Otherwise authorise `quote.amount` with the processor (outside any transaction; key `holdId`); refusal → return the reason, write nothing; processor unreachable → `authState = deferred`, `expiresAt = now_R + 24 h`, and a worker retries authorisation every 10 minutes.
4. Transaction at R: `seq = originLog.next`; write `holdRequest` with `requestState = open`, `expiresAt = min(authExpiresAt, deadline)` unless deferred. Let `E` = R's escrow pool for the SKU. If `E.available ≥ qty` **and** `poolQueue[E]` has no eligible older hold: append `poolLog(E, E.term, E.lastSeq+1, assign, holdId, qty)`, write `holdAssignment(assigned)`, `E.assigned += qty`, set `acceptedAssignment`, `requestState = confirmedUncaptured`, append `originLog(createLocalAssign)`. Otherwise append `originLog(create)` and, if `E.available > 0`, mark `E` for an allocation event (O2). Commit.
5. After commit: if confirmed, enqueue capture (O4). Return the view (O5).
*Atomic:* step 4 (origin log, request, escrow pool log and counters are all R's). *Eventual:* the home authority learns of the hold by replication; the global position. *Optimistic UI:* a spinner only — Held appears when R commits, one local round trip away; offline devices show "Queued on this device". *Conflicts:* same `holdId` from two devices → one hold; two customers for the last escrow unit → serialised by R, the second Held.

**O2. Allocation event** (system; the pool's authority; the core of the design).
*Inputs:* `poolId P`, trigger `enum(arrival|release|credit|healReplay|demotionReview)`.
*Authorization:* only the region that is `P.authorityRegion` at `P.term`; for a global pool the region's lease must be valid and not self-fenced; at most one event per pool at a time.
*Steps:*
1. `eventSeq = P.lastSeq + 1`; `avail = P.onHand − P.assigned`; if `avail = 0` append `allocationEvent(assigned = 0)` and stop.
2. Snapshot the candidates `C`: rows of `poolQueue[P]` with `eligible = true` (a `create` applied, no terminal request record applied, no assignment from this pool). For an escrow pool, `C` is restricted to `originRegion = P.region`. Holds arriving after the snapshot go to the next event.
3. `rankWithinOrigin` = 1-based position of the hold among members of `C` with the same origin and `(incidentPriority, priorityClass)`, ordered by `originSeq`.
4. Sort `C` by `(incidentPriority desc, priorityClass asc, rankWithinOrigin asc, (rosterIndex(origin) − eventSeq) mod 6 asc)`. The rotation keeps the region tie-break fair across events; nothing in the key is a clock.
5. Walk `C` in order, in chunks of 1,000 per transaction: skip a hold whose customer already holds `≥ perCustomerCap` of this SKU in the authority's view (record `skippedCap`); if `hold.qty ≤ avail`: append `poolLog(P, term, seq++, assign, holdId, qty)`, write `holdAssignment(assigned)`, `P.assigned += qty`, `avail −= qty`, mark the queue row ineligible; else leave it eligible and continue (smaller holds may backfill). Stop when `avail = 0` or `C` is exhausted.
6. Append `allocationEvent(eventSeq, assignedCount, remaining)`; write `queueSnapshot` with the positions of the still-eligible holds in sorted order.
*Atomic:* each chunk; the order is fixed at step 4, so chunking changes nothing a customer can see. *Eventual:* origins learn of assignments by replication and accept or reject them (O4). *Optimistic UI:* none. *Conflicts:* a second event on the same pool cannot start (one writer, one event at a time); a cancel arriving mid-event is applied after it and its release starts the next event.

**O3. Cancel** (customer, any device, any region).
*Inputs:* `holdId`, serving region S. *Authorization:* the hold's customer, or an operator of the origin region.
*Steps:*
1. If S = origin R, transaction at R: if `requestState ∈ {open, confirmedUncaptured, confirmedCaptured}` append `originLog(cancel)`, set `requestState = cancelled`, `refundState = pending` if captured. If `acceptedAssignment` is on R's own escrow pool, in the same transaction append `poolLog(E, release)`, `E.assigned −= qty`, mark an allocation event for `E`. Commit. Return Cancelled.
2. If S ≠ R: append `originLog(S, cancelIntent, holdId)` at S; return "Cancellation requested — applied when region R is reachable" (truthful, not optimistic). R applies `cancelIntent` as step 1.
3. Every pool authority that applies the `cancel` record while holding an `assigned` assignment for the hold appends `release`, decrements `assigned`, and starts an allocation event. If an assignment reaches R *after* the cancel, R appends `rejectAssignment` and the pool releases on applying it. `holdAssignment` moves `assigned → released` on the first of `cancel` / `rejectAssignment` applied, never twice.
4. If `refundState = pending`, enqueue a refund (O4 variant).
*Atomic:* step 1. *Eventual:* the release at a remote pool; the refund. *Optimistic UI:* none needed — Cancelled at R is durable at the owner. *Conflicts:* cancel at R vs assign at A → both records stand, status is Cancelled, one release.

**O4. Accept assignment and capture payment** (system at the origin; spans the payment processor).
*Inputs:* an `assign` record `(P, t, s, holdId, qty)` applied at origin R. *Authorization:* system; R must be the hold's origin.
*Steps:*
1. Transaction at R: if `requestState = open` and `now_R < expiresAt`: `acceptedAssignment = (P, t, s)`, `requestState = confirmedUncaptured`, append `originLog(acceptAssignment)`, insert an outbox row `captureJob(holdId, paymentAuthId, quote.amount, idempotencyKey = holdId)`. Otherwise append `originLog(rejectAssignment, (P, t, s))`. Commit.
2. Worker: if `authState = deferred`, authorise first; then call the processor `capture(idempotencyKey)`; invoice accounts record an invoice line instead. Success → transaction: `requestState = confirmedCaptured`, `capturedAt`, append `originLog(captured)`. Hard decline → `requestState = paymentFailed`, append `originLog(paymentFailed)` (pools release on applying it). Timeout/unknown → retry with the same key; after 24 h treat as a decline.
3. `bookRegion` applies `captured` → appends `bookEntry(sale)` under O11 rules. Refund variant: `refund(idempotencyKey = holdId + returnId)`; success → `originLog(refunded)`; `bookRegion` appends `reversal`.
*Atomic:* step 1 (state + outbox). *External:* the processor, at-most-once by idempotency key. *Optimistic UI:* Confirmed at step 1 with "payment processing"; a decline moves it to Released with the reason. *Conflicts:* two workers → the key; expiry between steps 1 and 2 cannot apply (the request left `open`).

**O5. Read a reservation with its position** (customer; any region S).
*Inputs:* `holdId`, or `customerId` for the list. *Authorization:* the hold's customer; operators of the origin region.
*Steps:*
1. Load `holdRequest` (the replicated copy if S ≠ R) and its `holdAssignment` rows; derive the status from the §4 table.
2. If Held: let `P` = the SKU's global pool. Position = its position in the latest `queueSnapshot[P]` plus the locally-known holds created since that sort ahead of it by the O2 key. `positionKind = exact` iff S is `P.authorityRegion`, S's lease is valid, and no `replicationFrontier` row is stale; else `lowerBound` with `staleRegions` listed. Also report the escrow-queue position at the origin (exact if S = R).
3. Attach `asOf = {originFrontier, poolFrontier}`, `certainHere` (band) and `worldwideAsOf` from O6, and, if lower bound, the promise-7 sentence.
*Cost:* a rank query over `poolQueue` indexed by the O2 key; the snapshot makes the hot path a lookup plus a small delta. Read-only.

**O6. Availability read** (customer, supplier, operator; any region S).
*Steps:* `certainHere = E_S.available` (exact — S is the authority), banded for customers, exact for operators and the supplier; `worldwideAsOf = P.available` at S's last applied `poolLog[P]` record with its `authorityClock` and `(term, seq)`; `queueDepth = count(eligible in poolQueue[P] at S)`, labelled lower bound if any frontier is stale; `partitionNotice` if the frontier from `P.authorityRegion` is stale. Suppliers also get per-region escrow counters and in-transit transfers with frontiers. Never a single unqualified number.

**O7. Escrow transfer** (pre-allocation and recall).
*Inputs:* `transferId`, `fromPoolId`, `toPoolId`, `qty`, `initiator`. *Authorization:* operator of the source pool's authority region, the SKU's supplier, or the rebalancer (O14); a recall is *requested* by the home and *executed* by the escrow region.
*Steps:*
1. Transaction at the source authority: if `from.available < qty` refuse; append `poolLog(from, term, seq, transferOut, transferId, qty, toPoolId)`; `from.onHand −= qty`; write `transfer(debited)`. Commit. For physical SKUs this record is the shipping order.
2. The destination authority applies `transferOut` — immediately for digital SKUs, on the receiving operator's scan for physical ones — in a transaction: append `poolLog(to, term', seq', transferIn, transferId, qty)`; `to.onHand += qty`; `transfer = credited`; mark an allocation event for `to`. A retired or refusing destination appends `transferRefused`; the source re-credits on applying it.
3. Recall: the home appends `originLog(recallRequest, skuId, qty)`; the escrow region, when its own escrow queue is empty, runs step 1 from `E` to `P`.
*Atomic:* each step at its own authority. *Eventual:* the credit. *Optimistic UI:* the supplier's view shows "in transit". *Conflicts:* transferOut vs assignments at the source → serialised by the single writer; the source never reclaims units in transit — only the destination can refuse them (if it is destroyed, O10 moves its escrow pool's authority and the new authority refuses).

**O8. Return received** (fulfilment operator in region Q; spans three regions).
*Inputs:* `holdId`, `returnId`, `qty`, `condition`. *Authorization:* fulfilment operator of Q; the hold must be Confirmed or partially Returned in Q's replicated view (a stale view may refuse wrongly; the operator may override with an `adjustment` reason).
*Steps:*
1. Transaction at Q: if `condition = resalable` append `poolLog(E_Q, credit, returnId, qty)` and `E_Q.onHand += qty`; append `originLog(Q, returnReceived, holdId, returnId, qty)`; mark an allocation event for `E_Q`.
2. Origin R applies `returnReceived`: `requestState = returned` (or partial); enqueue the refund (O4 variant).
3. `bookRegion` applies `refunded`: append `bookEntry(reversal)` under O11 rules.
*Atomic:* step 1. *Eventual:* refund, reversal. *Optimistic UI:* none. *Conflicts:* two scans → `returnId` idempotency; a return credited at Q during a partition serves Q's own queue first, the product's stated preference.

**O9. Heal reconciliation** (background; runs continuously, does real work after a gap).
*Trigger:* at region X, a `replicationFrontier` row that was stale advances. *Authorization:* system.
*Steps:*
1. For each source stream: pull records from `lastSeq + 1` in order; apply each through its ordinary handler (create → queue row; assign → O4 step 1; cancel → release where X assigned; transferOut → O7 step 2; captured/refunded → book entry; cancelIntent → O3 step 1).
2. Coalesce: for each pool X is authority for, run *one* allocation event (O2, `healReplay`) per batch of at most 10,000 applied records — not one per hold. The backlog competes in the O2 order for the stock left after X served the customers it could hear — the cost of not being the authority, mitigated by O14's partition reserve.
3. Term checks on every `poolLog` record: `term > known` → adopt the new term (O10 step 6); `term < known` → not an acceptance; if it is an `assign`, forward it to the current authority as a `demotionCandidate` (O10 step 5); same `(term, seq)`, different `contentHash` → fork: keep the record already held, open a `fork` incident, withhold lease attestations from the forking region.
4. Publish a `queueSnapshot` per touched pool; each customer's position label flips from lower bound to exact when no frontier is stale.
*Atomic:* per applied record or chunk. *Eventual:* everything. *Optimistic UI:* a "resyncing" notice on Held reservations. *Bound:* ≥ 10,000 records/s per stream per region; a 30-minute surge partition (§6) is ~9 M records → under 15 minutes worst case, seconds ordinarily. *Conflicts:* new local arrivals during replay join the next event; no hold placed during replay can jump ahead of the backlog.

**O10. Lease renewal, self-fencing and authority transfer** (scheduled every 2 s; forced transfer by operator).
*Authorization:* system for renewal; forced transfer requires an operator of the target region **and** attestations from ≥ 4 of 6 regions that the current authority has been unreachable for ≥ 30 s (or `sku.autoFailover` with the same attestations).
*Steps:*
1. Renewal: X asks every peer "attest that X holds `leaseTerm` now"; with ≥ 4 attestations (its own included) X sets `expiresAt = now_X + 10 s`.
2. If `now_X > expiresAt`: `selfFenced = true`; O2 refuses for X's global pools (holds are still accepted; escrow pools are unaffected); customers see "worldwide confirmations paused".
3. Planned hand-off of `P` from A to B: A appends `poolLog(P, t, s, handoffPrepare, cutoff = s)` and stops assigning from `P`; B applies it and appends `poolLog(P, t+1, 0, handoffAccept, base = (t, s))` with counters folded to the base; A then becomes a replica. A partition between prepare and accept leaves `P` hold-only until heal; planned hand-offs never demote.
4. Forced transfer of `P` to B, transaction at B: `base = (t, lastSeq B has applied for P)`; `pool.term = t+1`, `authorityRegion = B`, counters folded to base; append `handoffAccept(base, incidentId)`; write `incident(forcedTransfer)`. B may now run O2 for `P`.
5. Demotion review at B (an O2 with trigger `demotionReview`): candidates are the old authority's `assign` records with `(t, seq > base)`, in `seq` order, ahead of every other candidate. For each: if `avail ≥ qty` append `honorDemoted` (the customer sees nothing); else append `demote`. Old-term `release` / `credit` / `transferOut` records past the base are re-issued at `t+1` — they are quantity facts, not contested acceptances.
6. The origin applies `demote`: `requestState = demoted`; create a superseding `holdRequest` with `incidentPriority = true` and the same quote; refund if captured; write `compensation` (10 % of the quote as account credit, booked in `bookRegion`); the customer sees Revoked with the new reservation at the front of the queue. A region that learns of a higher term for a pool it believed it held marks its post-base assignments "under review" at once.
*Atomic:* each region's transaction. *Eventual:* other regions adopting the term. *Optimistic UI:* none. *Conflicts:* two regions attempting forced transfer of the same pool both need 4 of 6 attestations naming *them*; attestations are single-target, so at most one succeeds; a stale authority's records go through step 5.

**O11. Daily book close** (periodic bulk; per region at local 02:00).
*Authorization:* system; auditors read; nobody writes a closed day.
*Steps:*
1. Transaction at the book region: `range = [first, last]` of `bookEntry` with `bookDay = D`; `totals` from running totals maintained on every append; `hash = H(prevHash, entries in range)`; write `bookDayClose(D)`; set `openDay = D+1`.
2. From then on any append with `bookDay = D` is refused *by the book writer*, including entries it creates while applying replicated `captured` / `refunded` records: such an entry is written with `bookDay = openDay`, `asOfDay = eventDay`, `lateArrival = true`.
3. The monthly report lists each day's sealed totals plus later "as-of adjustments", so the filed number never changes and the correction is visible.
*Atomic:* step 1. *Cost:* O(1) at close with incremental totals; 1–5 M entries/day/region.

**O12. Customer moves regions.**
*Inputs:* `customerId`, `toRegion T`. *Authorization:* the customer or an operator.
*Steps:* home H appends `customerLog(H, movePrepare, T, cutoff)` and refuses profile writes with "account moving"; T applies it and appends `customerLog(T, moveAccept, homeTerm+1, base)`; H applies that and becomes a replica; new sessions choose the nearest region regardless. Reservations, books and assignments are untouched — none is keyed on the account's home. A partition between prepare and accept freezes profile edits (with the reason) until heal; reservations keep working from any serving region. *Atomic:* each append. *Optimistic UI:* none. *Conflicts:* an edit at H after prepare, or at T before accept → refused, never queued.

**O13. Hold expiry** (scheduled at each origin every 10 s).
*Steps:* for each `holdRequest` at R with `requestState = open` and `expiresAt ≤ now_R`: append `originLog(expire)`, `requestState = expired`. A later `assign` is rejected (O4 step 1) and released. Never expire because an authority is unreachable: `expiresAt` is bound to the payment authorisation (7 days), the customer's deadline, or the deferred-authorisation window. *Atomic:* per hold. *Conflicts:* expiry at R vs assign at A → whichever R applied first wins; a rejected assignment is released exactly once.

**O14. Escrow rebalancer and partition reserve** (periodic, every 15 min, at each SKU's home authority; restock rides on it).
*Authorization:* system; suppliers set `escrowPolicy`; restock is a `poolLog(P, credit, restockId)` appended by the home authority on the supplier's receipt scan, which starts an O2.
*Steps:*
1. For each SKU with demand in the last 24 h, for each region r: `forecast_r = max(holds_r(6h), 1.5 × holds_r(24h) / 4) × escrowPolicy[r].horizonHours / 6`; `target_r = min(forecast_r, escrowPolicy[r].cap)`; `delta_r = target_r − E_r.available − inTransit_r`.
2. Positive deltas share `P.available − reserveFloor` proportionally; issue O7 from `P` to each `E_r`. Negative deltas beyond 20 % slack send `recallRequest` to r.
3. Partition reserve: when the home's frontier from r has been stale for > 30 s, issue O7 from `P` to `E_r` for `min(forecast_r(6h), P.available × demandShare_r)`. The units sit in transit until heal, then serve r's escrow queue — this is how the home authority stops spending against customers it cannot hear.
*Atomic:* each transfer. *Eventual:* the credits. *Cost:* ~20,000 active SKUs × 6 regions per cycle; the transfers are the cost, not the arithmetic.

### 6. Scale and cost

A successful deployment: 6 regions; 250,000 SKUs, ~20,000 with demand on a given day, ~500 hot during a surge; 40 M customer accounts, 30,000 suppliers; baseline 300 reservations/s worldwide, surge 5,000/s for 4–12 hours on the hot SKUs; replication lag p50 80 ms, p99 400 ms; partitions a few times a year lasting 5 minutes to 2 hours; hot pools with 10–50 on-hand units and 20,000–100,000 Held reservations.

Rows: `originLog` ~25 M/day at baseline, ~400 M/day in a surge (90 days hot, then archived); `poolLog` per hot pool 50–200 records/s — one writer sustains it; `bookEntry` 1–5 M/day/region; `poolQueue` for a hot pool 100,000 rows; `queueSnapshot` ~2 MB per event on a hot pool, replicated five times.

What becomes expensive:

- **O5 positions and O6 availability** at millions of reads/s in a surge: served from `queueSnapshot` plus a bounded local delta and from the projected `pool` counters tagged `(term, seq)` — never a rank query or a log fold per read.
- **O2 at heal** on a hot pool: 100,000 candidates sorted once and walked in 1,000-row chunks — ~20 s per pool; 500 hot pools across six authorities run in parallel.
- **O9 replay**: ~9 M records after a 30-minute surge partition; per-record handler work dominates, and coalescing allocation events (one per 10,000 records) keeps it linear.
- **O11**: trivial at close with incremental totals; ruinous as a sum over the day.
- **O14**: cheap arithmetic, but every positive delta on a physical SKU is a shipment — the real cost is trucks, so the horizon is a supplier policy.
- **Fan-out**: every `poolLog` record reaches five regions; every `originLog` record reaches the pool authority, the book region and the customer's other serving regions — ~1.5 MB/s per link at surge. Ordering, not bandwidth, is the constraint.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

**U1. Term-fenced single-writer logs per key, enforced on the replica.** Every pool write must carry a `(term, seq)` minted by the pool's authority; a replica must refuse to *apply* a lower-term record while still *storing* it for demotion review, and must detect forks by content. Ordinary backends have one primary per database (not per key, not per term) or multi-primary with LWW/CRDT merge — the latter is T3. *Concession:* one global primary per SKU with no escrow, i.e. drop I11 and promise 4. *Cheat boundary:* the moment a region cannot confirm *anything* during a partition, "Held" has become "refused" and the concession has removed the axis rather than met it. A legitimate concession is fewer, larger authorities (per-region rather than per-pool terms) while escrow pools stay region-owned.

**U2. Two-writer rows with a convergent derived status and exactly-once side effects on first observation.** `holdRequest` and `holdAssignment` are one product object with two authorities; each half replicates independently; the status is derived; and the *release* and *capture* side effects must fire exactly once when the responsible writer first observes a transition, whichever order the halves arrive in. Ordinary backends replicate rows and run triggers on local writes only. *Concession:* route cancellations through the pool authority. *Cheat boundary:* the customer cannot cancel during a partition, or the cancel is "accepted" by a region that is not the request's durable owner and can be lost — promise 9 broken.

**U3. Reads that return the replication frontier, and counts labelled as lower bounds.** O5 and O6 need the result to carry the frontier vector it was computed from, and the app must render "exact" versus "lower bound, regions X and Y stale". Ordinary backends return rows. *Concession:* a global "data may be stale" banner. *Cheat boundary:* a position or availability count shown as a bare number while a frontier is known stale — T4 verbatim.

**U4. Deterministic, clock-free bulk adjudication as one logical event.** O2 sorts up to 100,000 rows by a key that depends on the *set* (`rankWithinOrigin`), assigns from a counter, appends N acceptances and a snapshot, and no other writer may interleave into the pool until the event ends — yet it must be chunked. Ordinary backends offer per-row mutations or one unbounded transaction. *Concession:* one hold per transaction in a precomputed order under a pool-level lock held throughout. *Cheat boundary:* a hold placed during replay served ahead of the backlog, or an order that depends on the wall clock of arrival.

**U5. Append-only books whose immutability binds late-arriving replicated writes.** I15 is a constraint on *arrival time versus event time* and must hold when the book region is applying a replicated `captured` record, not only on app-initiated writes. Ordinary backends let replication bypass constraints or leave them as conventions. *Concession:* re-open the day and re-issue the report. *Cheat boundary:* a closed day's total changing after the report was filed.

### 8. Confidence

**Standard practice, used as designed:** lease-based single-writer authority with increasing terms and fencing tokens, majority attestation for renewal and fail-over, replica-side rejection of stale terms (consensus practice, applied per pool rather than per cluster); per-region allocated inventory for local certainty (retail "allocated stock", airline seat pools, ticketing allotments); two-phase transfers with an in-transit state (inventory accounting); append-only journals with daily close, hash chaining and as-of adjustments (accounting); idempotency-keyed capture and refund (payment processors); request/decision row splitting with a derived status (order/fulfilment pairs); device-generated ids; capturing price, jurisdiction and class at acceptance rather than joining at read time (any ledger).

**Invented for this exercise, believed sound:** the fair interleave key `(priorityClass, rankWithinOrigin, rotated region index)` — clock-free, but "fair by region" is one legible choice; demand-proportional interleaving is another; the demotion review with "honour if stock remains, else revoke with front-of-queue and a 10 % credit" — the numbers are placeholders; the partition reserve (O14 step 3), an escrow transfer the destination cannot see until heal, sized by a forecast; the rule that a hold sits simultaneously in its origin's escrow queue and the global queue with the origin accepting the first assignment it applies — the most delicate invention, resting entirely on "a rejected assignment is released exactly once"; frontier-labelled reads as a product surface rather than an internal consistency token; banding "certain here" as the secrecy compromise; deferred payment authorisation so that promise 1 survives an unreachable processor.

**Not addressed:** roster changes (a seventh region changes `mod 6`), multi-SKU bundles (all-or-nothing across pools under the same accept-first rule, not designed here), price changes while queued, and the shipment layer beyond the transfer record. The scale figures are invented but shaped by public surge e-commerce and ticketing numbers.

## Fundamental issues

**1. The lifetime machine models a resumable pause that production never performs and that the custody layer cannot honor.**
Every production `pause()` is the first step of scope retirement: `app.ts` hide, release, fenceScopes, logout and the catch paths at lines 374, 408, 435, 836, 857, 883, 963 and 1002, and `app-persona.ts` 76, 110, 127, 164, 179 and 217. No caller ever calls `start()` on a handle it paused. The only pause-then-start sequences are the round 14 and 16 fixtures. Tab visibility is wired to `web-runtime.ts` and `web-worker-suspend.ts`, never to custody, so §3's "hidden-tab pauses are the normal browser lifecycle" does not describe this code. And `onlineCustody.pause()` at `app-online.ts:429` sets `active = false` permanently by LLP 3000.023 §3.4, while `start()` returns early on `started`. So `paused(installed) → start → installed` is not implementable without reworking custody against that ruling. Today that sequence leaves a dispatcher whose every mutate answers E_AUTH. Three rounds hardened a path only tests take. This is a third of the proposal built on a caller that does not exist. Instead: pause is retire. One terminal path, `opening | denied | recovering | installed → retiring → retired`. If an opening-pause-then-resume case is wanted, it is one state and needs a named production caller first.

**2. One table is evaluated at two times with facts that move between them, and the step that moves them is not in the table.**
The wrapper decides at arrival. The callback decides when `observe()` sees the persisted receipt (`progressive-writes.ts:312–322`). Between them `storeIdentity`, `incarnation`, `backend.generation` and `closed` can change, and RFC §6 requires the store check "immediately before installing cuts". The doc says the callback's input "differs only in `when` having elapsed". It does not. `authority`, `store` and `generation` are all re-derived, which is why `authorityCurrent` is a closure. A `when: after-persistence` output cannot carry a re-check. Worse, on a `sent` response under `current()` the wrapper first runs `onlineEvidence` (`local.ts:1967`, defined at 973), which can witness a shrink and retire the dispatcher, bump incarnation, clear `journalCuts` and change `storeIdentity`, and only then computes `qualified`. That is a decision with large outputs from the same response, and it changes the inputs the table then reads. The settlement table has no `metadata` output and no ordering. Totality is claimed over inputs that are not fixed at the event. Instead: two functions. One at arrival over dispatch-time facts, producing metadata, revocation, scope and retirement. One at settlement over the persisted receipt and current facts, producing the cut and its scope.

**3. The four rings are the per-id machine where rounds 11, 14 and 16 bit, and the proposal's own `when: now` rule makes most of them unnecessary.**
`receipts`, `admitted`, `completed` and `responding` (`progressive-writes.ts:56–85`) exist to carry live evidence from the wrapper to a settlement observed later, and to stop a late own response settling twice. If revocation always applies at arrival, `preserved` never needs retention. If `settle()` persists `storeId`, `generation` and `op` on the receipt at `app-online.ts:144`, the settlement callback needs nothing from memory, inspected peer receipts gain a known generation instead of today's forced all-table cut, and the `completion` and `evidence` dimensions vanish along with two rings. `valid()` at `app-journal.ts:105` checks only known fields, so older clients read the record unchanged. Intents already persist `op`, `args` and `storeId`, so the disclosure rule is untouched. What remains is a per-response handoff of store and generation from the wrapper to custody, which belongs on the existing `ownDispatch` hook surface. The doc rules the journal shape out of scope. This change is additive and compatible, and it removes state rather than tabulating rules over it.

**4. The matrix tests the rule layer, but about half the surviving findings were in fact extraction, which stays as conditionals.**
"Recovery gated on `current()`", "preservation gated on `current()` after peer completion", "closed flag precedes the device fence" and "refusal fixture not the wire shape" were wiring bugs. The row was fine and the inputs were computed wrong. A pure `decide()` over enumerations is cheap to get right. The mapping from `json`, request and client state to the input record is where the Highs came from, and the doc leaves it inside the executors. The product also has many unreachable rows: `store ≠ same` implies `incarnation-moved` except at first identification, and `inspected-peer` implies `preserved: absent`, so properties pass vacuously there. The acceptance rule "a High in the decision layer means the table is wrong" will misclassify the likely findings. Instead: make extraction a pure function over a plain state snapshot, test it with the rounds' wire fixtures, and mark unreachable rows.

**5. Store refusal has more than one deciding actor and the refusal table names only the wrapper.**
At `app-online.ts:154–165` the raw transport's `onWrite` independently calls `refuseStore(write.why)` with no adopted store, retiring every intent, while the wrapper calls it with `storeIdentity`, keeping adopted-store intents. Whichever runs first wins, because `refuseStore` returns the existing retirement. For E_STORE_RESTORE under obsolete authority the wrapper does nothing and the raw path retires everything. `bindIntentStore` refuses with E_STORE_REPLACED at dispatch binding and must not retire. The E_AUTH-after-rotation retry at `local.ts:1939` and `refusedSession` are also decisions in the same wrapper that neither table expresses. Instead: give the refusal decision a `source` input and a `dispatcher: retire-all | retire-foreign | keep` output, make the raw `onWrite` path an executor of it or remove it, and give E_AUTH a `transport: retry | settle` output or an explicit ordered exclusion.

## §5 questions

1. **Function or per-id machine.** Both, but not as drawn. The per-id machine already exists as the four rings. Make it one record per id, or remove it per issue 3, and put the evidence rules in two small pure functions at the two real decision times. Confidence 0.70.
2. **Revocation before persistence.** Yes. The preserved list is server authorization evidence as of the response. Receipt durability is local custody. Applying the bound first withdraws rows, the re-read parks on pending custody, and the cut lands at settlement. That is LLP 3000.023's rule. The one hazard is issue 2: the arrival scope must be computed against pinned facts, deterministically before or after the same response's metadata adoption. Confidence 0.80.
3. **Four-valued authority.** Sufficient for settlement outputs. The code treats credential-rotated and incarnation-moved identically there: the cut still installs when the store is the same and `preserved` becomes empty. They differ only for the E_AUTH retry and for metadata adoption, both outside this table. `lifetime-ended` should be the machine's state, not re-derived from two flags. Confidence 0.75.
4. **Where the machine lives.** In `progressive-writes.ts`, the document-level owner that spans nested installs. `onlineCustody` is per attempt, its `active` is terminal, and it serves non-progressive callers too. Leave it a resource with open and retire. The app's `pause()` becomes the machine's retire event. Confidence 0.75.
5. **Simpler design.** Yes, issues 1 and 3 together. Pause is retirement. Receipts persist store, generation and op. Revocation applies at arrival. No evidence or completed rings. Two small decision functions over facts fixed at their event. Fewer states, fewer rows, and it deletes the code where three of the six rounds' findings lived. Confidence 0.70.

**DIRECTION RIGHT WITH ONE CHANGE, 0.72.** The change: model the lifecycle the code actually has instead of tabulating the accretion. Pause is retirement, decisions happen at arrival and at persisted settlement, and the receipt carries its own store and generation so nothing has to be remembered between them. Issues 2, 4 and 5 follow from or shrink under that change.

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKER — session fence can admit an old-token commit after takeover
**IN-DELTA. §6.5 step 1, §6.6.**

Store-port §5.1 keeps the token change and the conflicting lock in one transaction, so a new fence either finishes before the token commit or reads the new token and refuses. Revision 5 replaces that row share for R bodies with a lifetime shared advisory lock, and only says the successor takes the exclusive lock "before" token publication. That permits acquire, unlock, then update the token.

PostgreSQL also grants a new shared advisory lock while an exclusive request is waiting (queue barging). A kill loop closes that only if the exclusive request is still the wait the watcher is polling.

Concrete interleaving:

1. Successor claims the expired lease, acquires the exclusive advisory lock, and releases it. The token is still T1. Reconciliation has not run.
2. The old process has not yet observed renewal failure. It opens a new data session, takes `pg_advisory_lock_shared`, and validates T1 in that later command (§6.6). Qualification succeeds.
3. That body does not take `_owner FOR KEY SHARE` (the point of the session fence). It inserts the attempt row and commits revision 501. The old owner advances `W` and acknowledges the client.
4. The successor publishes T2, scans records (it still sees 500), certifies `H = 500`, and reseeds. §4.4 then allows 501 to be reused as a lost trailing reservation.

The acknowledged write is either discarded, voided, or overwritten by the new owner's revision 501. Witness 5 checks that an exclusive lock was taken before publication and that a stale token refuses. It does not require the lock to be held through the token commit, and it does not run this window. The spike's same-statement `pg_advisory_xact_lock` plus token update is stricter than the text.

**Fix:** specify one transaction that takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock` and updates the token, with the existing `pg_blocking_pids` watcher already looping (barging included). A session-level exclusive lock must not be unlocked until that commit is visible. An R body runs only after the shared lock is granted and a later command sees the new token, incarnation, generation, and admitted digests. `DISCARD ALL`, unlock, or backend replacement clears qualification. The certificate transaction re-reads the maximum attempt row. Add that interleaving to witness 5.

### 2. MATERIAL — the 1,000-sequence evidence floor collapses at the N=1 rate
**IN-DELTA. §5.2 `/changes` and sync rows, §6.4.**

The floor is inherited. At one sequence per request and 4,525/s it is about 220 ms (about 440 ms even at today's ordered rate, and shorter than link B). Catch-up prunes `_change_evidence` below `H.s − 1000`. A point read that jumps to `V ≥ H` still sees a coherent prefix. A `/changes`, G5, or sync consumer whose cursor is older than the floor does not: the delta span is gone. Missing evidence must not mean "no change" (§6.4), so the safe behavior is revocation and resync. At this rate that is the steady state for any client a round trip behind, not a delay case. Witness 7 checks that the window is honored, not that a client one RTT behind still gets a contiguous delta.

**Fix:** size the floor in time at the qualified rate (several seconds, and at least the oldest in-flight reader), or keep evidence until every admitted cursor passes it. Fail qualification on a revocation storm under the W1–W3-plus-writer load. Do not treat resync as a passing live-query result.

### 3. MATERIAL — 4,525/s is not supported by the measured components, and the 6.0 gate omits the restoration that moves the mean
**IN-DELTA. §8.1–§8.2, §10.1 R0, §10.3.**

Round-2 fixed W4/A at 4,525/s is a sent mean of 7.04–7.24 ms against 6.4. The diagnostic split is flight 5.490 ms (pool wait 0.197 inside it), mailbox 0.805, HTTP about 0.4–0.6. Subtracting mailbox and pool wait on paper yields about 6.04–6.24 ms from different cohorts. That is the spike's own-commit residence.

What the spike does not include, and what §8.2 still treats as a 0.4 ms allowance between a 6.0 round-3 result and 6.4:

- Prefix-ordered acknowledgements. The spike replies at its own commit (§4.5 forbids that). One slow predecessor (flight max 37.8 ms in the 4,525 diagnostic) holds every higher reply. Equal flight times add nothing. The measured tail does.
- Two-image updates inside the same flight (0.05–0.30 PG CPU-ms/write, 5–25% WAL, unmeasured). With 29 data loans the occupancy cap is `29/4525 = 6.41` ms. The non-pool flight is already 5.293 ms, about 83% of that cap. Extra work in the flight brings pool wait back and spends the round-3 removal.
- Durable attempt-row work beyond the `_commits` and `_change_evidence` inserts already in the flight, catch-up flushes, and footprint holds through `W`.

PG (Postgres) CPU is about 0.44 ms/write versus about 0.245 on the ordered lane, already past the +30% stop and the 0.35 target. §8.4 and §13 say this honestly, and default-on still requires a restored 6.4 ms mean plus an author CPU ruling. Round 3's ≤6.0 gate is only the unrestored spike (§10.1 R0). It can pass while the restored path cannot.

No simpler shape in §11 reaches 4,525. C0′ (the product-shaped harness) stopped batched C at 2,871/s and ordered C at 2,149/s. Early acknowledgement would drop the prefix wait but is a different consistency contract. The ordered L′ lane (one flight on validated warm facts) remains the fallback if restoration eats the margin.

**Fix:** make the experimental gate the restored protocol: ordered release, two-image writes, attempt records, and the real 29-loan pool, with mean ≤6.0 before any promotion math. Report prefix lag and loan occupancy as their own series. Keep default-on at restored ≤6.4 on 450b, and do not let a 6.0 unrestored pass move the odds.

### 4. MATERIAL — default-on can be decided on the unkeyed cell while the SDK path drains R
**IN-DELTA. §4.3, §6.7, §10.3.**

The SDK sends keyed operations. Until the keyed-R slice, every keyed or receipt write routes to O (the ordered lane) only after an R→O drain: stop dispatch, resolve up to 512 reservations, settle catch-up, then the O head compare-and-swap. One keyed call in a hot R phase serializes the lane. The S1 (scale method) headline is the unkeyed W4/A cell. §10.3 says to measure keyed W4, but the default-on bar is unkeyed 4,525 at ≤6.4 ms, B (link B) at 2,262, and no regression on cells that already win. A keyed-path regression or a drain cliff does not veto default-on. Exact-key is already red in the spike (main 3/3, R 2/3 on both links) and is only a stop if someone treats it as an exact-rung regression.

**Fix:** before default-on, require keyed W4 and the 1% mix, with the drain in the path, to meet the same rungs as the unkeyed cell or an explicit author ruling that unkeyed-only R stays opt-in. Keep the "one collision must not pin disjoint traffic in O" rule as a measured gate, not a sensitivity note.

### 5. MINOR — raw `r` is not a prefix; inspect views are a longer committed prefix than `W`
**PRE-EXISTING. §5.3.**

Managed paths in §5.2 (queries, `/changes`, sync, G5/G6, receipts), bound to one paired `V` with the image `CASE`, do not show a commit above `W` or drop one below it, provided writer validation does not use that same `CASE` (it must see raw `orev` and fail the tripwire). Two unmanaged SQL paths differ on purpose. `_sb4_prefix()` can show a contiguous committed prefix ahead of in-memory `W` but not across a hole. Direct `r.data` can show revision 11 while 10 is still a hole. That is disclosed. It is not the product surface. The census has to be what keeps managed SQL off that path.

**Fix:** keep the disclosure next to the S1 configuration. In witness 6, add one planted unbound `r.data` predicate and require the census to reject it.

## What held

The serial argument in §4.6–§4.8 and §5.1 is sound for the admitted class: one unpublished writer per key, footprints held through `W`, `odata` covering `[owm, orev)`, voids as no-ops, and `H ≤ W` except where an O (ordered-lane) head compare-and-swap moves `H` in the same transaction as the images. Out-of-order commits stay invisible to a reader who binds `W` and then opens one snapshot. Per-client minima match prefix-ordered acknowledgements. They are not a substitute for two-image rows. Routing (allocate only at dispatch, drain before O, reseed from a fresh head on the way back) closes the spike's empty-queue re-entry bug. The void-versus-attempt key race still prevents a committed attempt and a void at the same key, and recovery after a real exclusion does not drop an acknowledgement that was already in `W`.

Default-on is otherwise strict enough: restored 6.4 ms, the CPU ruling, read and exact non-regression, and no automatic rerun of an unknown. It does not cover findings 1, 2, or 4.

1. **Medium — D5.5 and §A: a filtered ≤8-lane merge cannot require the asserted drain refill.**

   At `read.rs:840–842`, the outer page size **N** becomes `scan_prepared(..., N+1, ...)`. The filter’s early exit at `read.rs:642` therefore stops after **N+1 matches**, not N. An uncut shorter read is exhausted.

   Concrete scenario: two overlapping filtered lanes, `last 2`. Each non-exhausted lane opens with three matching rows. Before the third distinct result establishes `has_more`, a lane can have popped at most two distinct IDs. Cross-lane duplicates cannot exhaust its three-row supply sooner: each ID occurs once within that lane. Thus draining needs no refill. A tighter ceiling cuts opening instead and must not produce the proposed continuation.

   **Smallest correction:** replace D5.5 with a control covering filtered small merges and opening cuts; correct §A’s corresponding disposition. Do not change batching merely to manufacture this positive. The same argument applies through eight lanes.

2. **Medium — D2.4: specify how “its own word cut” survives an already-populated diagnostic field.**

   `read.rs:427` records only the first overflow. Moreover, a matching page discovers its words **before** entering `merge_with_words` (`read.rs:425–447`). Neither a comparison of the global field before/after traversal nor a snapshot taken only at merge entry implements the stated predicate.

   Concrete scenario: an auxiliary search first exceeds 32 words. The returned matching page subsequently also exceeds 32 words, with its omitted 33rd lane containing a row ahead of the last emitted key. The global field does not change. Treating “unchanged” as “no own cut” publishes an unsafe candidate and its continuation skips that row. Re-executing the same site can produce the same problem.

   **Smallest correction:** require a traversal-local overflow fact, recorded on every overflow independently of the first diagnostic. Carry it from word discovery into its generated merge and propagate relevant child-lane/filter cuts to the enclosing traversal. Preserve the runner-wide field for answer completeness and `read_cut`; do not clear it for scoping. Add an earlier-overflow/owner-overflow regression and an earlier-overflow/uncut-owner control.

   This makes the already-stated behavior implementable; it does not require the descoped answer-wide discharge accounting.

3. **Medium — D5: fix fixture arithmetic, differential labels and control scope.**

   D5.1 works when explicitly seeded as twelve eligible lazy lanes, with the first match at examination 100 and subsequent matches every 100 rows. Opening costs **1,200** examinations. Sixty-nine successful refills bring the ledger to **8,100**; the 70th refill fails at **8,193**. Under D’s stop rule, page one has **70 emitted rows** and a candidate. `scan_prepared`’s filtered early exit is what avoids lookahead here (`read.rs:625–642`).

   D5.2 is not yet a guaranteed positive. “More than eight” includes a terminal 33-word expansion; “rare” can make opening itself exceed the ceiling. A concrete valid fixture is **nine disjoint matching word lanes**, with the second term present every 100th row and enough rows in every lane. Each unfiltered one-head read examines through its **second** match: opening costs **9 × 200 = 1,800**, then 31 successful refills cost **6,200**. The 32nd refill cuts at **8,193**, during lookahead, after 32 emissions. Word discovery costs ten index-entry charges, comfortably below 16,384 (`read.rs:416–431, 591–638`; `meter.rs:23`).

   Additional concrete acceptance mismatches:

   - The current lazy gate still excludes non-unique/no-ID and nullable-unique/no-ID indexes (`rules.rs:90–94`). State the D0.1 matrix over configurations actually admitted to each path; do not silently imply a broader lazy admission change.

   **Smallest correction:** pin the fixtures above, relabel preservation assertions, narrow C2, and explicitly add rejection of a pre-change no-ID v1 token. The missing compatibility assertion should expect `CursorError::Descriptor`, not reinterpretation of the old position.

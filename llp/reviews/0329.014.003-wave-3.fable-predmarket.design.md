### 1. Product contract

**What it is.** Forecast is an exchange for shares in the outcomes of future events. A market asks a question with a stated resolution source ("Will the Fed cut rates in September? Source: the FOMC statement") and has one or more outcomes; each outcome has YES shares paying $1.00 if it happens and NO shares paying $1.00 if not. Prices are integer cents 1–99; the YES price reads as a probability. Traders place limit orders on a per-outcome book; the exchange matches them, holds every dollar of collateral until resolution, and pays all holders.

**Who uses it.** Traders (retail and algorithmic, via API keys); market creators; reviewers; a five-person resolution committee; oracle operators; support/admin. Spectators read everything public without an account.

**Promises.**
1. YES + NO of one outcome is always worth exactly $1.00; you can buy or sell either while the market is open.
2. Every order is a limit order with a time-in-force (GTC, GTD, IOC, FOK). You never pay more per share than your limit and may pay less.
3. An order is either accepted — with a sequence number and an immediate statement of how much filled at what prices — or refused with one named reason; nothing is "probably in".
4. The depth you see is really resting: if the book shows 12,000 at 54¢ and you buy 5,000 at 54¢, you get them minus only what someone ahead of you took since you looked — and the feed shows those trades.
5. Better price fills first; at the same price, whoever the exchange accepted first fills first.
6. A cancel and a fill of the same shares never both succeed; you are always told which happened.
7. Available cash is always withdrawable. Locked cash is exactly what your open orders could still cost, worst-case fee included, and returns the instant an order fills for less, cancels, or expires.
8. Fees are quoted before you submit as a maximum, charged only to the aggressor, computed from the schedule in force at the fill, and never restated.
9. Every share is backed by a dollar the exchange already holds; payouts do not depend on anyone's solvency.
10. You cannot exceed your position limit, even by accident — resting orders count as if they would fill. Caps and limits are shown on the market page.
11. Nobody but you and the exchange can see your orders, positions, balance, or which trades were yours. The public feed shows every trade without identities; your handle appears only if you opt in, and only as the aggressor.
12. Nobody resolves their own market: the creator cannot review, propose for, dispute, or vote on it; committee members who hold or ever traded in a market cannot vote on it.
13. A proposed result stands for a 6-hour dispute window. Anyone can dispute with a $100 bond; the committee decides, and its votes are secret until the round closes and public forever after.
14. Payout starts within a minute of finalization and every holder is paid; the market says "settled" only when every position is paid and the books balance to the cent. No payout is ever clawed back.
15. Your portfolio shows realized and unrealized profit by average cost, identical on every device; all your devices see the same orders, fills, and balances within a second, and a fill notifies each.
16. Offline, you can read your cached portfolio and books with a banner showing their age; you cannot place orders. Cancels queue and replay on reconnect; a queued cancel may find the order already filled, and the app says so.
17. Under load the exchange may be slow; it is never wrong. Thousands of people may hit one book in the same second; the result is as if they arrived one at a time in acceptance order.

### 2. Invariants, in plain language, before any code

Tags: [$] money, [S] secrecy, [T] time, [O] other people's data.

1. [$][T] A YES share pays 100¢ if its outcome resolves YES and 0¢ otherwise; NO pays the reverse; in a voided market both pay 50¢.
2. [$] At every commit, per outcome: YES outstanding = NO outstanding = the outcome's escrow in dollars. Shares are created only in pairs against a dollar and destroyed only in pairs releasing one.
3. [$] Cash never appears or vanishes. Every movement is a ledger entry; the entries of one fill, settlement chunk, or dispute outcome sum to zero across all accounts, escrow and house included; a balance equals the sum of its entries.
4. [$] Locked cash equals exactly the remaining reserves of the account's open cash-funded orders; available = total − locked; withdrawals and new reserves come only from available.
5. [$] An order never fills for more than its quantity, never at a price worse than its limit, never after the market closed, and never against an order of the same account.
6. [T][O] Within a book, a better-priced resting order fills before a worse one; at equal price the earlier-accepted fills first; every book-affecting operation carries a unique sequence number in acceptance order.
7. [$][T] A cancel and a fill cannot both succeed for the same shares; the cancel response states what happened.
8. [S][O] Public depth at a level equals the remaining quantity of open orders at that level in the committed state named by its sequence number — never more, never a state that never existed — and reveals no order count and no owner.
9. [S] Orders, positions, balances, and the parties to a fill are visible only to their owner and the exchange. The public feed carries price, quantity, aggressor side, time, and the taker's handle only if opted in; makers are never named.
10. [$] Actual plus potential position per outcome and token never exceeds the per-user limit; outstanding shares never exceed the open-interest cap. Potential means "if every open order filled".
11. [T][$] From the moment a market closes, no order fills or rests and no position changes until settlement pays it.
12. [$][T] A market pays only after its resolution is final: a proposal survived its window undisputed, or a committee round decided it. Nothing paid is ever reversed.
13. [$] Every position is paid exactly once; the sum paid per outcome equals its escrow to the cent; a settlement that cannot prove that halts and pages a human rather than paying or adjusting.
14. [O] A creator may not review, propose for, dispute, or vote on their market. A proposer may not dispute their own proposal. A committee member with any position, fill, or order in the market votes with weight zero, and the recusal is recorded.
15. [$][T] Fees use the schedule in force at the fill's time and are stored on the fill; a later schedule change never alters them, and the fee never exceeds the maximum quoted at placement.
16. [$][T] A dispute is accepted only inside the window and takes a bond at filing; the bond returns with a reward if the dispute prevails and is forfeited if not.
17. [S][T] Committee votes are unreadable by anyone but the voter until the round closes, and permanently public afterward.
18. [$] Realized profit is average-cost in integer cents with a stated rounding rule; the same history yields the same numbers everywhere.
19. [T][$] Each scheduled action (open, close, propose, finalize, settle) happens exactly once per market even if the scheduler runs twice.

### 3. The traps, ranked

**Trap 1 — Matching is one program, not a sequence of updates (concurrency, money).**
Wrong version: `place_order` reads the best resting orders, then applies fills one at a time, each its own commit; or reads them all, computes the fills, and commits with per-row version checks and retry-on-conflict. Why wrong: the first leaves half-applied sweeps — a cancel lands between fills 2 and 3, fill 3 hits a canceled order whose reserve was already released, `remaining` goes negative, and unlocked cash moves; a crash mid-sweep leaves a taker holding unpaid shares and an escrow that no longer equals outstanding. The second is atomic but violates priority: under contention the taker that arrived first keeps retrying (its read set is the largest), the later taker commits first and takes the price, and an 800-order sweep can starve indefinitely during a burst. FOK is the sharpest form: "fill what you can, then cancel the rest" is not FOK. Observation: after a burst, `Σ remaining over open orders at a level ≠ level.qty`, `escrow ≠ 100 × outstanding`, an order with `filled > qty`, or two takers whose fill timestamps are in one order and whose sequence numbers are in the other. Design's answer: every book has one acceptance sequence; a placement is one atomic unit that reads and writes an unbounded, predicate-selected set of resting orders plus 2N+3 balances, positions, levels, and the escrow; units commit in sequence order.

**Trap 2 — Depth is a sum over rows the viewer may not read (visibility).**
Wrong version A: levels are computed from the orders the viewer may read. Each viewer sees only their own orders, so "depth" is 0 or their own size — the aggregate is admitted and returns a confidently wrong number. Wrong version B: expose order rows to everyone and aggregate client-side; correct numbers, total secrecy failure — every order's owner, size, and lifetime are public. Wrong version C: maintain a public `book_levels` table asynchronously after fills commit; correct owners, but phantom liquidity — a level shows 12,000 for 300 ms after 9,000 was taken, and the "best bid" is a canceled order. Observation: two users at the same instant see different depth for one level; an IOC order is refused against a level the book showed as full with no intervening trade in the feed; an `orders` read returns rows carrying another account's id. Design's answer: `book_levels` is a derived public projection maintained *inside* the matching and cancel transactions, tagged with the book sequence number, exposing quantity only; the public price is updated by cancels as well as fills; own-order markers come from a separate private read overlaid client-side. Refusals reveal only public or own facts: `WOULD_CROSS` (the book is public), `CAP` (outstanding is public), `SELF_TRADE` (your own order).

**Trap 3 — Reserves and limits bind potential, not actual (money, concurrency).**
Wrong version: lock `price × qty` at placement, release on cancel or full fill; check `position.qty + qty ≤ limit`. Why wrong, four ways. (a) Price improvement: a bid at 60 fills at 55; the 5¢/share difference stays locked unless released per fill, and `locked` drifts upward until users with no open orders cannot withdraw. (b) Fees: the taker fee is proportional to p(1−p), so an order at 90 that fills at 50 owes a *larger* fee than quoted at its limit; reserving the fee at the limit price lets the account overdraw. The reserve must use the worst price reachable by the order — 50 if inside its range, else the limit. (c) Limits over actual position: two orders each at 60% of the limit both pass because neither has filled; both fill; the user holds 120% with no refusal on record. (d) Cash-funded asks: "buy NO at 40" is an ask on YES at 60 funded by cash; naive code locks nothing for "sells". Observation: `locked ≠ Σ reserve_remaining`; zero open orders with nonzero locked cash; a negative balance after a fee; a position above the limit. Design's answer: reserve = qty × (cost at limit + fee at the worst reachable price); every fill debits exactly its cost and fee from the reserve and releases the rest in the same commit; limits use actual + Σ remaining of cash-funded orders that would add that token, kept as a counter.

**Trap 4 — Resolution is a window and settlement is a bulk exactly-once operation (temporal, money, authority).**
Wrong version: pay holders when the oracle reports; iterate positions paying each in its own commit with no run marker; compute payout from a `positions` table that trading or admin tools can still change; show unrealized profit at 100¢ during the dispute window. Why wrong: an overturned proposal after payout is unrecoverable (users have withdrawn); a crash after paying 30,000 of 45,000 positions, then a restart, pays the first 30,000 again; a UI that says "you won $5,000" for six hours and then $0 is a broken promise. Observation: `Σ payout ≠ escrow` at the end of a run; two `payout` entries for one position; a withdrawal timestamped between a proposal and its overturning; a mark of 100 on a market whose state is `proposed`. Design's answer: the final resolution is a *derived temporal fact* — the proposal whose deadline passed undisputed, or the round's tally — recorded as one fenced event; settlement runs in chunks under system authority with a per-position marker, a run cursor, a running total, and a closing conservation assertion that halts rather than adjusts; during `proposed`/`disputed` the mark is shown as "proposed" and unrealized profit as a range.

**Trap 5 — Authority is relational and temporal, not a role (authority, others' data).**
Wrong version: `accounts.role ∈ {user, admin, committee}` and checks like `if role == committee`. Why wrong: a market's creator sits on the committee and votes on it; a reviewer approves their own submission; a member holding 20,000 YES votes YES; a proposer disputes their own proposal to extend the window; a bot's API key withdraws; the scheduler runs on two hosts and closes — or pays out — twice. Observation: a `finalized` event whose actor is also `creator_id`; a weight-1 vote by an account with a nonzero position at cast time; a withdrawal by an api_key without `withdraw` scope; two `closed` events for one market. Design's answer: authority is computed per operation from relationships (creator-of, holder-in, proposer-of, disputant-of), from time (inside the window; before close), from key scopes, and for scheduled work from a system identity plus a uniqueness fence on the event it produces.

**Trap 6 — Two devices and an offline queue (merge).**
Wrong version: the client keeps an order list with client-assigned states, queues "place" actions offline, replays them at reconnect, and resolves device disagreements last-writer-wins. Why wrong: a replayed order meets a book two hours older than the one the user saw and fills at a price they never looked at; a replace from the phone and a size edit from the laptop both "succeed" locally and one silently overwrites the other. Observation: a fill whose `at` is hours after the client's last book snapshot; two devices showing different states for one order id. Design's answer: orders are never optimistic — a device shows "sending" until the server returns a sequence number; offline placement is refused locally; only cancels queue (idempotent, always safe); replace carries the order's expected version and loses loudly; every device subscribes to the account's order and position stream.

### 4. Data model

Money is integer cents; prices are integer cents on YES (1–99); quantities are integer shares; times are UTC instants.

**accounts** — `account_id` PK; `handle` unique; `roles` ⊆ {trader, creator, reviewer, committee, oracle_operator, admin}; `trading_enabled` (derived: verified ∧ not suspended); `public_profile` bool; `suspended_at?`; `created_at`.
**api_keys** — `key_id` PK; `account_id` FK; `scopes` ⊆ {read, trade, cancel, withdraw}; `created_at`; `revoked_at?` (time-bounded validity).

**balances** — `account_id` PK; `cash_total`; `cash_locked` (derived: Σ `reserve_remaining` over the account's open cash-funded orders; checked, never trusted); `version`. `available = cash_total − cash_locked` is never stored. Escrow accounts `pool:<outcome_id>`, `house:fees`, `house:bonds`, and `external:bank` are rows in this table.
**ledger_entries** (append-only) — `entry_id` PK monotonic; `account_id` (any of the above); `kind` ∈ {deposit, withdrawal, withdrawal_reversal, fill_cash, fill_fee, payout, bond_take, bond_return, bond_forfeit, dispute_reward}; `amount` signed; `ref_type`, `ref_id` (fill / settlement_run / dispute / withdrawal); `at`. Entries sharing one `ref_id` sum to zero; deposits and withdrawals pair with `external:bank`.

**fee_schedules** (versioned, time-bounded) — `schedule_id` PK; `taker_bps`; `effective_from`; `effective_to?`. Fee per fill = ceil(qty × x × (100 − x) × taker_bps / 1,000,000) cents at execution price x. Makers pay nothing.

**markets** — `market_id` PK; `slug`; `title`; `rules_text` (frozen at approval); `kind` ∈ {binary, exclusive_multi}; `creator_id` FK; `state` ∈ {draft, in_review, rejected, scheduled, open, halted, closed, proposed, disputed, resolved, voided, settling, settled, settlement_halted} (cached from `market_events`, same transaction); `open_at`; `close_at`; `expected_resolution_at`; `resolver_kind` ∈ {oracle, committee}; `oracle_feed_id?`; `fee_schedule_id`; `max_position_per_user` (shares per outcome per token); `max_open_interest` (shares per outcome); `max_order_qty`; `resolved_outcome_id?` (null with `voided`); `resolved_at?`; `settlement_run_id?`; `version`.
**market_events** (append-only) — `event_id` PK; `market_id`; `kind` ∈ {created, submitted, approved, rejected, opened, halted, resumed, closed, oracle_failed, proposed, disputed, vote_cast, finalized, settlement_started, settled, settlement_halted}; `actor` (account_id, `system:scheduler`, or `system:oracle`); `payload`; `at`. Unique on (`market_id`, `kind`) for {opened, closed, proposed, finalized, settlement_started, settled}: the scheduler fence.

**outcomes** — `outcome_id` PK; `market_id` FK; `label`; `sort_order`; `outstanding` (derived: pairs minted − pairs merged; equals `pool:<outcome_id>` balance ÷ 100); `holder_count_yes`, `holder_count_no` (derived, public); `best_bid?`, `best_ask?` (derived, cached); `last_price?`, `last_trade_at?`; `next_seq`; `value_yes?`, `value_no?` ∈ {0, 50, 100}, set at finalization.

**orders** (private to owner) — `order_id` PK; `client_order_id` unique per `account_id`; `account_id`; `outcome_id`; `market_id`; `side` ∈ {bid, ask} on YES; `funding` ∈ {cash, shares}; `display` (derived: BUY/SELL × YES/NO); `price`; `qty`; `remaining`; `reserve_per_share` (cash bid: price; cash ask: 100 − price; shares: 0); `fee_reserve_per_share` (cash-funded only); `reserve_remaining`; `tif` ∈ {GTC, GTD, IOC, FOK}; `expires_at?`; `post_only`; `state` ∈ {open, partially_filled, filled, canceled, expired, rejected}; `close_reason?` ∈ {user, self_trade, market_closed, expired, ioc, fok, cap, suspended}; `seq` (per-outcome acceptance order; priority key = (price, seq)); `created_at`; `closed_at?`; `version`. Canonical mapping: BUY YES p → bid/cash @p; SELL YES p → ask/shares @p; BUY NO q → ask/cash @(100 − q); SELL NO q → bid/shares @(100 − q).
**pending_adds** — (`account_id`, `outcome_id`, `token`) PK; `qty` (derived: Σ `remaining` of open cash-funded orders that would add `token`; bid/cash adds YES, ask/cash adds NO).

**book_levels** (public projection) — (`outcome_id`, `side`, `price`) PK; `qty` (derived: Σ `remaining` of open orders at that key); `order_count` (internal, never served); `as_of_seq`. Row deleted when `qty` = 0.

**fills** (append-only; private to the two parties) — `fill_id` PK; `outcome_id`; `market_id`; `taker_order_id`; `maker_order_id`; `taker_account_id`; `maker_account_id`; `price` (= maker's price); `qty`; `kind` ∈ {yes_transfer, no_transfer, mint, merge}; `aggressor`; `taker_fee`; `fee_schedule_id`; `seq` (taker's); `fill_index`; `at`.
**public_trades** (derived projection of `fills`, same transaction) — `trade_id` (= `fill_id`); `outcome_id`; `price`; `qty`; `aggressor`; `at`; `taker_handle?` (only if the taker had opted in at fill time). No account ids; the maker never appears.

**positions** — (`account_id`, `outcome_id`, `token` ∈ {YES, NO}) PK; `qty` ≥ 0; `locked_qty` (derived: Σ `remaining` of open share-funded orders on this token); `cost_basis` (cents; average cost = `cost_basis` ÷ `qty`, derived); `realized_pnl` (cumulative); `settled_in_run?`; `payout?`; `version`. Cost rule on any decrease of `qty` by s at proceeds P: `removed = floor(cost_basis × s ÷ qty)`, except s = qty gives `removed = cost_basis`; `realized_pnl += P − removed`; `cost_basis −= removed`.

**candles** (derived, periodic) — (`outcome_id`, `interval`, `bucket_start`) PK; open/high/low/close; `volume`; `watermark_fill_id`. **leaderboard_days** (derived, nightly) — (`day`, `account_id`) PK; `realized_30d`; `rank`; opted-in accounts only.

**oracle_feeds** — `feed_id` PK; `name`; `endpoint`; `extract_rule`; `operator_id`.
**oracle_observations** (append-only) — `observation_id` PK; `feed_id`; `market_id`; `fetched_at`; `status` ∈ {ok, unavailable, unparseable}; `payload_hash`; `extracted_outcome_id?` (null = VOID when `status = ok`).
**resolution_proposals** (append-only) — `proposal_id` PK; `market_id`; `proposed_outcome_id?` (null = VOID); `proposed_by` (`system:oracle` or account); `observation_id?`; `proposed_at`; `dispute_deadline_at` (end of the dispute window); `state` ∈ {pending, disputed, upheld, overturned}.
**disputes** — `dispute_id` PK; `proposal_id`; `market_id`; `disputant_id`; `bond`; `claimed_outcome_id?`; `reason`; `filed_at`; `state` ∈ {open, prevailed, rejected}.
**committee_rounds** — `round_id` PK; `market_id`; `proposal_id`; `opened_at`; `deadline_at`; `state` ∈ {open, closed}.
**committee_votes** — (`round_id`, `voter_id`) PK; `voted_outcome_id?`; `weight` ∈ {0, 1} (0 = recused, computed at cast); `recusal_reason?`; `cast_at`. Readable by anyone other than the voter only when `round.state = closed`.

**settlement_runs** — `run_id` PK; `market_id`; `started_at`; `finished_at?`; `state` ∈ {running, completed, failed_conservation}; `expected_total` (Σ escrow balances at start); `paid_total` (running); `cursor` (last key processed).
**withdrawals** — `withdrawal_id` PK; `account_id`; `amount`; `state` ∈ {requested, sent, failed}; `external_ref?`; `requested_at`.

Every field marked derived above is a cached projection, never authoritative. Time-bounded values: api_key validity, fee_schedules, a market's trading window [`open_at`, `close_at`), a proposal's dispute window, a round's voting window, a GTD order's `expires_at`.

### 5. The hard operations

Notation: "sequenced on book(O)" means the step list executes as one atomic unit, in the outcome's acceptance order, receiving `seq = outcomes.next_seq++`; one unit at a time per outcome, outcomes concurrent. "Fenced by event K" means the unit inserts `market_events(market_id, K)`, whose uniqueness makes a duplicate run a no-op.

**Op 1 — place_order.**
Inputs: `account_id`, `outcome_id`, `display` (BUY/SELL × YES/NO), `price_display`, `qty`, `tif`, `expires_at?`, `post_only`, `client_order_id`.
Authorization: the account's session, or its api_key with scope `trade` and no `revoked_at`; `trading_enabled`.
Steps:
1. Canonicalize to (`side`, `funding`, `price`) per §4. Reject if price ∉ [1, 99] or qty ∉ [1, `max_order_qty`].
2. For cash funding: `fee_reserve_per_share = fee(1, w)` with w = clamp(50, lo, hi), range [1, price] for bids and [price, 99] for asks; `need = qty × (reserve_per_share + fee_reserve_per_share)`. For share funding `need = 0`.
3. Sequenced on book(O):
   a. If an order with (`account_id`, `client_order_id`) exists, return it unchanged (idempotent).
   b. Re-read `markets.state`; require `open` and now < `close_at`; else reject `MARKET_NOT_OPEN`.
   c. `adds` = YES if bid/cash, NO if ask/cash, else none. If adds: require `positions[account, O, adds].qty + pending_adds[account, O, adds].qty + qty ≤ max_position_per_user`, else reject `POSITION_LIMIT`.
   d. Cash: require `available ≥ need`; `cash_locked += need`. Shares (token = YES for ask/shares, NO for bid/shares): require `positions[account, O, token].qty − locked_qty ≥ qty`; `locked_qty += qty`.
   e. If `post_only` and the best opposite level crosses `price`: undo (d), reject `WOULD_CROSS`.
   f. Insert the order (`state = open`, `remaining = qty`, `reserve_remaining = need`, `seq`). If adds: `pending_adds += qty`.
   g. Sweep — while `remaining > 0`: let M = the open opposite-side order with the best price that crosses `price` (bid taker: `M.price ≤ price`; ask taker: `M.price ≥ price`), ties by lowest `seq`; none → break. If M is the taker's own: cancel M (`close_reason = self_trade`, releasing as in Op 2) and continue. `q = min(remaining, M.remaining)`, `x = M.price`. `kind` by (taker, maker) funding: bid/cash vs ask/shares or ask/shares vs bid/cash → `yes_transfer`; ask/cash vs bid/shares or the reverse → `no_transfer`; cash vs cash → `mint`; shares vs shares → `merge`. If `kind = mint`: `q = min(q, max_open_interest − outstanding)`; if `q = 0`, set `stop = CAP` and break. Apply the fill effect (below) for q at x with taker fee `f = fee(q, x)` under the market's `fee_schedule_id`. `remaining −= q`; `M.remaining −= q` (`filled` at 0, else `partially_filled`); M's `reserve_remaining`, `pending_adds`, or `locked_qty` fall accordingly; `book_levels[opposite, x].qty −= q`; append `fills` (with `fill_index`) and `public_trades`; update `last_price`, `last_trade_at`.
   h. Remainder: if `remaining = 0` → `filled`. Else if `tif = FOK` → roll the unit back, leaving only a `rejected` order row (`close_reason = fok`) and the consumed `seq`. Else if `tif = IOC` or `stop = CAP` → cancel the remainder (`close_reason` ioc/cap; release `reserve_remaining`; unlock shares; `pending_adds −= remaining`). Else rest: `book_levels[side, price].qty += remaining`.
   i. Recompute `best_bid`/`best_ask` from the touched levels; write `as_of_seq`.
Fill effect for q shares at x (YES-buyer B, NO-buyer N, YES-seller S, NO-seller R; the taker is one of these):
- yes_transfer: B `cash −q·x`, `YES +q`, `cost_basis += q·x`; S `YES −q`, `cash +q·x`, cost rule with P = q·x.
- no_transfer: N `cash −q·(100 − x)`, `NO +q`, `cost_basis += q·(100 − x)`; R `NO −q`, `cash +q·(100 − x)`, cost rule.
- mint: B `cash −q·x`, N `cash −q·(100 − x)`, `pool:O +100·q`, B `YES +q`, N `NO +q`, `outstanding += q`.
- merge: S `YES −q`, R `NO −q`, `pool:O −100·q`, S `cash +q·x`, R `cash +q·(100 − x)`, `outstanding −= q`, cost rule for both.
The taker also pays `f` to `house:fees`, from its reserve if it pays cash, else out of proceeds. A cash-paying party's `reserve_remaining` and `cash_locked` fall by `reserve_per_share × q` (plus `fee_reserve_per_share × q` for the taker); the excess over cash actually paid — price improvement and unused fee reserve — becomes available in the same commit. Each effect writes zero-sum ledger entries under `ref_id = fill_id` and bumps `holder_count_*` on 0↔positive transitions.
Atomic: all of step 3. Eventually consistent: candles, leaderboard, notifications, the *delivery* of depth (not its content). Optimistic UI: a "sending" row; no change to depth; balance shows "available (−$need pending)". Conflict: none visible — every competing placement, cancel, expiry, or close on the same book runs before or after this unit in `seq` order.

**Op 2 — cancel_order / replace_order.**
Inputs: `order_id` or `client_order_id`; replace adds new `price`, `qty`, and `expected_version`.
Authorization: owner session, or api_key with scope `cancel` (replace needs `trade`); admin suspension cancels all of an account's orders (`close_reason = suspended`), one unit per book.
Steps (sequenced on book(O)): 1. Read the order; if `state ∉ {open, partially_filled}` return its current state unchanged (idempotent: "already filled 700/1000"). 2. Replace: require `version = expected_version`, else reject `ORDER_CHANGED`. 3. `state = canceled`, `close_reason`, `closed_at`; release `reserve_remaining` (`cash_locked −=`) or unlock shares; `book_levels[side, price].qty −= remaining`; `pending_adds −= remaining`; recompute best bid/ask and `as_of_seq`. 4. Replace continues as Op 1 steps 3b–3i with a new `seq`, so it loses time priority.
Atomic: 1–4. Optimistic UI: a "cancel requested" badge until the ack, which may read "filled before cancel". Conflict: cancel vs fill is decided by `seq`; the cancel response reports which won.

**Op 3 — read_book (public depth) and subscribe.**
Inputs: `outcome_id`, `depth` (≤ 50 levels per side). Authorization: anyone, account or not.
Steps: 1. From one committed state, read the top N `book_levels` per side, `outcomes.best_bid/best_ask/last_price/outstanding/holder_count_*`, and the book's `seq`; return `{seq, bids[(price, qty)], asks[(price, qty)], last, outstanding, holders}`. 2. Subscription: after a snapshot at `seq = s`, deliver level deltas `{from_seq, to_seq, changes[(side, price, qty)]}`; deltas may be coalesced for up to 100 ms, but every delivered state equals a committed state; on a gap the client refetches. 3. The viewer's own open orders come from a separate private read; the client overlays "yours: n" on levels.
Atomic: the snapshot. Eventually consistent: delivery latency only. Optimistic UI: none. Conflict: none.

**Op 4 — read_portfolio.**
Inputs: `account_id`. Authorization: owner session or api_key with `read`.
Steps: 1. Strong read of `balances`, nonzero `positions`, and open `orders`. 2. Per position, the mark: `state ∈ {resolved, voided, settling, settled}` → `value_token`; `proposed`/`disputed` → a "proposed" mark with unrealized shown as the range [−cost_basis, qty × 100 − cost_basis]; otherwise the midpoint of `best_bid`/`best_ask` if both exist and the spread ≤ 10, else `last_price`, else 50. 3. `unrealized = qty × mark − cost_basis`; `available = cash_total − cash_locked`.
Atomic: step 1 (own rows). Eventually consistent: marks may lag ≤ 1 s. Conflict: none.

**Op 5 — create_market, submit, review, halt/resume.**
Inputs: create — `title`, `rules_text`, `kind`, `outcomes[]` (one for binary; 2–20 for exclusive_multi), `resolver_kind`, `oracle_feed_id?`, `close_at`, `expected_resolution_at`, limits; review — `decision` ∈ {approve, reject}, `open_at?`, `note`.
Authorization: create and submit need role `creator`; review and halt/resume need role `reviewer` and `actor ≠ creator_id`.
Steps (each atomic): 1. Create: insert market (`state = draft`), outcomes, event `created`. 2. Submit: require `state = draft` and actor = creator; `state = in_review`; event `submitted`. 3. Review: require `state = in_review`. Approve: freeze `rules_text`, set `open_at` (default now), `state = scheduled` — or `open` with event `opened` if `open_at ≤ now`; event `approved`. Reject: `state = rejected`, event with note. 4. Halt/resume (`state ∈ {open, halted}`): flip state with event; a halted book accepts no placements or fills; resting orders stay.
Optimistic UI: none. Conflict: a second submitter or reviewer is refused with the current state.

**Op 6 — open_market, close_market, expire_orders (scheduled).**
Inputs: none; the scheduler ticks every second. Authorization: `system:scheduler`; no user path.
Steps: 1. Open: markets with `state = scheduled` and `open_at ≤ now`: atomic, fenced by event `opened`: `state = open`. 2. Close: markets with `state ∈ {open, halted}` and `close_at ≤ now`: atomic, fenced by event `closed`: `state = closed`. 3. Then per outcome, sequenced on book(O): cancel every open order (`close_reason = market_closed`, releasing as in Op 2) and clear its levels and best bid/ask. 4. Expiry: per outcome, sequenced on book(O): cancel orders with `expires_at ≤ now` (`close_reason = expired`).
Atomic: 2 alone; each of 3 and 4 per book. Between 2 and 3 no fill can happen, because Op 1 step 3b re-reads `state` inside the book's unit. Eventually consistent: notifications. Conflict: a second scheduler host hits the `closed` uniqueness and stops; a placement queued before the flip is refused at 3b.

**Op 7 — fetch_oracle_and_propose (external + scheduled).**
Inputs: none; runs at `expected_resolution_at`, retrying every 15 minutes for 48 hours. Authorization: `system:oracle`.
Steps: 1. For each `closed` market with `resolver_kind = oracle` and no proposal: call the feed endpoint (10 s timeout) outside any transaction and append `oracle_observations` regardless of result. 2. If `status = ok`: atomic, fenced by event `proposed`: require `state = closed`; insert the proposal (`proposed_by = system:oracle`, `observation_id`, `dispute_deadline_at = now + 6 h`); `state = proposed`. 3. After 48 hours without an ok observation: event `oracle_failed`; a committee member not recused under Op 9's rule may call `propose_resolution`, which performs step 2 with `proposed_by = member`. Committee-resolved markets start here.
Atomic: 2. Eventually consistent: the observation log. Conflict: two runs → the second hits the fence; a later observation contradicting a pending proposal changes nothing by itself — it is evidence for Op 8.

**Op 8 — file_dispute.**
Inputs: `proposal_id`, `claimed_outcome_id?` (null = VOID), `reason`.
Authorization: `trading_enabled` account; `actor ≠ proposed_by`, `actor ≠ creator_id`; `available ≥ 10,000` cents.
Steps (atomic): 1. Require `proposal.state = pending` and now < `dispute_deadline_at`, or `proposal.state = disputed` and now < the open round's `deadline_at` (joining). 2. Ledger `bond_take`: account −10,000, `house:bonds` +10,000; `cash_total −= 10,000`. 3. Insert the dispute (`state = open`). If the proposal was `pending`: `proposal.state = disputed`, `state = disputed`, insert `committee_rounds` (`deadline_at = now + 48 h`), event `disputed`.
Optimistic UI: "submitting"; the bond shown as pending. Conflict: two first disputes race → one opens the round, the other joins; a dispute racing Op 10's finalization is ordered by the market row's version and refused `WINDOW_CLOSED` if it loses.

**Op 9 — cast_vote.**
Inputs: `round_id`, `voted_outcome_id?`. Authorization: role `committee`; `actor ∉ {creator_id, proposed_by, any disputant_id}`.
Steps (atomic): 1. Require `round.state = open` and now < `deadline_at`. 2. `weight` = 0 with `recusal_reason` if the actor has any `positions.qty > 0`, any `fills` as either party, or any `orders` ever in the market; else 1. 3. Upsert the vote (`cast_at = now`). 4. If one outcome has weight-1 votes from 3 of 5 members, the result cannot change: run Op 10 immediately.
Visibility: the vote row is readable only by its voter until `round.state = closed`. Optimistic UI: "vote pending". Conflict: re-casting overwrites the actor's own vote until the deadline.

**Op 10 — finalize_resolution (scheduled).**
Inputs: none; runs at each `dispute_deadline_at` and each `round.deadline_at`, or on Op 9 step 4. Authorization: `system:scheduler`.
Steps (atomic, fenced by event `finalized`): 1. Require `state ∈ {proposed, disputed}`. 2. `proposed` and now ≥ `dispute_deadline_at`: `final = proposed_outcome_id`; `proposal.state = upheld`. 3. `disputed` and (now ≥ `round.deadline_at` or the early-close condition): tally weight-1 votes; strict majority → `final`; a tie or fewer than 3 weight-1 votes → `final = VOID`; `round.state = closed` (votes become public); `proposal.state` = upheld or overturned. Each dispute: `prevailed` if `final ≠ proposed_outcome_id` — ledger `bond_return` (`house:bonds` → account 10,000) and `dispute_reward` (`house:fees` → account 5,000) — else `rejected` (`bond_forfeit`: `house:bonds` → `house:fees` 10,000). 4. Set `resolved_outcome_id` (null for VOID), `resolved_at`, per outcome `value_yes`/`value_no` (winner 100/0, every other outcome 0/100, VOID 50/50 on all), `state = resolved` (or `voided`). 5. Enqueue Op 11.
Eventually consistent: notifications, the public reveal of votes. Conflict: a `file_dispute` racing the deadline is serialized by the market row's version; two scheduler hosts hit the fence.

**Op 11 — settle_market (bulk).**
Inputs: `market_id`. Authorization: `system:scheduler`, or an admin re-trigger of a `running` run after a crash.
Steps: 1. Atomic, fenced by event `settlement_started`: require `state ∈ {resolved, voided}`; insert `settlement_runs` (`expected_total = Σ pool:<outcome> balances`, `paid_total = 0`, `cursor = ∅`); `state = settling`. 2. Loop: read up to 500 `positions` of the market with key > `cursor`, `qty > 0`, `settled_in_run = null`, ordered by (outcome_id, account_id, token). For the chunk, atomic: per row `payout = qty × value_token`; ledger `payout` entries (`pool:<outcome>` −payout, account +payout, `ref_id = run_id`); `cash_total += payout`; `realized_pnl += payout − cost_basis`; `qty = 0`, `cost_basis = 0`, `settled_in_run = run_id`, `payout`; `run.paid_total += Σ payout`; `run.cursor = last key`. Repeat until a chunk is empty. 3. Atomic: require every `pool:<outcome>` balance = 0 and `paid_total = expected_total`; if so `state = settled`, `run.state = completed`, event `settled`; else `state = settlement_halted`, `run.state = failed_conservation`, page a human — nothing is reversed and no further chunk runs.
Atomic: each chunk, and the opening and closing steps. Eventually consistent: badges, notifications, leaderboard. Optimistic UI: none — a position shows "payout pending" until its chunk commits, then the cash is withdrawable at once. Conflict: a restarted run skips rows already marked; two workers on one run are excluded by `run.state` plus the per-row marker; trading cannot interfere because the market has been `closed` since Op 6.

**Op 12 — withdraw (external payments).**
Inputs: `amount`, destination. Authorization: owner session with a second factor, or api_key with scope `withdraw`.
Steps: 1. Atomic on the balance row (version check): require `cash_total − cash_locked ≥ amount`; ledger `withdrawal` (account −amount, `external:bank` +amount); `cash_total −= amount`; insert withdrawal (`requested`). 2. Outside any transaction: submit to the processor with idempotency key `withdrawal_id`. 3. On callback, atomic: `sent`, or `failed` with a `withdrawal_reversal` entry restoring `cash_total`.
Conflict: a placement (Op 1 step 3d) and a withdrawal contend on the balance row; one commits, the other re-reads and succeeds or is refused `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`. Optimistic UI: a "pending" row; available shown reduced.

**Op 13 — periodic: candles, leaderboard, reconcile.**
Authorization: `system:scheduler`.
Steps: (a) Every minute: fold fills after each outcome's `watermark_fill_id` into candles and advance the watermark in the same transaction. (b) Nightly: for opted-in accounts, rank `realized_30d` into `leaderboard_days` — coarse by design so rank moves cannot be aligned with individual anonymous trades. (c) Hourly, per outcome, on one committed snapshot: assert `pool:<O> = 100 × outstanding = 100 × Σ positions.qty(YES) = 100 × Σ positions.qty(NO)`; per level `book_levels.qty = Σ remaining`; for a 1% sample of accounts `cash_locked = Σ reserve_remaining` and `cash_total = Σ ledger`. Any violation → `state = halted` with reason (or `settlement_halted` if `settling`) and a page; the reconciler never writes a correcting entry.
Eventually consistent: all outputs. Conflict: none; each assertion runs against one committed state.

### 6. Scale and cost

Successful deployment, year two: 300,000 accounts; 25,000 monthly active traders; 6,000 connected at a peak (a debate night). 4,000 open markets, about 9,000 books; 60 markets settle per day. Orders: 12 M per month (5/s average, 300/s peak exchange-wide, 1,500/s on one hot book for minutes at a time), 40% IOC/FOK from API users. Fills: 6 M per month; fills per taker unit: median 1, p95 6, p99 40, max about 800 (a $200 k sweep through a 25,000-order book with 150 populated levels per side). The hot market: 45,000 holders, 1.5 M lifetime orders, $15 M escrow, 40,000 depth subscribers. Rows: `positions` 6 M; `orders` 150 M/year; `fills` 70 M/year; `ledger_entries` 400 M/year. Settlement of the hot market: 90,000 position rows in 180 chunks, about three minutes. Withdrawals: 20,000/day.

What becomes expensive:
- **The sweep under contention.** One 800-maker unit on the hot book holds it for tens of milliseconds while 1,500 placements/s queue behind it. Per-book serialization is the design, so the cost is latency, not wrongness — provided the unit holds no *other* book and updates the 800 makers' balance rows in a fixed key order so it cannot deadlock with their withdrawals.
- **Depth fan-out.** 1,500 fills/s × 40,000 subscribers is 60 M level messages per second if delivered per fill; coalescing to 10 snapshots per subscriber per second caps it at 400,000/s — why Op 3 promises "every delivered state was a committed state", not "every committed state is delivered".
- **Potential-position and reserve checks** on every placement must be O(1) reads (`pending_adds`, `cash_locked`), never a sum over the account's open orders; a market maker has 3,000 open.
- **Settlement**: 90,000 rows and 180,000 ledger entries under a running conservation total; chunking is mandatory.
- **Reconciliation** (Op 13c) over 6 M positions is a full scan per outcome per hour; the Σ positions side is the expensive one, hence the sampled schedule.
- **Candles** from 6 M fills a month are cheap incrementally and ruinous if recomputed.

### 7. Where a backend would have to be unusual

1. **One atomic program over an unbounded, predicate-selected set of rows, committed in acceptance order per key.** Op 1 reads "all crossing opposite orders, best first", loops with early exit and a cap check, and writes 2N+3 rows across five tables plus escrow — and competing units must commit in the order they were accepted, or price-time priority is false and large sweeps starve. Ordinary transactional backends give atomicity; almost none give per-key acceptance-order commit without a client-visible queue. Concession: a per-book single-writer sequencer in front of the database, with the database transaction as the durability boundary. Cheat boundary: if "sequencing" is client-side retry with backoff, or if fills commit out of acceptance order and are merely *numbered* in acceptance order afterward, the priority promise is a lie and Trap 1's observation catches it.

2. **A public aggregate maintained transactionally over rows the reader cannot read, delivered as a sequence of committed states.** `book_levels` and best bid/ask are sums over private `orders`, must change in the same commit as the fill or cancel that changes them, and must be servable to a spectator with no rights to any input row. Concession: coalesced delivery, and an explicit denormalized public table written by the matcher — both fine. Cheat boundary: recomputing levels from viewer-visible rows (Trap 2, version A), updating the public table from a queue after commit (version C), or exposing `orders` with owners "because the UI needs it".

3. **Bulk, exactly-once, resumable settlement under system authority with an end-of-run conservation proof.** Op 11 is 90,000 per-row arithmetic writes and ledger entries that must survive a crash without double-paying, must never see a position row that trading can still change, and must end with an assertion that can *fail* — and on failure must stop, not insert an adjusting entry. Ordinary backends have jobs, not a system actor, a fence, a cursor, a per-row marker, and halt-on-invariant as one construct. Concession: an external job runner doing the chunks, provided each chunk is a transaction and the marker is in the row. Cheat boundary: a job that pays from a query it re-runs each chunk without the marker, or that "reconciles" a shortfall by moving money from the house account.

4. **Cross-row conservation invariants that bind every write path, including admin tools.** Invariants 2, 3, 4, and 8 are sums across rows and tables. Row constraints and unique indexes do not express them; if they live only in the matcher's code, a support tool that "fixes a position" or a migration that "recalculates locked" silently breaks them. Concession: the hourly reconciler that halts a market on drift — detection, not enforcement. Cheat boundary: a reconciler that corrects rather than halts, or that runs only on markets nobody is watching.

5. **Authority derived from relationships, time, and identity kind rather than a role column.** "May this account vote on this market" depends on whether it created, proposed for, disputed, held, or traded it; "may this dispute be filed" depends on the current instant against a deadline row; "may this scheduled action run" depends on a system identity and the absence of a prior event. Roles-on-tables cannot express recusal, windows, or once-only system authority, only approximate them with checks scattered through handlers. Concession: checks in operation code, *if* every path to the row goes through the operation. Cheat boundary: an admin path or migration that writes `committee_votes`, `market_events`, or `positions` without those checks — item 4's boundary, seen from the authority side.

### 8. Confidence

Standard practice, taken from real exchanges: the central limit order book with price-time priority and per-instrument sequencing; integer-cent prices on [1, 99] and $1 complete sets; the four match kinds (two transfers, mint, merge), which is how Polymarket's exchange crosses YES and NO orders and is equivalent to Kalshi's fully collateralized model; taker-only fees proportional to p(1−p); position limits that count resting orders; no optimistic order placement and no offline order queuing (no real exchange allows either); sequence-numbered depth snapshots with gap-triggered resync; a double-entry ledger with escrow and house accounts; proposal-then-dispute-window resolution with bonds (Polymarket via UMA; Kalshi's review has the same shape); void as 50/50; chunked settlement with a run marker; average-cost profit. High confidence.

Derived or chosen for this exercise: the fee reserve at the *worst reachable* price (clamped to 50) — it follows from the p(1−p) fee and is the only reserve that cannot overdraw; "a cap-blocked mint stops the sweep and cancels the remainder"; self-trade handling as "cancel the resting order"; recusal recorded as a weight-zero vote rather than a hidden ballot; the 6-hour window, 48-hour round, five-member committee, $100 bond, $50 reward; publishing depth quantity but not order count; the "proposed" mark and profit range during the window; the nightly leaderboard as a de-anonymization control. Medium confidence.

Deliberately out of scope: bundle mint/merge across all outcomes of an exclusive_multi market (real and useful, but a cross-book atomic unit that would only restate §7 item 1); market orders; automated-market-maker liquidity; margin beyond full collateralization — the archetype named "margin", and the honest answer is that Polymarket- and Kalshi-style markets do not extend it, so this design's "margin" is the potential-position reserve of Trap 3. Low confidence that omitting exclusive-multi bundles hides no second-order trap.

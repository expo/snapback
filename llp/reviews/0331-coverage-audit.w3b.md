# Authorization coverage audit — wave 3, part B (five apps) vs LLP 0331 core

Slice: fast-casual ordering, Notion-style databases, prediction market, real estate, Twitch chat.
Artifacts under `/Users/ccheever/projects/snapback/llp/reviews/`; synthesis `llp/0329.014.003-paper-archetypes-wave-3.research.md`.
Quotes are `file:line`. `D` = the `.design.md`, `V` = the `.v0.md` of each app.

Two reading conventions used throughout, flagged so the merger can normalize:

- **Global roles** (brand ops, support, admin, staff, steward, privacy worker, reviewer, committee). The brief lists them as
  deferred by 0331. They are nonetheless expressible with the core vocabulary and no new operator: an ordinary table
  `{ principal, role }` (unique on `(principal, role)`) probed one hop by `viewer`. 0331 says they are "not required for
  M1–M3", not inexpressible. I mark them **CORE (global-role table)** and count no extension.
- **Time-bounded authority** (a grant, sentence, share, key, or claim with a start/end). The rule side is a plain field
  compare on a `liveKey`/status column; the extension is the `schedule` kind that flips it. The brief puts "a status
  flipped by schedule (time-as-state)" under X6, so I mark X6 and say in the note whether the app's stated bar tolerates
  schedule lag (RFC dimension B) or demands interval containment at commit time inside the rule (the strong form).

**App verdict: NEEDS: X6 (strong), X11, X3** (X19 as the alternative lowering of row 13; X5 is a runtime extension the rule side does not need).

Authorization is genuinely one of the two hard problems — trap 1, "Permission and badges are intervals at a time, not `users.role`" (D:55–58) — and the design's own answer (interval rows, snapshots on messages, role sets as booleans) is 0331's shape almost exactly: it rejects role enums and `banned` booleans (D:56) as 0331 rejects roles in sessions. The residue is **time**, and here the design's bar and the RFC disagree on principle: RFC dimension B makes rules time-independent and flips state by schedule; D:58 and inv. 16 (D:47) forbid a late job from being load-bearing. V:541 names the contradiction. The design reached for nothing RFC §8 refutes on the authorization side; the v0's own delta 2 (V:557) asks for exactly the strong X6 (`existsContaining(index,key,t)` against `commitTime`). No single extension flips it: X6 and X11 are independent.

**Sprawl check**
- **X6 (strong)** — load-bearing at the stated bar (inv. 2/4/16, trap 1's observation). Honest reduction is on record: V:582 "Approximate expiry is explicitly promised" — loses "timeout 10 seconds" exactness and the "same-commit" wording for expiry; revoke/ban start remain exact because they are writes.
- **X11** — load-bearing: accrual/sampling/payout (D:266–303), automod finalization (D:174 step 15, V:318–326), and the head-row allocation every send performs (D:105). No reduction keeps chat.
- **X3 (anon device)** — droppable: signed-in viewers only; loses promise 12's anonymous component and "Anyone may watch" becomes "anyone signed in".
- **X19** — alternative to X11 for row 13, not additive.
- **X5** — a fan-out cost extension (V:576 "Cap hot channels at a few thousand" is its reduction); the authorization rule is CORE(neg)+X6 without it.

**What the slice says about sprawl:** Two extensions carry almost all the weight — **X11** (a system principal with `callable:'system'`, schedules, sealed reads for a named mutation, and a per-mutation `reads:` view) and **X6** (time-bounded authority — the `schedule` kind flipping a `liveKey`, which every app but Twitch accepts).

Review of LLP 3000.001.001, read against the scope fence, the device staging and boundary code, the server snapshot and stream code, the sync card, and LLPs 3000.001, 3000.002.000, 3000.021 and 3000.028.

The problem is real and the direction is right. Progressive per-group publication under server-named floors, with the open conversation first, is the correct response to a 500,000-row declared working set on a new phone. Three fundamental concerns, most serious first.

**1. The central mechanism solves a consistency problem the replica does not have.** Sections 5.1, 6.2 and 6.3 build private candidates at a base cut, revision-qualified immutable row images and tombstones stored on the server for every write, exact-cut replay to a publication cut, a second "common applied cut" beside the watermark, candidate rebase when the live image outruns it, and a reconciliation of the one-watermark fence in NOT-DOING. All of that exists to bring a privately staged candidate forward to the live image. But the published partition has never been a consistent cut. Repair B in LLP 3000.001 records that pages cut events at the watermark and read images at head, that rows a page touched run ahead of rows it did not, and that this converges. The only unsoundness was own-write doubling, closed by the images-at and committed-above deferral. The cold snapshot itself reads every page in its own transaction long after the pinned watermark. Per-group coverage is already a server-named floor selected at serve time, and a join expansion publishes that floor immediately on an ordinary stream page. I verified this in `expand_joins` in partition.rs and in `Boundaries::marker`, which applies markers from non-snapshot pages. Section 6.2 rejects current-image hydration because it does not prove membership in a historical cut. Nothing needs a historical cut. Staging was private to hide scattered rows from reads that would call them complete, and Repair A removed that failure by making a group without a floor unknown.

What I would do instead: acquire groups as join expansions at the device's current position. Publish an empty replica at head immediately, with every group unknown so pages fall back online as they do today. Interleave ordinary stream pages, which keep the one watermark moving, with acquisition pages that say "serve this group, or the next groups on your walk, as of your head". Answer them exactly as a join expansion: current readable rows through the horizon, a completion marker with the floor, images-at and committed-above computed against the device's cursor. Apply each page directly in its own transaction. The device may ignore that group's stream events at or below the acquisition head, as the stream already does for joins. No staging, no replay images, no second cut, and no separate discovery protocol, since the server already enumerates held groups on every page and resumes by group key. Keep the staged whole-replacement path only for re-acquisition, where stale rows above a new floor make staging earn its keep. This also removes the atomic publication of the whole staged set on the web, which peaked at 307 MB on the heavy fixture.

**2. The unit of progress is the wrong one for a messaging app.** Usable offline on a new phone means the inbox and navigation first, then conversation history. The membership and parent tables are small and horizonless. Section 3's contract table never says when the inbox becomes offline-usable. Section 4.2 ranks groups within a table and says nothing about table precedence. Section 5.2 correctly says cross-conversation reads stay server-bound until every domain is covered. Under the RFC's own ordering a phone can hold fifty conversations' recent slices and no offline inbox, which fails the section 1 disconnect scenario. State in the contract and the schedule that horizonless declared tables are acquired whole and first, published per page, and that horizoned tables follow group by group under the priority. The guide should add that an inbox preview must live on the thread row to be local before full acquisition.

**3. Sub-group suffix extension is machinery the declaration already makes unnecessary.** Section 5.1 extends a suffix "toward the declared sync horizon in later turns", section 4.2 deepens toward a requested page, and section 11 rejects whole-conversation publication because a large conversation delays the history the person wants. The declared horizon is the bound. A `last 500` group is at most 500 rows, about one page, and an unbounded group already gets N_NO_HORIZON. The existing marker protocol is exactly "unit equals the group's declared horizon, published at its completion marker". Make that the unit and drop floor extension, the deepening rule and the island rule. If the foreground group's full horizon measurably arrives too late on a slow link, the priority hint already serves that group first. Finer slicing should wait for evidence.

With change 1 the delivery sequence in section 10.3 shrinks to scheduling, direct application of acquisition pages, and the readiness semantics, and the 25 percent regression budget should be unnecessary because the pages are the same pages.

Verdict: DIRECTION RIGHT WITH ONE CHANGE. Acquire and publish groups directly at the live watermark through the existing join-expansion floor protocol, and drop staged candidates, exact-cut replay and the second cut. Confidence 0.8.

## New fundamental and major findings

### N1. Predicate validation must preserve false observations too — MAJOR — Read sets §§3–4; Committer §4.5

The [predicate entry, L83](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000.001-read-sets-and-validation.rfc.md:83) conflicts when the predicate becomes false. That works for an execution which observed it as true. It does not preserve an execution which observed false.

For example, A evaluates `require total < 2` at total 2 and prepares a refusal. B removes a contribution, making the total 1, and commits first. Re-evaluating A’s predicate now yields true, so the specified rule permits its cached refusal—even though serial execution at A’s revision would pass. The reference actually branches at the require and persists refusals: [runner.rs, L612](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:612), [engine/mod.rs, L1035](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:1035).

Record the observed Boolean, or normalize each observation into a condition that must remain true, including negation for false observations. Alternatively, retain value dependencies for these cases. The latter also reconciles the child’s extension to comparison-only maintained reads with the parent’s statement that [explicit total reads remain validated by value, L352](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:352).

### N2. An unread maintained total is not necessarily a commutative operation — MAJOR — Committer §4.5; Overlay §§3–4

The eligibility condition is that the **program** does not read the total. But the engine reads it and uses it to decide overflow, deletion versus replacement, source-existence probes, index charges, partition events and mirrored columns. These decisions are visible in [maintains.rs, L586](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/maintains.rs:586).

Two concrete failures of treating this as just `+delta`:

- A maintained sum starts at `I53_MAX − 1`. Two independent source inserts each add 1. Both pre-executions succeed; the second serial execution must refuse for overflow.
- A sum starts at 1. A deletes its sole `+1` contribution and buffers deletion of the aggregate. B inserts another `+1` contribution and commits first. A’s serial transition must now leave an aggregate valued 1, changing its write kind, charges and event.

Applying the arithmetic to the current value does not repair those already-executed decisions. Partition events contain the selected row image, and append serializes those buffered events: [partition.rs, L324](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:324), [L850](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs:850).

Keeping the engine’s ordinary `Row` dependency makes this safe but retains the shared-total conflict. Removing it requires a defined mechanism that preserves maintenance decisions and metering—such as validated transition conditions with fallback, or an ordered maintenance operation executed at commit. “The program did not read the total” is insufficient. The family qualification gate is appropriate, but the proposed eligibility rule itself needs correction.

### N3. Presence already participates in ordered durable reconnect handling — MAJOR — Committer §4.6

The premise at [L372](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:372)—that the reference does not order presence against commits—is false for effect admission.

Poll entry calls `presence_reconnected` and enters the registry while holding the engine lock: [serve_changes.rs, L48](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve_changes.rs:48). Reconnection durably marks existing `_effect_presence` records under the same lock used for emission and claiming: [effects.rs, L285](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/effects.rs:285).

Consider this interleaving, entirely within an old grace deadline:

1. A pre-executes an absence-guarded effect using that nearly expired deadline.
2. The recipient reconnects and finishes its poll before A commits. Reconnect finds no record for A’s buffered effect.
3. A commits the old deadline without validation.
4. At that deadline, claim finds neither a recorded reconnect nor a live poll and dispatches the effect.

The reference cannot produce that combination: A before the reconnect gets its effect marked satisfied; A after the completed poll observes the new grace deadline. The relevant deadline calculation and claim check are [serve.rs, L285](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:285) and [effects.rs, L263](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/effects.rs:263).

Preserve ordering through a validated presence/reconnect epoch or keep affected members in place. Replaying the old observation faithfully does not restore the lost ordering.

### N4. Reading admission inside S does not detect expiry while queued — MAJOR — Committer §§3.6, 6

[Section 3.6, L253](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:253) explicitly intends to preserve rejection of sessions that expire while waiting. Its mechanism catches revocation writes, but expiry requires no write.

A can authenticate and finish pre-execution just before its session expires, then wait behind an older long member until afterward. If the session row remains unchanged and preceding effective times do not exceed A’s, both `Auth` validation and the [clock-floor rule, L400](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:400) pass. This can happen well inside the two-second snapshot window.

Today, write authentication samples fresh `shared.now()` after acquiring the engine lock, and `live_session` compares expiry with that sample: [serve_identity.rs, L58](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve_identity.rs:58), [authority.rs, L207](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:207).

Retain a freshness check at serial admission into the batch, or validate recorded authority deadlines against a fresh authority-clock sample there. A fixed application effective time plus unchanged database facts cannot enforce this existing admission contract.

### N5. Publication ordering changes bounded advancement into draining the backlog — MAJOR — Committer §6

[Section 6, L411](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:411) requires a due row’s publication before the first member whose effective time reaches its opening. Applied to every due row, this requires **all overdue publications** before an otherwise unrelated member.

That changes the reference’s bounded scheduling. The engine processes at most one opening per invocation: [time_audience.rs, L114](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/time_audience.rs:114). HTTP query settlement prioritizes only an encountered dependency: [serve.rs, L1302](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1302). The governing design explicitly preserves bounded preludes and forbids draining unrelated openings: [LLP 3000.036.003 §10, L592](../../llp/3000.036.003-time-audiences-and-timed-disclosure.rfc.md:592).

After downtime or many simultaneous openings, the proposed rule makes unrelated writes and admission operations wait for work proportional to the overdue population. Adaptive batch sizes cannot remove that prerequisite.

Keep publication in the stream, but preserve bounded background advancement and dependency-directed settlement. If “before” is intended to mean only publications a member actually requires, state that explicitly and retain pending-publication behavior for the rest.

## Small notes

1. **Long-member isolation does not bound queue delay.** A 200 ms singleton still delays subsequent members on the one writer. [Section 7, L429](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:429) overstates what separate batches achieve; measure the SLO including execution, retries and waiting behind such members.

2. **One additional transcript input needs coverage.** Retained-asset deletion samples `crate::now_ms()` directly for `released_at`: [dormant.rs, L770](../../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/store/dormant.rs:770). Capture/inject that sample or deliberately unify it with effective time before qualifying this family. The explicit transcript/family gates make this a qualification item rather than another major finding.

3. **Singletons still need ambiguous-COMMIT handling.** The rerun wording at [Committer §3.4, L195](../../llp/3000.009.001.000.000-the-committer.rfc.md:195) should distinguish confirmed rollback from unknown commit status. An unkeyed singleton must retain the parent’s [ambiguity rules, L369](../../llp/3000.009.001.000-the-postgres-store-port.rfc.md:369); isolation alone does not authorize retry.

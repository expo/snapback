## 2. New problems revision 2 introduced

**Finding 1 — High — §5.1: the rebuilt reservation still isn't stated in meter terms.** The table uses prose units ("one probe", "`N(T) × seeks + N(C)` rows", "one scan", "three seeks", "`unit` plus a fetch", "examined count") without mapping them onto the Price meters that `check` compares against ceilings and that every host must charge identically. Concrete consequences: (a) the snapshot `delete_reservation` maps a probe to `[2·seeks steps, seeks index-entries, 2·seeks examined]` and contains *no* `N(C)` term — code and RFC disagree today, and a second implementer guessing from prose will produce a third variant, so cross-host `E_BOUND` equality (the disclosure claim's load-bearing premise) is unimplementable as written; (b) the witness rows are uncharged — proving a restrict probe unoccupied examines S-skips (amortized ≤ `N(C)` per reference) *plus one witness per probe* (`+N(T)` rows total), which the formula omits; (c) "one scan per reached row" for cascade/set-null discovery into an *expiring* child must be two seeks (two prefixes, exactly as `live_children` does) — as written it undercharges; (d) "each row of S at most once" holds *per reference*, not across references, and meeting the heap maximum requires streaming (the snapshot buffers whole groups and full closure images; step 5/6's "current image" refetch already implies ids-only retention). **Fix:** restate §5.1 meter-by-meter (each term as named meters or Price positions), define "examined count" as the Examined dimension, add the per-probe witness fetch, require two seeks for expiring-child discovery, scope the amortization to per-reference, and add one sentence: scans stream rows and the closure retains identities, refetching current images.

**Finding 2 — Low — §7: `N_DELETE_NEEDS_READ` over-fires when delete implies read.** Scenario: `read <- .owner = viewer or .public` with `delete <- .owner = viewer`. Every admitted deleter can read, so the step-1 gate is redundant, yet the structural test (delete neither *is* the read rule nor contains it as a conjunct) still fires the note. Harmless (review-tier noise). **Fix:** one sentence noting the test is a conservative structural heuristic that can fire where the gate is redundant.

**Finding 3 — Low — §2.2/§4.2: "feeds a maintained sum" doesn't say direct or transitive.** Scenario: child `B` feeds a count whose owner feeds a downstream sum — if maintain chains exist, the overflow refusal (and its disclosure bit) survives a direct-only refusal. **Fix:** define "feeds" as the transitive closure over maintains, or state with a reference that maintains never chain.

**Finding 4 — Low — §6.6 vs §2.2: "mapped tables" understates the rule.** §2.2 refuses any timeline role; §6.6 says "mapped". **Fix:** change §6.6 to "any timeline-member table".

Expected implementation lag (not RFC findings — the snapshot predates revision 2): add the `N(C)`/identities/witness terms to `delete_reservation`, the sum-target check to `write_target_refusal`, the union-cycle check to the binder, derived destinations to the touched envelope, `current_group` to `prove_reference_held`, and the `N_DELETE_NEEDS_READ` note.

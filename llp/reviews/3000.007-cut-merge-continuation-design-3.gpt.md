1. **High — D4/D6: an emitted partial contribution can be incorrectly discharged.**

   **Design:** [D4, lines 145–164](REVIEW-DESIGN.md:145), and D6, lines 230–238.  
   **Code:** [live aggregate computation, `read.rs:193`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:193), partial-row emission at `:612–621`, and cursor selection at `:665–668`.

   **Failing scenario:** Return a direct cursor page over an unranked maintain with one group containing 9,000 live, expiring source rows. The aggregate calculation exhausts the examined meter and returns a partial count. `scan_prepared` deliberately emits that partial aggregate row, then mints a cursor past the group.

   The failure occurs at the owning scan’s meter site. There need be no non-uniformity, horizon, overflow or lost-page flag, and no failed charge at another site. Under D4’s specified tests, the answer remains `cut: 'meter'`. Following its cursor returns an empty, complete page. D6 then reports the accumulated result complete while retaining the incorrect aggregate value.

   A second instance needs **no meter failure**: a direct parent page has `next`, and one parent’s nested matching page exceeds 32 words. The nested page sets `word_cut_at`; following the parent cursor cannot recover its missing children. D2.4’s merge-owner gate does not apply to this direct owner, while D6 discharges any non-null cut. See [word-limit recording, `read.rs:425`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:425) and [nested page depth, `runner.rs:235`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:235).

   **Smallest design change:** Explicitly classify an emitted partial aggregate and an off-owner word cut as contributions the continuation cannot repair. Such answers keep `next` but expose `cut: null`. Add end-to-end `usePage` regressions for both. D6 affects direct pages too, so protecting only merge results is insufficient.

2. **High — D4(b): another read can be starved without another failed charge.**

   **Design:** [D4(b), lines 154–164](REVIEW-DESIGN.md:154).  
   **Code:** [prefix probe charging, `read.rs:73–82`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:73), and the unconditional existing-cap check at [`read.rs:606–608`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:606).

   **Failing scenario:** Twelve uniform lanes each require one prefix authorization probe. Set the probe ceiling so opening succeeds and a refill cuts during draining, with ample examined budget remaining. Shape the emitted rows with a nested scan of a public table.

   That nested scan successfully charges an examination, obtains an allowed candidate, then encounters the **already-set** `capped_at` at line 606 and returns no candidate. It has failed no charge of its own. D4(b)’s new failure flag therefore need not record this missing contribution. The merge retains its valid drain continuation, and D6 can eventually declare completeness with permanently empty nested results.

   **Smallest design change:** Track incomplete read exits as well as failed charges. A read stopped by an inherited cap outside the owning traversal must make the answer undischargeable. Attribution must distinguish lane traversal—including predicates—from result construction, and distinguish repeated executions. Include the new accounting in the appropriate `MeterMark` restoration semantics; a final-owner comparison against only the first site cannot work.

   For the other requested cases: a cut predicate or authorization probe inside a merge refill is recoverable because D2.3 stops and the continuation retries the un-emitted candidate. Heap exhaustion currently clears `page` and returns null ([`runner.rs:170–184`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:170)); output exhaustion actually **refuses**, rather than returning a null page ([`runner.rs:740–754`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:740)). Neither supplies a dischargeable continuation. `nextExpiry` schedules re-evaluation; it does not repair a missing contribution. Successful examined rows already record expiry at `read.rs:611`, including conservatively examined lookahead rows.

3. **Medium — D2.4: outcome-time meter state deletes a previously safe continuation.**

   **Design:** [D2.4’s predicate, lines 103–107](REVIEW-DESIGN.md:103), contradicting D4’s “keeps its `next`” at line 161.  
   **Code:** [current recording, `read.rs:788–790`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:788), lazy recording at `:875–876`, and [outcome construction, `mod.rs:714–725`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:714).

   **Failing scenario:** A uniform merge finishes an ordinary full page, uncut, with `has_more = true`. Its subsequent shape performs a large uniform child scan and exhausts the meter. At outcome construction, `meter uncut` is false and `drain_cut` is false, so neither arm of D2.4 permits `next`.

   The merge’s position was safe; later work did not invalidate it. Today that cursor survives. The proposed formula strands the remaining merge rows, contrary to D4.

   **Smallest design change:** Preserve whether the **owning traversal** established an ordinary safe continuation before later work ran. Use that recorded fact for the ordinary `has_more` arm, while applying whole-answer non-uniformity and word-cut eligibility at outcome construction. Add this regression for eager and lazy merges. Preserve the current last-execution ownership rule and monotone discarded-page loss.

4. **Medium — D5: the proposed sparse-filter fixture can pass through the budget without a drain cut.**

   **Design:** [D5, lines 187–197](REVIEW-DESIGN.md:187).  
   **Code:** [filtered-page early stop, `read.rs:642`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:642), one-head batching at `:840`, and [default ceilings, `meter.rs:23`](snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/meter.rs:23).

   **Failing scenario:** Twelve fixed, distinct lanes use a pure `where` predicate matching every fiftieth row, with enough data and `last 100`. A filtered one-head read stops at its first match; it does **not** examine a second matching row as lookahead.

   Opening costs at most `12 × 50 = 600` examinations. The 100 emitted rows require at most another `100 × 50 = 5,000` for refills. Approximately 5,600 is below 8,192: page 1 is full and uncut with D either enabled or disabled.

   Expiry and further-term rejection take a different path: without `scan.filter`, lookahead seeks another surviving match. That interpretation can produce the intended cut, but the fixture must specify it.

   **Smallest design change:** Specify the actual Quarry predicate, seed ordering and budget arithmetic. For a pure filter, matching every **100** rows makes opening affordable and draining exceed the default ceiling. Require the duplicate-word fixture to discover more than eight lanes, and assert the drain boundary and `next` presence.

   Also add the regressions from findings 1–3; D0.1’s non-unique same-lane tie, nullable suffix and ascending cases; and D6 re-answers with unchanged/changed cursors and both cut-classification transitions. Already-correct non-uniform-before-merge and persistent-`cut: null` assertions should remain labelled controls.

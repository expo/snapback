## 1. Overall assessment

[LLP 0310.000](/private/tmp/llp-0310-r1-capsule/llp/0310.000-successor-identity-and-paved-loop-repair.rfc.md) is directionally strong but not ready. The core successor transition lacks an atomic commit and fresh-verification protocol; Tracks B, D, and E also leave material behavioral or safety decisions unresolved.

No files were modified.

## 2. Strengths

- §Summary, §Non-goals, and §Ruling 1 correctly distinguish a legal same-owner successor from a foreign backend. The RFC preserves `SNAPBACK_APP_BINDING_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` as a fail-closed protection.
- §Motivation and Tracks A–E map the observed failures to bounded owners without expanding into unrelated authoring or storage work.
- §Tracks A and C appropriately reject `pkill`, owner restart, and Metro death as paved recovery mechanisms.
- §Ruling 2 preserves caller-owned URL-first development while continuing to support supervised Expo.
- §Ruling 3 correctly recognizes that generation fencing remains necessary rather than simply removing it.
- §Acceptance appropriately treats a full Clique rerun as evidence rather than the sole acceptance gate.

## 3. Concerns

1. **MATERIAL — Track A has no satisfiable atomic transition or active-client fence.**

   §Track A reacts to `startup-successor-activated`, but step 4 requires failure to stop the successor before it serves the new identity. The RFC also does not coordinate the backend identity, generated graph, receipt, Metro extra, and existing verified streams. Under LLP 0302 §3, a live stream can remain verified until it drops; “before the next client handshake” therefore does not protect an already-connected client.

   **Resolution:** define a prepare/commit/abort transition. Stage the successor and client facts, quiesce application traffic, invalidate existing verification episodes, obtain the required web-arm acknowledgement, and only then commit activation. Failure must retain or restore one coherent old state, or enter a typed stopped state. No post-transition application request may precede fresh identity verification.

2. **MATERIAL — “same-owner successor” lacks a normative eligibility predicate.**

   §Ruling 1 and §Track A permit recooking from an activation event but do not define how the owner proves the event belongs to its current incarnation and expected predecessor. A stale, reordered, or unrelated event must not become authority to rewrite the binding.

   **Resolution:** require a trusted transition lineage including the live owner incarnation, currently committed predecessor identity, candidate successor identity, and transition epoch. Before commit, the candidate backend, generated `app`, and new receipt must agree. Foreign backends, stale supervisors, unmatched predecessors, and out-of-order events must remain fail-closed mismatches.

3. **MATERIAL — Track B conflates binding source, mint creation, and session adoption.**

   B1 injects `EXPO_PUBLIC_SNAPBACK_URL` “in addition” to the supervisor signal, but LLP 0302 §4 says a completed supervised rendezvous wins and ignores `url`. Thus B1 does not establish URL-first binding unless precedence changes. Separately, B1/B2 mix eager versus on-demand minting with automatic versus explicit adoption.

   **Resolution:** decide independently:

   - which binding source/provider supervised web selects;
   - whether mint creation is eager or on demand;
   - whether adoption is automatic or explicit;
   - how sign-out returns safely to anonymous/password UI.

   State explicitly which LLP 0300/0302 rules this RFC amends.

4. **MATERIAL — Track D’s normative language permits the current defect.**

   §Ruling 3 says a heartbeat “MAY reuse” its generation, while §Track D and Acceptance item 5 require one generation. An implementation that continues allocating per-touch generations remains compliant with “MAY.”

   **Resolution:** make reuse a `MUST` for one logical typing episode. State that withdraw, idle, unmount, key change, or session change fences all earlier asynchronous publishes, so a delayed completion cannot resurrect presence.

5. **MATERIAL — Track E creates a privileged control surface without defining its authority boundary.**

   A socket or FIFO capable of reloading Metro and stopping children needs normative owner-instance authorization. §Track E does not require receipt/incarnation binding, private access, or stale-endpoint refusal. Exact framing can remain implementation detail; authority cannot.

   **Resolution:** require commands to bind to the exact live owner incarnation using the existing receipt-verified lifecycle authority, with same-user/private access and stale or foreign endpoint refusal. Add acknowledgement semantics sufficient for agents to know whether a verb completed.

6. **MATERIAL — §Acceptance is not closed over the RFC’s own requirements.**

   It omits Track E entirely, Track A’s abort/foreign/stale paths, Track B’s successful password round-trip and safe sign-out, and Track D’s late-publish safety. Track C checks absence of one diagnostic without requiring that the same owner and Metro remain live and serve updated code.

   **Resolution:** add requirement-to-test traceability covering every track, including fault injection for Track A, authenticated no-TTY control verbs for E, and behavioral/liveness assertions rather than diagnostic absence alone.

7. **minor — Sequencing and metadata need correction.**

   §Sequencing puts C before A even though the observed Metro restart currently masks the stale-receipt defect. If these can publish separately, C should follow or co-land with A. Also, §Revision history does not record the r1 Review revision shown in the metadata.

   **Resolution:** state the co-landing constraint or reorder A/C, and update revision history.

## 4. Suggestions

- Add a compact successor-transition state machine with `prepared`, `committed`, and `aborted` events.
- Include a multi-tab, authenticated, in-flight-request successor test.
- Make source-selection diagnostics part of Track B acceptance.
- Add a track-to-ticket-to-acceptance table, including every guide obligation.

## 5. Open questions

- Is successor activation globally gated on every supervised web arm, or can an unacknowledged arm be disconnected while activation proceeds?
- Under B1, is the URL merely exposed while supervised binding remains selected, or should supervised web select `declared-dev`?
- What existing owner/receipt capability authenticates Track E commands?

## 6. Recommended next step

Revise the RFC around an explicit successor prepare/commit/abort protocol, settle Track B’s three independent choices, strengthen Track D’s modal requirement, and define Track E’s authority invariant and acceptance. Then run another focused readiness review.

### Verbatim body

NOT READY. Applying the `llp-super-refine` punch-list delta rule, two MATERIAL disposition errors remain:

1. Track A’s abort is not atomic. The candidate identity includes the generated `app` and backend, and prepare reloads the successor bundle, but abort permits restoring only predecessor extra/receipt ([Track A abort](/private/tmp/llp-0310-r1-capsule/llp/0310.000-successor-identity-and-paved-loop-repair.rfc.md:160)). That leaves the successor generated identity against predecessor state, contradicting “never serve split-brain” and A2’s “predecessor still served.” Abort must either restore every identity-bearing artifact and re-verify before unfreezing, or always enter the typed-stopped state.

2. Track B does not identify its LLP 0300 amendment. LLP 0300 P4 says supervised Expo issuance remains unchanged ([LLP 0300 P4](/private/tmp/llp-0310-r1-capsule/llp/0300-snapback-is-a-url.rfc.md:268)), while r2 changes mint creation to on-demand ([Ruling 2](/private/tmp/llp-0310-r1-capsule/llp/0310.000-successor-identity-and-paved-loop-repair.rfc.md:120)). The amendment clause mentions only injection and adoption, not LLP 0300 P4 or mint creation. It must explicitly amend P4’s supervised-issuance rule; LLP 0302 §4 can remain unchanged.

The eligibility predicate, Track D/E requirements, acceptance coverage, sequencing, revision history, and Track A success-path live-stream fence are correctly dispositioned.

VERDICT: NOT READY

**What to review.** A rigorous soundness review of the two properties the
design must hold:

1. **Disclosure.** A device never receives an image, an id or a group its
   viewer could not read when it is served. Look for any path in the
   design (the log, puts, snapshot, joins, converted deletes R1, the scope's
   trailing principal R2, windows/markers) that sends a non-reader
   anything, including an id through a `del` or a group through a `scope`.
2. **Retention / completeness.** After a device drains the stream, it holds
   every row its viewer may read (within each arm's window), and no device
   page that reports `complete` misses a row the server would return.
   Look for interleavings that break §4.5's argument: commits that change
   several things at once, paging boundaries, stale held images, rows in
   two arms, joins and leaves around moves, page-size dependence, snapshots
   racing commits, windows (§5: per-arm floors, `accept`, scope removing a
   boundary), predictions, schema evolution (§9).

Also assess whether the §8 exclusions are needed and sufficient, whether
§2.2's admission rule is exactly right (could an admitted rule deliver to a
non-reader, or an admitted union fail to deliver to a reader?), and whether
the simulator plan in §6 would catch a naive implementation and the
failures you find.

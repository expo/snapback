## Findings, most severe first

**1. High — a journal-owned document starves ordinary intents the device outbox already admitted.**
`snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:1300` and `:1682`.
Scenario: tab B is outbox-owned (custody present, journal empty) and queues O1 offline, then closes. Tab A is journal-owned and long-lived; its attached core adopts B's orphaned store, so O1 enters A's device queue. `flushOnce` now skips every non-upload entry while `journalWrites.owner === "journal"`, and `onSuspend` skips the exit send, so O1 is never dispatched for as long as A lives, while A keeps sending new journal writes past it. `QueryCustody.blocked` still counts O1, so a one-shot `client.query` touching O1's tables waits the full 30 s lifetime and returns `E_QUERY_RETRY`. This contradicts RFC §5.2 ("existing local pending writes cannot ... be overtaken by new online sends") and §7.2 rule 4 ("a write the outbox admitted is the device's, as today"); the RFC's rule 3 and rule 4 are in tension here and the lane chose rule 3. Orphan adoption is on the RFC §9 required-case list.
Smallest fix: delete both `owner === "journal"` skips; admission exclusivity is already enforced by routing `local.mutate`, and each store settling only what it admitted is what rule 4 says. If the author wants strict rule 3 instead, the journal must refuse admission while the attached outbox holds unsettled ordinary intents, which is a design change to record in the RFC.

**2. Medium — a repair introduced a regression: `E_STORE_RESTORE` now fences the whole app permanently and retires the journal on a provisional signal.**
`local.ts:328` (`if (journalWrites) options.onStoreChange?.(denied!)`).
Before this lane, a device app never reported `E_STORE_RESTORE` to `app.ts`; the local client fenced itself (`partitionDenied`) and the cold-restore witness could later validate the proof as obsolete and resume (`device-restore.ts:36-38` → `partitionDenied = null; client.sync()`). Now `refuseRestore` sets `changedStore` in `app.ts:696`, which `recoverStore` never clears for `E_STORE_RESTORE` (`app.ts:663`), so `scopedRequest` answers every non-auth request with `{denied: E_STORE_RESTORE}` (`app.ts:605`) even after the device recovers, and `custody.refuseStore` has already failed every pending journal intent as "store changed; whether this write executed is unknown". Scenario: a cold bypass read witnesses a lower head while the device control snapshot is unavailable (`local.ts:654`, `identityUnavailable = true`), the witness later reports `obsolete`, the device resumes, the app stays wedged until reopen and the journal's pending writes are gone.
Smallest fix: do not route `E_STORE_RESTORE` through `onStoreChange`; retire the journal from inside the local client only on a confirmed refusal (and keep the device-outbox parity of fencing, not failing, on a provisional one).

**3. Medium — every journal or transport status tick invalidates the entire read state.**
`local.ts:1847-1852`, driven from `progressive-writes.ts:91-92`.
`changed()` runs `answers.evidence()` (revision, server barrier and input barrier all advance), `serverAnswers.clear()` and `scheduleRead` for every watch. It is wired to `journal.onPendingWrites` (which fires on capture, admission, cross-tab `journalEvents`, every transport `pendingEvents.notify`, bind, settle) plus again after each `this.pending()` and on every `onWrite`. One click before attachment produces roughly fifteen full invalidations within one write round trip. Each advance makes `publishLocal` reject the in-flight `/q/` reply (`result.authority !== answers.revision`, and `covered()` fails because `serverBarrier` moved), so watches refetch repeatedly on the slow profile this RFC targets, and a one-shot `client.query` issued alongside a click spends its four `QUERY_ROUNDS` and returns `E_QUERY_RETRY` (`query-settlement.ts:85-108`). Remembered answers that offline recovery relies on are also discarded on every tick.
Smallest fix: on admission and settlement only, invalidate the footprint of the affected intent (`dependencies(backend, op).writes`) via `answers.changed`/`notified`, and drop the unconditional `evidence()` + `serverAnswers.clear()`; the `committed` callback already supplies the sequenced barrier.

**4. Medium — a journal storage failure during the open-time decision signs the user out and revokes the bearer.**
`progressive-writes.ts:49` (`journalReader(...)(true)`) and `:52` (`OnlineCapture.unsettled`, which throws when `localStorage` is unavailable).
A1 caught only the probe. A damaged journal whose quarantine write fails (`app-journal.ts:134`, `forWrite` rethrows) or a disabled `localStorage` rejects `decide()`, which rejects `createLocalClient`, which `open()` treats as a provisional failure: `revokeSession(url, scope.session.token)` and `publish("signed-out")` (`app.ts:872-876`). The device-less journal deliberately keeps sign-in alive and blocks writes in that case.
Smallest fix: read with `forWrite = false`, wrap the capture check, and on any failure choose `"journal"` ownership with `progressive = false` (A1's awaited device), reporting the storage warning.

**5. Medium — two journal reads under the Web Lock on every evaluation attempt, including outbox-owned documents.**
`progressive-writes.ts:57-67`; callers at `local.ts:216`, `:1085`, `:1098`.
`pending()` always does `custody.client.pendingWrites()` (one `locked(key, read)` IndexedDB read) and then a second locked read for the receipt floor. `runLocal` awaits it per attempt, `queryQueue` and the environment's `queued` call it again, and `install()` runs for the outbox owner too, so a returning device whose journal is empty still pays this on every read. Against RFC §9 "steady local performance: no material regression".
Smallest fix: skip `install()`'s transport and custody entirely when the owner is the outbox (return `[]` with no I/O), and keep the snapshot current from `onPendingWrites` notifications instead of re-reading per evaluation.

**6. Low — the journal floor withdraws displayed data to `loading` without retention.**
`local.ts:1101`. A served answer whose cut is below `journalFloor` becomes `{loading: true}` with no `custodyWaiting`, so `publishLocal` publishes it over a displayed answer (`ReadObservation.replace` always replaces, `read-observation.ts:208-237`), skipping LLP 3000.023 §10's bounded retention. Reachable when a reply's cut lags a settled receipt (replica lag, or `seq` undefined on a composed candidate). Fix: set `custodyWaiting = true` on that branch too, or mark the watch dirty instead of rewriting the read.

**7. Low — journal receipts are recorded as unbounded plain revalidation, not change evidence.**
`local.ts:1856` sets `watch.changeCut` by hand and calls `scheduleRead(watch)` with `changed = false`, so `answers.changed` is skipped and the re-read episode is unbounded. LLP 3000.023 §10 says acknowledged online mutation receipts bound every watch. Fix: `scheduleRead(watch, true, committed)`.

Only the direct `owner.client.mutate` refusal is proven. Add an assertion that the journal's receipts do not contain the first id, or that the device queue held it.

**10. Low — the journal fetch wrapper feeds `json.head ?? json.seq` into the restore witness without the replayed guard.**

## Verified sound

- Lock order in `decide()` (journal, then session) matches `admitCaptured` and `recover`; no inversion with `checkAuthority`.
- The owner decision is fixed per document; `dispatchEnabled = false` makes the journal refuse admission, skip `ownDispatch`, and skip recovery, and the transport's `mutate` cannot reach the server without an owner.
- The private transport never rotates or revokes the shared bearer: no `session` means no refresh timer, `raw.signOut` maps to `transport.close()`, and the wrapper substitutes the local client's current headers; its delivery queue is memory-only and never accepts a manifest, so there is no second acknowledger.
- `transport.write(id)` is an in-memory history read, so `local.write` falls through to the device for outbox ids without a network round trip.
- Replayed receipts carry a current `head` from the server, so the "lost response" and reopen replays do not trip the restore witness today.
- The pre-progressive fixture does prove the record shape is unchanged.
- Capture scope, persona authority stamp and journal key normalization match the device-less path, so an existing device-less journal is seen by the progressive device app.

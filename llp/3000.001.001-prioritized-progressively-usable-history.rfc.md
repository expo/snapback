## 1. Proposal

A person opens a messaging app on a new phone. Their account has a thousand
conversations and years of messages. The inbox appears from the server, then
becomes available offline; the conversation they open becomes available
offline next; the other conversations follow. The app stays usable and says
that offline history is still syncing. Losing the connection halfway through
preserves everything already made usable, including after the app is closed
and reopened.

Make acquisition incremental in **what the device can safely read**, as well
as in what it downloads. Today the device stages the whole declared partition
privately and publishes it in one transaction when the terminal page and its
catch-up have arrived, so a large account has no offline-usable data until
all of it has downloaded. Instead, acquire the declared partition **in
publishable units, in priority order, on the rails the replica already
runs on**:

- Horizonless declared tables (memberships, threads, profiles) are acquired
  whole and first, one page at a time, each page published as it lands.
- Horizoned tables (messages) are acquired group by group, each group as the
  server-certified newest-first suffix the join expansion already produces,
  published when its page lands, under one device watermark.
- The open conversation is served first; the rest follow the server's walk.

Keep one interpreter, ordinary queries, the declared replica, the one
partition watermark and the existing write owner. Priority changes order,
never authorization, the declared working set, or the eventual obligation to
finish it.

This is a proposed extension, not implemented behavior or implementation
authorization. The user commissioned this RFC on 2026-11-02. Implementation
belongs to the Snapback 4 core owner under LLP 3000's ownership rule;
acceptance must name that implementer and a start date. No performance result
is claimed here; the panel's verdicts are recorded in §12.

## 2. Orientation and current behavior

Scope: the server's partition snapshot/log, the Rust device's staging and
coverage, the local client's answer selection, acquisition progress, and
browser/native persistence. Snapback 1–3 runtime gates do not apply.

The covering parent is LLP 3000.001. This child separates a replication
*scheduling and publication-granularity* decision from that document's
historical repairs. Extending the startup RFC alone would obscure the
distinction: a usable online client (LLP 3000.002.000) and a partially
usable offline replica are different milestones.

| Governing document | Constraint and rationale |
| --- | --- |
| LLP 3000 §2 and `snapback4/NOT-DOING.md` | Replicate declared partitions, not saved query answers; one Rust interpreter; one watermark per replicated partition; no per-query receipts, coverage spans or extent facts beyond the server-named acquisition boundary; no exact horizon maintenance. |
| LLP 3000 §7; `partition.rs` `expand_joins` | The server serves **current readable images**. Catch-up re-fetches the row and emits its current image or a deletion; superseded or revoked content is never redelivered. |
| LLP 3000.001, Repair A | The server names coverage by key: one `acquisitions` marker per horizoned group, `whole` or the Nth delivered key. Neither row counts nor scattered holdings prove a range. A group without a floor is unknown. |
| LLP 3000.001, Repair B | The published partition is not an exact cut: a page cuts events at the watermark and reads images at head. That is inherent and converges; the only unsoundness was own-write doubling, closed by `images_at` and `committed_above` deferral. |
| LLP 3000.001, Repair C | Confirmed bases, pending writes, retention ownership and generation adoption remain separate facts. |

Current evidence, inspected for this draft and re-verified for revision 1:

- [`partition.rs`](../snapback4/crates/snapback4-core/src/partition.rs),
  `snapshot_page` / `SnapshotCursor`: the cold snapshot walks synced tables
  in schema order and groups in key order, newest first within a group up to
  the horizon. **Each page is its own server transaction**; the first page
  pins the watermark and the log repairs rows that change between pages.
  The cursor already resumes inside a group (`after`, `taken`). A group's
  floor marker is emitted when its walk finishes. There is no account-wide
  recency order and no foreground hint.
- `partition.rs`, `expand_joins`: a join served on an ordinary stream page
  delivers the group's **current readable rows through the horizon and its
  floor marker**, computed at serve time
  (`@ref LLP 3000.007#18.2 — ruling 36`). A group is therefore already
  acquired live, at the device's current position, with no staging.
- [`device/boundary.rs`](../snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/boundary.rs)
  keeps one boundary per (table, index, group) with four states: unfinished,
  `whole`, a hex floor key, or unknown. A descending page on the horizon
  index is complete only at or above its floor; a group with no floor is
  unknown and the client asks the server.
  [`device/deferred.rs`](../snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/deferred.rs)
  applies markers from ordinary stream pages straight into the published
  boundaries; [`device/snapshot.rs`](../snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device/snapshot.rs)
  applies them into a staged namespace during a replacement.
- [`device.rs`](../snapback4/crates/snapback4-device/src/device.rs),
  `require_acquired`: **one global gate.** Any query or mutation touching a
  synced table is refused until the generation's `ACQUIRED` marker is set,
  which happens only when the staged snapshot's catch-up publishes. The
  client treats that refusal as unknown and asks the server.
- The device holds one `WATERMARK` for the whole partition and advances it
  page by page; `images_at` and `committed_above` on every page carry
  Repair B's deferral evidence.
- [The sync card](../snapback4/crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/sync.rs):
  `last N` bounds acquisition per audience group; ascending reads and other
  indexes have unknown coverage; a 1,000-membership account with 90,500
  messages acquires in 18.40 s with a **307 MB peak wasm heap** on a fast
  host, with first inbox answers from the server at 0.40 s. The §1 account
  (1,000 × `last 500`) is several times that fixture.
- [`acquisition.ts`](../snapback4/packages/snapback4/src/acquisition.ts)
  exposes received snapshot row-image counts, not a total; counts do not
  permit reads of staged data.

The design below changes two things: the **order** of the existing walk and
the **granularity** of publication. It adds no second interpreter, no
per-query cache, no second cut and no server-side history of row versions.

## 3. User-visible contract

For the ordinary queries of a messaging app on a new device:

1. Online, render the server answer as today while acquisition proceeds.
2. The inbox (a page over the viewer's membership and thread rows) answers
   locally once those horizonless tables are acquired whole, before any
   message history has arrived.
3. A newest-first history page answers locally once its conversation's
   suffix is published, while `client.acquired` remains false.
4. Offline, everything published remains readable, including after closing
   and reopening the app. Answers are stale, with the existing `fresh` /
   `since` metadata.
5. Scrolling beyond a published floor returns the known suffix with
   `complete: false`, or loading where a partial value is unsafe. It does not
   claim the conversation has ended. Online, the client pages older history
   from the server as today; that does not enlarge the persistent working set.
6. A read that needs an unacquired table or group is unknown and routes to
   the server, as today.

"This page is complete" means this page is covered. "Offline history ready"
means the entire declared acquisition has completed. Neither means every
message ever sent is stored on this device.

An illustrative sequence, not a timing claim:

| Moment | What is usable offline | Indicator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signed in, device opening | Nothing yet; server answers on screen | Preparing offline access |
| Horizonless tables published | Inbox, thread rows, profiles, membership | Syncing offline history… |
| Open conversation published | Its declared newest-first window (`last 500` in the example) | Syncing offline history… |
| Further groups published | Their windows, in the server's order | Syncing offline history…; optional counts |
| Connection lost | Everything published; queued writes under existing custody rules | Offline · some history is not downloaded |
| Reopened offline | The same published data | Same incomplete-history indication |
| Walk finishes | All declared synced data | Offline history ready |

Media bytes retain their separate caching and authorization rules; message
availability does not promise that every attachment is downloaded.

## 4. Authoring and scheduling

### 4.1 Keep the existing declaration and query

No new Quarry spelling is required. The sync card's messaging shape already
has the right tables:

```quarry
table messages:
  conversation: threads
  body: text <=2000
  at: time
  read <- exists participants[.conversation, viewer]
  retain delivered-history
  sync to participants[.conversation] by .personId last 500 by .at, .id

query history(c: threads, page: cursor ?):
  return messages where .conversation = c last 50 by .at, .id after page
```

with `threads` (one row per group, no horizon) and `participants` (synced
`by .personId`, `cap 50 by threadId`, no horizon) declared as the card shows.

The binder derives a **foreground hint** when a mounted `useQuery` /
`usePage` observation pins one audience group of a horizoned table and reads
on that table's horizon index. The hint names the table and group internally;
it is not a row list or an application-maintained sync queue. Multiple
observations coalesce; unmounting removes the hint, not downloaded data or
background responsibility. The conversation ids an online inbox answer
displays may seed hints the same way (Grok). Scalar counts, searches,
unsynced tables and unsupported shapes produce no hint and keep ordinary
routing. No public manual-priority API is required initially.

### 4.2 Two tiers, then the walk

The scheduler has three obligations, in this order of urgency:

- Process identity, revocation, expiry and the ordinary stream, which keep
  published data valid. Background preference never delays a known authority
  fence.
- Serve interactive queries and writes.
- Acquire the declared partition, in this order:
  1. **Tier 1 — horizonless declared tables, whole.** Memberships, threads,
     profiles and every other synced table without a horizon are walked
     first, in the existing table order, one page at a time, each page
     applied to the live partition in its own transaction. Their size is
     already the declared property `N_NO_HORIZON` warns about; for the §1
     account it is about a thousand thread rows and a thousand membership
     rows. When a table's walk completes, the device records a whole-table
     fact. These tables are what the inbox, the local read rule and every
     insert proof need (§5.2); without them a published message suffix is
     unreachable offline (Fable, Grok, Muse).
  2. **Tier 2 — horizoned tables, group by group.** The foreground-hinted
     group first, then the server's walk order. Each group is one
     acquisition page (§6.1) and publishes when it lands.

Changing the foreground hint takes effect at the next page boundary; an
in-flight page finishes and must not monopolize the pipeline. Bounded work
turns share the existing request, byte and meter budgets.

This promises **inbox first, open conversation next, then the rest**. It does
not promise a strict global reverse-chronological order across conversations
in the first increment: that needs the server to order remaining groups by
their newest horizon key, one indexed probe per group, which is cheap but
unmeasured. It is named as a later increment in §10.3 and is not required for
the §1 scenario. A timestamp order expresses application recency; an
arbitrary id order is not relabeled time.

## 5. Coverage is a property of the replica

### 5.1 Coverage facts

Two durable facts, both already in the design, carry progressive coverage:

- **Per-group floor** on a horizoned table's horizon index (Repair A): the
  server-named `whole` or Nth delivered key. Unchanged in meaning; it is now
  also *established* one group at a time instead of only at the end of a
  whole-partition replacement.
- **Per-table whole fact** for a horizonless table: the walk completed that
  table at or before the device's current watermark. New, small (one entry
  per synced table), and the natural reading of the existing terminal
  condition applied per table.

The amount of durable coverage metadata grows with declared tables and
groups, not with queries, screens, scroll positions or subscriptions. The
first increment's publication unit for a horizoned group is **the group at
its declared horizon**. A `last 500` group is at most 500 rows, about one
wire page; the horizon is already the bound the author declared, and an
unbounded group already gets `N_NO_HORIZON`. Sub-horizon slices (a floor at a
partial `taken`, deepened later) are the same certificate — the floor is the
kth delivered key for any k — and the cursor already carries `after`/`taken`;
they are a second-increment mechanism, taken up only if the heavy fixture
shows the whole-horizon unit arriving too late (§10.3).

### 5.2 A safe read before full acquisition

Replace the single global gate with the coverage facts the interpreter can
already check per read:

| Read | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Descending history page on the horizon index | The group's floor is published and the page stays at or above it; ordinary page/meter rules apply. Unchanged from Repair A. |
| Page or point read on a horizonless synced table | That table's whole fact. |
| Found point row on a horizoned table | A published row with valid authority and lifetime; existence does not establish the surrounding range. |
| Missing point / `exists = false` | The key's domain is covered (whole table, or inside a published floor); not finding an unacquired row proves nothing. |
| Count, unread total, anti-join or other derived scalar | Every input domain and dependency fact is covered. A window cannot supply a whole-history total. |
| Cross-conversation read | Every required domain is covered, or the existing partial/server-routing semantics apply. The inbox's membership page is a tier-1 read and becomes local with tier 1. |
| Explicit `held` read | Device-only with truthful coverage; it cannot silently fall back online. |

Use the existing runtime coverage frames and binder plans. A missing
dependency makes the result unknown; it is never replaced by an empty table,
zero count, false boolean or fabricated membership witness. Profiles,
receipts, reactions and quotes stay separate queries beside history, as the
guide recommends; a read rule's membership witness is not a decoration and is
why tier 1 comes first.

### 5.3 One watermark

The device keeps its one partition watermark. There is no second "common
applied cut" and no per-group cut. An acquisition page is applied at whatever
watermark the device holds; the ordinary stream then keeps every published
group current, exactly as it keeps a joined group current today. Retained
history after leave keeps its existing separate stale/composition semantics;
this proposal does not relabel retained rows as current live rows.

## 6. Acquiring and publishing a unit

### 6.1 The acquisition page

An acquisition request is a scheduling request against the declared replica:
the device's durable walk cursor plus an optional foreground hint (table,
group). The server authenticates it, derives and validates table, index and
group from the served schema, refuses arbitrary audiences and unsupported
shapes, and serves **one unit as of its current head**:

- for a tier-1 table, the next page of that table's walk;
- for a horizoned group, the group's current readable rows through its
  horizon and its floor marker — the same selection, in the same server
  transaction, that `expand_joins` performs for a join (`whole` or the Nth
  delivered key; expired and unreadable rows consume no slots; an empty
  group is `whole`).

The page is shaped like an ordinary stream page carrying a join expansion and
no events of its own: it names the unit it served, carries `images_at` and
`committed_above` computed against the device's cursor so Repair B's
deferral applies unchanged, and returns the advanced walk cursor. The device
applies it **directly to the live partition in one transaction**, as it
applies a join expansion today: rows by id, the floor or whole-table fact,
affected indexes, media bookkeeping, and the existing prediction-base and
write-outcome transitions. Only after durable success are observations and
progress listeners notified. A crash before commit leaves the previous
published state intact; a crash after it reopens the unit as usable. A
partially written page can never be paired with its floor, because both are
written by the same transaction. On web this is IndexedDB durability before
publication; on native it is the device-store transaction.

Why this is sound without staging, replay images or a frozen cut: the page
delivers current images at head H, the device's watermark W is at most H, and
the stream events for that unit with sequence in (W, H] arrive later and are
applied idempotently — a put replaces the same row by id with a current image,
a deletion of an absent row is a no-op, a deletion followed by a re-insert
applies in order. That is the standing argument the sync card and LLP 3000
§7 already make for a join ("a row written after the join may arrive in the
acquisition and again as a later put"), and Repair B's `images_at` /
`committed_above` already defer the one hazardous case, the device's own
pending write inside the image. Exact-cut replay with revision-qualified
images would ship superseded or revoked intermediate versions that the
server's current-image rule exists to withhold; it is rejected in §11.

**A cold walk's first unit pins the stream** (revision 1a). A cold device has
no watermark. If its ordinary stream started at sequence zero, the server's
existing join expansion would replay every historical membership join in the
viewer's log into that group's current rows and floor, acquiring groups in log
order through ordinary pages and bypassing the walk; chunk 2's natural-log
witness observed exactly that. Today's cold snapshot does not have the
problem because its first page pins the stream at the server head
(`snapshot_page` sets `watermark = next_seq() - 1`; catch-up starts there), so
joins before the pin are never replayed and membership before the pin comes
from the snapshot's own enumeration. The first unit of a cold walk does the
same: its reply carries `watermark = images_at` (the unit's head) and the
device adopts it as the applied watermark in the same transaction that
publishes the unit, accepted only when the device has no applied watermark
and nothing published; later units keep `watermark = from`. Membership before
the pin is learned from the tier-1 tables and the walk's own enumeration;
joins and scopes after the pin arrive on the stream with their expansion, as
today. Repair B's evidence is unchanged: `committed_above` on the pinning
unit covers (0, head].

Events for a group the walk has not reached yet: a put may be held or
dropped (both are sound, since the group's acquisition page re-serves current
rows, and a group without a floor is unknown to reads); a deletion of an
unheld row is a no-op; a scope (leave) and every authority event is processed
as today. Holding costs storage, dropping costs nothing; implementation
chooses and measures.

### 6.2 The walk, its cursor and completion

The walk cursor is the existing opaque `SnapshotCursor` made durable on the
device and advanced one acquisition page at a time, interleaved with ordinary
stream pages that keep the watermark moving. Tier 1 is the cursor's pass over
horizonless tables; tier 2 its pass over horizoned ones. A foreground hint is
served out of order; when the walk later reaches that group the server either
skips it (the request carries a bounded set of already-published out-of-order
groups) or re-serves it idempotently at the cost of one duplicate download —
an implementation choice to measure.

Group enumeration is the walk itself: each table's groups are the viewer's
current memberships at serve time, resumed by group key, as `snapshot_page`
already resumes "at the first group after one the principal no longer holds".
No separate discovery protocol or certified discovery boundary is needed.
Membership changes after the pin arrive on the ordinary stream as joins
(served with their expansion, as today) and scopes; memberships before the
pin are the walk's own enumeration (§6.1, the pin).

`client.acquired` becomes true when the walk cursor is exhausted — every
synced table walked, every horizoned group floored — and the device has
applied the stream through the watermark at which the last page was served.
Partial progress is **never** serialized as the legacy whole-partition
`ACQUIRED` marker. Continuing writes do not require the account to become
quiet; a join after completion is an ordinary join expansion.

### 6.3 What this removes from the Draft, and why

The Draft's private candidates with individual base cuts, a second common
applied cut, candidate rebasing, exact-cut replay through a frozen P,
server-stored immutable revision-qualified images and tombstones, replay
retention pressure, and a certified group-discovery boundary are all removed.
Three panel seats found them to solve a consistency problem the replica has
never had (Repair B: the published partition is not an exact cut and
converges), to re-derive correctness the parent already paid seventeen blind
review rounds for, to reopen the historical-image storage path the parent
explicitly parked, and to reverse the current-image disclosure rule. The
orchestrator verified each claim against the tree; the records are in §12.
Nothing in §3 needs an exact cut.

### 6.4 Re-acquisition stays staged in the first increment

A replacement snapshot after a generation change, store replacement or
restore refusal keeps today's staged whole-replacement path, where stale
rows above a new floor make staging earn its keep. Making re-acquisition
progressive (delete and re-serve one group per transaction) is a later
increment (§10.3). A retained-history rejoin keeps its existing rule: unknown
until a full replacement acquires the group again.

## 7. Authority, retention, writes and observation continuity

- **Revocation:** a scope tombstone withdraws until-revoked rows and the
  group's floor together, as today. Delivered-history keeps only history
  already published under the acquiring identity. Rejoining needs a new
  acquisition. Offline, a device cannot learn a remote revocation; known
  revocation and expiry take effect immediately under existing policy.
- **Expiry and delayed disclosure:** reuse the persisted server-clock and
  lifetime rules. Never deliver unopened payloads. An acquisition page served
  before an expiry and applied after it is subject to the same expiry clock
  as any late ordinary page.
- **Identity and schema:** replacement or sign-out retires in-flight pages
  before they can publish; same-store regression keeps the existing restore
  refusal; adoption decides retention and reacquisition with the successor
  classifier and cannot reuse an old floor under a new index or audience
  rule.
- **Writes, first increment:** durable admission does not require complete
  acquisition; the existing dispatcher/journal/outbox owner stays. Prediction
  keeps today's global gate: writes lacking the facts queue or run online.
- **Writes, second increment (Muse 5):** admit prediction per program when
  every synced table the mutation reads or proves against is whole-acquired
  or inside a published floor. For the §1 account that makes a send in the
  open conversation predictable as soon as tier 1 and that group are
  published, which is the core offline messaging action. Never grant
  prediction merely because the visible history page is covered; the rule is
  the program's certified read and proof sites, not the screen.
- **Predictions already present:** apply confirmed images through the
  existing base/outcome fences. An in-flight mutation, ambiguous response or
  cross-tab receipt cannot be overwritten or replayed twice by an acquisition
  page; `images_at` / `committed_above` defer it as on any page.
- **Observations:** local results compete with server results under the
  existing store/cut and own-write fences. Becoming locally covered is not
  permission to replace a newer server answer with an older one, reset a
  revocation deadline or remount a query. Online cursors are not reinterpreted
  as local cursors; retain validated continuation rules or restart the chain.

## 8. Progress and UI

Preserve existing meanings:

- `app.ready`: usable client/custody, not acquisition completion.
- `useLocalStatus()`: the engine may be executable while state is `syncing`;
  `ready` / `acquired` retain their full-acquisition meaning.
- `useAcquisition().rows` / `.tables`: received row-image counts, not a
  percentage and not evidence that those rows are readable.
- `Read.complete`, `fresh`, `since`, loading and refusal: truth about the
  particular query, independent of the whole-account indicator.

Propose one additive acquisition field, `usable`, with the count of synced
tables acquired whole and the count of horizoned groups published, restored
from durable state on reopen and maintained transactionally with each
acquisition page. It is neither a message count nor a total to sync. It lets
an app distinguish "downloaded" from "available offline"; a simple app needs
only the existing hooks:

```tsx
const acquisition = useAcquisition();
const link = useLink();
const history = useQuery(api.history, { c: conversation, page: null });
// Render history through the existing read handlers, independently of this label.
const syncLabel = acquisition.acquired
  ? null
  : link === 'online'
    ? 'Syncing offline history…'
    : 'Offline · some history is not downloaded';
```

Applications with no local device show no offline-sync indicator. Surface a
typed storage, permission or unsupported-device failure through local status
rather than an endless active-sync message. An interrupted connection pauses
acquisition; already published reads remain. Do not add an ETA or percentage
without a measured denominator; "rows downloaded / rows currently known" is
misleading while discovery, writes and horizons move.

## 9. Scope, compatibility and costs

First increment: cold acquisition of single-audience declared tables —
horizonless tables whole and first, then horizoned groups at their declared
horizon — with immutable audience keys, including until-revoked and
delivered-history, timestamp ties, expiry, empty groups, restart and
continued live writes. Union audiences, inherited child families, timelines,
owner-key coverage and arbitrary aggregates keep their existing acquisition
behavior: their rows still sync on the walk, their reads stay unknown until
their own coverage rules are met, and full acquisition still waits for them.
If a schema's shape cannot be walked progressively, negotiate today's
whole-acquisition behavior for that schema and report why.

Negotiate the protocol through the existing engine/device handshake
(LLP 3000.001.000): one capability for the acquisition page and walk cursor.
Old clients keep the old full-acquisition path. New clients against old
servers keep server fallback and atomic full publication. The persisted
format adds the durable walk cursor and per-table whole facts beside the
existing boundaries; partial progress is never written as the legacy
`ACQUIRED` marker, and an older engine refuses an upgraded store before
reading it. Migration preserves published rows, retained history, pending
writes and outcomes transactionally; no storage-clearing upgrade recipe is
acceptable.

`snapback4/NOT-DOING.md` needs no reconciliation: one watermark per
partition stands (§5.3); the only coverage facts are the server-named
acquisition boundary that already exists and a per-table whole fact; exact
horizon maintenance stays excluded; no result cache, receipts or extent
facts are added.

The same semantics apply to wasm/memory+IndexedDB and native SQLite. Direct
per-page application removes the staged set that peaked at 307 MB on the
heavy fixture; that is a measured hypothesis for §10.2, not a result.

Costs to measure explicitly: more, smaller device transactions and their
index maintenance against today's bulk `replace_rows`; one duplicate download
per out-of-order foreground group if the walk re-serves it; per-table and
per-group metadata; server work per acquisition page; wasm bytes; foreground
contention with the ordinary stream. No new service, application-side sync
database, per-query receipt system, server row-version history or general
arbitrary-range offline cache is proposed.

## 10. Verification and delivery

### 10.1 Correctness witnesses

Extend the existing simulator and server/device differential tests. Observe
every publication boundary, not only the final converged state. For a read
claimed complete, compare its confirmed result to the authorized server model
at the device's watermark, then apply only the predictions permitted by
existing policy. Retained history uses its existing retention oracle.

Required cases:

1. Tier 1 published, no message group yet: the inbox answers locally,
   offline, after restart; every history page is unknown and falls back.
2. Publish conversation A's window while B is unacquired; disconnect and
   restart. A works, B is unknown, `acquired` is false.
3. Page exactly to the floor, across it, through timestamp ties and into a
   proven-empty group. No fabricated end-of-history or absent row.
4. Insert, edit, delete and move ordering keys while A's page is in flight
   and after it publishes, with the stream's events for A arriving before
   and after the page. Convergence to the server model; no image beyond the
   watermark's deferral rules; the device's own pending write deferred by
   `committed_above` exactly as on an ordinary page.
5. Revoke/rejoin between pages, including a delayed old acquisition page
   after a scope. Both retention policies.
6. One commit touching two published groups and an unacquired group; the
   unacquired group's events held or dropped per the chosen policy; later
   acquisition cannot resurrect deleted state.
7. Crash or fail persistence before and after every acquisition page; abort
   leaves the old readable image, commit survives, cursor and facts agree
   with rows on both host adapters including real browser close/reopen and
   native process restart.
8. Send and settle writes during acquisition, including predictions from an
   existing store, ambiguous receipts, cross-tab ownership and offline
   capture; in the second increment, a send in a published group predicts
   and one in an unpublished group does not.
9. Switch foreground conversations repeatedly under continuous traffic; each
   hinted group is served once or skipped, the walk still finishes, old
   replies are harmless.
10. Replace/restore the backend, adopt a generation, sign out and switch
    viewer mid-walk; no cross-identity publication or retained-data leak;
    re-acquisition takes the staged path.
11. Joins and leaves during the walk, a meter cut mid-page, an unsupported
    shape, and old/new peer negotiation; refusal or fallback preserves
    truthful coverage and valid published units.

At quiescence, compare final rows, floors, whole facts and query answers with
the existing full-acquisition oracle: completion must produce the same
selected working set as today's whole-partition path. A server answer
displayed before disconnect is not an offline witness.

### 10.2 Performance acceptance

Use the existing heavy messaging fixture and a second account with ten times
the unrelated older backlog. Pin declarations, device, server, network and
hardware; include cold browser and native runs. Compare today's path with the
proposal on equal declared data; record individual runs and medians.

Measure separately: time to first server screen; time to a durably usable
offline inbox (tier 1); time to a durably usable foreground conversation;
full acquisition; interaction latency and maximum main-thread stalls during
acquisition; bytes transferred; peak memory; server work per page; disk
writes; resumed-download work after an interruption.

Acceptance anchors on **time to foreground-usable**: a disconnected-and-
reopened inbox and foreground conversation before full acquisition finishes;
bounded foreground work independent of unrelated backlog once the hint is
known; background progress under sustained foreground activity. Full
acquisition is measured for regression, not as the definition of done: the
proposed stop rule is at most 25% median full-acquisition slowdown on the
pinned heavy fixture, and accepting more requires a recorded tradeoff. Peak
memory is expected to fall; report it either way. Absolute latency targets
are set from measured transfer/storage floors before qualification, not
invented here.

### 10.3 Delivery sequence

1. **Model it.** Add the acquisition page, the durable walk cursor, the
   two-tier order and per-unit publication to the simulator model. Prove the
   §10.1 cases, including convergence of current-image application at an
   arbitrary device watermark, before changing public readiness behavior.
2. **Build the seam.** Handshake capability, the server's acquisition page
   (tier-1 page or group expansion at head, with `images_at` /
   `committed_above`), the device's direct application and facts, the
   durable cursor, and `acquired` on cursor exhaustion. Opt-in while the
   witnesses run on both adapters.
3. **Integrate.** Replace the global read gate with §5.2's per-read facts,
   infer foreground hints, add the progress field and the messaging example;
   preserve the answer and write fences.
4. **Qualify and expose.** Measure §10.2; reconcile the parent, sync and
   client cards, package reference and `NOT-DOING.md` statement with what is
   built; flip the default only on scoped evidence. Publication uses the
   existing qualified path; landings use affected checks.
5. **Second increment, by measurement:** per-program prediction admission
   (§7); partial-`taken` floors with deepening if the whole-horizon unit
   measurably arrives too late; server-side recency ordering of remaining
   groups; progressive re-acquisition.

No new complete-battery-at-exact-HEAD landing requirement is introduced.

## 11. Alternatives and decisions for review

**Only prioritize network download.** Helps transfer order, but provides no
offline value while publication remains atomic over the whole account. Fails
the disconnect-halfway scenario.

**Only retain server query answers.** Can make a screen reopen, but lacks the
declared replica's range, deletion, composition and write-base semantics, and
reintroduces the result-cache architecture the project rejected.

**Exact-cut replay under a common publication cut (the Draft's §§5.1, 6.2,
6.3).** Rejected by three of four panel seats and by the tree: the replica is
not an exact cut today and converges (Repair B); the join expansion already
acquires a group live with current images and a server-named floor; the
parent explicitly parked the historical-image storage path; and
revision-qualified images would redeliver superseded or revoked versions the
current-image rule withholds. Astra defended it as "substantive, justified
work" because current images "cannot establish an exact historical cut" —
true, and not a requirement of anything in §3. **This is the one split for
the author** (§12); revision 1 proceeds on the three-seat side.

**Independent cuts per conversation.** Unnecessary once there is no second
cut to rebase: the one device watermark is the cut and the stream is the
catch-up. Would also break the one-watermark fence.

**Sub-horizon slices from the start.** The declared horizon is the bound and
about one page; slices are the same certificate at a partial `taken` and are
deferred to measurement (§5.1, §10.3).

**Strict global newest-first.** Needs a recency order over groups before the
first useful data; the foreground hint and tier 1 serve the §1 scenario
without it. Deferred to measurement.

Remaining implementation choices: hold versus drop events for unwalked
groups; skip versus re-serve out-of-order groups; the acquisition request's
wire spelling and capability name; measured page sizes. Those choices must
preserve the contracts above: finite progress, unchanged authority, no false
absence, one watermark, and durable reopening before full acquisition.

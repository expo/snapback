# Brief 4: where should the client data tier live?

You are on a three-family panel. The question is **high-level direction**, not
detail. Say what you think the right architecture is and why. Disagreeing with the
proposal on the table is more useful than refining it.

## The two systems

**Snapback 2** is a backend whose query/mutation language compiles to a bounded
program IR. Its defining property: **the client re-executes the same program the
server does.** That is what produces optimistic prediction and offline recompute.
Its client store hydrates from a **closure receipt** — `ClientStore::hydrate(schema,
receipt)` seeds rows *and* calls `install_verdicts(receipt.verdicts)` — so the
client holds not just data but proof of coverage and authorization outcomes. Local
state is `Fresh | Predicted | Partial(exact site ids)`. The client evaluator is
~7.8 KB gzip and byte-identical to the Rust server across a 75-case corpus.

**Aquifer** is Exact's data tier, currently in Draft. Data lives in **cells** with
layered identity, moving through one state machine, evaluated by a **Rust cell
engine**. Its scope is "async, external, route, and shared data" — component-local
UI state stays elsewhere. Its only edge is the **provider**: "a typed capability
declaring how to subscribe to rows and how to perform writes." Its stated position:
*"Snapback is a provider behind Aquifer — never the architecture itself; Exact's
knowledge of any backend ends at rows and writes."* Persistence, offline and
restoration are **cell policies**. A constraint says Aquifer must never block on
Snapback. It is right now breakingly re-versioning the provider channel to a typed
event set: `rows-arrived | request-failed | request-in-flight | subscription-ended`.

They overlap heavily: both own caching, staleness, persistence, replay, and
optimistic overlays for server-derived data.

## The founder's proposal

> Every Exact 2 app uses Snapback 2 **on the client**. That client can connect to a
> Snapback 2 server, or — the new part — to a foreign backend (Rails, GraphQL,
> whatever) via a bridge you write. Snapback 2's client replaces Aquifer and
> unifies the moving parts.

## The orchestrator's counter-proposal (not independent — I wrote it, attack it)

Keep Aquifer as the tier. Make Snapback 2's client the reference **rich provider**.
Widen the provider seam so `rows-arrived` can carry completeness
(`complete | partial{reason} | unknown`). A Rails bridge is then a **thin
provider** reporting `unknown` and offering no prediction. One tier, two provider
grades, and the difference is visible in cell state rather than hidden.

Reasoning: Snapback 2's client is not backend-neutral by construction — it needs a
receipt with verdicts. Point it at Rails and you lose completeness, local
re-execution, local authorization and OCC, leaving a row cache with an outbox.
A bridge cannot manufacture a receipt. The valuable parts are co-designed with the
Snapback 2 server.

## The hazard I am most worried about

This project's central defect, documented at length, is **capability that depends on
authoring form and is invisible at the authoring site** — two spellings of a query
where one silently loses offline and prediction. 74% of writes escaped to an opaque
tier because of it.

If `useQuery` against Snapback and `useQuery` against a Rails bridge look identical
but one has *proved* completeness and enforced authorization and the other has
*asserted* completeness and presentation-only authorization, **that is the same
defect on a new axis.**

## What to answer

1. **Which architecture?** Founder's (Snapback 2 client as the tier, backends
   bridged behind it), orchestrator's (Aquifer as tier, Snapback as rich provider),
   or something neither of us proposed.
2. **Is "proved vs asserted" a sound way to tier backends** — or does tiering
   itself reproduce the defect? If it is sound, how should the difference be made
   unmissable at the authoring site rather than discoverable at runtime?
3. **What is the actual cost of being wrong** in each direction? Which mistake is
   recoverable and which is not?
4. **Is there a version of the founder's idea that keeps its appeal** — one data
   story for every app, local-first regardless of backend — without the hazard?

Be concrete and take a position. Where you are guessing, say so. Length: whatever
the argument needs; this is direction, not detail, so do not pad with specifics.

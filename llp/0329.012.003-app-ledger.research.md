# Snapback contact report: double-entry expense ledger with settlement

Assigned archetype: Splitwise crossed with a real ledger. Groups share expenses; every expense splits across members; books must reconcile; people settle up; membership changes over time.

This is not a chat app with a running tab bolted on. The source of truth is an append-only journal. Balances are consequences. The mutation that looks like “edit the dinner” is the thing that destroys naive designs.

---

## Part 1 — Design it deeply, then find its hard invariant

### The temporal transition a naive design overlooks

Name: **zero-at-leave is not stable under restatement.**

The shallow app does this:

1. Store a mutable `expenses` row and a mutable `splits` row.
2. Define `member.net = f(current expenses) + settlements`.
3. Allow leave iff `net == 0`.
4. Allow “fix the amount” by patching the expense.

That design survives every demo that never combines *leave* with *later correction*. It is the expense-ledger analogue of a chat app that never models leaving a group.

The state transition that breaks it:

1. Alice pays 90.00, split three ways. Pot nets: Alice +60, Bob −30, Charlie −30.
2. Charlie pays Alice 30.00. Charlie’s net is 0. Charlie leaves. The app records `role: 'former'` because the leave invariant held *at that instant*.
3. Alice corrects the dinner to 60.00.

After restatement, Charlie’s economic position is no longer zero (he overpaid relative to the corrected document). Among *remaining* members the books no longer sum to zero unless Charlie is resurrected into the ledger. The leave decision was a function of a number that history later rewrote.

Two sibling traps, same family:

- **Posted-document restatement after cash has moved.** Settlements are derivative applications of the then-current books. Silently patching a source document makes every settlement receipt a lie, even when the arithmetic of a full recompute happens to still sum to zero.
- **Backdated expenses across a membership change.** “Split among current members” and “split among people who were members on the receipt date” are different predicates. Naive apps only have current membership.

A correct ledger does not patch postings. A correction is a reversing journal plus a new journal. Leave is not “delete the person.” Leave is “stop splitting *new* expenses onto them,” while their account remains addressable if a restatement or a write-off moves it.

That is the coterie join/leave problem in this domain: **membership lifecycle and balance lifecycle are different clocks.** Treating “net is zero right now” as a once-and-for-all closeout is the bug.

### Invariants the app must not violate

Stated in plain language, before any code.

1. **Local journal balance.** Every mutation that writes postings writes a set of lines whose signed amounts in group base currency sum to exactly zero. No mutation is allowed to “fix it up later.”
2. **Global books balance.** At every committed snapshot, the sum of member nets in a group is zero. This must be an inductive consequence of (1), not a nightly repair.
3. **Split coverage.** For every posted expense, the splits in expense currency sum to the expense total. After conversion at the *captured* rate, the base-currency postings for that expense also sum to zero.
4. **Append-only postings.** A posting row is never patched or deleted. A correction inserts a reversal that references the original, then inserts new lines. Reversing an already-reversed source is forbidden (no double-negation).
5. **Frozen FX.** The rate that turned expense-currency into group currency is part of the posting. Restatement does not revalue old journals at today’s rate. A rate change is a new document.
6. **Settlement does not invent money and does not overpay.** A settlement is a balanced transfer from a current debtor (net `< 0`) to a current creditor (net `> 0`), of an amount `≤ min(−debtorNet, creditorNet)`, in one atomic transaction with those two nets. It must not win a race against a concurrent expense that changed those nets.
7. **Leave does not destroy history.** A member with a non-zero net cannot disappear. A member who left at net zero can have that zero invalidated by a later reversal; the books must reopen for them. History of expenses they participated in is preserved.
8. **Active vs historical participation.** New expenses split only across *active* members. Visibility of old expenses for a former member is the interval `[joinedAt, leftAt)`, not “once a member, always a reader of the live group.”
9. **No silent rewrite of applied cash.** If a settlement has been posted, an “edit” of an earlier expense must not mutate the settlement. The settlement stands; the correction stands beside it.
10. **Deterministic remainder.** When an amount does not divide evenly, remainder minor units are assigned by a stable order (member id). Two offline clients predicting the same equal-split expense must produce the same lines.

Invariant 1 is the load-bearing one. If the backend cannot *check a sum over a bound set*, it cannot enforce a ledger. Counts will not substitute.

### Schema

Intended append-only tables: `postings`, and the posted form of `expenses` / `expenseSplits` / `settlements` / `membershipEvents`. Snapback has no table-immutability primitive, so that intent is not enforceable today — see Part 3.

```js
// schema.mjs
// Money is integer minor units. Never floats.
// Sign convention on postings and nets: positive => the group owes the user
// (the user is a creditor). Negative => the user owes the group.

export const groups = defineTable({
  name: v.string(),
  baseCurrency: v.string(),          // ISO code; immutable after first posting
  createdBy: v.id('users'),
  createdAt: v.integer(),
  booksVersion: v.integer(),         // bumped on every journal; OCC for whole-group races

  .index('by_creator', ['createdBy'])

    kind: 'membership',
    declaration: 'groupMemberships',
    resourceField: '_id',
    roles: ['member', 'former'],

  .sync();

// Current row, but leave is a cutoff, not a delete.
// A former member with net != 0 is a real, addressable account.

    roles: ['member', 'former'],
```

Visibility note, before functions: declaring `roles: ['member', 'former']` on live group tables is *wrong* for new expenses (former members would sync posts after they left) and *right* for historical rows they participated in. The membership visibility kind has no interval. That is not a schema typo; it is a hole the mutations cannot paper over. See `listVisibleExpenses` and Part 3.

#### 1. `postCustomSplitExpense`

Custom shares. This is the mutation that *is* the product. Equal-split-only would dodge a sum at the cost of lying about how people actually split dinners.

```js
// functions.mjs
export const postCustomSplitExpense = defineJsMutation({
  args: {
    groupId: v.id('groups'),
    description: v.string(),
    amountMinor: v.integer(),
    currency: v.string(),
    rateNum: v.integer(),
    rateDen: v.integer(),
    payerId: v.id('users'),
    occurredAt: v.integer(),
    splits: v.array(v.object({
      userId: v.id('users'),
      shareMinor: v.integer(),
    })),
    clientRequestId: v.string(),
  },
  reads: [
    'groups', 'groupMemberships', 'membershipEvents',
    'expenses', 'memberBalances',
  ],
  writes: [
    'expenses', 'expenseSplits', 'postings', 'memberBalances', 'groups',
  ],
  handler: async (ctx, args) => {
    const existing = await ctx.db.query('expenses')
      .withIndex('by_client_request', q =>
        q.eq('groupId', args.groupId).eq('clientRequestId', args.clientRequestId))
      .unique();
    if (existing) return existing._id;

    const group = await ctx.db.get(args.groupId);
    if (!group) throw new Refusal('GROUP_NOT_FOUND');

    const callerMem = await membership(ctx, args.groupId, ctx.authUserId);
    if (!callerMem || callerMem.role !== 'member') throw new Refusal('NOT_ACTIVE_MEMBER');
    if (args.amountMinor <= 0) throw new Refusal('NON_POSITIVE_AMOUNT');
    if (args.rateDen <= 0) throw new Refusal('BAD_RATE');
    if (args.splits.length === 0) throw new Refusal('NO_SPLITS');

    // Invariant 3: shares cover the total. This is a SUM, not a count.
    let shareSum = 0;
    const seen = new Set();
    for (const s of args.splits) {
      if (s.shareMinor <= 0) throw new Refusal('NON_POSITIVE_SHARE');
      if (seen.has(s.userId)) throw new Refusal('DUPLICATE_SPLIT_USER');
      seen.add(s.userId);
      shareSum += s.shareMinor;
    }
    if (shareSum !== args.amountMinor) throw new Refusal('SPLITS_MUST_SUM_TO_TOTAL');

    // Frozen FX: base amount exact, or we book a residual onto the payer.
    const amountBase = mulDivTrunc(args.amountMinor, args.rateNum, args.rateDen);
    const fxResidual = args.amountMinor * args.rateNum - amountBase * args.rateDen;
    // residual is in (expense-minor * rateNum) space; we instead require
    // each converted share, then fix any leftover onto the payer so lines sum to 0.

    const occurredAt = args.occurredAt;
    const splitLines = [];
    let shareBaseSum = 0;
    for (const s of args.splits) {
      const activeAt = await wasActiveAt(ctx, args.groupId, s.userId, occurredAt);
      if (!activeAt) throw new Refusal('SPLIT_USER_NOT_ACTIVE_AT_OCCURRED_AT');
      const shareBase = mulDivTrunc(s.shareMinor, args.rateNum, args.rateDen);
      shareBaseSum += shareBase;
      splitLines.push({ ...s, shareBase });
    }
    const payerActive = await wasActiveAt(ctx, args.groupId, args.payerId, occurredAt);
    if (!payerActive) throw new Refusal('PAYER_NOT_ACTIVE_AT_OCCURRED_AT');

    // Conversion leftover: integer FX can make sum(shareBase) != amountBase.
    // Book the difference on the payer so the journal still balances.
    const conversionGap = amountBase - shareBaseSum;

    const now = Date.now();
    const expenseId = await ctx.db.insert('expenses', {
      groupId: args.groupId,
      description: args.description,
      amountMinor: args.amountMinor,
      currency: args.currency,
      rateNum: args.rateNum,
      rateDen: args.rateDen,
      amountBaseMinor: amountBase,
      payerId: args.payerId,
      occurredAt,
      createdAt: now,
      createdBy: ctx.authUserId,
      status: 'posted',
      clientRequestId: args.clientRequestId,
    });

    const deltas = new Map(); // userId -> base delta
    const addDelta = (uid, n) => deltas.set(uid, (deltas.get(uid) ?? 0) + n);

    addDelta(args.payerId, amountBase);
    await ctx.db.insert('postings', {
      groupId: args.groupId, userId: args.payerId,
      amountBaseMinor: amountBase,
      sourceKind: 'expense-payer', sourceId: expenseId, createdAt: now,
    });

    for (const s of splitLines) {
      await ctx.db.insert('expenseSplits', {
        expenseId, groupId: args.groupId, userId: s.userId,
        shareMinor: s.shareMinor, shareBaseMinor: s.shareBase,
      });
      addDelta(s.userId, -s.shareBase);
      await ctx.db.insert('postings', {
        groupId: args.groupId, userId: s.userId,
        amountBaseMinor: -s.shareBase,
        sourceKind: 'expense-share', sourceId: expenseId, createdAt: now,
      });
    }

    if (conversionGap !== 0) {
      addDelta(args.payerId, conversionGap);
      await ctx.db.insert('postings', {
        groupId: args.groupId, userId: args.payerId,
        amountBaseMinor: conversionGap,
        sourceKind: 'expense-fx-residual', sourceId: expenseId, createdAt: now,
      });
    }

    // Invariant 1, checked in-process because the backend has no sum-guard.
    let journalSum = 0;
    for (const n of deltas.values()) journalSum += n;
    if (journalSum !== 0) throw new Refusal('JOURNAL_DOES_NOT_BALANCE');

    await applyDeltas(ctx, args.groupId, deltas);
    await ctx.db.patch(args.groupId, { booksVersion: group.booksVersion + 1 });
    return expenseId;
  },
});
```

`wasActiveAt` is an interval predicate over `membershipEvents` (last event at `<= occurredAt` is `joined`). `applyDeltas` patches every affected `memberBalances` row and asserts `sum(all nets in group) == 0` as a belt-and-braces check — that last check is itself a sum over a bound set.

#### 2. `postEqualSplitExpense`

The only expense-posting that *almost* looks like rung C. It still is not a plan, because remainder cents require a stable order and a per-row amount that depends on index.

```js
export const postEqualSplitExpense = defineJsMutation({
  args: {
    groupId: v.id('groups'),
    description: v.string(),
    amountMinor: v.integer(),
    currency: v.string(),
    rateNum: v.integer(),
    rateDen: v.integer(),
    payerId: v.id('users'),
    occurredAt: v.integer(),
    clientRequestId: v.string(),
  },
  reads: ['groups', 'groupMemberships', 'membershipEvents', 'expenses', 'memberBalances'],
  writes: ['expenses', 'expenseSplits', 'postings', 'memberBalances', 'groups'],
  handler: async (ctx, args) => {
    const members = await activeMembersAt(ctx, args.groupId, args.occurredAt);
    // Count of a resource-keyed set. Finding 3 allows this count to drive a refusal.
    if (members.length === 0) throw new Refusal('NO_ACTIVE_MEMBERS_AT_DATE');
    members.sort((a, b) => (a.userId < b.userId ? -1 : 1)); // stable remainder sink
    const n = members.length;
    const q = Math.trunc(args.amountMinor / n);
    const r = args.amountMinor % n;
    const splits = members.map((m, i) => ({
      userId: m.userId,
      shareMinor: q + (i < r ? 1 : 0),
    }));
    return postCustomSplitExpense.handler(ctx, { ...args, splits });
  },
});
```

#### 3. `reverseExpense`

Corrections do not patch. They negate.

```js
export const reverseExpense = defineJsMutation({
  args: { expenseId: v.id('expenses'), clientRequestId: v.string() },
  reads: ['expenses', 'expenseSplits', 'postings', 'groupMemberships', 'memberBalances', 'groups'],
  writes: ['expenses', 'postings', 'memberBalances', 'groups'],
  handler: async (ctx, args) => {
    const expense = await ctx.db.get(args.expenseId);
    if (!expense) throw new Refusal('EXPENSE_NOT_FOUND');
    if (expense.status !== 'posted') throw new Refusal('ALREADY_REVERSED');

    const callerMem = await membership(ctx, expense.groupId, ctx.authUserId);
    if (!callerMem || callerMem.role !== 'member') throw new Refusal('NOT_ACTIVE_MEMBER');
    if (expense.createdBy !== ctx.authUserId && expense.payerId !== ctx.authUserId) {
      throw new Refusal('NOT_AUTHORISED_TO_REVERSE');
    }

    const already = await ctx.db.query('expenses')
      .withIndex('by_reverses', q => q.eq('reversesExpenseId', args.expenseId))
      .unique();
    if (already) throw new Refusal('ALREADY_REVERSED');

    const lines = await ctx.db.query('postings')
      .withIndex('by_source', q =>
        q.eq('sourceKind', 'expense-payer').eq('sourceId', args.expenseId))
      .collect();
    // Real implementation unions all sourceKinds for this expense id.
    const allLines = await postingsForExpense(ctx, args.expenseId);
    if (allLines.length === 0) throw new Refusal('ORPHAN_EXPENSE');

    const now = Date.now();
    const reversalId = await ctx.db.insert('expenses', {
      ...expense,
      status: 'posted',
      reversesExpenseId: args.expenseId,
      amountMinor: -expense.amountMinor,
      amountBaseMinor: -expense.amountBaseMinor,
      createdAt: now,
      createdBy: ctx.authUserId,
      clientRequestId: args.clientRequestId,
    });

    const deltas = new Map();
    for (const line of allLines) {
      await ctx.db.insert('postings', {
        groupId: line.groupId,
        userId: line.userId,
        amountBaseMinor: -line.amountBaseMinor,
        sourceKind: 'reversal',
        sourceId: reversalId,
        createdAt: now,
      });
      deltas.set(line.userId, (deltas.get(line.userId) ?? 0) - line.amountBaseMinor);
    }

    await ctx.db.patch(args.expenseId, {
      status: 'reversed',
      reversedByExpenseId: reversalId,
    });
    // Patching expenses.status is the one documented exception: it is a
    // pointer, not a money field. Money lives in postings, which we did not patch.

    await applyDeltas(ctx, expense.groupId, deltas);
    // Restatement may reopen books for a former member whose net left zero.
    await reopenBooksIfNonzero(ctx, expense.groupId, deltas.keys());
    return reversalId;
  },
});
```

#### 4. `correctExpense` — the trap, as a single atomic mutation

User-facing “edit.” Implementation: reverse original + post replacement, one transaction. Settlements are not touched.

```js
export const correctExpense = defineJsMutation({
  args: {
    expenseId: v.id('expenses'),
    description: v.string(),
    amountMinor: v.integer(),
    currency: v.string(),
    rateNum: v.integer(),
    rateDen: v.integer(),
    payerId: v.id('users'),
    occurredAt: v.integer(),
    splits: v.array(v.object({
      userId: v.id('users'),
      shareMinor: v.integer(),
    })),
    clientRequestId: v.string(),
  },
  reads: [
    'expenses', 'expenseSplits', 'postings',
    'groupMemberships', 'membershipEvents', 'memberBalances', 'groups',
  ],
  writes: [
    'expenses', 'expenseSplits', 'postings', 'memberBalances', 'groups',
  ],
  handler: async (ctx, args) => {
    // Atomic: reverse then post. If we split these into two user-visible
    // mutations, a crash / offline queue could leave the group with the
    // expense deleted and the replacement not posted — books still balance
    // (reversal is balanced) but the dinner vanishes. Users will retry and
    // double-post unless clientRequestId is held across both.
    const reversalId = await reverseExpense.handler(ctx, {
      expenseId: args.expenseId,
      clientRequestId: args.clientRequestId + ':rev',
    });
    const original = await ctx.db.get(args.expenseId);
    const postedId = await postCustomSplitExpense.handler(ctx, {
      groupId: original.groupId,
      description: args.description,
      amountMinor: args.amountMinor,
      currency: args.currency,
      rateNum: args.rateNum,
      rateDen: args.rateDen,
      payerId: args.payerId,
      occurredAt: args.occurredAt,
      splits: args.splits,
      clientRequestId: args.clientRequestId + ':new',
    });
    return { reversalId, postedId };
  },
});
```

The naive alternative — `patch('expenses', id, { amountMinor })` plus rewrite splits — is exactly the false-confidence design. It will look fine until Charlie has left.

Worked numbers for the overlooked transition:

| Step | Alice | Bob | Charlie | Sum | Charlie’s membership |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Post 90, equal split | +60 | −30 | −30 | 0 | active |
| Charlie settles 30 to Alice | +30 | −30 | 0 | 0 | active |
| Charlie leaves (net==0) | +30 | −30 | 0 | 0 | **former, books closed** |
| Naive patch dinner to 60, recompute from current expenses+settlements | +10 | −20 | +10 | 0 | **former, but net is not 0** |
| Sum among *active* members | +10 | −20 | — | **−10** | invariant 2 broken for the live group |

Correct treatment after reversal+repost: Charlie’s account reopens at +10. Alice +10, Bob −20, Charlie +10. Sum 0. Charlie is still `former` (not split onto new dinners) with `booksOpen: true`.

Using today’s FX on the correction, rather than a newly declared captured rate, would also restatement-revalue. The handler requires the caller to pass `rateNum`/`rateDen` as part of the new document.

#### 7. `assumeDepartingMemberBalance`

The cooperative non-zero leave. Alice takes Charlie’s tab. Two postings that sum to zero. Charlie’s net becomes 0; then we run the same leave as above.

```js
export const assumeDepartingMemberBalance = defineJsMutation({
  args: {
    groupId: v.id('groups'),
    departingUserId: v.id('users'),
    assumerUserId: v.id('users'),

  reads: ['groupMemberships', 'memberBalances', 'groups'],
```

#### 8. `forceWriteOffToRemainingMembers`

Uncooperative closeout. Charlie is gone and nobody will assume. Remaining *active* members absorb his net so the live group still sums to zero. Allocation is proportional to absolute net among remaining members, remainder by stable userId order.

```js
export const forceWriteOffToRemainingMembers = defineJsMutation({
  args: { groupId: v.id('groups'), departingUserId: v.id('users') },
  reads: ['groupMemberships', 'memberBalances', 'groups'],
  writes: ['postings', 'memberBalances', 'groupMemberships', 'membershipEvents', 'groups'],
  handler: async (ctx, args) => {
    // Only an active member of this group (admin-equivalent) may force this.
    const caller = await membership(ctx, args.groupId, ctx.authUserId);
    if (!caller || caller.role !== 'member') throw new Refusal('NOT_ACTIVE_MEMBER');

    const remaining = await ctx.db.query('groupMemberships')
      .withIndex('by_group_role', q => q.eq('groupId', args.groupId).eq('role', 'member'))
      .collect();
    const others = remaining.filter(m => m.userId !== args.departingUserId);
    if (others.length === 0) throw new Refusal('NO_REMAINING_MEMBERS');

    const depBal = await balance(ctx, args.groupId, args.departingUserId);
    const N = depBal.netBaseMinor;
    if (N === 0) throw new Refusal('NOTHING_TO_WRITE_OFF');

    const otherBals = [];
    let absSum = 0;
    for (const m of others) {
      const b = await balance(ctx, args.groupId, m.userId);
      otherBals.push(b);
      absSum += Math.abs(b.netBaseMinor);
    }
    // Weighted allocation needs SUM of |net| (or equal split if all remaining
    // are at zero — then we fall back to count + remainder, Finding 3-ok).
    otherBals.sort((a, b) => (a.userId < b.userId ? -1 : 1));

    const allocations = new Map();
    let allocated = 0;
    if (absSum === 0) {
      const q = Math.trunc((-N) / otherBals.length);
      const r = (-N) % otherBals.length;
      otherBals.forEach((b, i) => {
        const k = q + (i < r ? 1 : 0);
        allocations.set(b.userId, k);
        allocated += k;
      });
    } else {
      for (let i = 0; i < otherBals.length; i++) {
        const b = otherBals[i];
        const k = i === otherBals.length - 1
          ? (-N - allocated)
          : Math.trunc((-N) * Math.abs(b.netBaseMinor) / absSum);
        allocations.set(b.userId, k);
        allocated += k;
      }
    }
    if (allocated !== -N) throw new Refusal('WRITEOFF_ALLOCATION_BUG');

    const now = Date.now();
    const sourceId = 'writeoff:' + args.departingUserId + ':' + now;
    const deltas = new Map([[args.departingUserId, -N]]);
    await ctx.db.insert('postings', {
      groupId: args.groupId, userId: args.departingUserId,
      amountBaseMinor: -N, sourceKind: 'write-off', sourceId, createdAt: now,
    });
    for (const [uid, k] of allocations) {
      deltas.set(uid, k);
      await ctx.db.insert('postings', {
        groupId: args.groupId, userId: uid,
        amountBaseMinor: k, sourceKind: 'write-off', sourceId, createdAt: now,
      });
    }
    let js = 0;
    for (const n of deltas.values()) js += n;
    if (js !== 0) throw new Refusal('JOURNAL_DOES_NOT_BALANCE');

    await applyDeltas(ctx, args.groupId, deltas);
    await leaveAfterWriteOff(ctx, args.groupId, args.departingUserId);
  },
});
```

This is not a count. It is a **sum plus a weighted fan-out insert**. Equal-split fallback still needs ordering for remainder.

When it would be acceptable to drop this mutation: consumer Splitwise-for-roommates, where you can nag Charlie until he pays. When it is not acceptable: the debtor is banned, dead, or the group must produce a closed statement for a landlord/tax authority without Charlie’s cooperation.

#### 11. `suggestedSettlements` (query)

Who should pay whom. Greedy match of debtors to creditors. Needs the full net vector, a sort, and a running remainder — not a count, not equality.

```js
export const suggestedSettlements = defineJsQuery({
  args: { groupId: v.id('groups') },
  reads: ['groupMemberships', 'memberBalances'],
  handler: async (ctx, args) => {
    const mem = await membership(ctx, args.groupId, ctx.authUserId);
    if (!mem) throw new Refusal('NOT_A_MEMBER_EVER');

    const bals = await ctx.db.query('memberBalances')
      .withIndex('by_group', q => q.eq('groupId', args.groupId))
      .collect();

    let sum = 0;
    for (const b of bals) sum += b.netBaseMinor;
    if (sum !== 0) throw new Refusal('BOOKS_DO_NOT_RECONCILE'); // audit tripwire

    const debtors = bals.filter(b => b.netBaseMinor < 0)
      .sort((a, b) => a.netBaseMinor - b.netBaseMinor);
    const creditors = bals.filter(b => b.netBaseMinor > 0)
      .sort((a, b) => b.netBaseMinor - a.netBaseMinor);

    const plan = [];
    let i = 0, j = 0;
    let dLeft = debtors.length ? -debtors[0].netBaseMinor : 0;
    let cLeft = creditors.length ? creditors[0].netBaseMinor : 0;
    while (i < debtors.length && j < creditors.length) {
      const k = Math.min(dLeft, cLeft);
      plan.push({
        fromUserId: debtors[i].userId,
        toUserId: creditors[j].userId,
        amountBaseMinor: k,
      });
      dLeft -= k; cLeft -= k;
      if (dLeft === 0) { i++; dLeft = debtors[i] ? -debtors[i].netBaseMinor : 0; }
      if (cLeft === 0) { j++; cLeft = creditors[j] ? creditors[j].netBaseMinor : 0; }
    }
    return plan;
  },
});
```

### Finding 3, specifically

Finding 3 survived. Every *count* this app actually needs is “how many members of *this* group (at this time),” or “how many lines of *this* expense,” both resource-keyed and inside the caller’s declared read visibility.

What the app needs and the ladder does not have is **a sum that drives a refusal**:

- `SPLITS_MUST_SUM_TO_TOTAL`
- `JOURNAL_DOES_NOT_BALANCE`
- `BOOKS_DO_NOT_RECONCILE`
- `OVERPAY_*` when nets are not materialized

The right extension of Finding 3 to aggregates:

> An aggregate may drive a refusal only when the aggregated domain is (a) filtered by the caller’s declared read visibility, and (b) keyed to a **resource**, not to a third-party principal.

Under that rule:

- Sum of splits of *this* expense in *this* group: allowed.
- Sum of member nets in *this* group: allowed.
- Sum of *Bob’s* unpaid balances **across groups Bob is in, as a precondition for Alice posting in Alice’s group**: **refused**. That is a third-party credit check. This app does not need it; a collections product might want it, and Finding 3 (extended) would correctly make that JS-and-elevated or forbidden.

I did not find a *count* this app needs that Finding 3 would refuse. I found that **count is the wrong aggregate**. Treating “we have `count(binding)`” as progress toward a ledger is the same class of error as treating “we have membership visibility” as progress toward coterie’s leave cutoff.

### Constructs this app needs that the ladder has no category for

Confirmed in contact, not hypothetical:

1. **Sum (and min/abs-sum) over a binding / over args, in guards and in computed postings.** Not count. Not optional.
2. **Fan-out patch** (`patchEach`) over a binding, with per-row computed amounts. D is insert-only.
3. **Append-only / deny-patch at table granularity.** JS handlers can `patch` and `deleteWhere` postings. Plans cannot promise what JS will not honor.
4. **Interval / range predicates**, both for statements (`occurredAt ∈ [from, to)`) and for membership-at-time (`joinedAt ≤ T ∧ (leftAt = ⊥ ∨ T < leftAt)`).
5. **Time-dependent visibility** distinct from current membership role. Former member × new expense is the coterie bug.
6. **Stable ordering / ranking** inside a bound set (remainder cents, greedy settlement).
7. **Map-with-index** when inserting from a binding (share `q` vs `q+1`).
8. **Composite uniqueness** (`(groupId, userId)` active membership, one reversal per expense, idempotency keys). Emulatable with A + guard if unique indexes error on insert; not stated.
9. **Weighted allocation** (write-off proportional to \|net\|). This is sum + map, not a fifth kind of take.

Multi-currency earned its place: it adds a second sum invariant, a frozen rate as a temporal capture, and an integer residual that must be booked so invariant 1 still holds. It did not add a *new* rung beyond sum + remainder; it made sum unavoidable even for equal splits (converted shares need not equal converted total).

## Part 3 — Verdict

**The model does not survive contact with this app.** It survives contact with a *shallow* roommate IOU: two people, equal split, no leave, no corrections, no FX. That subset is mostly A0/A and would be a false “74% can become plans” win. The ledger that people actually need is almost entirely JS under Finding 1, and still mostly JS after rungs A–D.

### What the ladder gets right

- **A** is real: membership and materialized balance lookups are `take(1)` on `(groupId, userId)`.
- **B** is real: “all current members,” “all lines of this expense.”
- **C** is real and Finding-3-clean for equal-split member count.
- **D** is real for “insert a posting per member / per original line.”
- **Settlement, given materialized nets and OCC, is a plan today.** `AssertCurrentRelation` on two balance rows is exactly the construct for “must not race a concurrent new expense,” *if* that assertion is a write-time OCC check and not only a sync primitive. I am guessing it is the former; if not, even settlement is not a plan.
- **Cooperative assumption of a departing tab is a plan (A).** That is a genuine, useful close of one leave-with-balance story.

### Ranked misses (what actually breaks the model)

1. **Sum over a bound set, in guards and in line amounts.** Without this, invariant 1 is unenforceable in a plan. Count cannot stand in. This is more important than D. A ledger whose split-sum is checked only in opaque JS has the same shape as today: 74% of writes escape, and they escape *because the invariant is a sum*.
2. **Schema-level append-only.** The product requirement is “postings are never updated; corrections are reversing entries.” That is not a missing `take`. It is a permission on *operations*, including JS. If only plans are analyzable, authors will write JS and then patch. Immutability that is a social convention is not a security/retention contract.
3. **Fan-out patch of materialized aggregates.** If you add sum-on-read instead, settlement and every UI prediction becomes a sum over unbounded history. You will want the cache. The cache needs `patchEach` (or “the plan is a transaction of N patches from a binding”).
4. **Interval membership and time-dependent visibility.** Restatement-after-leave and backdated expenses are the same hole as coterie’s active-vs-historical membership. `roles: ['member', 'former']` is not an interval.
5. **Stable order + remainder / map-with-index.** Money division. Non-deterministic remainder breaks optimistic prediction even after C exists.
6. **Weighted allocation** for uncooperative write-off. Niche relative to 1–5, but it is the closeout you cannot product-change away in the ban/death case.

### Rungs E and F (and the ones after)

Keep A–D as they are. They are the right ladder for *row-shaped* work. Then:

| Rung | Construct | Closes in this app |
|---|---|---|
| **E** | `sum(binding)` / `sum(arg array)` (and `min`/`max`/`absSum`) as an expression, legal in `guard` and in computed fields. Same security contract as Finding 3, generalized to aggregates. | Split coverage, journal balance, overpay without a cache, reconcile, FX residual checks, write-off weights. **This is the rung the archetype was built to demand.** |
| **F** | `patchEach` / `insertEach` over a binding with a **per-row expression** (map, including map-with-index). | Apply net deltas; reverse lines; equal-split remainder; write-off lines. D as specified (insertEach of a fixed shape) is not enough. |

After F, still missing, and I would not cram them into E/F:

- **G — table constraints:** `immutable: true` (deny patch/delete, including in JS), composite unique, maybe “signed amounts on this table must sum to 0 in the mutation.” G is how append-only becomes a backend property rather than an authoring style.
- **H — interval visibility and range predicates:** membership-at-time, statements, former-member sync. This is the coterie leftover. It is not a `take`.
- **I — order-by on a binding.** Remainder sinks and greedy suggestions.

If you only add E, custom-split *guards* become plans but you still cannot update N balances or emit N converted lines. If you only add D/F without E, you can fan-out whatever the client sent and never check it. **E without G means the one mutation you did not rewrite as a plan can still patch a posting.**

### Changing the product requirement vs changing the backend

Things you *could* change, and when that is a cheat:

| Product change | What it dodges | When it is acceptable | When it is not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal splits only, 2 people, ids in args | Sum, B, order | A couple’s IOU tracker | Any group of 3+ or “I paid 47, they paid 12” |
| Forbid editing expenses; only “add a new one” | User-facing restatement | Greenfield apps with trained users | Anyone migrating from Splitwise; anyone who mistyped 90 as 900 after Bob already paid |
| Implement edit as reversal **in the client**, two mutations | Nothing, unless both are plans | Never, as a substitute for atomic reverse+post | Offline/retry will drop dinners or double-post |
| Forbid leave until net == 0, and also forbid later edits | The combined trap | Roommates who all still have the app | Uncooperative leave; post-leave discovery of a wrong receipt |
| Drop materialized balances; always sum the journal | `patchEach` | Tiny groups, tiny history | Long-lived groups, settlement OCC, predicted nets on every keystroke |
| Drop multi-currency | Frozen rate + FX residual | Single-country consumer | Any trip, any cross-border household; also, equal-split *still* needs remainder |
| On leave, export a PDF and wipe the former member from sync | Interval visibility (H) | Consumer app with weak retention duties | Dispute, tax, “download my data,” or any restatement that must move their net |
| Cooperative assumption only; no force write-off | Weighted allocation | Most social groups | Ban, death, abandoned account, legal close of books |

The analogue of “maybe coterie should not model historical membership” is **“maybe the ledger should not allow retroactive edits.”** That is a coherent product: journals are final; users post reversing expenses by hand.

It is **not** acceptable as a way to protect the backend model when:

- The market expectation is “fix the dinner.” Hidden reversal is then a *backend* requirement, not a droppable one. The backend still must do reverse+post atomically, still must sum, still must reopen former members.
- Zero-at-leave remains in the product. Then you **must** either forbid restatement that touches former members, or treat leave as a visibility cutoff with always-open books. You cannot keep mutable history, closed membership, and `sum(active nets) == 0`.
- You claim offline retention / optimistic prediction. Then the mutations that maintain books (post, reverse, correct, write-off) cannot be the opaque 74%. Settlement prediction without expense prediction is a UI that updates the wrong number.

### One-sentence conclusion

A–D plus Finding 3 describe a backend that can *look up rows and count them*; a double-entry expense ledger is a backend that must **sum a set, refuse if the sum is wrong, never rewrite the lines that produced it, and keep a second clock for who was in the room when the line was true.** Until there is a sum primitive with the same security contract as count, an immutability constraint that binds JS, and interval membership, Snapback will classify this app the way it classified coterie before the join/leave cutoff: mostly plans in the demo, mostly handlers in the product.

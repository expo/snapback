## Summary

This document answers a founder question raised on 2026-08-29: Snapback has
durable SQLite/Postgres-shaped application data and a realtime ephemeral
channel, but applications also want logs, ledgers, queues, large objects,
analytics events, and other data that does not initially look like an ordinary
mutable table. What other kinds should Snapback include?

The central finding is that the question should be answered in terms of
**semantic contracts, not a catalog of database engines**. Many non-table-shaped
products can still use SQLite on the local target. What distinguishes them is
whether values are durable or lossy, whether every item matters or only the
latest value matters, whether work must eventually run, whether bytes are too
large for the row store, whether an external authority participates, and
whether a fact is source data or a rebuildable derivation.

The strongest uncovered product primitive is a **durable ordered application
event stream**: append-only, transaction-coupled to application writes,
cursor-replayable, retention-bounded, authorized, subscribable, and exportable.
It should not expose Snapback's internal sync journal and does not necessarily
require a second physical engine. Its implementation prerequisite is broader:
integrity declarations such as `appendOnly`, `immutable`, `balanced`, and
event-coupled transitions must bind every writer, not only one authoring tier.

The resulting candidate taxonomy is:

1. durable application data programs;
2. ephemeral latest-state channels;
3. durable append-only application streams;
4. schedules and durable jobs;
5. external effects coupled through a transactional outbox;
6. assets and object bytes;
7. maintained/ranked projections and specialized read sites;
8. bounded operational telemetry and declared product analytics; and
9. export streams into systems outside Snapback's workload envelope.

**Second pass (same day).** The ten-kind list above is sound as an inventory
and too flat as a choice set: it mixes *data kinds* (what an app stores) with
*work kinds* (durable intent), *derived classes* (never truth), and
*custody classes* (operator-side). Sorting them leaves **five data kinds** —
table, ephemeral latest-state, durable stream, blob/asset, and (candidate)
temporal rows — with the rest expressed as **modifiers** on those five
(`appendOnly`/`immutable`/`balanced`, `secret`, `rebuildable`+`ttl`, `opaque`,
retention) or as non-storage layers. Two gaps the first pass missed: an
app-authored **lossy counter** (nothing is shipped; the Draft RFC predicts only
an exact, transactional `rateLimit` constraint, which is the wrong tool for
abuse budgets that must stay off the app writer), and the **opaque partition**
that wave 4's vault and E2EE designs both reduced to. CRDT/OT
documents, actor-style room state, and device-local private state are
dispositioned as boundaries or client kinds, not new stores. See
[Second pass](#second-pass-four-layers-five-data-kinds-two-new-gaps).

This is Research, not an API proposal or implementation commission. Draft
[LLP 0329.014.004](./0329.014.004-bounded-data-programs-v1-design.rfc.md)
already names nearly the same surrounding kinds. This document isolates the
storage/data-kind question, grounds it in the existing shipped boundaries, and
identifies the missing contracts and priority order.

## Research question and scope

The question is not merely "what should replace SQLite?" It contains three
separate questions:

1. **Semantic shape:** What correctness and delivery contract does application
   code need: mutable current state, latest-only state, complete history,
   eventual work, large bytes, or a derived index?
2. **Physical substrate:** Can that contract be implemented by the app database,
   a sibling store, process memory, the filesystem/object storage, or an
   adapter-specific hosted service?
3. **Operational custody:** What is copied, backed up, restored, retained,
   deleted, exported, authorized, observed, and allowed to contend with the
   application writer?

This pass is a synthesis of the repository's current design and shipped guide,
not a new external database survey and not a performance measurement. It uses:

- the durable/ephemeral/cache vocabulary and explicit generic-KV rejection in
  [LLP 0013](./0013-ephemeral-realtime-state.rfc.md);
- the portable transactional row contract in
  [LLP 0007](./0007-storage-portability.spec.md);
- the narrow atomic activity append in
  [LLP 0110](./0110-bounded-children-and-activity-patterns.spec.md);
- the loss-tolerant sibling-store boundary in
  [LLP 0154](./0154-minimal-observability.rfc.md);
- durable job semantics in
  [LLP 0070](./0070-durable-background-work.rfc.md);
- whole-store custody in
  [LLP 0192](./0192-store-durable-state-set.decision.md);
- the ownership and single-commit-authority map in
  [LLP 0225](./0225-core-modularization.decision.md); and

  [LLP 0329.014.004](./0329.014.004-bounded-data-programs-v1-design.rfc.md)

direction here, not ratified product policy.

## Findings

### F1 — The useful taxonomy is by correctness contract

The following kinds have materially different correctness and lifecycle
contracts even when several share SQLite as their first local implementation:

| Kind | Load-bearing contract | Typical examples | Plausible local substrate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Durable application data | atomic mutation, current snapshot, portable queries, live/offline sync, backup | users, orders, documents, memberships | app SQLite |
| Ephemeral channel | TTL/lease, latest value, lossy/coalesced delivery, fresh snapshot on subscribe, no offline replay | cursors, typing, presence, drag state | process memory |
| Durable application stream | append-only, ordered replay, retention, every admitted item observable, transactional append | audit, activity, workflow events, modest operation logs | app SQLite initially |
| Schedule/job | durable intent, delayed or chunked execution, lease/fence, retry and terminal disposition | reminders, backfills, payouts, media work | app SQLite plus worker |
| External effect/outbox | app commit before I/O, idempotency, ambiguous outcome, reconciliation | webhooks, payments, email, push, provider calls | app SQLite plus adapter |
| Asset/object bytes | large byte custody, intent/finalize, metadata authority, grants, GC | images, video, PDFs, exports | filesystem/object storage |
| Maintained/ranked projection | rebuildable derivation, declared audience, bounded amplification, specialized lookup | counts, sums, ranks, search, vector/geo index | app SQLite or target index |
| Operational telemetry | bounded and loss-tolerant, independent retention, secret-read operator access, never app truth | logs, traces, health metrics | sibling SQLite |
| Product analytics | typed declared events, identity/deletion policy, bounded rollups, export | usage events, basic product metrics | sibling/hosted analytics adapter |
| Export stream | resumable disclosure to a named service principal, explicit ordering and checkpoint | warehouse, Kafka, ClickHouse, PostHog | outbox/checkpoint plus sink |

This is why a raw `ctx.redis`, `ctx.kafka`, or generic second database is a weak
product answer. It names a mechanism but leaves the application to reconstruct
TTL, replay, authorization, backpressure, idempotency, retention, and recovery.
[LLP 0013](./0013-ephemeral-realtime-state.rfc.md) reaches the same conclusion
for ephemeral state: KV and Redis are plausible implementation primitives and
the wrong app-facing contract.

### F2 — "Logs" names four different products

Conflating the following would silently weaken at least one of them:

| Product called a log | Loss acceptable? | Same-transaction requirement | Retention and authority | Current Snapback posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operator/runtime log | yes, with drop accounting | no; must stay off the app writer | short/bounded; secret-read operator surface | shipped locally under LLP 0154 |
| Application activity/audit | no | usually yes, with the business write | application retention and row/resource authorization | one narrow partial profile under LLP 0110 |
| Product analytics event | policy-dependent; raw events may be sampled after admitted rollup/export | normally no, but admission identity must be honest | deletion-compatible identity policy and typed disclosure | LLP 0154 Phase 3, unbuilt |
| High-volume analytical stream | not before handoff/checkpoint | outbox-coupled, not a distributed transaction | external sink owns long retention and OLAP | deliberate export boundary |

The shipped telemetry guide explicitly says not to use telemetry for billing,
audit ledgers, or durable application state. That boundary is correct. A
transactional audit entry and a lossy backend log may describe the same request,
but they are not interchangeable records.

### F3 — A durable application event stream is the strongest missing primitive

Ordinary append-only rows can represent events today by convention. The missing
product is the closed contract around them. A first-class durable application
stream would need at least:

- a declared event schema and stable stream identity;
- an engine-assigned sequence in a declared partition, with the ordering scope
  visible rather than implied;
- append-only enforcement, including repair/admin and opaque-handler paths;
- atomic append with the application mutation whose effect the event records;
- idempotent retry and response-loss recovery;
- an initial bounded snapshot/page plus cursor-based live replay where the
  consumer contract requires every item;
- declared retention by time, count, and/or bytes, with an explicit floor and
  stale-cursor outcome;
- authorization, disclosure, and offline-retention rules distinct from current
  membership alone;
- backup/export/store-custody classification; and
- a resumable `exportStream` projection for volumes or queries outside the
  in-product envelope.

The stream must not be the public face of `_snapback_changes`. That journal is
an internal sync and commit-visibility mechanism whose retention, operation
vocabulary, and authorization obligations follow durable row synchronization.
Application events are authored domain facts with their own schemas, visibility,
and retention. Exposing the internal journal would couple the product API to
physical mutation layout and leak facts that were never declared as product
events.

The first local implementation may therefore be an ordinary managed table plus
framework-owned sequence and integrity enforcement. A second engine is justified
only after workload evidence shows that the semantic contract cannot meet its
declared envelope on the app store.

### F4 — Transactional and ledger data first need stronger constraints, not a second database

Most "transactional data" is exactly what the SQLite/Postgres application store
is for. The hard cases are not non-relational bytes; they are invariants that
must survive every writer:

- postings are appended, never patched or deleted;
- a correction is a reversal plus a new posting;
- all lines in a journal balance in one commit;
- child sums equal a declared parent amount;
- an externally visible state transition requires its matching event;
- a cap, exclusive-open rule, or immutable field cannot be bypassed by changing
  authoring tiers.

[LLP 0329.015](./0329.015-app-archetype-stress-test.research.md) finds that
append-only and aggregate constraints are orthogonal to read expressiveness.
The double-entry artifact
[LLP 0329.015.003](./0329.015.003-app-ledger.research.md) makes the key failure
concrete: a sum invariant and immutability cannot remain comments or conventions.

The Draft bounded-data-program design's `appendOnly`, `balanced`, `sumEquals`,
`immutable`, and event-coupled constraints are therefore directionally stronger
than adding `ctx.ledger` backed by an unrelated store. A specialized ledger
adapter such as TigerBeetle may become appropriate for a measured workload, but
[LLP 0054](./0054-tigerbeetle-lessons-for-snapback.research.md) correctly rejects
adopting a specialized financial engine as Snapback's general storage model.

### F5 — An external transaction is an outbox and reconciliation problem

No application-database transaction remains open across network I/O, and no API
should imply that an application commit and an external provider commit are one
atomic act. The coherent model is:

1. commit application intent and an effect/job identity together;
2. claim the effect under a bounded lease;
3. invoke the provider with a stable idempotency identity;
4. record definitive or `sent_unknown` outcome facts; and
5. reconcile ambiguous outcomes without inventing a new request identity.

That shape appears in the durable-work and payment-boundary research. It should
become the common answer for webhooks, notifications, payments, external ingest,
and export streams rather than one bespoke queue per subsystem.

### F6 — Physical separation is justified by pressure or custody, not novelty

A semantic kind should move off the app database when at least one of these is
load-bearing:

1. **Byte shape and size:** asset bytes do not belong inline with portable rows.
2. **Writer contention:** lossy telemetry must not delay correctness-bearing app
   commits, which is why LLP 0154 uses a sibling store and asynchronous buffer.
3. **Routing topology:** ephemeral rooms may need scope-sticky memory, a Durable
   Object, or Redis-compatible infrastructure rather than the durable app writer.
4. **Retention or sensitivity:** operator logs, product analytics, secrets, and
   app data have different readers, deletion rules, and export surfaces.
5. **Specialized lookup:** FTS, vector, geo, rank, or OLAP may justify a derived
   index or external engine while leaving source authority in the app store.
6. **External authority:** a payment provider or other remote system owns facts
   Snapback can observe and reconcile but cannot commit atomically.

Otherwise, sharing the transactional kernel is an advantage: one authorization
fence, one commit outcome, one backup boundary, and no invented distributed
transaction.

Any new durable sibling or object-store member must join LLP 0192's durable-state
registry with an explicit `Copy`, `Rebuild`, `Exclude`, `InstanceLocal`, or
`Refuse` disposition. "Stored somewhere near the database" is not a custody
contract.

### F7 — A durable broadcast projection fills the realtime middle

The current endpoints leave a meaningful middle case:

- an ephemeral collection is latest-only, TTL-bound, lossy, and intentionally
  gone after restart;
- a durable live query is authoritative and replayable, but its evaluation and
  fan-out shape may be per subscription; and
- a durable event stream preserves every admitted item.

Some products want **one durable current projection evaluated once and delivered
to many authorized subscribers**: an auction's public price, a modest sports
score feed, a leaderboard head, or a public market snapshot. Draft LLP
0329.014.004 calls this the broadcast projection: commit-coupled, audience-keyed,
cursor-bearing, and explicit about delay and backlog policy.

This need not become a new primary store. It is more plausibly a maintained
projection plus a subscription execution class. The distinction still belongs
in the product taxonomy because falling back to ephemeral delivery loses
durability, while naively re-running an identical durable query for every
subscriber can make fan-out the dominant cost.

### F8 — High-frequency firehoses and OLAP remain export boundaries

The root explainer names high-frequency write firehoses and analytics/data
warehousing as non-goals. This research does not reopen them. A small typed
product-event stream, fixed rollups, and bounded in-product history can belong
in Snapback. Ad-tech clickstreams, huge IoT ingestion, long-retention arbitrary
slicing, and warehouse joins should leave through a declared export stream.

The product promise should be coherent handoff—transactional admission,
checkpoint, auth/disclosure, failure visibility, and replay—not that Snapback
quietly becomes Kafka plus ClickHouse while preserving its scale-to-zero shape.

## Candidate selection test for agents

An agent choosing a data kind should ask, in order:

1. **Must it survive restart or be available offline?** If no and only the
   latest value matters, use an ephemeral channel.
2. **Does every item matter, or only current state?** Complete ordered history
   points to a durable stream; current authoritative state points to a table or
   maintained projection.
3. **Must it commit atomically with application state?** Keep the source fact in
   the transactional kernel.
4. **Does work need to happen later or after retry?** Use a schedule/job.
5. **Does it cross a network or external authority?** Commit an outbox effect and
   reconcile; do not hold the database transaction open.
6. **Is it a large byte object?** Use assets/object storage and retain only its
   metadata and references transactionally.
7. **Is it rebuildable from authoritative data?** Treat it as a maintained
   projection/index or internal cache, not a second source of truth.
8. **Is it operator evidence or product truth?** Operator evidence may be
   bounded and lossy; product truth may not inherit telemetry's shedding.
9. **Does its volume require a firehose or OLAP engine?** Export it under a
   declared checkpoint instead of widening Snapback's core claim.

## Candidate priority order

The corpus evidence suggests this order if the research becomes commissioned
work:

1. **Integrity substrate:** make `appendOnly`, immutability, balance/sum, and
   event-coupled constraints bind every write path.
2. **Durable application stream:** specify append, partition sequence, replay,
   retention floor, authorization, offline posture, and transactional coupling.
3. **Export stream:** specify a named service principal, checkpoint identity,
   disclosure, retry, and external-sink handoff.
4. **Common durable effect/outbox:** finish one semantic model shared by jobs,
   webhooks, notifications, payments, and export.
5. **Maintained/broadcast projections:** add only behind measured amplification
   and fan-out certificates.
6. **Declared product analytics:** build typed events and bounded rollups on the
   separate identity/deletion policy already anticipated by LLP 0154.

Product priority may describe items 1 and 2 together as "durable streams";
implementation cannot honestly ship the stream before its append-only guarantee
binds all writers.

## Rejected framings

### "Add Redis/Kafka/document-store access"

These may be adapter substrates or explicit portability opt-outs. They are not
complete Snapback contracts because they do not answer authorization, retention,
offline behavior, recovery, backpressure, diagnostics, or target portability.

### "Use telemetry as the audit log"

Telemetry is allowed to shed, rotate, and be disabled. Audit/application truth
cannot inherit those semantics.

### "Expose the sync change journal as the event stream"

The journal records storage changes for synchronization, not authored domain
events. Its physical granularity, retention, and visibility are the wrong public
contract.

### "Put every unusual thing in a sibling database"

Separating physical files gives up atomicity and adds custody, backup, and
reconciliation work. Use it only for an explicit pressure or authority boundary.

### "Guarantee a distributed transaction across the app store and provider"

The honest contract is application commit plus outbox, provider idempotency,
ambiguity, and reconciliation.

## Relationship to the current 0329 design

Draft LLP 0329.014.004 already proposes `schedule`, `job`, `channel`, `effect`,
`exportStream`, identity, assets, host invocation, ranked-index sites, maintained
structures, and the relevant integrity constraints. This research does not add a
competing architecture. It contributes four narrower conclusions to that branch:

1. data kinds should be classified independently of their physical engines;
2. durable application streams are distinct from both the internal sync journal
   and operational telemetry;
3. the realtime model has three reliability shapes—ephemeral latest-state,
   durable current/broadcast state, and durable complete streams; and
4. physical separation has a small set of checkable justifications and always
   incurs store-custody obligations.

If this direction advances, prefer extending LLP 0329.014.004 and authoring a
focused child Spec for the first durable-stream profile over creating another
unrelated top-level storage RFC. That Spec would need same-landing amendments or
explicit relationships to LLPs 0007, 0008, 0013, 0070, 0110, 0154, 0192, and the
guide capability registry.

## Second pass: four layers, five data kinds, two new gaps

This pass re-reads the first pass against the shipped capability index and the
wave-4 premise-breaker designs
([LLP 0329.014.005](./0329.014.005-paper-archetypes-wave-4.research.md)). It
does not retract F1–F8. It changes the *shape* of the answer: fewer peers, more
modifiers, and two additions.

### S1 — The ten kinds are four layers

| Layer | What the app declares | Members from F1 | Belongs in the "which kind?" choice? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Data kinds** | where a fact lives and what it promises | table, ephemeral latest-state, durable stream, blob/asset | yes |
| **Work kinds** | durable *intent* to do something | schedule/job, external effect/outbox | no — chosen by "does work happen later / cross a network?", after the data choice |
| **Derived classes** | rebuildable views over data kinds | maintained/ranked projection, broadcast projection, cache | no — chosen by read need; never truth |
| **Custody classes** | operator-side state with its own readers and retention | operational telemetry, deployment config/secrets ([LLP 0036](./0036-app-secrets-and-config.rfc.md)), framework-owned durable state (sessions, tokens, job tables — registered in [LLP 0192](./0192-store-durable-state-set.decision.md)) | no — the app does not choose them |

Product analytics and the export stream are **profiles of the durable stream**
— the first adds an identity/deletion policy and sampling-after-rollup, the
second adds an external consumer with a `(generation, seq)` checkpoint — not
kinds beside it. Collapsing this way is what open question 12 asks for: an
agent can pick among four or five data kinds and a handful of modifiers
without reading the subsystem corpus; it cannot pick among ten peers.

### S2 — The five data kinds and the modifiers

| Data kind | Truth shape | Durability | Server sees content | Status today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Table | current state | durable, backed up | yes | shipped |
| Ephemeral latest-state | latest value per key | lossy, TTL | yes | shipped ([LLP 0013](./0013-ephemeral-realtime-state.rfc.md)) |
| Durable stream | every admitted item, ordered | durable, retention-bounded | yes, or opaque | convention only; `write-activity.v1` partial; `exportStream` in the RFC |
| Blob / asset | bytes plus metadata | durable | metadata only; bytes may be opaque | shipped (assets) |
| Temporal rows (**candidate**) | current plus bounded prior versions | durable, bounded | yes | storage-side only: `backend.record-history` is shipped (≤ 30 days, ≤ 1,000 versions per record) but is explicitly not an app read surface |

Modifiers are orthogonal declarations on one of those kinds. They change
integrity, custody, or visibility; they never create a second store:

- **Integrity** — `appendOnly`, `immutable`, `balanced`, `sumEquals`,
  event-coupled transitions (Draft LLP 0329.014.004 §5). F4's point stands:
  these must bind every writer before any stream ships.
- **`secret` field** — write-only from the app's point of view; usable only
  inside effects that name it; redacted from reads, telemetry, export, and
  store copy. The motivating case is per-user connected-account tokens
  (OAuth refresh tokens, provider API keys), which LLP 0036's deployment
  config does not cover.
- **`rebuildable` + `ttl`** (the cache posture) — declares LLP 0192
  `Rebuild`, exempts the table from backup weight, and licenses a sweep. This
  is the honest home for external-API memoization (geocodes, exchange rates,
  model outputs) that today lands as an ordinary table with none of those
  properties. LLP 0013's "internal cache" vocabulary named the concept but
  kept it framework-internal.
- **`opaque`** — the server stores, delivers, and structurally validates but
  never evaluates a rule over content; padded sizes; purge barriers. See S3.2.
- **Retention** — time, count, or bytes, with a declared floor, on streams,
  temporal rows, and ephemeral collections.

### S3 — Candidates the first pass missed, with dispositions

1. **Lossy counters (abuse budgets, edge rate limits) — ADD, as an
   ephemeral profile.** Nothing app-authored is shipped; every rate limit
   today is server-owned (ephemeral publish budgets, `SNAPBACK_AUTH_THROTTLED`,
   the activation bundle limiter). Draft LLP 0329.014.004 §4 predicts
   `rateLimit({ by, per, max })` as a **schema constraint** — exact,
   transactional, enforced on every write. That is the right tool when the
   limit is product truth ("five invites per user per hour" is a business
   rule and may ride the app writer). It is the wrong tool for abuse and
   edge budgets ("a thousand requests a minute per IP", "ten failed
   lookups per session"), which must be approximate, must not touch the
   correctness-bearing writer, and must be checkable before the handler
   runs. That second contract — atomic add within a window, TTL, lossy —
   is the gap. It is the *inverse* of scoped sequences
   ([LLP 0135](./0135-sequence-allocation-and-snapshot-batch.spec.md): dense,
   exact, serialized) and distinct from a **quota**, which is durable truth
   and belongs to a table with a cap constraint. The cheapest spelling is
   `ephemeral.counter` on the existing room machinery, with the RFC's
   `rateLimit` constraint kept for the transactional case.
2. **Opaque partitions — ADD, as a modifier on stream and asset.** Wave 4's
   vault and E2EE designs both reduced to the same shape: membership and
   metadata server-authoritative; content stored, sequenced, and delivered
   without the server evaluating it. The E2EE design needed exactly
   `OpaqueEvent` (append-only, server-sequenced), `DeviceQueue` (per-consumer
   cursors with acknowledgement), `OpaqueBlob`, and an `AccountSyncEvent`
   stream — i.e. the durable stream and the asset with `opaque` set, plus
   per-consumer cursors. Wave 4 has since ruled (0329.014.005 §Findings,
   row 4): **new dimension plus new kind** — a content-opaque,
   device-authoritative partition whose post-decrypt reads run as a
   `localQuery` kind (not live, not twin, not certified: a second
   *evaluation* class), with an opaque `blob`, `retain({ serverDays,
   replica })`, and uniform-read logs. That ruling is about programs and
   authority; it does not add a store. The data-kind consequence stands:
   "may the server read the content?" is an axis of the stream and the
   asset, and the partition's server-side storage is those two kinds with
   `opaque` set plus per-device queues — not a fifth engine.
3. **Temporal rows (user-visible history, undo, restore) — CANDIDATE,
   already named.** Wikis, CMSs, settings, documents, and Notion-class
   workspaces all want "show me version N / restore it". Draft LLP
   0329.014.004 §4 lists `versioned()` and `hold()` under "Temporal and
   audit" but gives them no read contract; the guide says to model history
   as ordinary rows; and a bounded, authorized version store already exists
   for host machinery (`record-history`). Two honest models: event-sourced
   (stream plus rebuild) or snapshot-versioned (a bounded `asOf` /
   `versions(id)` read over the existing history store). Recommendation: no
   new store; specify `versioned()`'s read surface over `record-history`
   with the stream as the alternative. Medium priority; frequent in
   applications, cheap to defer.
4. **App-facing cache — NOT a kind.** `rebuildable` + `ttl` on a table, or a
   long-TTL ephemeral collection. What changes is custody and contention,
   which is why it needs a declaration rather than a convention.
5. **Per-user secret material — NOT a kind.** The `secret` field modifier.
   The gap (connected accounts) is real; the store is the table.
6. **Mergeable documents (CRDT/OT) — BOUNDARY.** The corpus is consistent:
   server-authoritative replay, not merge ([LLP 0008](./0008-local-first-sync-and-realtime.rfc.md));
   the no-server mesh is a named non-goal (LLP 0000); the editor engine is a
   host `sequence` outside the data plane (0329.014.004 §6). The one thing
   Snapback should *document* is the storage recipe it offers such an
   engine: a per-document durable stream of operations, a periodic snapshot
   as an asset or blob column, and ephemeral awareness. That resolves the
   RFC's open "is `sequence` a kind or a boundary?" as **boundary**, with the
   stream as its storage.
7. **Actor/room state (in-memory authoritative, periodically checkpointed) —
   BOUNDARY.** Game simulations and matching engines are host-invoke
   processes writing through named mutations; their read side is F7's
   broadcast projection. The write-side "durable enough" middle is not a
   Snapback store.
8. **Device-local private state (drafts, preferences, the client outbox) —
   CLIENT kind.** It belongs in the agent selection test as question zero
   ("does the server ever need this?") because agents otherwise sync it. When
   the device is the *authority* for shared data, that is wave 4's
   authority-placement dimension, not a new server store.
9. **Claimable work queues consumed by humans or external workers — RECIPE.**
   Table plus a lease field and OCC; not a kind.
10. **Time series, bulk imports, graphs, JSON documents — compositions.**
    Stream plus rollups; asset plus job; table; structured stored fields.
    None needs a kind.

### S4 — Revised selection test

0. Does the server ever need it? No → device-local client state.
1. Must it survive restart? No, latest value only → ephemeral latest-state;
   no, a windowed count → ephemeral counter.
2. Every item, or current state? Every item → durable stream; current →
   table.
3. Are prior versions user-visible? → temporal rows, or stream and rebuild.
4. Large bytes? → asset, with references in the table.
5. May the server read the content? No → `opaque` on the stream or asset.
6. Rebuildable from truth? → projection, index, or `rebuildable` cache —
   never a second source of truth.
7. Work later, or across a network? → schedule/job, effect/outbox (work
   kinds; the data choice is already made).
8. Operator evidence or product truth? → telemetry, or one of the above.
9. Beyond the envelope? → export stream.

### S5 — Effect on the priority order

Items 1 (integrity substrate) and 2 (durable stream) stay at the top. The
**counter profile** moves near the front because it is small, the gap is
total, and it removes a class of contended writes from the app store. Product
analytics (old item 6) folds into the stream as a profile. `secret` and
`rebuildable`+`ttl` are modest modifiers that can ride behind the stream.
The temporal read surface follows the stream and is demand-gated. Nothing in
this pass argues for a second physical engine; F6's justifications are
unchanged.

### S6 — Ephemeral rooms have no query language, and that is a choice

The question "should rooms speak the SQLite-style bounded-program language?"
is answered by the shipped surface, the panel ruling, and the waves:

- **Shipped:** an ephemeral subscription is `(collection, scope values)` →
  the room's whole current `key → value` map, snapshot then deltas; filtering
  happens on the client; server handlers publish after commit and do not
  read. Rooms are budget-bounded (1,024 pending keys, 4 KiB values, 30 s max
  TTL) precisely so the whole room is cheap to ship.
- **Ruled:** the panel added `channel` outside the data plane by unanimous
  dissent — publish/subscribe predicates are rule expressions over durable
  rows; no persistence, trace, retention, or prediction (LLP 0329.014 point
  1).
- **Tested:** across 37 paper applications the one rich read over live state
  — nearest-driver over positions — was ruled *not a channel* ("positions
  need durable live-query truth"; nearest-N is a certified geo/knn read
  site), and the ticker/auction cases went to the durable broadcast
  projection. Every remaining ephemeral use was scope → map.

Running the data-program language over rooms would be that language minus
every guarantee it exists to give (no commit, no trace, no closure receipt,
no prediction, no offline replay) — the silent degradation the panel forbade
— while buying `where` over a result set that is already bounded and shipped
whole, and while inviting the hazard LLP 0013 names: enforcing inventory,
ownership, or ordering from presence.

**Principle: in ephemeral, scope is the query and the room is the result
set.** A narrower view is a scope refinement (add `pageId` to scope), not a
predicate. What is shared, and should be unified, is everything *around* the
values: the `v.*` validators (already shared), the publish/subscribe
predicates as the same rule grammar tables use (the RFC's path; it replaces
the `allow.all` / `roomPermission` / `members` trio), the client
"current value + subscription" seam with epoch/version cursors, and the
diagnostics. The one derived value worth adding to a room is a
server-maintained `count` delivered as a single value — "312 viewing" without
disclosing who or shipping every entry — spelled like the durable
`maintain.count` but exposed as a room option, not a language. Reads from
server handlers stay unsupported or advisory-only with distinct types.
Revisit only if a wave finds ordered, filtered, or joined reads over state
that is genuinely lossy; the two apparent cases so far were durable data.

## Confidence

**High** that ephemeral state, operational telemetry, transactional activity,
assets, and durable jobs have different correctness contracts. Their governing
documents make the differences explicit, and several are shipped surfaces.

**High** that telemetry cannot serve as audit truth and that external side
effects cannot honestly share one atomic commit with the application database.

**Medium-high** that a durable ordered application stream is the most useful
missing data kind. It recurs across activity, audit, workflow, collaboration,
analytics, and export examples, but this pass did not measure one concrete app's
volume or implementation cost.

**Medium** on the proposed priority order and on durable broadcast as a distinct
execution class. Both are design synthesis rather than shipped measurements.

**Low** on concrete API spelling, retention defaults, stream partition ceilings,
and the threshold for graduating from SQLite to a specialized engine. This
document intentionally does not choose them.

## Open questions

1. Is the first user-facing stream a table modifier (`appendOnly` plus
   framework sequence), a top-level `defineStream`, or a generated profile over
   the same underlying declaration?
2. Is ordering global per app, per declared stream, or per declared partition?
   Which comparisons are explicitly undefined?
3. What time/count/byte bounds are mandatory, and what happens when a subscriber
   cursor falls below the retention floor?
4. Which streams may be retained offline, and how do history visibility and
   removal differ for former members, revoked users, and content deletion?
5. Does `write-with-activity.v1` fold into a general `audited(into)` or remain a
   narrow profile with stronger provenance guarantees?
6. What is the exact relationship between durable streams, one-off jobs, and
   export-stream consumer checkpoints?
7. Is durable broadcast an explicit declaration or an optimizer-selected
   capability whose certificate proves one evaluation can serve the audience?
8. Where should declared product analytics reuse a durable stream, and where
   must its identity/deletion and sampling contract stay physically separate?
9. What measured local workload should trigger a specialized log, ledger,
   time-series, or ranked-index adapter instead of SQLite?
10. How do Cloudflare and Postgres implement the same stream and outbox contract
    without inventing cross-target ordering or durability claims?
11. Which new durable members join `snapback store export`, backup/restore, and
    replication, and which are declared rebuildable or excluded?
12. What capability and guide vocabulary lets an agent choose among table,
    channel, stream, job, effect, asset, projection, telemetry, and export
    without reading the whole subsystem corpus?
13. Is the lossy counter an extension of the ephemeral collection (a room
    with atomic add and a window) or its own room type; what restart posture
    do abuse budgets need beyond ephemeral loss; and where is the line
    between it and the RFC's transactional `rateLimit` constraint stated for
    an agent?
14. Does `opaque` on a durable stream carry per-consumer acknowledged cursors
    as part of the stream contract (the E2EE `DeviceQueue`), or is that a
    separate delivery kind?
15. Is user-visible history a bounded authorized read over the shipped
    record-history store, or always event-sourced from a stream?
16. How does a `secret` field redact under telemetry, export, and store copy
    — `Refuse` under LLP 0192, or an encrypted copy?
17. When `publish`/`subscribe` become rule expressions over durable rows,
    does the room keep any authorization vocabulary of its own (connection-
    and session-owned keys), and is a per-room maintained `count` a room
    option or a `maintain` declaration that happens to target a channel?

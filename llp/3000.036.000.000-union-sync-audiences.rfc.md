## 0. Summary

A synced table today delivers each row to exactly one audience: the public,
the principal one column names, or the members of one group. Builders keep
asking for "the owner **or** the row's team", "the buyer **or** the
seller", "the owner **or** staff". LLP 3000.036.000 §4 deferred it twice
because of a retention hazard, not a disclosure one: a device deletes a row
when it leaves one partition even when the row still belongs to the viewer
through the other audience, and drops a whole group's rows when the viewer
leaves the group, so rows vanish while local pages still say `complete`.

This RFC admits a **union of audiences** on one table:

```quarry
table tasks:
  owner: principal
  team: teams
  title: text <=120
  at: time
  read <- .owner = viewer or exists members[.team, viewer]
  sync last 200 by .at          -- or: sync to .owner or members[.team] last 200 by .at
```

A device holds a row while **any** audience admits its viewer, and pages
that claim `complete` are complete. Three mechanisms make that hold, each
small: the server serves a `del` of a union row as the row's current image
when the viewer may still read it (§4.2); a device leaving a group keeps the
group's rows that name it in a column audience (§4.3); each audience has its
own window and acquisition boundary, and a device page is certified only
through the audience whose group its pins name (§5). Disclosure is
unchanged in kind: every image is re-checked against the whole read rule at
serve time, exactly as today (§3).

The first version admits **any number of principal-column audiences and at
most one group audience** (§2.2). That covers every shape the three app
farms reported (§1). Two group audiences, a public audience in a union,
and the combinations listed in §8 are refused with `E_SYNC` and a rewrite.

## 1. What it covers

| shape (farm lanes) | read rule | sync |
|---|---|---|
| owner or team (045, 053, 012) | `.owner = viewer or exists members[.team, viewer]` | `sync` or `sync to .owner or members[.team]` |
| owner or staff role (043, 069, 082) | `.owner = viewer or exists staff[viewer]` | `sync` (the roster is the empty-group audience, LLP 3000.036.000 §3.2) |
| two parties (007, 019, 035, 061, 092) | `.buyer = viewer or .seller = viewer` | `sync` or `sync to .buyer or .seller` |
| three parties | `.a = viewer or .b = viewer or .c = viewer` | `sync` |

Not covered, still refused with the existing recipes: public-or-owner
(LLP 3000.036 §1 B2, a conditional public audience), two groups
(`exists members[.team, viewer] or exists watchers[.id, viewer]`), and any
disjunct with a conjunct (`(.owner = viewer and .draft) or …`), which no
partition can invert.

## 2. The language

### 2.1 Syntax

```
sync [by .principal] [last N by .col[, .col ...]]                -- inferred
sync to ARM or ARM [or ARM ...] [last N by .col[, .col ...]]      -- explicit
ARM := .column | table[.col, ...] [by .principal]
```

- The inferred `sync` infers a union when the read rule's audience rule
  (§2.2) is a disjunction of admitted arms. `sync by .principal` names the
  recipient column of the group arm, as today.
- The explicit form must agree with the inferred union exactly, arm for arm
  (order is free), as the single-audience explicit form must today.
- `sync to .a, .b` keeps a diagnostic, now with the rewrite
  `sync to .a or .b`. A comma is not an arm separator because the window's
  column list uses commas.
- A union window is written in column form. `last N by .at` gives **each
  arm** its own window index: the arm's audience columns, then `.at`, then
  `id` (LLP 3000.019 §3.1), and every arm keeps the newest N rows of its own
  group. A named window index (`last N by byAt`) on a union is refused with
  the column-form rewrite, because one index cannot lead with every arm's
  columns.
- `Sync::line` prints a union as `sync to .owner or members[.team]`, column
  arms first in rule order, the group arm last; an empty-group roster arm
  has no `to` spelling (LLP 3000.036.000 §3.2), so a union containing one
  prints as the inferred `sync`.

### 2.2 Admitted unions (`E_SYNC`)

The audience rule is the read rule with whole service-grant disjuncts
removed (LLP 3000.007 §20.6) and self-witness disjuncts absorbed (LLP
3000.036.000 §3.1). A union is admitted when, after that:

1. the rule is an `or` of at least two disjuncts;
2. every disjunct is, by itself and with no extra conjunct, one of the two
   private invertible forms: `.c = viewer` with `c` a required `principal`
   column (a **column arm**), or `exists T[.p1, ..., .pk, viewer]` with no
   equality terms (a **group arm**, k ≥ 0);
3. no two disjuncts are the same arm (`.a = viewer or .a = viewer` is one
   arm; the binder folds it);
4. at most one disjunct is a group arm;
5. the group arm, if any, satisfies every check a single target audience
   satisfies today: a unique audience index `T[p…, principal]`, a principal
   index, required group keys on both sides, and the independent grant
   guard on `T`'s insert and update rules (LLP 3000 §7, 2026-09-13);
6. each column arm has an index leading with its column (for the snapshot
   and the window), planned by the compiler when absent, as today;
7. none of the exclusions in §8 applies.

A `public` disjunct, or a rule that is uniformly readable, is never part of
a union: `public or .owner = viewer` keeps the B2 refusal. A disjunct with a
conjunct or a literal key term keeps the existing refusal, whose rewrite
(LLP 3000.036.000 §2.1) now also names the union form when the rest of the
rule is a union. A second group arm is refused: "a union syncs one group
audience; give the second group's readers rows in the first group's
membership table, or replace the sync line with `online only`".

### 2.3 Representation and the capability fence

```rust
pub struct Sync {
    pub audience: Audience,          // the group arm if any, else the first column arm
    pub horizon: Option<Horizon>,    // that arm's window
    #[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Vec::is_empty")]
    pub also: Vec<SyncArm>,          // the further arms: principal columns only
}
pub struct SyncArm { pub audience: Audience, pub horizon: Option<Horizon> }
```

`Sync::arms()` yields every arm with its window. A single-audience table
serializes exactly as before, so no existing schema hash or generation
changes. The group arm, when present, is always `audience`, so every piece
of group machinery that reads `sync.audience` (membership intervals, joins,
scopes, revocation of a grant, held groups) keeps working for the one group
arm without change; the sites that must see every arm are enumerated in §7.
Either every arm carries a window or none does, and every window has the
same `last`; so `sync.horizon.is_some()` still answers "does this table
have a window".

A schema with a union declares the capability `union-audience`
(`schema/capabilities.rs`). An older device decoder refuses that backend
with `E_SCHEMA_CAPABILITY` instead of ignoring `also` and silently holding
only the first arm (which would be the naive, row-losing behaviour). The
client then stays on the server for that backend, which is the existing,
fail-closed response to a capability it does not have.

## 3. Disclosure

The contract is the one the single-audience design already keeps, stated
precisely (round 1 found the draft's one-line version stricter than the
system it described):

- **D1, images.** A device receives a row image only when its viewer may
  read that image at the moment it is served.
- **D2, ids and groups.** A `del` names only a row the viewer could read at
  a commit boundary while it held the partition the `del` was logged to; a
  `scope` names only a group the viewer held. This is historical authority:
  a revocation must name what it revokes. `exhaustive.rs` checks exactly
  this ("a delete names a row the viewer never could read", "a scope names
  a group the viewer never held"), and so does the union world (§6).

The union keeps both and adds no exception:

- **The log.** A union row is logged to the partition of each arm that
  names a recipient: `p:<value>` for each column arm whose column holds a
  principal, and `g:<table>:<group>` for the group arm. Each arm's rule is a
  whole disjunct of the read rule (§2.2.2), so every recipient of every
  partition is a reader of the image at commit. Nothing is logged to a
  partition no disjunct names; there is no "union partition". Two column
  arms naming one principal log one `p:` event (the partition set is
  deduplicated).
- **Commit-boundary accounting (new, every synced table).** A row that
  existed before a commit and changes in it is accounted from the
  partitions it was in **at the commit's start** to the partitions it is in
  at its end: the first change records that set and the position of the
  row's events; every later change in the commit withdraws the row's events
  since then and logs puts to the final set and `del`s to (start set −
  final set). A partition the row only passed through inside the commit
  receives neither its image nor its id. Round 1 (GPT finding 2) found the
  gap: an existing row with both parties Bob, changed to buyer Alice and
  back in one mutation, logged a `put` then a `del` to `p:alice`, and
  coalescing kept the `del`, naming to Alice a row she never read at a
  commit boundary. The gap predates unions (a single `sync to .owner` row
  moved to Alice and back in one commit does the same); rows born in the
  commit already had this accounting (`born_rows`), and the fix gives it to
  every changed row (`Runner::touched_rows`).
- **Puts.** The stream re-fetches every `put`'s current image and re-checks
  the whole read rule for the viewer at serve time (`expand_joins`); a row
  the viewer cannot read now is not sent. Join expansion and snapshot pages
  select through `group_rows_from`, which runs the same check per row. A
  snapshot of a union walks each arm's groups (§4.4); every row it sends is
  checked against the whole rule.
- **Converted deletes (§4.2).** A `del` becomes a `put` only after the same
  serve-time fetch, whole-rule check and projection a `put` passes.
- **Scopes.** A scope is logged in the viewer's own partition and names a
  group it held (unchanged). The union scope's trailing element (§4.3) is
  the recipient's own principal.

## 4. Retention: the device holds what any arm admits

### 4.1 The holding invariant

For a union table without windows: **after a device applies the stream
through the head, it holds exactly the rows its viewer may read at serve
time.** Before the head it may hold rows readable at an earlier commit
(the existing lag), never an image it could not read when served.

The hazard is that one viewer receives one row through up to two
partitions it follows (its own `p:` and the group's `g:`) and the device
keeps one copy per `(table, id)`. Two events break the naive device:

- **H1, a `del` from one arm while another still admits.** The owner moves
  from Alice to Bob while Alice is in the row's team: the commit logs a
  `put` to the team and a `del` to `p:alice`, in that order, and Alice's
  device applies both and loses the row. The same happens when the row
  moves between two groups Alice holds, or between two of her column arms.
- **H2, a group `scope` while a column arm still admits.** Alice leaves the
  team: her device drops every row of the team, including the ones she
  owns.

### 4.2 R1: the server serves a union `del` as the current image when the viewer still reads it

In `expand_joins`, for a table with a union audience, every `del` the
stream serves is decided at serve time: the stream reader fetches the row;
when it exists and the whole read rule admits the viewer, the event is sent
as a `put` of the current image projected for the viewer
(`project_fields(row, Some(principal))`, exactly as a `put`); otherwise as
the `del`. This is the field-owner rule that already exists (a `del` is
suppressed while the owner still reads the row), generalized to union
tables. The stream's reader runs under the stream's own unbounded internal
ledger, as every `put`'s re-check does; neither path charges a caller's
meter, so a converted `put` and an ordinary one cost the same.

Two column arms never produce H1: the log emits a `del` only to the
partitions in the start set and not the final one, so a row that keeps
naming Alice in another column arm logs no `del` to `p:alice`. H1 needs two
different partitions one viewer follows: a column arm and the group arm, or
two groups of the group arm. The second case also exists today for a single
group audience whose content group column is mutable (LLP 3000.036 §1 B1
names it); R1 is scoped to union tables here, and the single-audience case
is recorded as a ticket rather than changed in this work.

Why it is correct: the event's meaning is "you may have lost this row";
the server answers that question with the same state every `put` it serves
is re-read from. If the viewer still reads the row now, the row is held
with the current image; if a later commit makes it unreadable, that commit
logged an event to a partition the viewer follows (§4.5), which the device
receives after this one.

### 4.3 R2: a device leaving a group keeps the rows that name it in a column arm

A `scope` on a union table carries one more element after the group
values: the recipient's principal (`data: [team, "dev:alice"]`). The server
adds it at serve time to every scope it streams for a union table (logged
scopes, the synthetic logical-expiry scope, and the expired-predecessor
scope of a join); the log itself is unchanged. On a union table, both
device stores apply the scope by dropping the group's rows **except** those
whose held image names that principal in one of the table's column arms
(`row.owner = "dev:alice"`). The same rule applies to staged snapshot
catch-up pages. The scope still removes the group's acquisition boundary
(§5.2), so pages over the group stop certifying.

- **The walk passes spared rows.** The single-audience leave rescans the
  group's first 500 rows until none remain, relying on deletion to make
  progress; with spared rows that loop would never end once 500 of them
  fill the first batch (round 1, GPT finding 4). The union leave advances a
  cursor past every examined row, spared or deleted.
- **Committed images only.** R2 reads the device's committed images, never
  a prediction's overlay: every apply path withdraws predictions
  (`predictions::restore`) before it applies a page, including the deferred
  staging path, and replays them afterwards (round 1, Muse finding 2,
  checked against `device.rs` `apply_publication_inner` and
  `deferred::stage`).
- **A malformed union scope fails closed.** A union scope whose element
  count is not the group's width plus one, or whose last element is not a
  principal, is refused as malformed, as a scope of the wrong width is
  today. The device cannot check that the principal is its own: it has no
  principal of its own (the store serves any viewer the host names), and it
  already trusts the server for every image and every `del`; a server that
  sent another principal here could as well send that principal's rows.
  The element is the recipient's own principal, so it discloses nothing
  (Muse finding 1, answered rather than adopted).

Why the device and not the server: the alternative, the server re-putting
the rows the viewer still reads after the scope, must find them (a scan of
the group or of the viewer's column partition, unbounded) and must cover
the device's own window floor, which the server does not know (§5). The
device has exactly the rows in question, in one group scan, the same scan
a leave already does.

Why the held image is enough: the device's image of a row may be newer or
older than the scope's commit. If it names the viewer but the row has since
left the viewer's column arm, that change logged a `del` to the viewer's
own partition, which the viewer follows at every commit; the device
receives it after the scope (or already applied it, in which case the row
is gone). If it does not name the viewer but the row has since entered the
viewer's column arm, that change logged a `put` to the viewer's partition,
received after. Either way the next event of the row in the viewer's own
partition settles it, and R1 makes that event say the truth.

**Order inside one commit is not load-bearing** (Muse finding 6). A commit
that both leaves the group and changes a row of it logs the scope and the
row's events at one sequence, in the order the writes ran. A group span
ends *before* the sequence of its scope (log reads use `[from, to)`), so
the row's `g:` events in that commit are not streamed to the leaver; its
`p:` events are. If the row names the viewer after the commit, its `p:`
`put` re-holds it whether it comes before the scope (spared) or after it
(re-added); if it named the viewer before and not after, its `p:` `del` is
served at serve time and removes it either way. The simulator has
mutations that do both in each order (§6).

### 4.4 R3: snapshot and joins

A snapshot of a union table walks each arm in turn: the column arms' one
group (`[viewer]`, read through the arm's audience index or window), then
the group arm's groups (the viewer's memberships now, as today). A row in
two arms is sent twice; both stores stage snapshot rows by `(table, id)`,
so the second copy replaces the first with the same image. The snapshot
cursor gains the arm it is walking (`SnapshotCursor.arm`, opaque on the
wire, default 0). Each arm emits its own acquisition markers (§5).

A join expands the joined group's rows as today (the group arm). A row the
device already holds through its column arm is put again; the put is
idempotent.

### 4.5 The argument

For the no-window case, take any union row `r` and viewer `V`, and the
device's state after it drains to the head. Every commit that changes
whether `V` reads `r` logs an event `V` receives after its current state:

| change at commit c | logged | V's device |
|---|---|---|
| `r` created/updated, a column arm names V after c | `put` to `p:V` | holds the current image |
| `r` updated, a column arm named V before c but not after | `del` to `p:V` | R1: kept iff V still reads `r` (through the group) at serve time |
| `r` enters V's held group, or is updated inside it | `put` to `g:…` V holds | holds the current image (serve-time check) |
| `r` leaves a group V holds (group column change) | `del` to `g:old` | R1: kept iff V reads `r` now (owner, or the new group) |
| `r` deleted | `del` to every partition of the old image | R1: absent at serve time, so `del` |
| V joins the group | `join` in `p:V`, expanded to the group's readable rows | holds them |
| V leaves the group (or its grant expires) | `scope` in `p:V` (or synthetic) | R2: drops the group's rows except those naming V in a column arm |

The device applies events in `(seq, ord)` order, and every put and every
converted del carries the image current at serve time, so the last event
`V` receives about `r` decides `r`'s presence, and it decides it from the
state at serve time. The cases where the deciding event is in a partition
`V` stopped following are exactly H2, closed by R2; the cases where two
partitions disagree within one commit are H1, closed by R1. With windows,
§5 replaces "holds exactly" with "holds exactly, within each arm's acquired
window, plus rows it retains below a floor that no page certifies".

The simulator (§6) checks this at every node of an exhaustive world, from
every watermark, at two page sizes; the argument is the reason to expect
it to pass, not the evidence.

## 5. Windows, coverage and `complete`

### 5.1 Per-arm windows

With `last N by .at`, arm `a` with audience columns `cols(a)` has the
window index `cols(a) ++ [at, id]` and keeps the newest N rows of each of
its groups: for a column arm, the one group `[viewer]`; for the group arm,
each held group. The device holds the union of the arms' windows.

### 5.2 Acquisition markers and boundaries are per arm

Acquisition markers already name `(table, index, group)`. A union arm's
marker names its own window index and its own group, so markers and
boundaries are per arm with no wire change. On the device:

- **marker validity** finds the arm whose window index the marker names
  (instead of comparing with the table's one horizon) and types the group
  by that arm's columns;
- **accepting a put of an unheld row** (`Boundaries::accept`): the row is
  admitted when no arm has a boundary for the row's group under that arm,
  or when some arm that has one admits the row's key (`whole`, unfinished,
  `unknown`, or key ≥ floor). A row below the group arm's floor but inside
  the owner arm's window is held; a row below every present floor is not;
- **a scope** removes only the group arm's boundary for that group;
- **reading a boundary** (`acquired_boundary(table, index, prefix)`) finds
  the arm by index and takes that arm's group width.

### 5.3 What a device page certifies

A device page over a union table is complete only when it is complete
through **one** arm:

- **no windows:** a scan is complete as today, because the device holds
  every readable row (§4.1); the read rule filters the scan exactly as the
  server's would.
- **windows:** a scan is certified only when its index is one arm's window
  index, its prefix pins that arm's group, and the device holds that arm's
  boundary for the group (`by_boundary`, generalized from "the table's
  horizon index" to "an arm's window index"). Every other scan, including a
  scan over another index, a scan pinning a group the viewer does not hold,
  and a count, takes the existing `unknown_fact` path and is answered by
  the server online. Rows the device holds through another arm below this
  arm's floor are never shown past the floor (the scan stops at the floor,
  as today).
- **rule probes over a union table** (`exists tasks[...]` in another rule,
  `authorization_absence_covered`): absence is known when **some** arm's
  pinned key is wholly held by the viewer (a column arm pinned to the
  viewer, or the group arm pinned to a group whose membership the device
  holds and `witness_covered` accepts, the existing per-arm guard) and the
  table has no window. Presence of a held row is a witness, as today. The
  existing delivered-history currency guard is unreachable here because §8
  refuses delivered history on a union.
- **counts and `exists` in programs** over a union table follow the scan
  they lower to: complete without windows (the device holds every readable
  row), and with windows only through an arm's window index and pinned
  group. A maintained count over a union source is refused (§5.4).
- **search** over a union table is never planned as an ordinary membership
  search (`search_plan::ordinary` requires a single `exists` read rule), so
  a word scan takes the generic device path: no window, complete by the
  holding invariant; a window, the `unknown_fact` path.
- **`T[id]`**: absence is known without windows (the device holds every
  readable row, so an absent id is absent or unreadable on the server
  too); with windows it is unknown, as today.

### 5.4 Aggregates

A maintained aggregate whose source is a union table is refused unless it
is `online only` (`E_SYNC`): its inherited audience would have to be the
union's, and a count over "the rows of group X the viewer reads" is not a
count over group X. A device read of an online-only aggregate already goes
to the server. Row-keyed views over a union source are refused the same
way.

## 6. The simulator

`crates/snapback4-sim/tests/union.rs` enumerates every sequence of up to
`DEPTH` writes (2 in the blocking suite, 3 opt-in) against the real
engine, in worlds built so that every hazard is reachable at depth 1, with
a model of the rules written from the schema text, not from the engine.

**Owner or team** (`.owner = viewer or exists members[.team, viewer]`).
Alice is seeded in both teams and Bob in team 2; a task owned by Alice
sits in team 1 and one owned by Bob in team 2; Mallory is a stranger.
Writes: create a task, give a task to another owner, move a task to the
other team, delete a task, add a member (by a member), leave a team, and
compound commits in both orders: give-then-leave and leave-then-give, move
then give, and a transient (give to someone and back, in one commit). At
depth 1 this reaches H1 through a column arm (give away a task in a team
the giver holds), H1 between two groups (move a task between Alice's two
teams), H2 (Alice leaves a team with a task she owns), and the transient.

**Owner or staff** (an empty-group roster arm,
`.owner = viewer or exists staff[viewer]`): the same writes over a roster;
a staff leave is a scope of the one group, which spares owned rows.

**Two parties** (`.buyer = viewer or .seller = viewer`): give, sell, the
transient (buyer to Alice and back), delete. Two column arms cannot fail
the naive implementation's retention (§4.2); this world guards exactness
and the commit-boundary accounting of §3.

At every node of every world:

- write authority against the model; a resent operation applies nothing
  new;
- each principal's snapshot is exactly the readable rows;
- a stream from every watermark, at page sizes 1 and 1000, sends only
  images readable now and at some node of the path (D1), names in a `del`
  only rows the viewer could read at some node and in a `scope` only groups
  it held (D2), ends at the head, and applies to the same rows at both
  page sizes;
- every device that bootstrapped at an earlier node, or that applied only
  the first stream page at an earlier node (commits between stream pages),
  holds **exactly** the readable rows after draining (no over-hold, no
  under-hold);
- the device model applies events as both stores do, including R2;
- the **real** `snapback4-device` memory store acquires by snapshot at the
  root and at each node, follows the stream across nodes, and answers the
  world's queries: the viewer's tasks, a team's tasks, one task by id, a
  count, and an `exists`. Every answer it reports `complete` must equal the
  server's; without windows every such query **must** report `complete`
  for a viewer that reads its pins (a device that always deferred to the
  server would fail this).

A windowed owner-or-team world (`last 1 by .at`, distinct owner and team
floors) checks §5: the device certifies a page only through an arm's
window and pinned group, every certified page equals the server's, and
the newest row of each held arm group is held.

Run against the naive implementation (partitions per arm, no R1, no R2, no
commit-boundary accounting), the worlds must fail; the commit that lands
them records which properties failed. Schema evolution (§9) is checked by
a targeted test (activation adds and removes the group arm, with a leave
and a rejoin between, and writes during re-acquisition), not by the
exhaustive worlds.

## 7. The sites that assume one audience

Every `match` on `Audience` and every read of `sync.audience` or

## 8. Refused in the first version

Each is an `E_SYNC` at the sync line with a rewrite that keeps privacy:

1. **Two group arms.** Needs arm-tagged group partitions (`g:` names would
   collide across arms) and per-arm held groups on the device for R2.
2. **A `public` arm.** That is B2 (LLP 3000.036 §1), with its own
   commit-boundary accounting.
3. **`retain delivered-history`** on a union table. Retained rows of a left
   group and current rows of a column arm would share the group's retention
   markers.
4. **`read through`** a union parent, or a union table as a read-through
   child. A child would need the parent's union with captured columns.
5. **A synced aggregate, a row-keyed view, or `with own keys`** over a
   union source (§5.4).
6. **A timeline** whose posts or reposts table has a union audience.
7. **Private field owners** (`written by` without `readable by audience`)
   on a union table: they add owner partitions that R2 would have to
   distinguish from column arms.
8. **An expiring group arm** (the membership table has `expire at`). The
   synthetic expiry scope carries the principal like any other, so R2
   applies; the refusal holds until the simulator has an expiring world.
9. **A named window index** (§2.1); the rewrite is the column form.

LLP 3000.036 §1 B1 (an explicit `sync to` naming one whole disjunct, the
rest answered online) was recommended there but never built, so refusing
`sync to .owner` over an owner-or-team rule removes nothing that compiled:
before this RFC that line was refused as a disagreement, and it still is,
now with the union line as its rewrite. The union serves every reader B1
would have served and the ones it would have sent online (Muse finding 5,
answered).

## 9. Predictions, replay and schema evolution

- **Predictions.** A device predicts a write to a union table as it
  predicts any write: a provisional image is shown only when the read rule
  admits it locally (`provisional_readable`), with probe absence decided by
  §5.3. A write that moves a row out of every arm the viewer holds leaves a
  provisional image the rule hides until settlement. Applying a page
  withdraws predictions first and replays them after, so R1 and R2 see only
  committed images (§4.3).
- **At-most-once.** Operation receipts are unchanged; a resent operation id
  applies nothing new (checked at every simulator node). Stream replay from
  any watermark is idempotent: R1 decides from serve-time state, R2 from the
  held image and the scope, and both converge to the same rows from every
  watermark, served at one head and one clock (checked from every watermark
  at two page sizes).
- **Schema evolution: devices.** Adding or removing an arm changes the
  table's `sync`, so the new backend is a new generation whose images are
  not kept (`successor::keeps_literal_default_images` compares the whole
  table): a device drops its images, re-acquires by snapshot, and certifies
  nothing until the snapshot publishes.
- **Schema evolution: the server's ledger (new).** A stream decides which
  groups a principal held at its watermark from the last join or scope the
  ledger logged before it, keyed by (content table, group). That history
  belongs to one audience. If a table drops its group arm, the viewer
  leaves and rejoins while no join can be logged, and the arm is added
  back, the ledger's last word is the old scope, and a stream after the
  re-acquisition would treat the group as closed and miss its new rows
  (round 1, GPT finding 3). So each activation records an **audience
  epoch** for every content table whose group audience it adds or rebinds
  (another target, group columns or recipient): the first sequence of the
  new audience (`audience_epoch:<table>`). Membership history before the
  epoch is ignored, and the stream reads the groups a principal holds from
  the membership table until a transition is logged under the new
  audience, the existing fallback for a group with no ledger entry. The
  log is not rewritten. This also fixes the same hazard for a single group
  audience whose target changes.

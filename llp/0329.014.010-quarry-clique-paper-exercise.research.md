## Summary

After LLP 0329.014.009 measured toy-scale authoring, the untested cell was
**app-scale spelling**: one evolving backend, dozens of interacting
operations, the kind boundary under product pressure. This exercise took the
Clique reduced-run brief (M1 accounts/profiles, M2 posts + follow graph, M3
messaging core — `benchmarks/briefs/social-messenger.md`) and had two blind
lanes from different model families author the complete backend data plane
in Quarry v0.2 (the one-pager plus the .009 verdicts: auto-deref, bare-name
shape fields, optionals, `enum`, `asset`, a boundary-kinds section),
milestone by milestone against their own evolving schema, cold, one shot per
milestone, sandboxed from this repository. The oracle is the real Coterie
backend (`demos/coterie/snapback/`: 13 tables, 42 ops, ~107 KB), and the
grading key maps its production machinery (hand-maintained counter columns,
`pageKey` cursor encodings, the membership-incarnation split, per-recipient
receipt rows) onto what the notation should absorb.

**Headline.** The grammar's core held at app scale — across ~80 operations
in two lanes: zero unbounded reads, zero page-counts-as-population-facts,
zero clock misuse, kind boundaries intact, schema evolution disciplined.
Coverage matched the oracle almost exactly (codex: 26/27 product
operations, 42 ops vs the oracle's 42) at **≈2× the size economy** (37 KB vs
~79 KB product surface). But the exercise's real result is a **pincer**: the
two lanes attacked the Following feed from opposite sides — codex with a
shared event lane under a viewer-dependent read rule, grok with
fan-out-on-write capped at `last 100` followers — and each landed in exactly
the territory the v1 design's policy families exist to govern (uniform-read
logs / hidden-take on one side; `maintain.rows`, the broadcast projection,
engine fan-out on the other). Grok even wrote the hidden-take ambiguity into
its notes as an assumption, blind. **At app scale, the policy families are
the language**: a one-page grammar without them admits programs the
seventeen-dimension design refuses, or silently caps delivery. The blind
cross-reviews (§2.4) then confirmed real defects in both designs — a
reinvented SQL-NULL trap that refuses ordinary sends, cross-client
intent-clock comparisons on both sides, an undeclared disclosure the
spec's own rule makes checkable, a refusal-code existence oracle — with
roughly half of all confirmed findings tracing to one-pager elisions of
facilities the v1 RFC already has.

## 1. Protocol

- **Fixture:** the Clique reduced run (M1→M2→M3), spec + acceptance sections
  verbatim from the brief; client/UI/grader items framed as context. This is
  NOT a benchmark run (no clock, no client, no published-surface fence — see
  LLP 1000 §9 for why the real instrument cannot run against Quarry).
- **Lanes:** codex gpt-5.6-sol and grok-4.6, xhigh, one fresh CLI invocation
  per milestone with the lane's own prior milestones inlined ("evolve, do
  not restart"), cwd an empty sandbox. Output contract: complete schema
  (changes marked), every program the screens need, a boundary block, and
  notes with `-- INVENTED` declared where the reference lacked a spelling.
- **Spec:** `spikes/quarry-clique-paper/spec/quarry-spec-v0.2.md` — v0.1
  plus the .009 verdicts. Deliberately still a one-pager: what it omits from
  LLP 0329.014.004 (constraint vocabulary, visibility-scan policy, the
  maintain-rows family, retention) is part of the experiment.
- **Grading:** `grading/grading-key.md` (oracle op inventory, real-backend
  machinery diff, per-milestone strain checklist);
  `grading/lane-notes.md` (verdicts); cross-review of each lane's M3 by the
  opposite family in `grading/cross-review/`.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 What held (both lanes, ~80 operations)

Zero unbounded reads — the mandatory-bound slot held everywhere. Zero page
reductions returned as population facts — every UI number is a maintained
`#` read, with `readable:` declared where disclosure crosses visibility
(grok went further: private like rows, public maintained count). Zero
intent-clock misuse. Kind boundaries intact under the brief's KDF bait — no
password tables, no crypto, typing stayed an ephemeral channel, and the
removed-member history question landed correctly in both lanes (codex on
the client custody boundary; grok server-side via a read-rule cutoff —
§2.3). Evolution discipline held (`-- NEW` marking, no restarts). Coverage:
both lanes 26/27 oracle product ops (only a user-directory query missing
from each). Factoring diverged — codex 42 ops, matching the oracle's 42;
grok a leaner 28 (one `createPost` with a kind enum versus six variants).
Size: codex 37.3 KB / 1,426 lines, grok 37.1 KB / 997 lines, versus the
oracle's ~79 KB product surface — **≈2.1× smaller** at app scale (toys
measured 2.9×).

### 2.2 The pincer (the finding)

The Following feed forced each lane past the one-pager:

- **codex**: one shared `followingFeedEvents` lane, READ rule
  `r.actor = viewer or exists follows[viewer, r.actor]`. Elegant — unfollow
  hides history instantly, self-posts free — and **refused by v1 policy**: a
  completeness-sensitive timeline under a non-uniform rule is the
  uniform-read-log / hidden-take defect family (0329.014.004 §2.1-A, the
  ruling-2 rows of §8), with page starvation and a hidden-traffic oracle.
- **grok**: fan-out-on-write into per-follower `home` rows — bounded by
  `last 100`, so the 101st follower never receives the post; grok flagged it
  itself ("unbounded celebrity fan-out … inexpressible") and separately
  wrote: "Assumes unreadable rows are skipped, not slotted, in `last 20`" —
  naming hidden-take as an assumption, blind.

This is the exact pincer that forced `maintain.rows`, the broadcast
projection, and engine fan-out (`propagate`) into the v1 design (dimensions
K/O, §2.2-E) — reproduced independently by two blind families in one
afternoon. Neither lane could reach the paved answer because the one-pager
does not contain it.

### 2.3 M3 under pressure

codex's messaging core is near-production (the cross-review later found
four real defects the grader missed — §2.4): receipt rows minted per
current member at send, keyed by the
membership *incarnation* id (rejoin mints a new id — leavers stay dropped);
the monotonic matrix as an update rule plus recipient-authored batched
`markDelivered`/`markRead`; DM-pair unique sorted key with `match`
idempotency; 2–50 spelled `firstMember + [users] <=48`; the whole admin
policy in row rules, including first-admin bootstrap via
`#memberCount = 0` — an unmarked reinvention of v1's `cap()` constraint.

grok produced a *third* coherent design point: soft-delete tombstone
membership; **watermark receipts** (per-member `lastDeliveredAt`/`lastReadAt`
with monotonic guards) where codex chose per-message receipt rows — the
production oracle contains BOTH mechanisms, and each blind lane found one;
and **server-side pre-removal history** (leave snapshots `#lastSeq` into a
`historyThru` read-rule cutoff), a stronger reading of the removed-member
acceptance item than codex's client-custody boundary — the language turned
a real product fork into one rule line versus one boundary bullet. Its
`#lastSeq + 1` message sequencing is sound only under trace-validated OCC
(which is the system's semantics); its forgeable-forward watermark argument
is the one graded semantic nit. It also surfaced a new gap: no spelling to
**unset an optional column**. Both lanes independently wrote admin policy as
giant rule disjunction matrices — the missing per-field grant vocabulary
(v1's `immutable`/`immutableExcept`) made visible twice.

### 2.4 Cross-review (blind, opposite families)

codex's review of grok's M3 (13 findings; adjudicated in
`grading/cross-review/adjudication.md`: 9 confirmed, 2 runtime-dependent, 1
known, 1 refuted as a prompt-assembly artifact) found two defect classes the
orchestrator's grading missed:

- **The NULL trap, reinvented (CRITICAL, confirmed).** grok's hand-written
  field-unchanged rule matrix requires `next.historyThru = old.historyThru`;
  both are absent on fresh rows, and the spec makes predicates over absence
  *false* — so the authorization rule refuses ordinary DM and group sends.
  The language recreated SQL's `NULL = NULL` semantics and it broke a real
  program. Verdict: absence-equality must be defined deliberately, an
  `unchanged(f)` primitive added, and v1's `immutable`/`immutableExcept`
  restored so field-unchanged matrices never get hand-written.
- **Cross-client intent-time comparison (confirmed).** Recipient sets
  inferred from `joinedAt <= message.at` compare two different clients'
  intent clocks. The `time!intent`/`time!commit` typing guards clock-versus-
  column comparisons but not stored-intent-versus-stored-intent; order-
  bearing semantics need engine-issued sequence/epoch values (codex's
  incarnation-id design is structurally immune).

Also confirmed: an **undeclared disclosure** the spec's own rule makes
checkable (tombstoned ex-members receive `#memberCount` over rows they
cannot read — no `readable:` declared); the `ensureMessage` hidden-vs-absent
**existence oracle** (the corpus's ruling-2 family, committed once at app
scale, in a seed path); and two "racy as declared, sound only under
trace-OCC" findings whose real lesson is that the one-pager never states
the OCC/trace contract at all. The forgeable-forward watermark was found
independently by both the orchestrator and the reviewer (an agreement
datum).

grok's review of codex (15 findings; 10 confirmed, 4 of them missed by the
orchestrator's grading) is symmetric in the most instructive way: the
**LWW activity rewind** (no monotonic guard on chat-list ordering, driven
by cross-client intent clocks — the same clock family codex caught in
grok); a **last-admin-leaves orphaned group** (adminless forever); batch
receipt mutations that **abort wholesale on one stale incarnation id**
(v1's per-row-outcome vocabulary is the elided fix); and a
**refusal-code existence oracle** (`NOT_MEMBER` vs `CONVERSATION_NOT_FOUND`
— the corpus's taint-through-refusal-detail family, dimension L,
reproduced blind). It also surfaced a spec ambiguity promoted to a
verdict: mid-body `match` yield-vs-statement semantics are undefined and
there is no `return`. Three finding families — cross-client clocks,
get-or-create, racy-caps-without-a-stated-OCC-contract — were found by
*both* reviewers against *both* designs. Neither design is defect-free;
codex's is the stronger (grok carries the confirmed-critical NULL-trap
refusal); and roughly half of all confirmed findings trace to one-pager
elisions of facilities LLP 0329.014.004 already has. Both reviewers wrote
adversarial-grade analysis in a notation that did not exist for them two
days earlier — reading fluency is itself a result.

### 2.5 Convergent spec verdicts

From the lanes, independently: restore `text({min, charset, nonBlank})` and
a URL form (v1 has them; the one-pager elided them); a composite
`(time, id)` pagination cursor (the oracle's `pageKey` strings are the
workaround); an optional range bound that doesn't force query duplication;
a defined absence predicate; defined `#agg[empty group]` (absent vs zero);
the constraint vocabulary (`cap`, cross-row) instead of rules-reading-
aggregates; bounded `deleteWhere`/`patchWhere`; a spelling to *unset* an
optional column; a `min` on list arguments; and above all the two policy
families of §2.2. From the cross-review: defined absence-equality plus an
`unchanged(f)` primitive and v1's per-field grants (the NULL trap);
engine-issued sequences/epochs for order-bearing semantics (the
cross-client clock trap); `insertIfAbsent`/get-or-create restored; a
delivery-latency term on channels; one sentence stating the live-query
default; and a paragraph stating the OCC/trace contract, without which
"sound under the runtime" is unstatable.

## 3. Reading the result

1. **The certificate-as-grammar core survives contact with a real app.** The
   properties the toy test established scaled: bounds, completeness typing,
   clock discipline, kind boundaries — across two orders of magnitude more
   surface, under two families that had never seen the language before
   yesterday.
2. **The one-pager is the wrong artifact to author real apps against — by
   measurement, not opinion.** Every serious gap either lane hit is
   something LLP 0329.014.004 already contains and the one-pager elided.
   The v1 design's dimensions are not reference-manual appendix material;
   at app scale they are load-bearing grammar. A v0.3 spec that folds in
   the constraint vocabulary, the visibility-scan policy, and the maintain
   family is the next authoring artifact.
3. **Blind cold lanes are a cheap, repeatable design review.** The exercise
   re-derived, from product pressure alone, three of the v1 design's
   hardest-won rulings (hidden-take, fan-out placement, retention-as-
   custody) and priced the oracle's production hacks (`pageKey`,
   hand-maintained counters) as exactly the things the notation absorbs.
4. **Limits:** paper only — nothing executed; one lane per strategy, so the
   fan-in/fan-out fork is n=1 per side; the grader also wrote the spec
   (cross-reviews partially mitigate); and the brief's acceptance items
   telegraph requirements, so this tests execution, not requirement
   discovery.

## 4. What this gates

Per the sequence in the 0329.014.009 discussion: the notation held at app
scale, so the expensive fork — a Quarry→IR front end plus a repair-loop
authoring experiment — remains live, and its prerequisite is now sharper: a
**v0.3 spec** whose content is the v1 RFC's §2 dimensions rendered in the
notation, not a bigger grammar. The cheap immediate step stays the printer
(IR → Quarry) for `why`/trace/certificate display, which forces the grammar
to be exact and starts the corpus flywheel without betting authoring on it.

## Confidence

High on what-held (mechanical properties over in-tree artifacts). High on
the pincer reading — both lanes' own notes state their halves. Medium-high
on coverage/size (fixture-share estimate is approximate). Medium on
generality: one app family, two lanes, paper only.

   assumed yes), or is that exactly what the constraint vocabulary is for?
3. The feed answer the lanes couldn't reach: is `maintain rows` +
   broadcast projection expressible in one one-pager paragraph, or is the
   feed simply not a paper-spec-sized feature?
4. Does the ≈2× app-scale token economy (vs 2.9× toy) hold after the
   verbose nested shapes get a `fragment`/reuse spelling — and should they?

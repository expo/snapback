## Summary

This document answers two founder questions asked on 2026-08-29, after the
v1.2 design RFC ([LLP 0329.014.004](./0329.014.004-bounded-data-programs-v1-design.rfc.md))
landed:

1. *How does the one-language design change Snapback's chances of being a
   better backend for agents than Supabase and Convex?*
2. *Is it much better for local-first — consistency, completeness, and the
   rest — and what would make it better still?*

It is an assessment by one model family, written from the corpus and the
measured records, not a review of the design and not a commissioning. It
authorizes nothing.

**On the competitive question.** The design changes *which bet* Snapback is
making. As built, Snapback is a worse Convex: the same TypeScript-functions-
plus-live-queries axis, with a thirteen-flavor query surface, a ~212k-line
client, and a dev loop that lost milestones. Under bounded data programs it
becomes a different product with a claim neither competitor can make —
*every program you can write is live, offline-capable, row-authorized,
cost-certified, and locally predictable, and the compiler pastes a rewrite
when it is not.* That claim is agent-shaped for a specific reason: agents
are the population that pays the retrieval tax every session, that is worst
at behavioral debugging of "why didn't my live query update", and that is
best at consuming a compile-time refusal that carries a rewrite. The
assessment is: materially better odds against Convex (§F1–F4), a modest
direct change against Supabase whose moat is Postgres-in-the-weights plus
hosted batteries rather than a query language (§F5), and **none of it counts
until two numbers exist** — a cold agent's first-draft refusal rate, and a
dev loop landed alongside the language (§F6). The RFC's own confidence line
— "low that any of this moves the Clique scoreboard without the dev loop" —
is endorsed.

**On local-first.** This is the axis where the design is *most* clearly
better than today's Snapback, because local-first is exactly where
capability-by-spelling hurts worst: today four retention mechanisms with
different survival rules, cold retention only for public single-table
declarative queries, local prediction only for generated plan mutations, and
two consistency models chosen by how a function was spelled. The design makes
retention a per-table policy, prediction mode a compile-time fact per
transition site, consistency one trace-validated OCC model, and completeness a
typed certificate label — and spike S1 ran the receipt-only client mechanism
7/7 with no per-program special case (§F7–F10). Its limits are real and are
stated as trades, not defects: it is not a replica, `partial` is a real UI
state, OCC rejections happen on reconnect, and `maxOfflineAge` is a hard
fence (§F11).

**Eight candidate improvements** for local-first are proposed in §Candidate
improvements, ranked. The first two apply the design's own principle — a
static certificate with refusal-and-rewrite — to *offline-ness itself*: a
derived **hold set** with an `offline: 'required'` gate that turns `partial`
from a runtime surprise into a `check`-time refusal, and a
**dependency-aware outbox** whose rejection cascade is derived from the local
trace. The rest are mobile product reality (delta resume by range version,
asset bytes under the row's retention lease, a store budget with eviction
priority) and ergonomics (per-field `unheld` in result types, local replay of
token indexes, cancelling a queued intent). None appears in the v0 spec or
the v1.2 RFC; each names the evidence that would settle it.

## Research question and scope

The questions are comparative and forward-looking, so the evidence is of
three kinds and each finding labels which it rests on:

- **Measured:** the Clique 0.1.29 matrix
  (`benchmarks/runs/2026-08-27-clique-published-0129-matrix/record.md`), the
  LLP 0317 correction-cause disposition, Diary 0114, the LLP 0329 surface
  measurements, spike lanes S1–S3 (0329.014 §8, `spikes/bounded-programs/`).
- **Design-stated:** the v0 spec ([LLP 0329.014.000](./0329.014.000-bounded-programs-language-v0.spec.md))
  and the v1.2 RFC, which are Draft and may move.
- **Inferred:** claims about Convex and Supabase architecture, which LLP
  0329.004 already labels inferred rather than measured against any release.

Out of scope: pricing, hosting economics, ecosystem size, and anything about
the hosted targets; the device-authoritative partition regime of dimension P
(vault, E2EE) except where a candidate improvement touches it.

## Findings — the competitive question

### F1 — The scoreboard measures cost, not capability, and the language attacks cost

The honest record (LLP 0329 §Evidence) is: convex 7/8 with two independent
deep runs and holistic 74–79; supabase 7/8, holistic 44–74; pocketbase 7/8;
snapback-explicit 7/8 (one lane, 8 h 46 m, holistic 61); snapback-familiar
2/8. Snapback reaches the same ceiling and pays more for it. LLP 0317's
priority synthesis clusters the correction causes into six bundles, and
bundle 1 — *authority and reactive causality* (O14–O19, O34, O43, O46) —
"consumed the most debugging time and drove semantically costly polling and
public-read fallbacks."

The design addresses bundles 1, 3 (paved mutation/offline patterns), and 4
(generate-time prevention), plus C5 (read models masquerading as mutations),
by construction: one trace is the invalidation predicate, so "why didn't this
update" is answerable from an artifact; reads are reads and system
continuations are `phase: 'system'`; and the certificate-with-rewrite *is*
generate-time prevention. It does not touch bundles 2 (published media
coherence), 5 (template/start-packet closure), or 6 (Familiar parity), which
are the time-to-first-green half of the losses. Confidence: high on the
mapping; the bundles are the corpus's own classification.

### F2 — The language is worth more per session to an agent than to a human

A human builds a mental model over weeks and routes around the capability
matrix. A cold agent pays the whole retrieval tax every session: the
2026-08-21 eval runs reached the correct page in 7, 11, and 12 of 15 tool
calls before doing any work (LLP 0329 F5). Collapsing 31 `define*`
constructors and 13 query flavors into `query`/`mutation` plus a bounded set
of kinds is a per-session saving that compounds for the agent population
specifically. Confidence: high on the measurement, medium on the inference
that a smaller surface yields fewer retrieval calls (the retrieval eval must
be rerun against a guide written for the new surface).

### F3 — Compile-time refusal with rewrite is the agent-favorable trade against Convex

Convex's authoring model — "a query is just code over the database", dynamic
read-set recording as the evidence — has a zero refusal rate by construction,
and agents preferred it for exactly that reason (LLP 0120, LLP 0329.012 F4).
Its structural costs for agents are runtime costs: no genuine offline
(inferred, LLP 0329.004 §trilemma), app-authored optimistic reducers, and
"the function is the authorization" so the read set is not a disclosure
boundary and no trace can ship (0329.014 §1).

The design keeps the Convex-shaped prior on purpose — the panel called
TypeScript control flow with inferred nested result types "the year-one
product metric" (0329.014 §2.1; the B-half of the Clique rubric is 55 of 100
points) — and adds the static certificate. The measured signal that agents
benefit from strictness is the paper waves: writing the designs in the strict
language found bugs in 10 of 14, 13 of 13, and 8 of 8 designs, including a
security finding (RFC §Summary). The diaries already name diagnostic
precision as the thing agents love ("the platform told me exactly what was
wrong every time", Diary 0114). What the design does is make that property
the *whole* mechanism rather than a property of some paths.

The counter-evidence is spike S2 (0329.014 §8): 8/8 realistic first attempts
were refused-with-rewrite, and 66.7% of raw gauntlet code lowered unchanged
or mechanically against a 70% gate (76% of the snippets that were actually
data programs). Roughly a third of an agent's first drafts will bounce. Each
bounce carries a compiling rewrite, so this may be a cheap retry loop rather
than thrash — but it is the single largest year-one product risk and it is
unmeasured with a cold agent. Confidence: medium-high that the trade is
favorable; low until the cold-agent number exists.

### F4 — The design removes the insecure-but-rewarded path by construction

LLP 0310 P18 — a lane set conversations, members, and messages to
`public-read` because `auth: 'public'` was the predicate that unlocked
retention — is the RLS-shaped failure class agents hit on Supabase, reproduced
on Snapback. Under the design, retention is a per-table policy (dimension F),
disclosure is a declared item with a rate (dimensions F, J), and constraints
bind every writer because no write path exists outside the language
(dimension C). There is no capability an agent can obtain by widening
visibility, so the rational-but-insecure move has no payoff. LLP 0329 F2
("the offline design and the security failure are one defect") is resolved
at the substrate. Confidence: high; this follows from the dimensions as
stated.

### F5 — Against Supabase the direct impact is small; the story impact is real

Supabase's moat for agents is not its query language. It is Postgres and SQL
in the training data (the `sol-supabase` lane hit 7/8 with a schema score of
92 — LLP 0329.013 §2.1 calls it "a measured existence proof that agents write
SQL well for reads") plus hosted auth, storage, edge functions, and realtime.
The design's `kinds` (identity, assets, effect outbox, schedule,
`exportStream`) are the right list, but every one must be built and paved;
the language makes the data plane coherent and does not make batteries
appear.

Where the design does change the comparison: security by construction (F4)
against RLS-as-a-second-language; scale-to-zero with a *legible* per-request
cost (the certificate's `maxExamined`) against a bill; and offline, which
Supabase does not have natively and which its RLS makes hard to bolt on
because the server cannot state what a client may hold. Confidence: medium;
the Supabase architecture claims are inferred.

### F6 — Two numbers gate every claim above

1. **The cold-agent first-draft refusal rate.** Rerun the S2 gauntlet after
   composite write keys and the specified standard library land in v1 (RFC
   §9), then have a *cold* agent — not a design family, with only a guide —
   build Clique end to end and count refusals and tool calls to first green.
   Treat the refusal rate as a product gate, not a spec detail.
2. **The dev loop.** One generation identity across schema, server, generated
   client, bundler, and cache — named co-equal by every panelist (0329.014
   point 10) and untouched by the language. Commission it as a parallel
   track, not a follow-on; without it the RFC's "low" confidence on the
   scoreboard is the correct forecast.

Until both exist, the defensible claim is "the design survived thirty-seven
applications on paper and its core mechanism ran in three spikes" — strong
for a design, zero for a scoreboard.

## Findings — local-first

### F7 — Today's local-first contract is the capability matrix at its worst

From `guide/sync/offline-and-retention.md` (reviewed 2026-08-25) and the
corpus:

- **Cold retention** on the JS arm requires a declarative `defineLiveQuery`
  over **one table** with `auth: 'public'` and a plan that reads no
  membership, `authUserId()`, time, or randomness. Protected data takes a
  separate route (`retainable: true` plus an `AuthorizedLease`); owner-scoped
  composites take a third (the LLP 0166 materialization store and hydration
  bridge); background demand a fourth (`registerQuery`). The page itself
  names "two dead-ends the maze invites."
- **Local prediction** exists only where a generated mutation plan exists:
  "generated mutation-plan presence is the offline replayability predicate"
  (LLP 0320). A `defineJsLiveQuery` or JS handler is opaque and predicts
  nothing.
- **Two consistency models by spelling**: HostPlan mutations are
  "unconditional sequences executed inside the commit transaction against a
  client-supplied stale baseline, while JS handlers capture dependencies
  across the read-to-commit window and validate at commit" (LLP 0320), so
  the same application has different race outcomes depending on how each
  function was authored.
- **Completeness** is LLP 0157's per-profile witness (single-table,
  bounded-children, relational composite, snapshot-batch v1/v2), implemented
  on the client — which is why `packages/snapback-client` is the fix-touch
  hotspot at 746 (LLP 0329 F3).
- **Operational edges**: rows persist on a 1 s / 10 s debounce, so a killed
  client can lose them; retained rows keep asset *references* but no
  rendition bytes ("Snapback currently provides no privacy- or
  revocation-aware offline image-byte cache"); no real-process restart
  witness is proven on any carrier.

### F8 — The design's local-first contract, side by side

| | Today | Bounded data programs (0329.014.000 §6; RFC §5, §7) |
|---|---|---|
| **What retains** | Public, single-table, declarative, time-independent; three other routes for everything else | Every admitted program. Retention is a per-table policy: `until-revoked`, `delivered-history`, `purge-on-block`, `all-owned`; the private conversation was wave 1's baseline case |
| **What predicts** | Generated plan mutations only | Every mutation; mode `closed \| conditional \| partial` per transition site is in the certificate at `check` |
| **Consistency** | Two models by spelling | One: trace-validated OCC at commit; intents `predicted → queued → committed \| rejected`; reconnect re-authorizes before outbox replay; intent `now` and `commitTime` are distinct modeled inputs and security windows use the latter (the intent-clock trap, found by seven applications); `serialBy` for per-key continuation order |
| **Completeness** | A client-side matrix per profile | A typed certificate label `page \| capped \| maintained \| all \| replica \| frontier(vector)`; a page reduction is never a population fact unless `maintained`; `countCapped()` carries `atCap`; `hasMore` is receipt-only; under non-uniform rules absence and hidden are indistinguishable and range facts ship only when every examined candidate was readable; local replay is exact while `Reads ⊆ Coverage`, otherwise `partial`, **never invented** |
| **Revocation** | Accumulated across leases, custody, and store rules (LLP 0329 OQ4) | Stated once: reconnect evicts rows held by no range; sign-out destroys the per-principal store key; one store-wide `maxOfflineAge`; per-table `retain({ offlineLease, onDeny: 'purge' })` |
| **Time offline** | Client-clock workarounds | Dimension B: expiries, windows, and seals are schedule-flipped statuses; rules are time-independent; offline reads never depend on the device clock |
| **Client** | ~212k lines implementing the matrix | 7.8 KB gzip TypeScript twin (S3: 75/75 corpus, byte-identical under Node and JavaScriptCore, 3.2 ms cold replay) over a per-principal encrypted range store at one version, a verdict cache, an outbox; eight states |

### F9 — Consistency and completeness get better for the same structural reason

Both are the same defect seen from the offline side. Today the client
*implements* honesty as a capability matrix; the design moves both into
artifacts the compiler emits and the runtime asserts — S1 enforces
`receipt ⊆ trace ⊆ certificate` at runtime. The client cannot lie about
completeness because it cannot compute a fact the receipt does not cover, and
it cannot diverge on consistency because the server's OCC over the
authoritative trace is the only settlement path. That is a structural
improvement, not a tuning one. Confidence: high on the design statement;
medium on the implementation, which has one in-process spike behind it.

### F10 — The measured part

Spike S1 (`spikes/bounded-programs/REPORT.md`, landed `f77958de5` +
`0ca0f2526`) built a second in-process SQLite client store *from a closure
receipt only*, running the same interpreter for local mutations and replay:
an ordinary local `send` is `predicted`; one held-row delete stays complete
from the halo and the second goes `partial` at exactly `thread.messages`; a
first `@mention` is `partial` only at `thread.mentionUser` (a computed-key
site); server-side membership deletion followed by rehydration evicts every
message row. 7/7 tests, no program-name branch or operation-specific opcode.
S1 also recorded ten spec ambiguities, four of which bear on offline (halo
reservation, hidden rows consuming `take`, receipt extents under non-uniform
rules, `(old, next)` predicates); the v0.1 rulings folded them.

### F11 — What it is not, stated as trades

- **Not a replica.** Under LLP 0008 DK-9 the client is "a result cache +
  cursor/epoch state + outbox, never a relational replica", and the spec says
  it "cannot issue a query the model did not name." Exact local replay of
  named programs over delivered rows, not ad-hoc local queries over a synced
  partition as in Zero, Electric, or PowerSync. Deliberate: it keeps hidden
  range extents and cardinality oracles off the device and the client at
  7.8 KB. A product that wants freeform local exploration will feel it.
- **`partial` is a real UI state.** The RFC says its diagnostic "must be
  excellent." An offline `send` whose author join needs `users[viewer]` is
  `partial` unless that row is held; agents will meet this (and §Candidate
  improvements 1 and 5 address it).
- **OCC means rejections on reconnect.** No automatic merge for `occ`
  tables; long-offline edits to the same row lose. `merge: lww | append` and
  the `sequence` CRDT exist per table; conflict-as-evidence is a design
  choice, not a gap.
- **`maxOfflineAge` is a hard fence** (24 h / 7 d / 30 d, ruling pending).
  Past it the store is not trusted. Device-authoritative partitions sit
  outside it and need a second runtime — unmeasured.
- **Zero real-device evidence**, on either side: today's contract has no
  real-process restart witness on any carrier, and the design has S1
  in-process on Node. Implementation defects of the persist-debounce kind are
  orthogonal to the language and can recur.

### F12 — Against the field, on local-first

Convex has no genuine offline (inferred) and app-authored optimistic
reducers; Supabase has none natively, and its RLS makes partial replication
hard by construction. On offline the design is better than both by a wide
margin. Against replica systems it trades expressiveness for
authorization-preserving honesty — for the private-data applications agents
actually build, the right trade, and one to state as a trade.

## Candidate improvements

Ranked. Each names what it adds, which stated gap it closes, how it fits the
design's own principles, its cost, and the evidence that would settle it.
None appears in the v0 spec or the v1.2 RFC (checked 2026-08-29 by grep for
hold/prefetch/resume/rebase/cascade/unheld/asset-retention/local-token/
offline-required). These are proposals for the author, not amendments. Each is worked through at spec level — declarations, certificate lines, states, diagnostics, a Clique example, interactions, security, and settling evidence — in [LLP 0329.014.008](./0329.014.008-local-first-improvements.research.md); the paragraphs here are the summary.

### 1. Hold sets — the prediction closure as a subscription, and `offline: 'required'` as a gate

**What.** The certificate already carries `predictionSites[{ transition,
reads, mode }]`. Three additions: (a) `check` derives, per mutation, the
**hold set** — the shape-keyed rows its prediction sites need that are
reachable from rows the client already holds (`users.byId([viewer])`, the
membership row for every conversation in a held inbox) — and lists it in the
certificate as `holds[]` with a byte cost under a `maxHoldBytes` ceiling; the
client subscribes to the hold set by default. (b) Prediction mode is reported
*relative to the application's declared subscriptions plus hold set*, not
only relative to the mutation's own arguments. (c) A per-mutation or app-wide
declaration `offline: 'required'` makes `check` refuse when the mode over
that hold set would be `partial`, pasting the rewrite ("hold
`users.byId([viewer])`", or "this site is computed-key; declare `predict:
'conditional'`").

**Closes.** F11's first trade: `partial` surfacing at runtime in the common
cases. **Fits.** It is the design's own principle — static certificate,
refusal with rewrite — applied to offline-ness. **Cost.** Hold-set bytes are
bounded by the certificate; computed-key sites (`@mention`) remain `partial`
by nature and the refusal must say so. **Evidence.** Fraction of the S1 Clique
mutations whose `partial` sites become `closed` under a derived hold set,
and the hold set's byte cost for a 50-conversation inbox.

### 2. A dependency-aware outbox — rejection cascade and the re-read closure

**What.** The spec defines local re-execution over `D₀ ⊕ Δ` and settles
intents as `committed | rejected`, but not what happens to an intent `Δ₂`
that read rows `Δ₁` wrote when `Δ₁` is rejected. Derive `dependsOn` edges
from the client's local trace of each intent (`Δ₂` read a row `Δ₁` wrote →
edge); on rejection of `Δ₁`, re-predict dependents over the new baseline in
outbox order, or settle them `rejected: dependency`, deterministically. A
rejection carries the **re-read closure** — the authorized current rows the
server's OCC compared — so re-prediction needs no round trip.

**Closes.** The consistency contract for multi-intent offline sessions, which
today lives in per-arm code (`packages/snapback-client/optimistic-rebase.mjs`)
and in the design is unstated. **Fits.** The trace is already the closure;
this uses the *local* trace for the same purpose. **Cost.** The re-read
closure must obey the receipt rules — `insertIfAbsent`'s `already_acted`
stays opaque, and nothing ships the principal may not read. **Evidence.** A
corpus test: a chain of three intents, reject the first, assert the
dependents' states and that replay is byte-identical between twin and Rust.

### 3. Delta resume by range version

**What.** Reconnect today performs a full resync before replay when the
outbox is non-empty. The receipt holds ranges "at one version"; make
reconnect send `{ readSite, prefix, version }` pairs and receive per-range
diffs, tombstones, and re-verdicts (the re-authorization step), with
`hydrating` scoped to the ranges that changed.

**Closes.** Reconnect cost on mobile — data and time-to-`fresh`. **Fits.**
Read sites and ranges are already the unit of the trace and of subscription
invalidation ("a commit overlapping a traced range reruns and diffs").
**Cost.** A per-range version vector in the store; the tombstone retention
window becomes a server parameter. **Evidence.** Bytes and time on reconnect
for a 50-conversation inbox after one hour offline, delta versus full
rehydration.

### 4. Asset bytes under the row's retention lease

**What.** An asset referenced from a retained row inherits that row's
retention policy: `retain({ assets: 'renditions' | 'none', maxBytes })`
per table; eviction or revocation of the row purges the bytes; sign-out
destroys the key that wraps the byte cache; the purge receipt covers bytes.

**Closes.** The guide's named gap — "no privacy- or revocation-aware
offline image-byte cache" — which is the canonical mobile-offline complaint
for a messaging application. **Fits.** The `assets` kind already has
revocation and purge receipts; dimension F already has per-table retention.
This joins two existing declarations. **Cost.** A byte budget separate from
row bytes; renditions only, never originals. **Evidence.** The Clique media
milestone with the device offline.

### 5. Per-field `unheld` in result types

**What.** S1 reports exact `partial` site ids. Lift that into the result
type on an opt-in basis: a read site declared `{ unheld: 'allow' }` types its
field `T | Unheld`, so a thread renders messages `fresh` with a placeholder
author instead of the whole query going `partial`. A query is `partial` only
when a *control-flow* read is unheld (a branch it could not take).

**Closes.** The UI cost of `partial` (F11). **Fits.** `Visible<T>` is the
precedent for a typed viewer-relative value. **Cost.** Type noise, hence
opt-in per site; the default remains whole-query `partial`. **Evidence.** The
Clique thread with an unheld author row, before and after.

### 6. Local replay of token indexes — local search for free

**What.** `byToken` indexes have declared normalization. If normalization is
pinned in the value model the twin is held to, `scan(table.byToken, [term],
{ take })` over **held** rows is locally replayable, labeled with a new
completeness value `held` — exact over the held set, never a population
claim.

**Closes.** Local search without making the client a replica; generalizes
dimension M's "declared local alternative" (photos, `all-owned`) and takes
the one thing LLP 0329.013 wanted from a SQLite client store. **Cost.** The
tokenizer's size in the twin; a conformance corpus for normalization.
**Evidence.** Twin size delta and a normalization corpus run under Node and
JavaScriptCore.

### 7. A store budget with declared eviction priority

**What.** The certificate has `maxClosureBytes` per program; the device
store needs `maxStoreBytes` with `retain({ priority })` or `pin: true` per
table, LRU by range within priority. Eviction is a ninth transition (`fresh
→ evicted`; the next read is `hydrating`), never a silent `partial`.
*(Corrected in LLP 0329.014.008 §7: no new state is needed — eviction only
shrinks coverage, so the existing exact-or-`partial` rule covers it; what is
added is a `reason` on `partialSites`.)*
`maxOfflineAge` may then differ by priority (pinned tables at the store
fence, others shorter).

**Closes.** An unstated behavior: what the store does under quota. **Cost.**
One more state and one more policy knob. **Evidence.** Clique with 10k
messages across 50 conversations under a 50 MB budget.

### 8. Cancel a queued intent, and `asOf` on every read handle

**What.** A `queued` intent is an outbox pop before dispatch → a `cancelled`
state; a `predicted` intent already in flight is not cancellable and needs an
app-authored compensating mutation, so only `queued` gets the primitive
("unsend while offline"). Separately, every read handle exposes the version
watermark it is at (`asOf`), so `stale` carries an age for "as of three
minutes ago" surfaces.

**Closes.** Two small ergonomic gaps agents will otherwise route around
(reaching for a `delete`, or a client clock). **Cost.** Trivial. **Evidence.**
None needed beyond a corpus case.

### Not proposed, and why

- **A replica with local ad-hoc queries** (Zero/Electric-shaped). Refused:
  it re-opens the client as the complexity sink (LLP 0329 F3) and breaks the
  disclosure boundary — hidden range extents on the device are the
  cardinality oracle the design spent dimension A closing.
- **CRDT-everything.** Table `merge` modes and the host `sequence` kind
  already cover the editor and cart cases; conflict-as-evidence for `occ`
  tables is a choice the wave-4 cash design defended.
- **Automatic conflict merge for `occ` rejections.** Same reason; §2's
  re-read closure gives the application what it needs to decide.

## Confidence

High that the today-versus-design comparison (F7, F8) is accurate to the
guide and the spec as of 2026-08-29; both are quoted. High on F1's mapping
of the language to the LLP 0317 bundles. Medium-high on F3 (the trade is
favorable for agents) — the paper-wave bug counts and the diary quotes
support it; the S2 gauntlet result is the counter-evidence and no cold-agent
run exists. Medium on F5; the Supabase and Convex architecture claims are
inferred, as LLP 0329.004 labels them. High on F9 as a design statement,
medium as an implementation claim (one in-process spike). Medium on the
ranking of the candidate improvements; each is a proposal with its settling
evidence named, and none has been reviewed by a second family.

## Open questions

1. Does the hold set (§1) generalize, or is it Clique-shaped? Booking's
   `reserve` and ledger's `addExpense` should be checked for what their
   `closed` prediction would need held.
2. Is the rejection cascade (§2) deterministic under `serialBy`, and does
   the re-read closure need its own certificate line (`maxRejectionBytes`)?
3. Should `held` (§6) be a completeness label or a scope label beside the

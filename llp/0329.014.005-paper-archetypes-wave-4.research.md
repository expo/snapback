## Summary

Three waves and twenty-nine applications ([LLP 0329.014.001](./0329.014.001-paper-archetypes.research.md)–[.003](./0329.014.003-paper-archetypes-wave-3.research.md))
stretched the bounded-data-programs design along its dimensions and found no
second query class. This wave is different in intent: the three model families
were each asked, blind to one another, for **applications that would break a
premise** of the design — one server authority; deterministic programs over
traced bounded reads; rules the server can evaluate; a static certificate as
the gate; the trace as the offline closure; a store-wide offline fence. Their
lists are retained verbatim as `llp/reviews/0329.014.005-wave-4.breakers.<family>.md`.
They converged on one premise above all — **authority placement** — and then
fanned out; the eight applications below cover each distinct premise once.

Method as before (design by one family with no Snapback vocabulary; write in
the v0.1 language plus the v1.1 constructs by a different family; the §11
rubric), with one addition to the writer's brief: where the design cannot be
written without violating a premise, the report must name the premise and
classify the honest outcome — **new dimension**, **new kind**, **product
boundary**, or **not this system** — rather than fake an admission.

## Pre-registered predictions (orchestrator, 2026-08-29 04:00 PDT, before any design existed)

1. **Offline bearer cash.** As stated, *not this system*: no trace is a closure
   over peer transactions, and immediate acceptance by two disconnected
   recipients with eventual no-double-spend is a cryptographic-cash problem.
   The honest product form is **server-preallocated, disjoint device
   allowances** spent offline and settled on reconnect — which the payments
   report already named. If the design absorbs that form, it is the same
   construct as #3's regional quotas: **allocation is authority placement**.
2. **Password vault.** The fence is the wrong instrument: the unit is "the
   vault, unbounded". Expect a **device-authoritative partition** synced as
   encrypted blobs with per-item vector clocks, conflicts resolved client-side,
   the server never evaluating item rules; the eight-state machine gains an
   `authoritative-local` state. New dimension (**authority placement** with an
   unbounded horizon) or *not this system*.
3. **Multi-region inventory.** **Home authority per key** with away-region
   escrow quotas; `serialBy` and `commitTime` are per home; OCC is evaluated at
   the home. Same dimension as #1 and #2 — the wave's central bet is that
   authority placement is *one* dimension with three faces (device, region,
   allocation).
4. **E2EE messenger.** Membership and metadata stay server-authoritative;
   content is a **content-opaque partition** whose search, mentions, and
   decryptability are client-evaluated. "No second query class" survives by
   definition — a client program over data the server never had is not a
   query over the data plane — but the design must *state* opaque partitions,
   and key rotation atomic with a membership write needs a client-produced
   payload the server stores and validates structurally. New dimension.
5. **PIR contact discovery.** The guarantee **cannot hold** as written: the
   authoritative trace records the examined bucket, and that is the query.
   The honest outcome is a declared **untraced read site** (batched/mixed or
   cryptographic PIR) that retracts "the trace is the closure" for that site —
   a third read-site class beside ranked and geo — or *not this system*. This
   is the cheapest falsifier in the wave: one trace dump.
6. **Zero-trust oracles.** Rule oracles are refused; the honest form is a
   **mirrored posture row with declared freshness** (`fresh(row, maxAge)` as a
   rule input using `receivedAt`), TOCTOU accepted and *labeled* — a product
   boundary with one new rule input (row age), not a rule class.
7. **Spreadsheet.** The first genuine **second evaluation class**: the workbook,
   not a named operation, is the certificate unit; recalculation is
   engine-owned dependency maintenance over a *dynamic* graph (Notion's
   rollups without caps); `INDIRECT` makes dependencies data; volatile
   functions tick on a schedule; iteration needs a fixed cap. Expect a
   `formulas` kind outside the plane, like `sequence`, with visible staleness.
8. **App platform.** New dimension, no language change: **rule ownership and
   composition** — rules conjoin across owners (deny wins), each app's programs
   run under the app's principal with a declared capability set over the
   host's tables, quotas are fairness, and a tenant's rule may read only its
   own tables and host-granted views.

Cross-cutting: this wave is expected to produce the family's first
**"not this system"** verdicts on stated products (1, 5, and possibly 4) and
its first *fourteenth dimension* (authority placement, from 1–3). The
"no second query class" claim is predicted to survive all eight.

## Verdict

Eight applications built to break one premise each, 326 named operations,
about 110 of them classified `premise-violation`. **No premise broke outright,
and one — the static certificate as the gate — survived every attack**: the
cash report says so in as many words, the spreadsheet moved the certificate
up to a workbook-level envelope rather than removing it, PIR added negative
claims to it, and the platform *composed* it across owners. Every other
premise was **amended, not abandoned**, and the amendments are specific enough
to write down (the premise ledger is now [LLP 0329.014.004 §2.2](./0329.014.004-bounded-data-programs-v1-design.rfc.md)):

1. **One server authority is the default of a dimension, not an axiom.**
   Authority placement (**P**) is the fourteenth dimension, as bet — but with
   **two regimes**, not three faces. In the *delegated* regime (a home region,
   a preallocated allowance) a settling authority remains and can still refuse
   later; the schema declares `home(table, cols → regionId)`, one engine per
   region evaluates OCC/trace/certificate at the home, foreign log rows enter
   through an `apply` path that is still the language, and completeness gains
   `atLeast(frontiers)` because `maintained` is exact only at the home. Within
   it, allocation (disjoint quotas, no cross-cut invariant) and replication
   (origin logs, apply, frontiers, a cross-cut invariant with independent
   writers) are distinct lowerings. In the *cryptographic* regime (the device),
   the settling authority is **absent**: `partition(name, { authority:
   'device', horizon: 'unbounded', … })` retracts, for the tables it covers,
   server execution, the trace as OCC and settlement, the closure receipt,
   `predicted`/`rejected`, and the fence, and keeps the language, the
   certificate as a device-evaluator bound, the schema vocabulary, the rules,
   and local live queries. Its cost is a second runtime on the device — the
   interpreter *and* the storage engine — which reverses the "never a native
   module" measurement for that partition. Three families reached the two
   regimes independently (vault, cash, multi-region).
2. **The trace is *a* closure, not *the* closure, and not the first
   disclosure channel.** Cash showed a verified provenance chain is a second
   closure form (the device accepts with no server trace at all). PIR showed
   that `ctx.args` in the envelope and `rows`/`absences` in the result name
   the query before any trace does, so the pre-registered repair — an
   untraced read site — changes nothing; the third read-site class is
   characterized by trace **constancy** (`class: oblivious, examined: |image|,
   trace: (image, epoch)`), executed by an engine kernel outside the
   interpreter. The vault showed device tables never *enter* the eight-state
   machine rather than adding a state to it.
3. **The store-wide fence governs the server plane only.** Custody
   (`retain({ serverDays, replica })`), `maxIntentAge`, and per-authority
   horizons sit outside it; a 45-day server custody under a 7-day fence made
   "complete history" unrecoverable in the messenger, and a 400-day partition
   must never age out queued uploads in the vault.
4. **Rules the server cannot evaluate are evaluated by the device's
   interpreter for the author at a policy head** (`db.evaluate(rule, { as,
   asOf })`), and **remote oracles are never rule inputs**: the honest form is
   commit-fresh mirrored evidence (`fresh(row, { observedAt, receivedAt,
   maxAge, at: 'commit' })`, re-evaluated on OCC retry) with audited, fenced
   delivery — a product boundary the design must state, since agreement *at
   the commit instant* is not this system unless the oracles join the commit
   protocol.
5. **Rule ownership and composition is a fifteenth dimension (Q), and it is
   a language change**: a `ruleset` constraint class (N owned predicates per
   effect, deny-wins, trigger mask, bound on every write), a `plugin` kind run
   under catchable fuel, and a certificate *composed* at install time —
   `host ⊕ Σ plugins`, keyed by `installGeneration`.

**"No second query class", restated honestly.** On the server data plane,
none — in thirty-seven applications. But two evaluation classes now stand
*outside* that plane and the design must say so instead of leaning on a
definition: the `formulas` kind (an engine-owned dynamic dependency graph), and
the programs of a device-authoritative partition, which the E2EE writer
correctly called a second evaluation class — the same language and certificate,
run by a second runtime that is not live, not a twin, and not certified in the
server's sense. The multi-region `apply` site and the platform's plugins are
further evaluation *sites*, still in the language.

**The first "not this system" verdicts** are on stated products, each with
the honest product boundary written beside it: cryptographic double-spend
impossibility for offline cash (detect-and-punish under stated terms, or
preallocated disjoint allowances — a voucher product); remote-oracle agreement
at the commit instant (commit-fresh evidence within a published budget); the
PIR mechanism (an `oblivious` kind, executed outside the interpreter); and
quorum (`majority()` is refused in rules; lease attestations are a host
boundary). The vault is the case the founder must decide: the language
survives on the device plane and five of six premises do not, per table, so
the honest description is *this language, that runtime*.

**Writing the designs in the strict language found bugs in all eight
designs**, including a security finding (a concurrently removed grantor's
grants merge into a vault's policy under the design's own commutation rule), a
quorum-arithmetic failure (a 3+3 partition leaves a global pool nobody's), an
impossible predicate (a zero freshness budget that can never pass), and a
schema that contradicts its own adjudication rule (a unique key on the very
pair both forks must preserve).

## Confidence

High on the classifications and on the premise ledger: every amendment is
forced by a named operation that cannot be written without it, and the
artifacts are verbatim. High that authority placement has two regimes — the
vault, cash, and multi-region writers (three families) each refuted "three
faces" from a different side. Medium on whether `apply` is a kind or the
runtime of P's delegated regime (the writer says either). Medium on certificate
arithmetic throughout: no checker ran on wave-4 programs, `G` is unpublished,
and the spike implements neither device partitions nor plugins nor rulesets —
those claims are paper-only. High on the design bugs; each has a one-line
observation independent of this language.

## Open questions

1. Is a device-authoritative partition *this system* — the same language and
   certificate under a second runtime — or a sibling product that shares the
   language? (The RFC's open question 6.)
2. Is `apply` a kind, or the runtime of dimension P's delegated regime; and
   are `atLeast(frontiers)` and the vault's `frontier(vector)` one label?
3. Is "the trace is independent of the argument" the checkable form of every
   secrecy claim the family has met — sealed refusals, padded execution,
   equal-work declarations, the `oblivious` site?
4. Is `fresh(row, …, at: 'commit')` the one primitive for every external
   authority, or do ordered feeds need a fourth clock beyond intent, commit,
   and ingress? Is peer-attested acceptance time (cash) a rule input or only
   evidence?
5. Where does a composed certificate live — an install-time `check` inside
   the engine — and what does a plugin's `E_STEPS` do to the host's
   transaction: a caught posture, or rollback?
6. The device plane needs the interpreter and the storage engine on the
   device; D-A's measurement (7.8 KB twin vs 109 KB WASM) does not transfer.
   What is the honest size of a device-plane runtime, and does it ship as
   native code?

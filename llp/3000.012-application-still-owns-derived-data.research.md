## Summary

After calibration #16 (`snapback4@0.2.32`: holistic 88, `data_modeling` 82),
Charlie asked three questions. Is there a cross-cutting way to make the system
more elegant? How can the weakest categories improve, `data_modeling` above
all, which is both the lowest and one of the most important? Should Snapback 4
be refined in place or started again? He then asked for this research and
plans. The two plans sit under the LLPs that own their subjects:
[LLP 3000.007.003](./3000.007.003-the-engine-writes-derived-data.rfc.md) for
derived data and [LLP 3000.009.000](./3000.009.000-the-server-half-opt-in.plan.md)
for the server half's packaging.

The finding is about levels, not trends. Across all sixteen Astra-medium
calibrations, the three weakest categories are the same in every window.
They are `queries_and_live` (median 83.5), `scale_and_limits` (84.5) and
`data_modeling` (85.0). In each, the application still builds its own read
models and chooses the numbers that bound them. The strongest categories,
where a responsibility became a declaration on a table, are
`media_assets` (97), `authorization` (95), `writes_and_transactions` (94) and
`offline_sync` (93). Every one of the sixteen `data_modeling` notes charges
application-built read models, author-chosen bounds, or both. All six notes
since 0.2.27 charge application-built read models.

LLP 3000.010 §4 traced the `setup_and_docs` charges to the landings that
closed them, and the search-completeness charges in `queries_and_live` have
not appeared since 0.2.29. The derived-data charges are different: no wave has
removed them.

The programme named the cause on 2026-09-17: "derived data is second-class"
(LLP 3000.007 §21.2). §21.7 then excluded join views and general incremental
view maintenance, because an engine-kept derivation must cost O(1) per write.
Its one accepted remedy is maintained columns. MC1's activation gate stopped
them on 2026-09-19. They are also the only pre-registered `data_modeling`
lever (W14c), void at #15 and #16 for that reason.

This document argues that the exclusion is wider than its reason:

- An aggregate keyed through an immutable reference costs one point read per
  write. Every Clique builder's rule already makes that read to verify a
  copied key.
- The Following feed's fan-out is bounded by declared caps and already runs
  inside application mutations.

The engine can own both without a planner or unbounded deltas.

Adjacent medians one or two points apart are not distinguishable on this
instrument. The gap between the bottom three and the strongest four, eight to
thirteen points, is the same in both windows.

**Interpretation.** The strongest four are features Snapback turned into
declarations on a table:

- **Assets:** `image <=N once`, with bytes served under the home row's rule.
- **Access and delivery:** read rules, `sync to` and `retain delivered-history`.
- **Writes:** client-minted ids with at-most-once application.
- **Lifetime:** `expire at`.

`diagnostics_dev_loop` is the compiler's own strength and belongs to neither
group. The bottom three are where the application still materializes read
models by hand and picks the numbers that bound them. The read models are the
Following feed, rankings, attribution copies and digest staging.

On the 2026-09-05 record, the same builder family scored `data_modeling` at
Supabase 94, Convex 88 and Snapback 2 68.

- Supabase was credited with "relational keys, constraints, joins and
  recipient ledgers directly expressed the product … SQL familiarity and
  composability as strengths."
- Convex was charged because "joins, aggregation policies and paginated
  counter backfills remained explicit application code."

Per §1, this comparison is directional.

### 2.3 What the builder wrote

**Observed.** Calibration #16's backend, in raw lines. `feed.q` is the
generated recipe; the builder counted one of its lines as hand-authored.

| File | Lines | What it holds |
|---|---:|---|
| `schema.q` | 32 | identity, profiles, people search, profile writes |
| `chat.q` | 156 | conversations, DM pairs, members, messages, typing, receipts; eleven operations |
| `media.q` | 51 | reactions and their counts; edit, delete, media send |
| `search.q` | 23 | in-chat and all-chat search; customization |
| `push.q`, `config.q`, `digestConfig.q` | 18 | the notify effect; compiled time windows; the digest period and schedule |
| `social.q` | 100 | posts, likes, five counts, shapes, feed queries, follow, like, repost |
| `insights.q` | 51 | seven insight maintains, the Trending query, a backfill job |
| `digest.q` | 38 | period markers, the per-recipient digest, the scheduled job |
| `feed.q` | 258 | the Following recipe |

Where the platform owns the problem, declarations are compact. `messages`
states all of the following in 25 lines, and `send` takes 12:

- its read and write rules;
- its audience (`sync to members[.conversationId]`);
- its retention on leave and its lifetime (`expire at .expiresAt`);
- its word index;
- three media columns, one of them `once`.

Where the application owns derived data:

- **The feed.** `feed.q` holds 8 tables, 1 maintain, 13 helper functions,
  1 query, and a purge job with its schedule. It also holds 22 size and bound
  constants the application now owns (`cap 35200`, `first 51`, `> 50`,
  `= 865`, …). `createPost`, `repost`, `follow` and `saveProfile` call into it,
  and `posts` and the recipe's `feedPosts` record each original twice.
- **Attribution copies.**
  - `likes` stores `authorId`, `postAt` and `original` so that `receivedLikes`,
    `dailyLikes` and `trend` can group by them.
  - Its insert rule verifies all three against the post, in three clauses.
  - `like` writes the copies.
  - `posts.replyAuthor` does the same for `receivedReplies`.
  - `insights.backfill` fills both for existing rows.
- **Trending.** `trend` groups by the copied `postAt`. The query merges 25
  hour lanes, then fills zero-like posts with a second read.
- **The digest.** Seven bound constants (`first 12001`, `require n <= 12000`,
  `first 1001`, …) keep one recipient inside one chunk.
- **Smaller cases.**
  - `dmPairs` writes two rows per DM, because a table has one `sync` line.
  - `conversations.lastAt` and `lastSender` are kept by hand in two mutations.

From the builder's essay:

> The published feed recipe had real machinery … the result was larger and
> more constrained than the phrase "follow a user" suggests. I had to
> understand its budgets and delivery modes, not merely paste it.

> Trending needed a more deliberate design … Copying a post's timestamp into
> a like also required preserving its trust boundary and backfilling existing
> data. The compiler did not make that migration disappear.

### 2.4 The prior diagnosis, and where its line falls

**Observed.**

- On 2026-09-13, LLP 3000.007 §3.4 named the gap as "the last ten percent of a
  feature — an aggregate, a ranking, a digest, a notification preview, a
  grouped result."
- On 2026-09-17, §21.2 named the cause: "`maintain` … is the only derivation the
  engine owns. Every other derived fact … is materialised by hand into an
  ordinary principal-written table."
- §21.2 then excluded the general remedy: "General incremental view
  maintenance is out: a derivation the engine keeps in the writing transaction
  must have an O(1) per-write delta, and a fan-out belongs to a read (P1)."
- §21.7: "No join views and no general incremental view maintenance."
- The maintains card: "Author attribution remains a validated source field,
  with no automatic join."
- §21.3 P5 gives the frame this document uses: "A table has one writer class,
  and the compiler knows which. Principals through rules; the engine through a
  declaration; the system through a job."

**Interpretation.** The stated reason is right. The exclusion is wider than
the reason, for three reasons:

1. **An immutable reference is an O(1) join.** Take an aggregate grouped
   through a reference whose target column is `immutable`, such as
   `count likes by postId.authorId`. It needs one point read per contribution
   to resolve its key, and the key can never move afterward. That is the O(1)
   delta §21.2 asks for. It is also the same point read the builder's insert
   rule makes today to verify its copy. "No join" excludes this case;
   "O(1) per write" does not.
2. **The feed's fan-out on write is already bounded.** It is bounded by
   declared constants: at most 50 pushed followers before promotion, a
   107-write promotion allowance, and an 865-event backfill (§20.3). It runs in
   application mutations under the same meter. So the O(1) rule did not keep
   fan-out out of the write path. It kept it out of the engine, and it now
   lives in 258 lines of application code. The generated bridges of
   [LLP 3000.011.002.000](./3000.011.002.000-simpler-feed-integration.rfc.md)
   removed 8 of the recipe's 36 handwritten wiring lines.
3. **"A fan-out belongs to a read" was measured and lost.** Merge-on-read
   Following ran at 2.5–3.1× on metered requests and 7.3–9.0× on bare clients
   at 500 versus 5 follows (§21.3 P1), against the brief's 3× degradation
   check. The recipe stayed paved for that reason.

So the engine's writer class has one declaration shape: `count` or `sum` by
the source's own columns. Every derivation that does not fit it falls to the
principal class, which is why rules verify copies and why recipes exist.

### 2.5 Each derived form is built as its own subsystem

**Observed.**

- **Maintained columns took five blind review rounds.** §21.3 records that
  "the 'small delta' was not small — the reservation, the touched-table set,
  write results, adoption and activation each needed a rule of their own."
- **MC1's gate 5 failed** at milestone zero on 2026-09-19. Adding one integer
  field grew a pre-existing stream page from 16,744,164 B to 16,804,164 B,
  which is 26,948 B over the unchanged 16,777,216 B ceiling. MC0's
  adopt-before-shrink recovery worked. The lane's stop rule left the gate to
  the lead's ruling, where it remains
  ([record](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-19-snapback4-column-activation/README.md)).
- **The maintains card spends a paragraph on composition.** It explains how an
  aggregate composes with horizons, retention, prediction, expiry and
  disclosure ("An inherited aggregate has no horizon and remains until-revoked
  even when its source retains delivered-history …").

**Interpretation.** Four kinds of derived state each carry their own answers
for adoption, activation, prediction, horizons and disclosure: sync
partitions, maintains, maintained columns, and the application's feed tables.
So the cost of the next derived form is its interaction with each of them.
That cost is architectural and separate from any one form's semantics. It is
the second half of LLP 3000.007.003: derived forms should share one
substrate. Otherwise the engine stays expensive to extend at exactly the point
where builders need it extended.

### 2.6 The package carries the server half on the default path

**Observed.**

- **Diagnostic codes.** By prefix, 43 of 151 codes belong to families
  introduced for LLP 3000.009's server half: `E_ACTION` 12, `E_ROUTE` 10,
  `E_OIDC` 10, `E_STEP` 5, `E_SERVER` 4 and `E_SQL` 2. That is a prefix count,
  not an ownership census (LLP 3000.010 §7).
- **Documents and cards.** `SERVER.md` is 46,564 B. The `actions` and `routes`
  cards are 18,579 B and 6,818 B.
- **The core cards carry the server half.**
  - The language card's first sentence lists routes, and its single compiled
    program declares two actions and a route.
  - The app card's first line lists actions.
  - The services card begins with the service seats that Clique's effects
    need (LLP 3000.010 §7), then continues with action-bound identity-session
    minting (`x-snapback-action-lease`, `binding`, `replaceLostDelivery`).
- **What calibration #16's builder did.** It opened the services and effects
  cards for its push seam and wrote no worker. It imported neither
  `snapback4/worker` nor `snapback4/server`, and its backend uses no
  server-half form.

**Interpretation.** A local-first application's builder reads the server half
in order to use the core. Whether that costs score is not established, and
the record points both ways:

- The 0.2.16 step happened while the guide grew 31% (LLP 3000.010 §3).
- W14b's pre-registered README reduction moved `setup_and_docs` as predicted
  at #15 (LLP 3000.007 §20.24).

LLP 3000.010 §8.4 calls packaging "a product question, not a score question".
Its §7 keeps two constraints: service credentials are on Clique's path, and
integration is what the product wins on (LLP 3000.007 §3.4).
[LLP 3000.009.000](./3000.009.000-the-server-half-opt-in.plan.md) answers the
product question within both constraints and claims no score effect.

## 3. Refine in place, or start again

**Interpretation.** Keep the engine, and change what the programme does with
it.

- **The engine is what works.** The strongest categories come from the
  substrates: rules and partitions, assets, the outbox, expiry. The
  correctness landings since 0.2.16 (the horizon P1, the merge-position P1,
  retained history, the sign-out and revocation races) are knowledge a rewrite
  would have to learn again. LLP 3000.010 §7 keeps refusing a revert to 0.2.16
  on the same grounds.
- **More waves of the same kind are not a plan.** The waves since the 0.2.16
  step have not moved the instrument (LLP 3000.010 §5). The weakest
  categories' charge list has had one lever, W14c, and it is stalled.
- **The test for a rewrite is LLP 3000.007.003's substrate requirement.**
  Suppose maintains, maintained columns and timelines cannot share one
  adoption, activation and prediction path without restructuring the core.
  That would be the evidence for a refined successor, with the simulator's
  invariants and the test suites as its specification. Until then, no
  rewrite.

## Confidence

- **0.9** that the level split (§2.1) and the classification (§2.2) are right.
  The ordering is the same in both windows, and the classification was done
  by reading.
- **0.8** that grouping through an immutable reference meets §21.2's own safety
  argument. The engine owner's review of dangling references, rebuilds and
  device prediction may add conditions (LLP 3000.007.003 §3.2).
- **0.6** that removing the derived-data charges would raise `data_modeling`
  and `queries_and_live` by a step this instrument can see. Quarry's learning
  cost is also charged in every run, and only one step has been observed in
  sixteen runs.
- No score claim is made for §2.6.

## Non-claims

- No trend is claimed in either direction. The medians are levels at N = 1 per
  version.
- The 2026-09-05 comparison was scored side by side and is directional.
- Line counts are raw physical lines from the retained lane.
- Nothing is accepted, commissioned or built. No ruling of LLP 3000.007 or
  3000.009 changes here; LLP 3000.007.003 asks for the one it needs.

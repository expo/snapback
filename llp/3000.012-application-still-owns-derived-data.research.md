## Summary

After calibration #16 (`snapback4@0.2.32`: holistic 88, `data_modeling` 82),
Charlie asked three questions:

- Is there a cross-cutting way to make the system more elegant?
- How can the weakest categories improve, `data_modeling` above all?
- Should Snapback 4 be refined in place or started again?

He then asked for this research and plans. The plans sit under the LLPs that
own their subjects:
[LLP 3000.007.003](./3000.007.003-the-engine-writes-derived-data.rfc.md) for
derived data, and
[LLP 3000.009.000](./3000.009.000-the-server-half-opt-in.plan.md) for the
server half's packaging.

**The robust observation is about levels, not trends.** Across all sixteen
Astra-medium calibrations, the three weakest categories are the same in every
later-start window of at least four runs, and in every leave-one-out
population:

| Category | Median |
|---|---:|
| `queries_and_live` | 83.5 |
| `scale_and_limits` | 84.5 |
| `data_modeling` | 85.0 |

The strongest are features that became declarations on a table:

| Category | Median |
|---|---:|
| `media_assets` | 97 |
| `authorization` | 95 |
| `writes_and_transactions` | 94 |
| `offline_sync` | 93 |

**The recurring cost is derived data the application maintains.** Every one of
the sixteen `data_modeling` notes charges a stored read model the application
maintains, a capacity bound the author chose, or both, and all six since 0.2.27
charge application-maintained read models. That is a substantial recurring
authoring cost, and the notes return to it run after run. It is not a
demonstrated single cause of all three low categories.

- Quarry's learning cost is charged in all sixteen notes as well.
- `scale_and_limits` also charges search completeness, broad replication,
  unmeasured acquisition and the digest's heap limit.

**No landing has targeted most of that cost.** The search-completeness charges
in `queries_and_live` stopped after 0.2.29, following the programme's landings
against them. The one pre-registered `data_modeling` lever, W14c (maintained
columns), targets rankings only, and it has been void for two calibrations
because MC1 is stopped at its activation gate. The feed machinery and the
attribution copies have no lever.

LLP 3000.007 §21.2 named the cause on 2026-09-17: "derived data is
second-class." §21.7 then excluded join views and general incremental view
maintenance on an O(1)-per-write argument. This document argues that the
exclusion is wider than its reason in two admitted cases:

- attribution recorded through an immutable reference to a public target;
- the Following recipe's capped fan-out, which already runs inside application
  mutations.

LLP 3000.007.003 turns that argument into a proposal. Its revision 3 answers
two rounds of review, and its questions were decided under the author's
delegation (LLP 3000.007 rulings 45–51). F2 proceeds to implementation. F3
gets one more focused review first.

**Observed.** Calibration #16's backend, in raw physical lines. `feed.q` is the
generated recipe; the builder counted one of its lines as hand-authored.

| File | Lines | What it holds |
|---|---:|---|
| `schema.q` | 32 | identity, profiles, people search, profile writes |
| `chat.q` | 156 | conversations, DM pairs, members, messages, typing, receipts; 15 operations (6 queries, 9 mutations) |
| `media.q` | 51 | reactions and their counts; edit, delete, media send |
| `search.q` | 23 | in-chat and all-chat search; customization |
| `push.q`, `config.q`, `digestConfig.q` | 18 | the notify effect; compiled time windows; the digest period and schedule |
| `social.q` | 100 | posts, likes, five counts, shapes, feed queries, follow, like, repost |
| `insights.q` | 51 | seven insight maintains, the Trending query, a backfill job |
| `digest.q` | 38 | period markers, the per-recipient digest, the scheduled job |
| `feed.q` | 258 | the Following recipe |

**Where the platform owns the problem, declarations are compact.** `messages`
takes 23 lines (`chat.q:46–68`) to state all of the following. `send` takes 13
lines, including its declaration.

- its read and write rules;
- its audience (`sync to members[.conversationId]`);
- its retention on leave and its lifetime (`expire at .expiresAt`);
- its word index;
- three media columns, one of them `once`.

**Where the application owns derived data:**

- **The feed.** `feed.q` holds 8 tables, 1 maintain, 13 helper functions,
  1 query, and a purge job with its schedule. It contains 22 occurrences of
  size and bound literals (`cap 35200`, `first 51`, `> 50`, `= 865`, …).
  `createPost`, `repost`, `follow` and `saveProfile` call into it, and `posts`
  and the recipe's `feedPosts` record each original twice.
- **Attribution copies.**
  - `likes` stores `authorId`, `postAt` and `original` so that `receivedLikes`,
    `dailyLikes` and `trend` can group by them. `like` writes all three, and
    the insert rule verifies them against the post in three clauses.
  - `posts.replyAuthor` is copied for `receivedReplies`; the posts insert rule
    does not verify it.
  - `insights.backfill` fills `replyAuthor`, `postAt` and `original` for
    existing rows.
- **Trending.** `trend` groups by the copied `postAt`. The query merges 25 hour
  lanes, then fills zero-like posts with a second read.
- **The digest.** Seven occurrences of bound literals (`first 12001`,
  `require n <= 12000`, `first 1001`, …) keep one recipient inside one chunk.
- **Smaller cases.**
  - `dmPairs` writes two rows per DM, because a table has one `sync` line.
  - `conversations.lastAt` and `lastSender` are kept by hand in two mutations.

From the builder's essay:

> The published feed recipe had real machinery … the result was larger and
> more constrained than the phrase "follow a user" suggests. I had to
> understand its budgets and delivery modes, not merely paste it.

> Trending needed a more deliberate design … Copying a post's timestamp into
> a like also required preserving its trust boundary and backfilling existing
> data. The compiler did not make that migration disappear.

### 2.4 The prior diagnosis, and where its line falls

**Observed.**

- On 2026-09-13, LLP 3000.007 §3.4 named the gap as "the last ten percent of a
  feature — an aggregate, a ranking, a digest, a notification preview, a
  grouped result."
- On 2026-09-17, §21.2 named the cause: "`maintain` … is the only derivation the
  engine owns. Every other derived fact … is materialised by hand into an
  ordinary principal-written table."
- §21.2 then excluded the general remedy: "General incremental view
  maintenance is out: a derivation the engine keeps in the writing transaction
  must have an O(1) per-write delta, and a fan-out belongs to a read (P1)."
- §21.7: "No join views and no general incremental view maintenance."
- The maintains card: "Author attribution remains a validated source field,
  with no automatic join."
- §21.3 P5 gives the frame: "A table has one writer class, and the compiler
  knows which. Principals through rules; the engine through a declaration; the
  system through a job (rules `deny`)."

**Interpretation.** The stated reason is right. The exclusion is wider than
the reason, in two cases.

1. **Recorded attribution through an immutable reference to a public
   target.** An aggregate such as `count likes by postId.authorId` needs one
   point read per contribution to resolve its key.
   - Revision 1 of LLP 3000.007.003 stopped there, and the review showed that
     is not enough. A deleted and recreated target makes incremental
     maintenance and rebuild disagree.
   - The fix is to record the resolved fact on the source row at contribution
     time, in a field only the engine writes. That is what the application
     does by hand today, minus the forgeability, the verifying rule and the
     backfill.
   - The work is still one point read per write.
2. **The feed's fan-out on write is already bounded by constants.**
   - The bounds: at most 50 pushed followers before promotion, a 159-write
     promotion allowance (three writes per pull route), and an 865-event
     backfill (the feeds card).
   - It runs in application mutations under the same meter. The O(1) rule did
     not keep fan-out out of the write path; it kept it out of the engine.
   - The generated bridges of
     [LLP 3000.011.002.000](./3000.011.002.000-simpler-feed-integration.rfc.md)
     removed 8 of the recipe's 36 handwritten wiring lines.
   - The proposal moves the rest, with the recipe's exact contract stated
     rather than implied: history limited to 865 events, permanent promotion,
     capacity refusals, and tombstone-then-purge deletion.

"A fan-out belongs to a read" was measured. Merge-on-read Following ran at
2.5–3.1× on metered requests and 7.3–9.0× on bare clients at 500 versus 5
follows (§21.3 P1), against the brief's 3× check.

### 2.5 Derived forms share a tail, not a lifecycle

**Observed.**

- **Maintained columns took five blind review rounds.** §21.3 records that
  "the 'small delta' was not small — the reservation, the touched-table set,
  write results, adoption and activation each needed a rule of their own."
- **MC1's gate 5 failed** at milestone zero on 2026-09-19. Adding one integer
  field grew a pre-existing stream page from 16,744,164 B to 16,804,164 B,
  which is 26,948 B over the unchanged 16,777,216 B ceiling. The
  oversized page was reconstructed from two successful half-pages; it was
  never sent. MC0's adopt-before-shrink recovery worked. The record keeps
  recoverability distinct from the gate's no-oversize clause, and the lane's
  stop rule left the gate to the lead's ruling, where it remains
  ([record](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-19-snapback4-column-activation/README.md)).
- **Derived writes share the row-change tail** (`Origin::Maintained`). That tail
  deliberately skips downstream maintains and asset placement, and the
  engine-owned-table refusal recognizes maintain targets specifically
  (`engine/write.rs:301`, `:497`).

**Interpretation.** Derived forms should share an executor, a transaction
boundary, a catalog and a sync protocol. Their lifecycle policies are allowed
to differ: activation, rebuild, device behavior, provenance and internal
dependencies. F1 already chose a different device and activation policy from
ordinary maintains. Needing another policy hook is not evidence that the core
needs restructuring (LLP 3000.007.003 §4).

### 2.6 The package carries the server half on the default path

**Observed.**

- **Diagnostic codes.** By prefix, 32 of 151 codes belong to families specific
  to LLP 3000.009's server half: `E_ACTION` 12, `E_ROUTE` 10 and `E_OIDC` 10.
  - `E_STEP`'s 5 codes and `E_SERVER`'s 4 serve actions and effects alike. An
    effect-only handler can raise `E_SERVER_CONFIG` or `E_SERVER_HANDLERS`.
  - `E_SQL`'s 2 codes concern the developer `sql` inspection verb, which
    predates LLP 3000.009.

  These are prefix counts, not an ownership census (LLP 3000.010 §7).
- **Documents and cards.** `SERVER.md` is 46,564 B. The `actions` and `routes`
  cards are 18,579 B and 6,818 B.
- **The core cards carry server-half forms.**
  - The language card's first sentence names routes, and its compiled program
    declares two actions and two routes.
  - The app card's opening paragraph lists actions.
  - The services card begins with the service seats that Clique's effects need
    (LLP 3000.010 §7), then covers action-bound identity-session minting and
    OIDC.
- **Effects share action machinery publicly.** Their generated `EffectContext`,
  secrets, deadlines, `ctx.step` and parked outcomes run through
  `snapback4/server`.
- **What calibration #16's builder did.** It opened the services and effects
  cards for its push seam and wrote no worker. It made no authored imports of
  `snapback4/worker` or `snapback4/server`, although its generated
  `snapback/generated/server.ts` imports the latter. It used no action or route.

**Interpretation.** A local-first application's builder reads the server half
in order to use the core. Whether that costs score is not established, and the
record points both ways:

- The 0.2.16 step happened while the guide grew 31% (LLP 3000.010 §3).
- W14b's pre-registered README reduction moved `setup_and_docs` as predicted
  at #15 (LLP 3000.007 §20.24).

LLP 3000.010 §8.4 calls packaging "a product question, not a score question".
Its §7 keeps two constraints: service credentials are on Clique's path, and
integration is what the product wins on (LLP 3000.007 §3.4). LLP 3000.009.000
answers the product question within both constraints, gates declaration forms
rather than the shared work machinery, and claims no score effect.

## 3. Refine in place, or start again

**Interpretation.** Keep the engine, and do the work in Snapback 4.

- **The engine is what works.** The strongest categories come from the
  substrates: rules and partitions, assets, the outbox, expiry. The
  correctness landings since 0.2.16 (the horizon P1, the merge-position P1,
  retained history, the sign-out and revocation races) are knowledge a rewrite
  would have to learn again. LLP 3000.010 §7 keeps refusing a revert to 0.2.16
  on the same grounds.
- **These proposals do not need a fork.** They change who owns derived data and
  how the surface is packaged, and they rely on Snapback 4's interpreter,
  rules, partitions and accumulated correctness work. The Astra max review
  makes the same point: a fenced successor would be useful as a controlled
  experiment for a genuinely different storage or lifecycle bet, and these
  moves alone do not justify permanent duplication.
- **Snapback 4 0.2.32 stays the control.** It is an immutable published pin, so
  a later 0.3 carrying these moves can be calibrated against it on the frozen
  instrument.
- **What the waves have and have not shown.** Waves since the 0.2.16 step have
  produced no robust aggregate movement, and one pre-registered confirmation
  (W14b). The weakest categories' read-model charges have had one lever,
  W14c, and it is stalled.

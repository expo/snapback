## 1. What the builders met

In wave 1, 100 agents each built one app on `snapback4`. Ten lanes (008, 011,
012, 018, 034, 045, 061, 092, 097, 099) lost 79 minutes at `E_SYNC`, and four
voted it "fix first". Three of them (045, 061, 092) gave up offline reads and
prediction for the most ordinary sharing shapes. Each reported shape is
reproduced with a minimal schema on `96ac00cc61`:

| shape (lanes) | diagnostic before | cause | after |

| two parties, `.customer = viewer or .seller = viewer` (061, 092) | same; the readers-table rewrite omitted the insert rule that the next check then refused | a union of two column audiences | still refused; the rewrite is a compiling parties table spelled against the user's columns (§2.1) |

## 2. Diagnostics and recipes

### 2.1 The union refusal prints the recipe for its shape

`audience_split` (`bind/schema.rs`) classifies the read rule's disjuncts
after service grants are removed and self-witnesses absorbed (§3.1):

- **all `.col = viewer`** (two parties): a `{table}Parties` table with
  `item`, `party`, `unique`/`immutable item, party`, `read <- exists
  {table}Parties[.item, viewer]`, `insert <- created {table}[.item]`, `sync`;
  here `read <- exists {table}Parties[.id, viewer]`, `sync`, and one `after
  insert` per party column.
- **owner columns plus one group probe**: drop the owner disjunct, give the
  owner a row of the group table (`after insert <- exists shares[plant = .id,
  principal = .owner]`), and guard the group table's insert with `created
  {table}[.col] or exists shares[.col, viewer]`.
- **owner columns plus a row-independent probe** (a role): one table per
  audience, with mutations writing across (the owner-plus-staff recipe),
  saying that sync does not deliver this union (§4).
- otherwise the readers table, now including the insert rule it needs.

Every arm keeps "or replace the sync line with `online only`" and the
message keeps "do not make private rows public to get past this". The two
rewrites that print schema are compiled after being applied by hand in
`snapback4-lang/tests/sync_audiences.rs`.

### 2.2 The grant refusal sits at the insert rule and prints the conjunction

The core check (`validate_sync`) still raises "`members` grants the private
audience of `boards`: every insert rule branch needs an independent grant
probe" per synced table; its message now spells the probe
(`exists members[.board, viewer]`). The binder gathers these per audience
table (`audience_diagnostics::Grants`) and emits **one** `E_SYNC` at that
table's insert rule (or update rule for retargeting), naming every synced
table it governs and the branches that prove nothing. The rewrite is the
whole repaired rule from `core::schema::audience_grant_repair`: guarded
branches as written, `exists members[.group, viewer]` conjoined to every
other branch, and, when no branch can admit a new group's first member, the
`or created group[.col]` branch to add. The rule itself is unchanged: an
owner who is not a member must not grant membership (LLP 3000 §7,
2026-09-13), so ownership is never read as a grant.

### 2.3 Named window indexes and audience columns

A named index used as a horizon must lead with the audience columns. On an
inline audience the refusal now prints both repairs against the table: the
column form (`last 100 by .at, .id`, which plans the index with the group
first) or the declaration (`by byAt: team, at, id`). On a read-through
audience the group is a captured parent column (`$standup.team`) that no
declaration can name, so the planner builds the window index as the
captured group followed by the named index's columns (and `id`), exactly as
the column form does. The named index therefore orders the window inside
each group, in its own column order; the sync and sharing cards say so,
and the sharing card uses `last N by .at` for a newest-first window.

### 2.4 The sharing card

A new card, `guide sharing` (`snapback4-lang/src/guide/sharing.rs`), holds
the recipes: an org roster (§3.2), a team the owner manages with a child
carrying the group key inline and checked against its parent (`.team =
.task.team`) beside a one-hop child (`.task.team`), two parties, and an owner
plus staff. Every block compiles clean (no diagnostics, no notes) under
`tests/guide.rs`. They live on their own card because the sync card's length
is budgeted (`openings_fit_without_growing_the_eight_reference_cards`); the
sync card, the `E_SYNC` explanation and every union rewrite point to it. The
published cards name no LLP, so the rewrite says why a union is refused in
words.

## 3. Admitted forms

### 3.1 A row is its own witness

`.principal = viewer or exists members[.board, viewer]` on `members` itself
reads exactly the same rows as the probe alone: a live row whose principal is
the viewer is a members row with that group and principal, so it witnesses the
probe. `RuleExpr::sync_audience_rule(table, definition)` removes service
grants (as `device_audience_rule` does) and then every `.col = viewer`
disjunct that `engine::rules::self_probe_absorbs` (LLP 3000.007 §16, already
used for completeness) absorbs into a same-table probe on a total unique key
whose other components are the row's own columns. `validate_sync` and
`audience_binding` use it; the binder's `intent_sync::audience_rule` mirrors
it for planning and inference, and the core remains the authority. The stored
read rule is not rewritten: the server evaluates it as written, and the bound
`Sync` equals the one the probe alone produces (tested). A literal key term,
another table's probe, a non-unique or optional key absorbs nothing.

### 3.2 A roster with no group columns

`read <- exists staff[viewer]` over `unique principal` was already inferred
as `Audience::Target { prefix: [] }`: one group, the whole roster. The
partition log handled the empty group (`group_of` is `[]`, one group
partition, `groups_now` through the principal index, revocation as a `scope`
of that group); only `grant_guard` refused, because its probe arm required a
non-empty column list. With no group columns, a probe binding the recipient to
`viewer` proves the caller already holds the one grant, so it now guards. A
self-join, `allow`, or any branch without the probe is still refused. The
first member comes from fixtures, `/seed`, or a job writing as the system.
`Sync::line` spells this audience as the inferred `sync`, since `sync to
staff[]` is not Quarry.

`snapback4-sim/tests/roster.rs` checks it exhaustively in the style of
`exhaustive.rs`: every sequence of up to three writes (add, self-join,
remove, post, unpost) over three principals, 142 nodes, against a model
written from the schema text. At every node it checks write authority, each
principal's snapshot, streams from every watermark at page sizes 1 and 1000
(no image the viewer could not read; same rows at both sizes), and that every
earlier device converges to exactly the readable rows. A deliberately wrong
model (one extra reader) fails 321 checks.

## 4. Union audiences: assessment

The ask is `sync to .owner` **and** `sync to members[.group]` for one table:
every device holds every row its viewer may read through either disjunct.
This session did not implement it. The reasons are specific:

1. **Disclosure is the easy half.** Partitions are per row; `partitions()`
   could return one per arm, and the stream re-checks each `put` against the
   whole read rule at serve time, so no non-reader receives an image.
2. **Under-holding is the hard half, and it is a correctness bug, not a
   disclosure.** The device keys rows by `(table, id)` with no per-partition
   count. A `del` from any partition deletes the row
   (`snapback4-device/src/device/deferred.rs`), and a group `scope` deletes
   every row of the group (`device.rs`). A row that leaves one arm while
   another still grants it (owner changes but the viewer stays a member; the
   viewer leaves the group but owns the row) would vanish from a device whose
   pages then claim `complete`. Two fixes, both needed for the group arm:
   - generalize the field-owner rule (`partition.rs`, a `del` is suppressed
     while the recipient still reads the row) to every union table, checked
     against the current image at stream time, so a stale `del` never removes
     a row granted by another arm;
   - make a group `scope` on a union table either re-put, in the same page,
     the group's rows the viewer still reads through another arm (a group
     scan at stream time, bounded by the horizon), or teach both device
     stores to keep rows another held arm grants (the column arm is local:
     `row.owner = me`; a group arm needs the device's held groups per arm).
     Either is a protocol change on the web and native device.
3. **Horizons and coverage are per arm.** Each arm needs its own window
   index leading with its own audience columns, its own acquisition
   boundaries (already keyed by index), and per-site completeness: a page is
   device-complete only through the arm whose audience its pins equal, as LLP
   3000.036 §1 B1 lays out for the narrowed form. Aggregates over a union
   source must be refused or marked partial (`maintain_subset_unknown`).
4. **Every audience match site changes.** About twenty `match` arms on
   `Audience` (partition, revocation, read authorization, through-reads,
   row views, maintained keys, search plans, ephemeral and column checks)
   assume one audience per table.
5. **It needs the review LLP 3000 decision 4 reserves.** Partition inversion
   is one of the two disclosure-sensitive mechanisms that take a blind
   two-family review before landing.

**Recommendation.** Keep union on NOT-DOING for now. Build it as option C of
LLP 3000.036 §1 in this order: (a) LLP 3000.036 §1 B1 (an explicit `sync to`
naming one whole disjunct, the rest server-class), which needs no partition
change and gives the owner offline in owner-plus-staff and each party offline
for its own disjunct; (b) server-side `del` suppression for union tables; (c)
the scope re-put; (d) per-arm horizons and per-site coverage. Gate (b)–(d) on
an extension of `exhaustive.rs` to a two-arm world (an owner column plus a
membership group, two viewers, two groups) whose model reads through either
arm, checking exact convergence (no over- and no under-hold) after owner
moves, group moves, leaves with owned rows, and rejoins. The roster world in
`roster.rs` is the template. The two-arm shape builders hit most often, an
owner column plus a global staff roster, becomes expressible once (b)–(d)
exist, because §3.2 already makes the roster a syncable audience.

Until then the recipes in §2.1 keep every reader offline for two parties and
owner-plus-shares; owner-plus-staff gets offline for both sides only with one
table per audience.

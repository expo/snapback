# LLP 1025.001: Timers drain before chunks — one clock and a due-timer barrier

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Implemented (2026-09-12)
**Systems:** Snapback 2 owner writer loop (`writer_jobs.rs`, `writer_timers.rs`), job driver semantics (LLP 1016 D3), packaged guide (`job.md`, `schedule.md`)
**Author:** Claude Fable 5.1, for Charlie Cheever; implementation/refinement by Codex for Charlie Cheever
**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Revised:** 2026-09-12 (implemented after session-delegated review; exact-64 drain and qualified expiry guarantee)
**Related:** [LLP 1025](./1025-digest-final-emission-at-hub-scale.research.md) F6, [LLP 1025.000](./1025.000-recipient-level-period-custody.plan.md) §5, [LLP 1016](./1016-administrative-plane.rfc.md) D3 and D5, LLP 1002 §5

## Summary

A job chunk runs only after the due-timer index is empty at the turn's pinned
clock, and the chunk uses that same clock. A timer backlog therefore delays
jobs across bounded background turns. This closes the backlog and within-turn
clock gaps without changing any ceiling, firing limit, transaction, queue
order, or schedule syntax.

This is a **timer-settlement guarantee**, not a universal expiry guarantee.
A rejected cleanup mutation, a false schedule predicate, a target that does
not delete, or a timer that merely enqueues a cleanup job can leave an expired
row after its timer settles. Maintained expiry counts are exact only when the
row's cleanup succeeds synchronously and atomically removes all dependent
custody. A chunk can also write an already-due timer itself; this guarantee
holds immediately before chunk execution, not after its writes.

## Motivation

Previously `background_turn` fired up to 64 timers using a newly sampled clock
per firing, then ran a chunk using another new clock. A queued digest could
therefore count rows whose cleanup timers were still due after the first 64,
or whose deadline fell between the last timer clock and the chunk clock.
The owner already owns the clock and ordering, so the repair belongs there.

## Design

### D1 — The rule

At the start of a background turn sample `now` once. Perform at most 64
oldest-due firing transactions at `now`, retaining each transaction's live
reruns. If the due index is empty, run one chunk of the head runnable job at
that same `now`. Otherwise skip only the chunk and write
`{"kind":"job","job":id,"skipped":"timers-due","now":now}` to the existing
watch log. Channel sweep, connected-state flush, and channel flush still run.

After 64 transactions, inspect the next indexed timer deadline: a chunk may
run if it is absent or strictly greater than `now`. Reaching the firing limit
alone is not a reason to skip; exactly 64 firings may empty the due index.
This is an indexed lookahead, never an extra firing transaction.

Invariant: immediately before a chunk executes at `now`, `_timers` has no row
with `at <= now`. It does not imply that every row has an effective expiry
schedule, or that every settled target successfully removed its row.

Rejected targets settle as before, so they cannot indefinitely pin this
barrier. A store fault in timer firing still fails the owner as before.

### D2 — One clock per turn

`background_turn` delegates to `background_turn_at(self.now())`.
`fire_due_timers_background_turn(now)` receives the sampled value and returns
whether the due index drained. `run_chunk_at(&id, now)` receives that same
value; neither firing nor chunk execution resamples the data clock. Live
reruns and existing maintenance retain their own clock semantics. A dev clock
advance takes effect on the next background turn and can need several bounded
turns before a queued chunk becomes runnable.

### D3 — Restart

Owner restart returns a `running` job to `queued` with its committed cursor.
Its first resumed chunk uses the same barrier, draining any restart backlog
first. There is no new durable state or recovery protocol.

### D4 — Observability

Use existing `dev --status` timer counts (`due`, `next_at`, `rejected`) and the
per-skipped-turn watch log naming the waiting job and pinned clock. A new
process-lifetime counter is unnecessary: the status reports the current
cause, and the log records each observed delay.

## Liveness

The existing policy takes a background turn after at most eight client turns
or when the client queue is empty. Jobs keep the idle receive timeout at zero,
so a skipped chunk does not sleep while its finite timer backlog drains.
Each nonempty firing pass performs up to 64 transactions and yields to the
existing writer policy; network/channel flushes continue during the backlog.

Finite draining assumes timer targets do not perpetually replenish due work.
Targets can create new due timers, and wall time can make more deadlines due
between turns. An unending due workload can delay jobs indefinitely; this
change promises no deadline or starvation bound. The firing limit stays 64.

## Acceptance

Implemented witnesses in `writer_timers.rs::tests` use the real writer State,
timer transactions, live-rerun hook and chunk path with explicit turn clocks:

1. 100 due timers: first turn commits 64 firings, logs one skipped job and
   executes no chunk; second commits 36 then runs the chunk. All timer and
   chunk commits use the supplied clock, and the job observes only the live row.
2. Due sets of 0, 1, 63 and exactly 64: the chunk runs that turn; a timer at
   `now + 1` remains armed and its row's job-written observation equals `now`.
   The supplied clock is far before wall time, detecting any resampling in
   either timer firing or chunk execution.
3. A partially executed running job survives a real SQLite snapshot/reopen;
   it becomes queued with the same cursor, waits for 65 due timers to drain
   over two turns, and resumes correctly.
4. Rejected and predicate-skipped row targets settle and allow the chunk in
   the same turn, while the expired row remains. This is a positive witness
   for the stated limit of the expiry guarantee.

The existing real-binary owner witness in `owner_cases/timers.rs` advances a
period schedule and 70 row deadlines together: the period plus 63 rows fire
on the first turn, the remaining seven on the next, and all 70 expiry commits
now precede the digest chunk. It formerly asserted that seven followed it.

## Amendments and non-claims

LLP 1016 D3 and D5 and `guide job`/`guide schedule` now state the barrier.
The guide makes the successful synchronous cleanup obligation explicit.
No snapshot across chunks, new schedule form, timer-order change, job-queue
change, occurrence catch-up, resource ceiling change, or lateness promise.
Rehearsal still fires no timers; its discarded-snapshot result does not prove
live timer ordering or expiry cleanup.

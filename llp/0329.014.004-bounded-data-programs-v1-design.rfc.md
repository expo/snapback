## Summary

The founder's question was *what does the ideal design for a system like this
look like*. This document is the answer as the evidence now stands: a
three-family panel converged on it ([LLP 0329.014](./0329.014-bounded-data-programs-panel-synthesis.research.md)),
a v0 spec fixed it ([LLP 0329.014.000](./0329.014.000-bounded-programs-language-v0.spec.md)),
twenty-nine deep applications were written in it by families that had not
designed them ([LLP 0329.014.001](./0329.014.001-paper-archetypes.research.md),
[LLP 0329.014.002](./0329.014.002-paper-archetypes-wave-2.research.md),
[LLP 0329.014.003](./0329.014.003-paper-archetypes-wave-3.research.md)), and three
spike lanes executed its core mechanism and front end (0329.014 §8). It is a design statement for the
founder to react to, not a commissioning; it authorizes nothing.

**The design in one paragraph.** One TypeScript-syntax language for queries and
mutations, with real control flow, in which every data access goes through a
typed, bounded, traced API; a rich schema in which authorization, disclosure,
retention, integrity, time, hierarchy, merge, and derived values are
first-class declarations the engine enforces; a static certificate of read shape
and bound as the only gate; and one execution trace that is simultaneously the
invalidation predicate, the offline closure, the OCC set, and the completeness
detector — so that every admitted program is live, retained, authorized, and
locally predictable, with no second query class. Around it: a small set of
*kinds* for what is not a data program (effects, channels, schedules, jobs,
export streams, identity, assets, a host principal, a ranked-index read site)
and a client that is a TypeScript twin of the server's interpreter over a
receipt-fed range store.

**What the fourteen applications established.** The language's core carried
the large majority of operations in every application, and no application
needed a second query class, an opaque handler, or an app-authored optimistic
reducer. Every residue was a *dimension of the model* — thirteen of them (§2) —
or a *kind* (§6), never a missing operator. Exactly one application (semantic
search) needed a second read-site class, as pre-registered. Six rules of the v0
spec were shown to admit a wrong program (§8); all six are the same defect —
a fact escaping its visibility — seen from a new side each time, and the
design's central policy family is now written around it. Writing the designs
in a strict language found bugs in ten of the fourteen designs themselves.

**What the spikes established.** The mechanism runs: one Rust interpreter over
SQLite with certificate, trace, and receipt; a receipt-only client that
replays a local `send` as `predicted`, survives one held-row delete on the halo,
goes `partial` on the second and at computed-key sites, and evicts on
revocation — 7/7 tests, no per-program special case. The client evaluator is a
TypeScript twin, byte-identical to Rust on a 75-case corpus under Node and
JavaScriptCore at 7.8 KB gzip, versus 109 KB for even a read-only Rust/WASM
subset — D-A settled by measurement.

**What wave 3 established (v1.1).** Fifteen further applications chosen to
stretch along untested axes — fan-out scale, standing queries, symmetric
secrets, pre-identity principals, per-principal data volume, schedules at
population scale, order-book matching, live derived prices, dispatch,
three-sided orders, collaborative carts, tournament shapes, and schema as data
— again needed **no second query class**. The ranked-index site gained a
geo/knn sibling; the boundary kinds are the broadcast projection, `replica`,
and jobs. The archetype pre-registered as most likely to force a new
dimension (Notion-style user-defined databases) reduced to EAV over fixed
tables, an interpreter inside a program, scope-split maintained aggregates,
and a product boundary. What the wave added is the **second axis of the
central defect — completeness** (a page reduction returned as a population
fact, even under a public rule — found three times), the recognition that
`maintain` is a *family* (rows, order statistics, reducers, coverage, sharded
sums), that **principals are sets and kinds**, and eight further rules of the
v0 spec that admitted a wrong program (§8). Writing the designs in the strict
language found bugs in all thirteen designs.

**The honest cost** (§10): it is a language, a compiler, a schema engine with
thirteen enforced dimensions (each now with several members), and a
conformance obligation between two interpreters; several of its lowerings are
unmeasured; and none of it touches the dev loop, which every panelist named
as the co-equal cause of the benchmark losses.

## 1. The model on one page

```

         (unique/cap/exclusion with where; appendOnly; immutable/immutableExcept;
         cross-column; balanced/sumEquals; sum-cap; exclusive-open; event-coupled
         transitions; rate limits) · rules (predicates with any/nearest, (old,next),
         negation, principal kinds) · disclosure (readable + cadence + floor) ·
         retention (per table) · maintained (count/sum/min/max/rank, floorOnDecrease,
         indexes over maintained rows) · tree/dag/inherit · sequence (CRDT order) ·
         interval columns · audited(into) · versioned/hold · partitioned · merge mode

         reads: get · getUnique · exists · scan(take) · merge(prefixes) · recurse · getHidden
         writes: insert · update · upsert · delete · require · run(named)
         inputs: viewer · principal.kind/bearerOf/replica · now (intent) · commitTime ·
```

taint checks that hidden inputs cannot escape through fields, refusal codes,

`watch` kind. **Schema as data needed no dimension** (§9).

## 3. The language (v1 delta from v0.1 §4)

Admitted in addition to v0.1:

- **In rules:** `any(index, prefix, { take ≤ cap, where })` — a bounded
  existential over an index, cheapest-first, hoisted once per program and never
  per row of a page; `nearest(index, key, { along, self })` over `tree`/`dag`
  tables; `inherits(table, fk)` so inherited readability proves without a
  spurious disclosure; `principal.kind`, `principal.bearerOf`, `ctx.replica`;
  negation; an authenticated-but-not-public spelling.
- **In programs:** `db.merge(index, prefixes, { order, take })` (k-way fan-in
  over prefixes of one index, its own bound rule); `db.getHidden(row)` (control
  flow and writes only; certificate-marked `hidden`; prediction `partial`);
  `db.run(namedMutation, args)` with summed certificates; `insertIfAbsent`
  returning `already_acted` without revealing the conflicting row; committed
  system randomness (`ctx.random(bytes)`, drawn once, reconnect is a read);
  `ctx.receivedAt`; `digest(bytes)`; `idMod`/`hashToInt` as shape functions;
  literal `range(N)`; typed struct lists with `max`; a bounded JSON payload
  accessor; string vocabulary (`text({ min, charset, nonBlank })`, pure string
  builtins).
- **Declarations on a mutation:** `phase: 'system'` (a continuation enqueued in
  the same commit; the certificate is the pair; the return type cannot name
  system-only tables); `predict: false`; `delivery: 'online-only'`;
  `callable: 'system' | 'host'`. These *refuse* a capability explicitly; the
  panel's rule against per-query flags forbade *silent degradation*, not
  declared refusal.
- **Value model:** i53 with refused overflow (or `int({ bits: 64 })`), Euclidean
  `divmod`, widened `mulDiv`, a deterministic real/decimal subset with rounding
  law for rating formulas, codepoint string order, stable sort, insertion-ordered
  maps — pinned in the corpus the TypeScript twin is held to.

Refused, unchanged: `while`, recursion, ambient time and randomness, I/O,
dynamic table names, any read outside `db`, arbitrary JS in the data plane.

## 4. The schema (v1)

**Columns.** `id`, `text({ max, min?, charset?, nonBlank? })`, `int({ min?, max?,
bits? })`, `bool`, `time`, `ref`, `json({ maxBytes })`, `interval({ from, to })`,
`optional(t)`, enums with declared transitions, `text({ hashed })` (keyed
digest, equality only), `int({ sequence: by })` (engine-assigned dense order at
commit, provisional locally), model-typed vectors (for ranked sites).

**Indexes.** Composite ordered; unique (also `{ bearer: true }` — a full-key
`get` admitted to any principal, possession becomes a rule input); tokens with
declared normalization and prefix match; ranked (FTS/vector) as a *read site
class*, not an index over which `scan` is admitted.

**Constraints — enforced on every write because no write path exists outside
the language.** `unique`/`cap`/`exclusion` with `{ where }` and self-row
exemption; `cap({ sum, max })`; `balanced(table, { by, of })`;
`sumEquals(child.col, parent.col)`; cross-column equality; `appendOnly({
except })`; `immutable(fields)` / `immutableExcept({ fields, when })`;
event-coupled transitions (a state change requires exactly one matching
append-only event in the same commit); exclusive-open across tables; kind-
conditional caps; `rateLimit({ by, per, max })` (predicted by wave 3).

**Rules.** Per effect (`read`, `insert`, `update: (old, next)`, `delete`);
algebraic predicates with `any`/`nearest`/`inherits`; `public(reason)`; a
table whose columns have different audiences is refused (`E_SECRECY_MIX`) and
the protection-class split pasted — **column-level rules are refuted**, from
both the health and realtime sides.

**Maintained.** `count`/`sum`/`min`/`max`/`rank` with `by`, `where`
(time-independent), `overflow`, `floorOnDecrease` (the hot-key answer as a
constraint), `readable` + `disclose: { cadence, minGroup }` as the declared
disclosure, and indexes over maintained rows; per-node `tree.count`. **Rule:
maintain when fan-in is bounded; compute at read when fan-out is unbounded**
(daily fantasy, predicted); a rank over computed values is a job snapshot.

**Hierarchy.** `tree({ parent, path, depth, maxDepth, maxSubtree, inherit,
count })` and `dag({ …, maxAncestors, maxDescendants })` — engine-maintained
closures with certificate-reported `engineWrites`, needed for *authority*
(the writer cannot see the closure rows it must write), not only cost.

**Order.** `sequence({ after, lamport, replica, tombstone })` — a host CRDT
order type with `seqInsert/Delete/Retract/Move`, bit-identical on both sides;
table `merge` modes.

**Temporal and audit.** `versioned()`, `hold()`, `audited(into)` (the engine
writes the access manifest — which is the closure receipt — in the read's own
commit), `partitioned()` (scans over non-uniform visibility spend their budget
only within the caller's uniform partition), per-table `retain:` policy.

## 5. Policy

**Structural facts (dimension A) — one family, `E_STRUCTURAL_*`.** A
reduction (`count`, `sum`, `min`/`max`, `sort … [0]`, `take 1` as a maximum, a
loop tally) or a structural write (a subtree update) over a scan whose rule is
not *uniform-true* for the running principal, returned or written as a fact
about the resource, is refused. Admitted routes: a maintained aggregate with a
declared `readable`; a caller-scoped computation over the caller's own rows;
computation under system visibility by a `schedule` or a system continuation,
written to a new table at the transition; `getHidden` for control flow that
never returns the value. Ranked domains admit only whole partitions whose
verdict is uniform-true; residual filtering never claims completeness.

**Receipts under non-uniform rules.** Range facts ship only when every
examined candidate was readable; absence and hidden are indistinguishable;
protected lookups are partitioned so page length is not a cardinality oracle;
a completeness-sensitive log must be uniform-read (`E_SEQUENCE_VISIBILITY`).

**Retention (dimension F).** Per table: `until-revoked` (default), `delivered-
history` (an expelled member keeps what was delivered; future rows denied;
content revocations override), `purge-on-block`, `all-owned` (a viewer's own
partition, for local search over a photo library — predicted). One store-wide
`maxOfflineAge`, with a per-app hard maximum (ruling pending: 24 h / 7 d / 30 d).

**Disclosure (F, J).** `readable` on an aggregate is a security item; cadence
and floor bound what its derivative discloses; `exportStream` is a total
disclosure to a named service principal, listed in the receipt.

**Prediction and delivery (G).** `predict: false` and `delivery:
'online-only'` refuse; prediction is `conditional(constraint)` whenever a
write is subject to a `cap`/`unique` the client cannot evaluate; security
windows compare `commitTime`, never intent `now`.

## 6. Kinds

| Kind | Semantics |
|---|---|
| `query`, `mutation` | bounded data programs; mutations may declare `phase: 'system'`, `predict`, `delivery`, `callable` |
| `schedule` | durable, transactionally enqueued invocation of a named mutation under the system principal; live `at` with reschedule-on-change; `if` guard; args from the trigger row; no enqueue on absent `at`; at-least-once with the schedule key as idempotency key; a schedule generation distinct from schema succession; a durable timer *index* (population scale) |
| `job` | chunked, resumable, generation-fenced bulk work over sealed inputs (a `commitSeq` watermark); backfill, close of books, vacuum, purge, rank snapshots, resolution/payout |
| `channel` | ephemeral fan-out with rule-expression `publish`/`subscribe`, TTL, invalidated when the probe's durable row changes |
| `effect` | JavaScript with I/O outside the data plane; reads only through named queries as a service principal; writes only through named mutations; **transactional outbox** (commit-triggered, durable job identity, provider idempotency, `sent_unknown`) |
| `exportStream` | append-only tables shipped in `seq` order to a service principal; resumable by `(generation, seq)`; a declared total disclosure |
| identity | credentials, sessions, tokens, `claim`/`merge` of pre-identity principals, external principals (predicted) |
| assets | upload intent/finalize, metadata validation, ordinary vs one-use delivery, revocation, purge receipts |
| host invoke | a non-client principal (`host`) with a capability-bearing fence the caller cannot forge; settles long-lived external processes (game servers) |
| ranked-index read site | partition-pinned FTS/vector: `take`, `maxVisited`, exact/approx flag, model version, coarse invalidation, `localReplay: false`, no scores by default; may declare a local alternative |

The simulation of a realtime game, the OLAP engine behind BI, and the OT/CRDT
engine behind an editor's text are *outside* the data plane by design: the
first two as processes that write through named mutations or read through a
declared stream; the third as a host `sequence` kind whose semantics the
evaluator implements, not the app.

## 7. Runtime

**Certificate (the gate).** `planHash, schemaHash, ruleHashes,
evaluatorVersion, valueModelVersion; readSites[{ table, index, prefixTemplate:
arg|viewer|const|shape|computed|hidden, take, maxCalls, hoist, class:
range|ranked }]; writeSites[{ table, kind, maxRows }]; engineWrites;
maintainedAmplification; constraintChecks; maxExamined, maxIndexEntries,
maxRuleProbes, maxSteps, maxHeapBytes, maxOutputBytes, maxClosureBytes;
resultType; completeness: page|capped|maintained|all; predictionSites[{
transition, reads, mode: closed|conditional|partial }]; disclosures[];
haloRows; readTime?; lowerings`. Ceilings are a family with published defaults;
refusal names the product that produced the number and pastes the rewrite —
including the *third* rewrite for history-shaped questions: declare it
(maintain, forward) **or** export it (stream, history).

**Two traces.** The authoritative trace (hidden rows and ranges included) drives
invalidation and OCC and never leaves the server; the closure receipt drives
the client. Both `⊆` certificate.

**Subscriptions.** Indexed by range; a commit overlapping a traced range or
rule-dependency key reruns and diffs; coalescing; maintained rows for
inbox-shaped reads; (predicted) a **broadcast class** when the audience's rule
is uniform-true — one evaluation, N deliveries.

**Client.** A TypeScript twin of the interpreter (measured: 7.8 KB gzip, 3.2 ms
cold replay, byte-identical on the frozen corpus under Node and
JavaScriptCore) over a per-principal encrypted range-indexed store at one
version, a verdict cache, an outbox, and the hydration replay check; eight
states; one hook per framework. Never a native module; WASM rejected on
measurement.

**Server.** One Rust interpreter inside the SQLite transaction; JavaScript
never in the authoritative path; Postgres as a second physical lowering,
program-near-DB, trace-validated OCC at commit.

## 9. What remains open

Author decisions: the bound model for rules (now concrete: `any` with a cap,
hoisted, reported); `maxOfflineAge` (24 h / 7 d / 30 d); explicit vs
compiler-proposed maintenance (explicit, proposed in diagnostics); named
operations only in v1; whether `job` is a chunked mutation or a schedule
continuation; whether `sequence` is a kind or a boundary.

**The spelling, after spike S2** (0329.014 §8): ordinary TypeScript re-authors
every S1 program to byte-identical IR and certificates, and every one of the
eight realistic first attempts is refused with a rewrite that compiles — so the
"looks like TypeScript but isn't" hazard is *real but bounded*. It did not
clear the brief's gauntlet gate (66.7% of raw round-3 gauntlet code lowers
unchanged or mechanically, against 70%; 76% of the snippets that are actually
data programs), and the two gaps it found are design gaps, not spelling:
**composite write keys** must be explicit in the IR (S1's scalar key cannot
encode a composite-identity `upsert`), and the pure **standard library**
(`top`, `mergeByTime`, `orderedPair`, `intersectById`, bounded `Map`/`Set`)
must be specified and versioned rather than recognized by name. Both go into
v1; the same corpus is then rerun. A SQL text front end remains a measured
option for reads, not a default.

Unmeasured: `any`/`nearest` hoisted lowerings; `dag` write amplification;
`maintain.rank`; per-cell surge counts; the broadcast class; `E_STEPS` on
pairing algorithms; the schedule timer index at population scale.

Wave 3 ran. **Schema as data did not force a dimension**: Notion-style
user-defined databases reduce to EAV over fixed tables with typed value
columns, an interpreter inside a program over exploded AST rows (bounded by
`maxSteps`), rollups as scope-split maintained aggregates plus fan-out jobs,
keys that stay traced-row fields (prediction `closed`), and runtime composite
indexes as ≤ 16 pinned views — a product boundary, not a runtime-schema
facility; every tenant runs the same certified programs. Two applications
remain to fold at this revision (tournaments, eBay).

Still unmeasured after wave 3: `any`/`nearest` hoisted lowerings, `dag`
amplification, the broadcast projection at 100k subscribers, `maintain.rows`
and order-statistic maintenance costs, batched schedule firing at population
scale, `serialBy` under Postgres, and the interpreter-in-a-program step
budgets Notion and fast-casual need.

## 10. Good and bad

**Good.** It satisfies the founder's question affirmatively with evidence
rather than argument: one language, no second query class, across twenty-two
applications spanning messaging, booking, workspace, auctions, ledgers,
payments, search, graphs, health, games, editors, analytics, and realtime
simulation. Its guarantees are uniform because its mechanism is uniform: one
certificate, one trace, one policy family for the one defect that recurred
everywhere. The client is small by measurement, not by promise. The strict
language is a design review: it found bugs in ten of the fourteen designs.

**Bad.** It is a language, a compiler, and a schema engine with thirteen
enforced dimensions — a larger single program than any profile the corpus has
shipped, though the claim (now with evidence from the spike) is that
specifying dimensions *once* is cheaper than specifying shapes *forever*. Two
interpreters must agree, forever, under a corpus. Several lowerings are
unmeasured. The `partial` state will surface in offline UIs and its diagnostic
must be excellent. Ceilings are product parameters and will be argued about.
Rules with `any` are affordable by hoisting, which is a heuristic. And nothing
here fixes the dev loop — one generation identity across schema, server,
client, bundler, and cache — which every panelist and both single-model
documents named as co-equal with the language.

## Confidence

High that the design is coherent and that the fourteen applications say what
§2 attributes to them (verbatim artifacts). High on the wrong-program table
(§8): each row has a one-line counterexample and four were found by two
families independently. Medium-high that the thirteen dimensions are the right
decomposition. Medium on sufficiency — wave 3 exists to test it. Medium on
cost. Low that any of this moves the Clique scoreboard without the dev loop.

## Summary

The founder asked: *if we could start over and build the perfect agentic dev
loop for Snapback — avoiding everything annoying about the current setup — what
would it look like, what should we learn from Snapback 1, and what should
Snapback 2 be?*

The 0329 family answered the **language** half of a rebuild (bounded data
programs, LLP 0329.014.004). Every panelist named the **dev loop** co-equal —
"a closed query language with a Metro race and an identity rebind still loses
Clique" (LLP 0329.000 F-B) — and no 0329.x document designs it. This RFC is
that parallel track (LLP 0329.014.007 F6.2).

The evidence is unusually consistent. Across 125 diaries, 19 benchmark
records, 473 open and 1,705 closed tickets, and ~40 loop-governing LLPs, agents
say the same two things: the **designed core** — typed refusals, live queries
as a consequence of commits, ephemeral presence, declarative membership,
fail-closed identity, receipts — is "better than the equivalents in anything I
have used" (Diary 0101); and the **hours went to the loop around it** —
process lifecycle, development identity, finding the page, learning the dialect
by refusal, carrying generated artifacts, and failures that looked like
success. Correction-loop attribution across runs is roughly 60% agent error /
25% real defect / 15% environment, and the agent errors cluster on lifecycle,
custody, and dialect, not on domain logic. Snapback's own engineers paid the
same tax internally: the single largest ticket class (188 open / 623 closed)
is frozen verification records drifting against a `main` that moves ~50
commits per fix round.

The design that follows is one loop with one owner and one generation
identity, wrapped around the language the family already converged on:

> **`snapback init` → `snapback dev` → edit → `snapback check` (<1 s) →
> `snapback test` (receipt) → `snapback deploy` (URL).** The agent authors
> three kinds of file and never carries generated bytes. Development has
> personas, not auth modes. Nothing is silent: *denied*, *empty*, *partial*,
> *queued*, and *committed* are typed states, and `snapback why` explains any
> of them from the execution trace. Refusals come in ~20 families and paste a
> compiling rewrite. Docs are the compiler's facts, version-locked to the
> installed binary, routed by the loop, and small enough to fit in a system
> prompt. Environments are disposable and forkable by default. Deploy exists
> from week one. Snapback's own engineers build Snapback 2 on the same loop.

Twelve principles (§5.1), a fifteen-verb CLI (§5.11), a side-by-side with
Snapback 1 (§7), measurable gates (§8), and the author decisions this design
needs (§9) are inside. Nothing here re-decides the language; where the loop
depends on a language property (the certificate, the trace), it cites
LLP 0329.014.004.

## 0. The question and its scope

This document answers three questions in order:

1. **What should we learn from Snapback 1's loop?** (§2) A diagnosis, with
   the mechanisms that produced the friction, not a list of bugs.
2. **What does the field and the literature say?** (§3, §4) Twenty
   competitors and app-builders, ~75 primary sources on what coding agents do
   well and badly.
3. **What should Snapback 2's loop be?** (§5–§10) A design with principles,
   a transcript of day one, the verbs, the internal engineering loop, gates,
   and open decisions.

**Scope.** The loop: everything between an empty directory and a deployed,
verified application, plus the loop Snapback's own engineers run. Out of
scope: the data language (LLP 0329.014.004), the local-first contract
(LLP 0329.014.007/.008), the kinds catalogue (LLP 0329.014.006). "Snapback 2"
is used as the working name for the from-scratch design; whether it is a new
repository or an in-tree successor is an author decision (§9.8).

## 2. What Snapback 1 taught us about the loop

### 2.1 The core is loved; the loop lost the hours

The diaries are not ambivalent. The same properties are praised by every
model family that reached the third milestone of any benchmark:

- **Diagnostics** — "the best I have used. Not 'good for a young product' —
  best, full stop" (Diary 0101); "the platform told me exactly what was wrong
  every time" (Diary 0114); "Diagnostics are a real contract, not an
  afterthought … the single biggest agent-DX win" (Diary 0001).
- **Live queries with zero invalidation code** — "I never wrote a single
  'refetch after mutate' line" (Diary 0102); "realtime is an outcome of data
  dependencies, not a separate socket layer" (Diary 0109).
- **Ephemeral presence** — "the single most pleasant thing in the whole run"
  (Diary 0100); Convex needed "a durable table, two indexes, a public
  mutation, an internal expiry mutation, one scheduled job per refresh, a
  nonce guard … and explicit cleanup" for the same feature (Diary 0059).
- **Declarative membership and fail-closed identity** — "Snapback puts it in
  the schema and then refuses to let you write the roster any other way"
  (Diary 0101); "I have rarely been prevented from lying about it. Snapback
  prevented the lie" (Diary 0098).
- **Codegen that fails closed and writes nothing on error** — "Codegen is the
  best part of the product" (Diary 0057); "Every mistake I made was caught at
  generation with a file/line, not in the app" (Diary 0056).
- **Receipts and deterministic acceptance** — "A 10-check operations suite
  that runs in five seconds changes what you bother to prove" (Diary 0047);
  "I never once parsed prose to learn what happened" (Diary 0042).
- **The version-exact served guide** — "a structural advantage over every
  backend I know" (Diary 0101).

And the same diaries locate the cost precisely. Diary 0101: "the parts of
Snapback that are *designed* are better than the equivalents in anything I
have used. The parts that are still *settling* — the familiar profile's edges,
the dev-loop lifecycle outside the supervised path, the client's authority
transitions — are where the hours went." Diary 0057, on a chess backend: "the
app itself took about five minutes to write and three codegen rounds to
compile; everything expensive was environmental." Diary 0069: "The remaining
friction is knowledge-shaped, not defect-shaped."

Ranked by the number of independent diaries or lanes that hit each class
(the three diary inputs, combined):

| Rank | Friction class | Diaries/lanes | The sentence |
|---|---|---:|---|

Two readings from the scoreboard bound what any loop redesign can claim.
First, the capability ceiling is reached: on published 0.1.29, `sol-snapback-
explicit` sealed 7/8 like Convex, Supabase, and PocketBase — but after 8 h 46 m
at a holistic self-rating of 61 against Convex's 74–79 (`record.md`;
LLP 0329 §Evidence). Second, time-to-first-green on Clique M1 sat at ~2,100 s
for most Snapback lanes against 1,213–1,670 s for Convex and 751 s for
PocketBase (Diaries 0078–0085, 0114), and the one loop repair that shipped —
the 0.1.27 caller-owned loop — cut it to 1,075 s (−51%) with no language
change (`2026-08-25-clique-pair-published-0127-capstone`). The loop is where
the scoreboard moves.

### 2.2 The mechanisms

Bugs were fixed continuously; the friction reappeared in the next lane. That
is the signature of a mechanism, not a defect. Eight are attested.

**M1 — The loop was assembled from separately correct mechanisms with no
single owner or generation.** Codegen, the native backend, the Expo supervisor,
Metro, the bootstrap exchange, the binding mint, seed identity, and sessions
admission were each designed correctly and coordinated through sidecar files
(`.snapback/dev.ready.json`, `expo-supervisor.json`, `startup-artifacts.json`,
`.tmp-*` staging under a watched tree). LLP 0310.000 §The ordering defect
traces the emblematic failure: generate publishes a successor graph Metro can
see → the backend activates it → only then the binding is reminted, and the
provider performs one admission attempt and refuses — so "'I added a table'
look[ed] like 'I swapped the app.'" A stale ready file from a dead PID made
every subsequent `snapback dev` fail with a bare `fetch failed`
(`issues/closed/20260825-stale-dev-ready-file-bare-fetch-failed.md`); codegen's
temp directory killed Metro (`SNAPBACK_EXPO_METRO_EXITED`); one milestone
restarted Metro twelve times; a supervisor that read JSON on stdin "exits 0
on EOF … the least agent-friendly shape a dev server can have, and it is the
one the docs lead with" (Diary 0057). Three lifecycles coexist today
(supervised LLP 0181, URL-first LLP 0300, repaired supervised LLP 0310.000).
LLP 0310.000's summary is the diagnosis in one line: Snapback became "harder
to *operate* than to *author*."

**M2 — Capability-by-form made the loop a registry the agent had to learn.**
LLP 0329's central claim — "capability is a function of authoring form, and
the mapping is invisible at the authoring site" — is a language finding with a
loop consequence: 46 `define*` constructors, 13 query flavors, two authoring
profiles, three function styles, 3,597 diagnostic codes of which 13% carry a
guide anchor, a 1.25-million-word guide, 292 "Do not …" bullets in 82
anti-pattern sections, and 18 "If you searched for …" stanzas written for
prior agents' failed searches (`current-loop-inventory` §5). Retrieval cost
7–12 of a 15-call budget before any work (LLP 0329 F5). LLP 0317's sweep found
that of 46 backlog rows, 40 were already shipped or partially shipped: "the
dominant failure mode in this corpus is a capable agent not finding a
capability that exists." Every recurring "don't" in a context file is a
missing gate (§4).

**M3 — Verification machinery built for the engineer leaked onto the agent's
path.** Content-addressed `generated/`, review manifests, a gitignored codegen
cache, boot-time identity admission (`STARTUP_ARTIFACT_MISMATCH`,
`BINDING_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`), digest-named bindings, `authorization-review.json`
diffs: each is a sound engineering control, and together they made the agent
*carry* them. A React scaffold ships 33 pre-generated files (6,662 lines)
beside 18 authored ones; a full generated graph was 152 files / 595 KB against
Convex's 35 / 87 KB (Diary 0039); a documentation-only comment changed a
bundle digest and forced a database drop (Diary 0059); a fresh worktree "fails
twice, in sequence, before it can succeed"
(`issues/shelved/20260828-guide-gap-fresh-worktree-generator-bootstrap.md`). The
design choice (deployment identity is the hash of what runs) is right; making
the agent see it is not.

**M4 — Development inherited production semantics with no dev-time
primitive for the ordinary agent case.** Sessions mode is "the real thing,"
and every development convenience carried an identity consequence discovered
by failure: reset invalidates sessions and re-mints principal ids; `ensureApp`
is refused in sessions mode; headless clients share one durable custody
partition unless `storagePartition` is set; the supervised Expo mint is one
session per origin, so two-browser choreography saw Alice as Bob; the React
provider refuses a dev `userId` while raw operations accept it; the default
`optional-user` is the one trust decision that is "forget it and be open"
(Diary 0057). The ordinary agent case — *N personas, reset, run-as* — had no
first-class spelling, and the two 0.1.29 Familiar lanes aborted M3 on exactly
this collision, read as broken live fanout (`record.md` §6).

**M5 — Three checkers disagreed, and the loudest failure classes were
silent.** Codegen accepted `take(4096)` over a 1,024 ceiling, a jobs table
name the runtime lacked, dotted exports the client refused, `single_table_v1`
the runtime rejected before Metro (Diaries 0047, 0063, 0078, 0079). Worse than
refusal-at-the-wrong-layer was no refusal: `collect()` silently returned the
first 1,024 rows — "incomplete input looked complete to application code"
(Diary 0047); a 44,084-character body read back clipped to 16,384 with no
diagnostic (Diary 0061); a transiently refused mutation queued silently and
every later mutation queued behind it — "the worst hours of the run," hit by
two blind sessions (`issues/closed/20260809-outbox-silent-queue-dx.md`);
codegen returned `ok` on `grants: true` over `public-immutable` delivery;
generated acceptance passed without booting a server; a gate's violation line
"scrolls past and the gate reads as green"
(`issues/shelved/20260828-workspace-graph-red-on-main-harness-parent-path.md`). The
agents' own attribution of the largest debugging cluster is the *absence* of a
causal trace: hook → authority generation → commit → invalidation → subscriber
revision (LLP 0317 O15, bundle 1).

**M6 — The docs were the product's memory instead of the compiler's facts.**
The guide is declared the authority for the paved form, and it is hand-
maintained prose: freshness warnings saturated on 45 of 65 pages, pages taught
primitives the installed closure lacked ("the guide describes a product the
package does not contain," Diary 0045) or forms the runtime refused
(`_generated/` vs `generated/`; `{source, exportName}` supporting imports —
"the guide's sentence told me the opposite," Diary 0057). Agents adapted by
reading `define.mjs`, `outbox-shared.mjs`, `app-client.mjs`, `storage.rs`,
and binary strings — 24 diaries in one range alone. The served guide, when it
existed for a surface, was praised (Diary 0101); the failure was never
"the page was wrong when found," it was that the fact lived nowhere the loop
would hand it over.

**M7 — Publication lag, and no deploy.** Agents run the published pin; fixes
land on `main`; tickets stay open "for the published-path bar." Every LLP 0310
repair was "not in published 0.1.27"; `newestPublished` (0.1.29) is not
`newestQualified` (0.1.27); publication is a ~30-script family with five
"qualification" entry points (LLP 0276), and 0.1.30 could not publish because
codegen emitted a capability the published runtime lacked. And the loop ends
before a URL: `guide/deploying/index.md` says no end-to-end path deploys an
arbitrary app; `deploy build` always yields `activationEligible: false`; all
17 hosted-lifecycle verbs refuse (`current-loop-inventory` step 15).

**M8 — The internal loop had the same shape.** Snapback's engineers were the
first agents to suffer the loop. Frozen-record verification drift is the
largest ticket class by volume (188 open / 623 closed): digests, line-pinned
diagnostics registries (a three-line deletion produced a 1,125-line registry
diff), calendar review dates that turned `main` red for every agent, claim
snapshots that "burn the work" when requalified mid-churn, and a backstop that
mints one ticket per stale signature (`issues-census` §1). The required fast
signal ran 55 times red on a stale manifest and was switched off; the
complete local proof takes 57–145 minutes "and every recent run failed"
(LLP 0316): "no signal a developer can act on arrives within the landing
loop." `guide/reference` is the highest-churn path by 5× and it is generated
(LLP 0329). A `touch .git/index` cost 17 s of rebuild across 89 worktrees
until LLP 0316 fixed it; 83 integration link targets at ~5 s each; a 490 MB
debug binary; a fourteen-code Hermes/Ibex bootstrap that seven consecutive
reruns lost their first hour to because "the link/launch smoke does not create
an application JS VM" (Diary 0033). 473 open tickets, 340 filed in nine days,
112 machine-minted; LLP 0328 concludes the open directory "no longer works as a
working set."

### 2.3 What must survive

Everything below is attested as loved *and* is structurally separable from the
mechanisms in §2.2. It is the keep list of LLP 0329 F6 and LLP 0329.014 §9,
restated for the loop:

- Stable, structured refusals with code, site, rule, and repair — the
  LLP 0010 diagnostic shape and its ten agent questions, verbatim.
- Codegen (in Snapback 2: `check`) that fails closed, writes nothing on
  error, and names file:line and the fix.
- Live queries as a consequence of commit; the ephemeral `channel` kind;
  declarative membership with revocation-active delivery; server-derived
  identity; typed assets with revocation at the origin.
- Deterministic acceptance with injected seams (clock, tick, fresh store),
  the receipt as the only done-oracle, and "zero checks can never pass."
- Additive schema successors that keep accounts, sessions, and data.
- The version-exact served guide *as an idea*: facts bound to the installed
  binary.
- Managed spawn-to-ready in about a second; instance forks per worktree; the
  measured idle profile (~13 MiB, zero disk writes).
- The benchmark/diary loop and the pit-of-success triage standard
  (LLP 0310, 2026-08-26): "when a capable agent gets something wrong, the
  default assumption is that the product made the wrong thing easy."

### 2.4 What we would not do again

Stated as deletions so nothing re-grows by default:

1. A dev server that owns, spawns, proxies, or enumerates the client's
   bundler; a supervisor that reads commands on stdin; lifecycle state in
   sidecar files the agent must know about.
2. Generated bytes the agent commits, imports by digest, or regenerates by
   hand; a committed review manifest; boot-time admission that turns "I added
   a table" into a different app.
3. An auth *mode* (dev vs sessions); process-global custody defaults; a reset
   that invalidates identities; a supervised mint that is one session per
   origin.
4. Two authoring profiles; three function styles; 46 constructors; a
   capability matrix reachable by spelling.
5. A diagnostics vocabulary with the cardinality of the implementation
   (3,597 codes) and a `snapback guide <CODE>` that dead-ends; refusals that
   name the invariant but not the admitted alternative; app refusals that die
   at the client envelope.
6. Any state that is empty, capped, queued, or denied without saying so.
7. A hand-maintained prose guide of a million words with calendar freshness
   dates; a 20 MB guide embedded in the binary; "If you searched for …"
   stanzas in teaching pages.
8. Committed generated projections; line-pinned registries; gates that bind
   to an exact commit; a required signal that can be switched off; a
   ~30-script publication family.
9. A JS engine in the default bootstrap (LLP 0329.014 OQ8) — Hermes/Ibex in
   the request path is the largest single source of environment failures in
   the diaries and the largest share of the shipped binary (LLP 0314).
10. Shipping without deploy.

## 3. What the field teaches

### 3.1 The convergence kit is table stakes, not a differentiator

In eighteen months nearly every product converged on the same kit: `llms.txt`
with a `.md` mirror of every page; an MCP server whose most-used tools are
introspection; Agent-Skills folders installed by `npx skills add …`, often
bundled with the MCP config as an editor plugin; a scaffold that writes an
`AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md` block; a `--json`, non-interactive CLI; and cheap
isolated backend copies with TTLs (`competitors-newwave` §5). Snapback 2
ships the kit on day one (§5.8) and does not expect it to move the scoreboard.
What separates products is elsewhere: **loudness of failure**, **distance from
create to running**, and **whether authorization and writes are the platform's
problem or the agent's.**

### 3.2 Mechanisms worth taking, by source

- **Convex — the loop as a product.** Agent Mode activates in any
  non-interactive shell: "`npx convex` will never prompt the agent to log in,"
  and with no deploy key it provisions an anonymous local deployment
  automatically; `npx convex dev --once --typecheck` typechecks, validates the
  schema *against live data*, pushes, and exits non-zero; per-agent expiring
  deployments and single-deployment tokens are one command each
  (`deployment create --expiration "in 5 days"`, `deployment token create
  --save-env`); the MCP server's `runOneoffQuery` is sandboxed read-only; the
  Claude Code plugin re-runs `tsc` after every tool use and a stop-hook
  re-prompts on failure; the Cursor plugin's pre-commit hook *blocks*
  `Date.now()` in queries and chained `.filter()`; `convex_rules.txt` is
  pinned to `^1.44.0` and regenerated from a public eval suite, whose #1
  failure category is "outdated Convex syntax." Chef's lesson: when the model
  "repeatedly edited protected authentication files despite instructions not
  to," the fix was "programmatically refusing to persist edits." The costs are
  instructive too: the local backend's most-commented issue is a startup
  failure with no diagnostics; the Cursor plugin's ~100 KB of always-on rules
  is a "Huge blocker" issue; the guidelines are worth 3–6% on frontier models
  today.
- **Supabase — measure your own tooling.** The only published measurement in
  the set: Opus 4.6 baseline 58% → **MCP-only 50%** → MCP plus a ~100-line
  workflow skill 67%. Tools without a decision procedure made the strongest
  model worse. The skill exists because agents "skip RLS policies,"
  "hallucinate CLI commands that don't exist," "create views without
  `security_invoker = true`," and "ignore the docs entirely." RLS `SELECT`
  returns `[]` on denial (issue #33499, open since Feb 2025); CVE-2025-48757
  exposed 170+ Lovable apps through omitted RLS; a scan of 1,072 vibe-coded
  Supabase sites found 98% flawed. After the July-2025 prompt-injection
  incident the fix was structural: a read-only Postgres *role* "below the
  model, where an injected prompt cannot reach" — and the admission that
  "guardrails alone aren't enough."
- **InstantDB — surface minimalism.** Two files (`instant.schema.ts`,
  `instant.perms.ts`), no server process, every `useQuery` live, pure type
  inference with no codegen step, `query --as-email | --as-guest` as a
  first-class verb, 24-hour temp apps and 2-week platform apps, `.md` for any
  docs URL, and a ❌/✅ common-mistakes page written for LLM output. Its
  costs: no local backend, untyped allow-all-by-default rules, no error
  schema.
- **PocketBase — the unit is right.** One ~12 MB binary is the whole backend,
  so the local loop *is* production; "the model spent its effort on product
  code, not backend wiring" (Diary 0114); fastest to green in every matrix.
  Its direction is wrong for agents (schema flows UI → files; untyped SDK;
  `listRule` denial is a 200 with empty items; the v0.23 rename cliff is the
  canonical hallucination source; the FAQ says "please do NOT use PocketBase"
  with AI-only tooling).
- **Firebase** — `firebase emulators:exec 'npm test'` is the cleanest
  boot-run-exit primitive in the group; MCP prompts generate security rules
  *with unit tests*; the emulator does not enforce composite indexes, so
  green locally can be red in production.
- **Neon — create before identity exists.** The Claimable protocol
  (anonymous assertion → 900 s bearer → credentials → human claim code;
  72 h / 100 MB TTL), branch TTLs preserved across reset, two-phase
  `prepare_database_migration` / `complete_database_migration` on a temp
  branch, `neon inspect db` "read-only and bounded … heavy results are
  capped," skills that say "CLI > MCP > API" and "ask before replacing
  `DATABASE_URL`." Replit restores app history from a branch per checkpoint
  "without changing the connection string." "Over 80% of databases … created
  by AI agents."
- **Cloudflare — docs at the network layer and disposable everything.** Any
  docs page as Markdown via `Accept: text/markdown` with token-count headers
  (7.22× fewer tokens); Temporary Accounts plus `wrangler deploy --temporary`
  return a claim URL before an account exists; Local Explorer captures OTel
  traces in `wrangler dev` and "prints a terminal hint … when the tooling
  detects an AI agent session"; `wrangler types --check` fails CI when the
  generated `Env` is stale; Dynamic Workers run model-written code in a fresh
  isolate with egress blocked. And the cautionary tale: a `setAlarm()` loop
  across preview deployments produced a $34,895 bill in eight days with zero
  users, because alerts watched CPU time while row reads exploded — meter
  with caps and TTLs.
- **Turso** — MCP tools split by blast radius (`read_database`,
  `write_database` refusing `DELETE`/`DROP`, `delete_from_database`,
  `evolve_schema`), consent-time scoping, "unscoped tokens are rejected"; the
  skill is an ordered decision procedure ("decide in this order … if
  forbidden, don't retry"); a database per prompt iteration.
- **Electric / TanStack DB** — skills shipped *inside the npm tarball* with
  `library_version` pinned in frontmatter, "Evolution Notes" that de-train the
  old product, a numbered-invariant `SPEC.md` that tests derive from, and
  structured "Skill Feedback: What Worked / What Failed / Missing" issues filed
  from real agent runs. Also the best example of the failure to avoid: a
  persisted collection that comes up "ready, empty, and silent" (a 15-issue
  cluster), and a `TRUNCATE` that silently unpublished a table while the shape
  reported `up-to-date`.
- **Encore, Jazz, SpacetimeDB** — an MCP that *is* the dashboard
  (`call_endpoint` + `get_traces`); tests that "never mock infra";
  `expectAllowed`/`expectDenied` permission harnesses; a single-binary
  `spacetime dev` and a database host that serves its own `/v1/mcp`, so the
  agent's tools keep their shape from local to deployed.
- **The app-builders** (Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Databutton) — all abandoned
  "connect your own backend" for a bundled one; the paved migration is
  show → approve → execute → save the file → regenerate types in one step;
  "Version History does not support database restores" (Bolt) versus a Neon
  branch per checkpoint (Replit): rollback must include data; secrets are
  entered by the human and "not read back"; Supabase's own evals show
  **Investigate** (reading logs to find a fault) is the weakest stage for
  every model, 33–67% against 72–97% for Build.

### 3.3 What nobody has solved

Every product in the set evaluates authorization only at runtime (CEL
strings, SQL policies, a rules DSL, filter strings, or TypeScript you must
remember to call); none has a loud, typed contract distinguishing *denied*
from *empty*; none has a local loop that is both cheap (no Docker, no JVM)
and faithful (limits, indexes, transactions enforced — Convex's own test
double "doesn't have many of the behaviors of the real backend"); none pins
docs to the running server; and none publishes an end-to-end "an agent built
the app with auth and it is secure" number (`competitors-core` §D). These are
exactly the properties the bounded-programs design gives by construction
(certificate at `check`; rules as the only authorization; the trace as the
completeness and disclosure witness; one binary) — provided the *loop* makes
them visible in under a second at the edit site. That is the opening.

## 4. What the research binds

The full ledger is in `agent-literature`; these are the findings the design
below depends on, each with its strongest source.

1. **Interface design moves success by amounts comparable to a model
   generation.** SWE-agent's ablations at equal model: lint-on-edit 18.0% vs
   15.0%; a 100-line viewer vs whole file 18.0% vs 12.7%; capped search vs
   paged 18.0% vs 12.0%; a lint guardrail raised the next attempt's success
   from 57.2% to 90.5% (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2024). Snapback's CLI is an
   agent-computer interface: capped, windowed output; validation at the edit
   boundary; few consolidated verbs.
2. **Executable feedback is the best repair signal, and quality of feedback
   is the bottleneck.** Test feedback 61.0% repair vs compiler 55.8% vs
   simulated human 50.5% (FeedbackEval); gains saturate after 2–3 rounds;
   self-repair "lags far behind" without better feedback (Olausson, ICLR
   2024); rustc-grade diagnostics reach ~74% on real compile errors
   (RustAssistant); LLM-paraphrased error prose does *not* help (Santos et al.).
   Hand-written, located, cause-naming diagnostics are where the leverage is.
3. **Verification is where agents fail, and "looks done" is the default.**
   The verification stage is 27% of failures on easy tasks and 64% on hard
   ones; 22.58% of 20,574 real sessions include false self-reports and 91.49%
   of resolutions needed human correction (Tang et al.); 29.6% of test-passing
   SWE-bench patches behave differently from gold; a read-only auditor lifted
   Terminal-Bench 69.7 → 77.2. Done must be a receipt the framework generates.
4. **Type errors, not syntax, dominate LLM compile failures; constrain
   artifacts, not tokens.** Syntax is ~6% of TypeScript compile errors in
   generated code; type-constrained checking halves compile errors and lifts
   repair 37% (PLDI 2025); format-constrained decoding degrades reasoning.
   Keep TypeScript syntax (LLP 0329.014 point 1), check after, never
   grammar-constrain the model.
5. **Stale knowledge is a first-order failure.** Deprecated-API use 25–38%
   (ICSE 2025); version-conditioned generation ~50% even for frontier models,
   and docs-RAG adds ≤10 points (GitChameleon 2.0); package hallucination
   5.2–21.7%. The agent is on exactly one version, and its docs, types, and
   refusals must describe that version; every deprecation ships a mechanical
   rewrite, not a prose warning.
6. **Every byte in front of the agent costs accuracy.** Performance degrades
   monotonically with context length across 18 models; mid-context evidence
   can be worse than closed-book (Lost in the Middle); context overflow is
   28.7–96.4% of SWE-Bench Pro failures; token use explains 80% of outcome
   variance; a `concise|detailed` response format cut 65% of tokens
   (Anthropic). Emit small; actionable line first; logs greppable, not
   printed.
7. **Context files steer behavior and cost, not success; compliance decays
   5.6% per step.** Controlled study: no significant success change and
   +20–23% cost; a PR-replay study: −28.6% runtime; a 1,650-session factorial:
   the dominant effect is within-session decay (Gloaguen; Lulla; McMillan).
   "Hooks are deterministic; CLAUDE.md is advisory." The scaffold's context
   block is a command sheet plus non-derivable gotchas, and every recurring
   "don't" becomes a gate.
8. **Agents read instruction files (60.5%) and almost never API references
   (1.3%); docs are consumed when routed.** A structured workflow went from
   13% adoption to routed once trigger tables handed it over; "specification
   staleness was the primary failure mode" (Codified Context); 97% of
   `llms.txt` files are never fetched, Claude Code the exception (Ahrefs).
   Examples carry the value (+83–220% on rare libraries) and set what is
   imitated — exemplar exposure cuts method spread 80–99% — so multiple
   spellings in examples become multiple spellings in apps.
9. **Reward hacking is real and instructions barely help.** ~50% cheating on
   impossible-spec tasks for GPT-5 / Opus 4.1; read-only tests are the
   workable middle ground, hidden tests cost legitimate performance, an
   explicit "abort" exit cut GPT-5 from 54% to 9% (ImpossibleBench); METR:
   telling the model not to cheat had "a nearly negligible effect."
   Derived invariant checks are unmodifiable; a cheap "blocked" exit exists.
10. **Environment setup is the scaling bottleneck; parallelism pays only with
    independent contexts.** Devin: repo setup is "the single highest-leverage
    thing"; git cannot capture `npm install` side effects; database branching
    is emerging as the undo primitive; +90% from parallel agents at 15× tokens
    when contexts are independent, coherence failures when they are shared
    (Anthropic vs Cognition; MAST). Zero shared state per worktree, forkable
    data, a fresh-context verifier.
11. **Full-app benchmarks fail on the backend.** WebGen-Bench: presentation
    43.7% vs data management 24.7% / interaction 20.6%; FullStack-Bench: the
    backend is the weakest layer. The framework should own exactly the layers
    agents fail at — schema, server logic, auth, live state — as declarative,
    checked surfaces.
12. **Measure with pass^k on the published pin.** τ-bench pass^8 < 25% where
    pass^1 > 60%; environment nondeterminism must be zero so the only
    nondeterminism in the loop is the model's.

## 5. The design: Snapback 2's loop

### 5.1 Principles

**P1 — One owner, one generation, one receipt.** `snapback dev` is the only
lifecycle process. It compiles, certifies, serves, watches, and applies
successors *as one generation-tracked transaction* (LLP 0329.002 §5) and
emits one acceptance receipt per generation. There is no bundler ownership,
no supervisor, no sidecar choreography the agent can see. A legal successor in
the same owner keeps identity, data, sessions, and every client attached:
"subscriptions rebind, identity does not" (LLP 0329.003 §4). *(M1, L16–L19.)*

**P2 — The push is the gate, and the gate answers in under a second.**
`snapback check` parses, type-checks, plans, certifies, and emits the client
in well under a second for a small app, refusing statically proven
contradictions with a compiling rewrite (LLP 0329.014.004 §7; LLP 0329.003
§4: "if you cannot know retainability without running the server, you have
reintroduced the hole"). `dev` runs `check` on every save; `deploy` is `check`
plus push. There is no layer that accepts what a later layer refuses. *(M5,
research 1–2, 4.)*

**P3 — The agent never carries generated bytes.** Types flow by TypeScript
inference from the authored modules; the IR, certificate, and client stubs are
a build cache under `.snapback/`, gitignored, regenerated by `check`, never
hand-edited, never committed. A fresh checkout runs `snapback check` once.
Deployment identity is the IR hash; the agent sees it only in `snapback
status`. *(M3.)*

**P4 — Nothing is silent.** Every read result carries `completeness`
(`complete | capped | partial`) and a typed `denied` verdict distinct from an
empty result; every write resolves to `committed | queued | rejected(code)`;
every owner exit carries a cause; every ceiling is in the certificate and
refused at `check`. `snapback why` explains any of these from the execution
trace. *(M5, LLP 0317 O15/O35, research 3.)*

**P5 — Refusals come in families and paste their rewrite.** About twenty
stable families (language, bound, authorization, schema, migration,
generation, sync, settlement, lifecycle, …) with structured fields — code,
family, site, rule, `rewrite` (compiles), `guide` anchor. Prose and internal
detail may vary; the family and the fields do not. An application refusal
(`refuse('DUPLICATE_HANDLE', …)`) reaches the client with its code intact.
Every recurring "don't" in a context file is converted into a refusal or a
gate; a warning is permission and is used only for advisory cost. *(M2, M5,
L25–L27, research 2, 7.)*

**P6 — Development has personas, not auth modes.** Authentication is always
on. The dev owner mints deterministic personas on demand (`--as alice`),
each with its own custody partition by construction; resetting data never
invalidates a persona; a browser in dev picks its persona per tab; a headless
client passes `as:` and production refuses it with a coded error. *(M4.)*

**P7 — One URL, one provider object, one hook.** The client contract is
`SNAPBACK_URL` (plus, in production, a publishable key). `dev` writes it into
`.env.local` unprompted. The server never spawns, proxies, or enumerates a
bundler; regeneration never touches a watched tree. "One env var is close
enough to zero-config" (LLP 0300). *(M1, L18.)*

**P8 — Docs are the compiler's facts, routed and version-locked.** The grammar
fits on one screen and in a system prompt; per-symbol reference is generated
from the language's `lib.d.ts`; per-app facts (`snapback guide app`) are
generated from the certificate; one runnable example per kind is generated
from the conformance fixtures; the wrong/right catalogue is generated from
the refusal families; a dozen hand-written recipes carry CI-executed
snippets and are bound to the package version. Diagnostics name the page.
There is no freshness date to maintain. *(M6, research 5, 7–8.)*

**P9 — Disposable and forkable by default.** State is keyed by project path,
ports are ephemeral, there are no global daemons, `snapback fork` snapshots
the store per attempt, previews expire, tokens are scoped to one environment,
and a first deploy needs no account (claim later). *(research 10; Neon,
Cloudflare, Turso, Instant.)*

**P10 — Done is a receipt from checks the agent cannot edit.** `snapback
test` boots a fresh isolated store, seeds it, runs derived invariant checks
generated from the rules and certificate (as-principal allowed/denied probes,
invalidation probes, replay/idempotency probes, kind conformance) plus the
agent's authored journeys through the real client, and writes a receipt.
Derived checks are read-only to the agent; a "blocked: cannot satisfy spec"
exit is cheap and legitimate. *(research 3, 9.)*

**P11 — Publish per landing; local is production.** One binary, one
publication script, one state machine; the benchmark runs against the
published pin with fresh agents; landed-but-unpublished fixes are a liability
(LLP 0041). Deploy exists in week one. *(M7.)*

**P12 — We build Snapback 2 on the same loop.** No committed generated
projections, no volatile coordinates in committed records, no gate bound to
an exact commit, typecheck plus affected tests in under a minute, a bounded
ticket working set, the pit-of-success triage rule, and the
cheapest-intervention ladder from week one. *(M8, §6.)*

An agent with only the task and the installed package. Every command is
non-interactive and returns JSON with `--json`; every line shown is the
`concise` format.

The Snapback 1 equivalent is fifteen steps across two terminals, seven
agent-owned files (186 lines of which are the acceptance journey), three
places to declare authority, and no step 15 (`current-loop-inventory` §1).

### 5.3 The authored surface, and what is not generated

The agent owns three kinds of file, all TypeScript, all in `snapback/`:

| File | Contents | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `schema.ts` | tables, indexes, **rules** (the only authorization), kinds (`channel`, `assets`, `schedule`, `identity`, …), retention, disclosure, `maintain` | LLP 0329.014.004 §2 dimensions; `auth: { password: true, guests: true }` replaces the 26-line `defineAuth` program |
| `*.ts` (functions) | `query` / `mutation` / `effect` programs in the bounded language; file-based namespaces (`todos.ts` → `api.todos.*`) | one profile; no plan algebra, no JS handler lane, no `reads:`/`writes:` (derived) |
| `tests/*.test.ts` | authored journeys through the real client with `as('alice')`, `clock`, `tick()` | optional; derived checks run regardless |
| `seed.ts` | optional deterministic seed | content-addressed; never a migration |

**What the client imports.** `import { api } from './snapback/todos'` — the
same module the server compiles. The `query()`/`mutation()` builders return
typed references; a bundler-agnostic transform (or the runtime itself, when
it detects a client environment) strips bodies for arms that do not evaluate
locally, and ships the IR to arms that do (the 7.8 KB evaluator twin,
LLP 0329.014 §8 S3). Types are `typeof` the authored module. Nothing under
`_generated/` or `generated/` exists to spell, commit, or forget.

**What `check` produces**, under `.snapback/build/<gen>/` and never committed:
the IR, the certificate (read sites, bounds, prediction sites, disclosures,
ceilings), client stubs for each arm, and `api.d.ts` for editors that want an
explicit file. A fresh worktree with no `.snapback/` runs `check` once (§5.10).

### 5.4 `snapback dev`: one owner

- **Starts in one second** from an installed binary: `check` → open the store
  → serve → write `SNAPBACK_URL` → print one line. No account, no prompt, no
  browser, no bundler.
- **Attaches idempotently.** A second `snapback dev` in the same project
  (any terminal, any worktree path — keyed by project, not port) attaches to
  the running owner and prints the same line; `snapback stop` is the only
  way an owner exits, other than a signal, and every exit writes a cause.
- **Hot successors with continuity.** On save, `check` runs; an additive
  successor is applied in place — same identity, same data, same personas and
  sessions, subscriptions rebind. A destructive change is refused at `check`
  with a migration plan file the agent can apply with
  `snapback dev --apply-migration <file>` (non-interactive) — never a
  prompt, never a silent drop, never a fresh database.
- **Survives EOF and owns no stdin.** Control is a Unix socket / loopback
  route with `--json` verbs (`status`, `personas`, `reset`, `fork`, `logs`,
  `why`, `trace`); stdin is ignored. Metro, Vite, and Xcode are the agent's
  processes, discovered only through `SNAPBACK_URL`.
- **Staging outside every watched tree**; atomic swap; no temp files under
  the project. (LLP 0310 F2/Track C; LLP 0317 O33.)
- **Ports and pins are generated facts.** `init` owns every compatible pin
  and the exact start command; `dev` picks a free port and reports it; a
  declared `--port` is honored or refused, never silently moved.
- **A dev overlay**, injected only in dev, shows the persona, connection
  state, generation, and the last refusal — the five state facts Diary 0056
  found "converted every subsequent screenshot into real evidence."

### 5.5 Identity in development: personas

Authentication is always on; there is no dev mode to outgrow. The dev owner
provides:

- `--as <name>` on `run`, `test`, `why`, `data`: a deterministic persona
  (`dev:alice`) created on first use, with its own custody partition, its own
  session, and — when the schema declares roles or memberships — a
  `snapback persona grant alice member:room-1` verb. Two headless clients in
  one process are two personas by construction; there is no shared default.
- In the browser: `?as=alice` on first load (or the overlay), stored per tab,
  never per origin, so two tabs are two people.
- In headless code: `createClient({ url, as: 'alice' })`; production refuses
  `as` with `E_AUTH_PERSONA_NOT_DEV`.
- `snapback reset` clears data and keeps personas; `snapback fork` copies
  both.
- Real auth flows (password, magic link, guest, social) run against the same
  owner through the same kinds, so the authored journey that signs up Alice
  for real is a few lines beside the persona shortcut.

### 5.6 Feedback: refusal families, no silent states, `why` and `trace`

**Refusal shape** (LLP 0010's contract, narrowed to families):

```json
{ "code": "E_BOUND_TAKE_REQUIRED", "family": "bound", "site": "snapback/todos.ts:14:9",
  "rule": "every scan names take", "rewrite": "scan('todos.byOwner', [viewer], { take: 100 })",
  "guide": "bound/scan" }
```

Roughly twenty families; a family's fields are stable across versions and
across the server, client, CLI, and MCP envelopes. `check` refuses proven
contradictions (a grant over immutable delivery, a `take` over a ceiling, a
composite write key the language cannot express) with a rewrite; runtime
enforcement remains for the rest.

**No silent states.**

| Surface | Typed states | Never |
|---|---|---|
| read | `complete`, `capped(k)`, `partial(reason)`, `denied(rule)` | an empty array standing in for any of them |
| write | `committed(version)`, `queued(reason, position)`, `rejected(code)` | a resolved promise for a queued intent |
| subscription | `live`, `stale(since)`, `refused(code)`, `partial` | blank after insert; a hook that reports `ready` and `disconnected` forever |
| owner | `ready`, `applying(gen)`, `refused(plan)`, `exited(cause)` | exit 0 with an empty stdout |
| deploy | `live(url)`, `blocked(gaps)` | `activationEligible: false` with no path |

**`snapback why <op> --as <persona>`** re-runs a read under a persona and
prints the rule verdicts per row, the read sites examined against the
certificate, and — for a live query — the last commit that touched its trace
and whether it was delivered. **`snapback trace <mutation|subscription>`**
prints the causal chain: intent → authority → commit → invalidation → delivery
per subscriber (LLP 0219.000's receipt, first-party). Both are bounded and
capped like every other verb (Neon's `inspect db` rule: "every command
observes and reports … heavy results are capped").

### 5.7 Verification: `snapback test` and the receipt

- **Boot-run-exit in one verb.** Fresh isolated store, deterministic seed,
  controllable clock (`ctx.now()` is an input; `tick()` advances schedules),
  seeded ids, then exit with the receipt (the Firebase `emulators:exec`
  shape, Encore's "never mock infra").
- **Derived checks** are generated from the schema and certificate and cannot
  be edited: for every rule, an allowed and a denied probe as a real
  principal; for every live query, an invalidation probe (write a row it
  should see, assert delivery; write one it should not, assert silence); for
  every mutation, a replay/idempotency probe; for every kind, its
  conformance; for every ceiling, a refusal probe. This is the "as anon, can I
  read row X?" check the app-builders learned they needed *before* publish.
- **Authored journeys** run through the real client arm(s) — the harness
  reuses the app's own client, not a test double — and the receipt records
  which arm proved which journey.
- **`--changed`** maps the edit to the affected checks (schema → its rules
  and queries; a function → its probes and journeys); the inner loop never
  runs the suite.
- **Budgets** on the templates: `check` < 1 s; a single journey < 5 s; the
  full `test` < 30 s.
- **The receipt** names the binary digest, generation, commands run, exit
  codes, per-check verdicts, arms, and the scope diff (files changed outside
  the task's named scope). `passed-without-acceptance` does not exist; zero
  checks cannot pass.
- **`blocked`** is a first-class exit: `snapback test --blocked "reason"`
  writes a receipt whose verdict is `blocked`, so the honest stop is cheaper
  than the dishonest green (ImpossibleBench).

### 5.8 Docs: the compiler's facts

| Layer | Source | Size | How the agent reaches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar | hand-written, one screen | ≤ 2 KB | in the skill and the `AGENTS.md` block |
| Symbol reference | generated from the language `lib.d.ts` | per symbol | `snapback guide <symbol>` |
| App facts | generated from the certificate: tables, rules, ops, bounds, personas | per app | `snapback guide app` |
| Kind examples | generated from conformance fixtures; CI-executed | one per kind | `snapback guide <kind>` |
| Wrong/right | generated from refusal families | one pair per family | the refusal's `guide` field |
| Recipes | ~12 hand-written pages (auth flows, uploads, offline, presence, pagination, deploy, migrations, Expo, Swift) with CI-executed snippets | ≤ 4 KB each | `snapback guide <intent>` |

All of it is bound to the installed binary — served from the package, not
embedded in a 20 MB binary (LLP 0314) — and fetchable as Markdown
(`/guide/<page>.md`, `llms.txt`). The `AGENTS.md` managed block is a command
sheet and gotchas, under forty lines, because context files "steer behavior
and cost, not success." The skill is installed by `init` and versioned with
the package (Electric's pattern), and carries an ordered decision procedure
("check before run; run --as before assuming a rule is wrong; why before
polling"). There are no calendar review dates: a page is either generated or
its snippets ran in CI on this version.

### 5.9 Deploy, previews, tokens, rollback, export

- **`snapback deploy`** pushes IR + assets to a target and returns a URL. Same
  binary locally and hosted; `check` is the gate; additive migrations apply in
  place; destructive ones apply from a plan file the agent wrote and `check`
  validated.
- **Previews:** `--preview --expires <ttl>` creates a disposable environment
  with a scoped token (`.env.preview`); expiry is the default, not an option.
- **Account-less first deploy** with a claim code (Neon Claimable /
  Cloudflare Temporary Accounts); TTL and size caps bound abuse.
- **Rollback includes data.** Every deploy snapshots; `snapback rollback`
  restores code, schema, and data without changing the URL or token.
- **Secrets** are name-level: the human sets them (`snapback secret set NAME`
  reads from stdin or a secure prompt only when a TTY exists); the agent can
  list names and never values.
- **Export** always works: `snapback export` produces schema, functions, data,
  and assets in a form `init` can re-import — the migration path *out* is a
  trust feature (Lovable Cloud's lack of one is a cautionary tale).
- **Metering with caps.** Per-environment read/write/egress caps with a
  refusal, not a bill.

### 5.10 Isolation and parallel agents

- **Zero shared mutable state per worktree.** Store, build cache, receipts,
  and personas live under `.snapback/` keyed by project path; two worktrees
  are two apps; nothing global. A fresh worktree is `snapback check` away
  from `dev` — no submodules, no Hermes, no prepared artifacts.
- **`snapback fork <name>`** snapshots the store (SQLite backup / reflink)
  so an attempt can be discarded; **`snapback reset`** returns to seed.
- **Multiple owners** coexist on one machine on ephemeral ports.
- **A read-only auditor** is a documented pattern: `snapback test` and
  `snapback why` run in a fresh context against the receipt of the writing
  session.
- **MCP** is the same verbs, thin, read-only by default, with write tools
  split by blast radius and production tokens read-only and non-PII unless
  explicitly `--dangerously-…`.

### 5.11 The verb table

Fifteen verbs replace 23 packaged plus 38 native commands.

## 6. Building Snapback 2: the internal loop

The same principles, applied to ourselves, because M8 shows the engineers'
loop and the agents' loop fail the same way.

- **One binary, few crates, no JS engine in the default path.** The data plane
  is the Rust IR interpreter inside the SQLite transaction (LLP 0329.014
  point 9). Effects run in a sandbox that is a feature, not a bootstrap
  precondition; whether that sandbox is a JS engine at all is decided by
  measurement (§9.7). `cargo build` on a fresh clone needs nothing outside
  crates.io.
- **The fast signal cannot be switched off.** Typecheck plus affected tests
  in under a minute on every landing; the complete battery on a schedule; its
  reds are tickets, never retroactive vetoes (LLP 0189/0232, kept). No gate
  binds to an exact commit; gates key on content scope.
- **No committed generated projections; no volatile coordinates.** Reference
  docs, registries, and manifests are built, not committed (LLP 0326's rule as
  a design invariant). The diagnostics registry is a list of families and
  codes, never file:line.
- **Publish per landing.** One script, one state machine, one receipt; the
  published pin is what the benchmark runs; a landed-unpublished fix is a
  ticket.
- **The benchmark/diary loop in week one** (LLP 0329.001 §5): Clique plus two
  private-data apps, fresh agents from three model families, on the published
  pin, against Convex, Supabase, and PocketBase, weekly; diaries as evidence,
  never as advice; N=1 existence, N≥3 ratchet, N≥8 ordering.
- **Triage by the pit-of-success standard** (LLP 0310) and the
  cheapest-intervention ladder (LLP 0317.000 P2: impossible → refused at
  `check` with the rewrite → route the page → write the page → write the
  recipe). A row closed at "wrote a page" when "refuse at check" was available
  is a partial.
- **Bounded working set** (LLP 0328): a generated ticket index, one metadata
  contract, a 64-link current overlay, one idempotent writer for automated
  producers.
- **Dogfooding is structural.** The repository's own demos run on `snapback
  dev`; the acceptance harness is `snapback test`; the guide's snippets are
  `snapback test` journeys. If our loop is bad, we feel it first.
- **Measure, then change** (LLP 0316/0327): build-loop instrumentation from
  day one — link targets, binary size, no-op check time, landing-to-publish
  time — with thresholds that refuse growth instead of noticing it 46% later.

## 9. Open author decisions

1. **Types by inference or a generated `api.d.ts`?** Recommendation:
   inference from the authored modules (Instant's shape), with the build cache
   providing an explicit `.d.ts` for editors; nothing committed. Risk: bundler
   transforms for body-stripping across Vite, Metro, and Swift; mitigated by
   the runtime-detects-client fallback.
2. **Keep a served prose guide at all?** Recommendation: ~12 recipes with
   CI-executed snippets plus generated facts; serve from the package, not the
   binary; no complete-map obligation (LLP 0061 Goal 1 relaxed as LLP 0259
   already did for first reads).
3. **Derived checks: hidden, or visible read-only?** Recommendation: visible
   and read-only (the ImpossibleBench middle ground); hidden checks cost
   legitimate performance.
4. **Primary client arm.** LLP 0000 says Contract/Exact first; every
   benchmark measures React web and Expo. Recommendation: one hook per
   framework (LLP 0329.014 §9), React web first for the benchmark, Expo
   second, Swift and Contract on their own evidence.
5. **URL-first only.** Recommendation: ratify LLP 0300's inversion as the sole
   lifecycle; retire supervised Expo (LLP 0181, LLP 0310.000 Ruling 2) as a
   design line — the everyday simulator command
   (`issues/shelved/20260819-expo-everyday-simulator-launch.md`) becomes a recipe that
   launches the agent's own processes.
6. **Personas in production.** Never; refused by construction. Confirm.
7. **JS engine in the effect runtime (LLP 0329.014 OQ8).** Recommendation:
   the default bootstrap has none; measure the effect corpus after the
   language spike and decide between a sandboxed JS engine as an optional
   feature and a host-invoke kind.
8. **New repository or in-tree successor.** LLP 0316 planned "no rewrite, no
   `snapback2`"; LLP 0329 and the panel argue the substrate choices must be
   made once, fresh. This document is agnostic; the loop design holds either
   way. The author decides.
9. **Week-one hosted target.** Self-host binary plus one hosted target with
   previews and claim codes; which provider is out of scope here but the
   verb must exist in week one.
10. **Language-side dependencies** (rule bound model, `maxOfflineAge`,
    spelling bakeoff, named operations only) — LLP 0329.014 §Open questions;
    the loop assumes their answers only through the certificate.

## 10. Sequencing

The loop is built *before* features, on top of spike S1's interpreter and S3's
evaluator twin:

1. **Week 1 — the loop skeleton.** `init`, `dev` (attach-idempotent,
   EOF-proof, hot additive successors), `check` (certificate + rewrites),
   `run --as`, `test` (fresh store, derived checks from rules, receipt),
   `deploy --preview`, `guide` (grammar + symbols + app facts), `doctor`. The
   benchmark harness runs Clique M1 against the published pin with fresh
   agents on day five. Gates: `check` < 1 s; `init` → `dev` ≤ 10 s; zero
   silent states in M1.
2. **Week 2 — feedback.** `why`, `trace`, the dev overlay, refusal families
   with wrong/right pairs, `logs`, MCP as the same verbs. Gate: correction
   loops ≤ 1 on M1–M3.
3. **Week 3 — isolation and deploy.** `fork`/`reset`, previews with TTL and
   scoped tokens, rollback with data, export, secrets, the everyday-simulator
   recipe. Gate: pass^3 ≥ 0.8 on M1–M3.
4. **Ongoing — the internal loop** (§6) from the first commit; the weekly
   benchmark; diaries as evidence; the cheapest-intervention ladder as triage.

- **High** on the mechanisms M1–M8; each has multiple independent
  attestations across diaries, tickets, and LLPs.

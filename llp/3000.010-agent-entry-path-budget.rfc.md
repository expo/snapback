# LLP 3000.010: Re-budget the agent entry path — what a builder reads before line 1

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Draft
**Systems:** The published package's authoring surface; the guide cards and `snapback4 guide`; diagnostics that assert ceilings (`check` notes); LLP 3000.009's server surface inside the same package; the feeds recipe; the Clique calibration instrument
**Author:** Claude Opus 5 (for Charlie Cheever)
**Date:** 2026-09-18

**Revised:** 2026-09-18 (initial: the fitted calibration series, the surface delta 0.2.16 → 0.2.28, the four causes and their separate owners)
**Related:** [LLP 0259](./0259-agent-first-read-budget.rfc.md) (the same re-budget, performed once for Snapback 1 — this RFC is its application to Snapback 4), [LLP 3000](./3000-snapback4.explainer.md) (root), [LLP 3000.007 §20.22](./3000.007-relaxed-quarry-and-explicit-residency.rfc.md) (total reads; the bounds probe this argues from), [LLP 3000.009](./3000.009-general-purpose-backend.rfc.md) (the second deployment shape, §7.1), [LLP 3000.007.001](./3000.007.001-maintained-columns.rfc.md) (the engine work that would shrink the feeds recipe), [`benchmarks/TREND.md`](../benchmarks/TREND.md) and [`benchmarks/scripts/score-trend.mjs`](../benchmarks/scripts/score-trend.mjs) (the fitted series, regenerated per calibration), [the 0.2.28 record](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-17-snapback4-clique-astra-0.2.28/README.md)

## Summary

Across ten Astra-medium Clique calibrations (0.2.16 → 0.2.28), **every category
that measures the cost of *using* the surface declines, and no capability
regresses.** `authorization` is the only category rising. The cause is not
defects and not documentation quality — the scorer never once establishes that a
document misled. It is **surface volume**: the guide grew 138 KB → 339 KB and
diagnostic codes 81 → 141 in two weeks.

This RFC says what to do about it, and its central claim is that **the problem
has four distinct causes with four distinct owners, and only one of them is
documentation.** Treating all four as a docs problem is how twelve waves
answered a comprehension cost with more guidance.

It also says what *not* to do. It does not propose splitting Snapback 4 into
separate products (database / feeds / actions): the integration is what the
record shows it winning on, and the split would manufacture the seams Supabase
builders pay for. It does not propose reverting to 0.2.16: the declines are
surface-side, while the correctness landings since — the horizon P1, the
merge-position P1, retained history — repair silent wrong answers that 0.2.16
still has.

Snapback 1 met this exact problem and solved it in [LLP 0259](./0259-agent-first-read-budget.rfc.md).
Its answer was not a capability split. It was a **re-budgeted first read**, and
its fourth diagnostic point describes this situation one product later:
*"Competing experimental surfaces must be absent from the start packet."*

Confidence is **0.85** on the diagnosis, **0.6** on the relative sizes of the
four causes (§5), which the experiment in §9 exists to settle.

| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |

| queries_and_live | 89 → 80 | −8.8 | −8.3 | falling |
| setup_and_docs | 93 → 88 | −5.1 | −4.7 | falling |
| data_modeling | 88 → 84 | −4.1 | −2.3 | falling |
| operations | 88 → 85 | −3.4 | −2.9 | falling |
| media_assets | 97 → 96 | −1.6 | −0.8 | falling |
| **overall** | 90 → 88 | **−2.7** | **−0.5** | **rests on one run** |
| authorization | 92 → 97 | +4.3 | +4.9 | **rising** |
| background_work, client_integration, writes_and_transactions, offline_sync, diagnostics_dev_loop | — | — | — | flat |

Two things this table is careful about, both of which earlier readings got wrong:

- **The overall number is not the evidence.** Its verdict rests entirely on
  0.2.28, whose own record declines to call it a regression: the 54,982 ms cold
  feed behind it was measured on a host at load 56–70, in a window where a
  600-second suite took 3,239 seconds, and it is browser cold *acquisition*, not
  engine reads — which were `insights p95 3.41 ms` and `trending p95 20.32 ms` in
  that same window. **The six category verdicts survive dropping it.** They are
  the finding.
- **Nothing regressed.** No category shows a capability going backwards, and the
  one rising category is the dimension this backend is designed to win.

## 2. What the scorer actually charges

The holistic scorer reads the builder's own narrative and charges the backend
for defects, missing capabilities and documentation that *misled* — never for
agent or environment loops. Its notes name what it charged. Across ten runs,
`setup_and_docs` is charged for **volume**, and repeatedly exonerated on
quality:

> "the **2,600-line README** required orientation" (0.2.22) · "Reading the
> **extensive surface** imposed substantial upfront learning" (0.2.24) ·
> "Discovering the complete workflow required **the README, client reference,
> keyword cards, and generated API**" (0.2.25) · "comprehensive but
> **orientation-heavy**" (0.2.26) · "required navigating **README, guide cards,
> worker/wire references and generated types**" (0.2.28)

And, in as many words: **"no misleading documentation was established"**
(0.2.16, 0.2.26). The documents are not wrong. There are more of them each week,
because there is more product each week.

`queries_and_live` is charged for something different — the machinery a builder
must construct to stay inside the limits:

> "feeds require **substantial bounded read models**" (0.2.23) · "N_WIDE_MERGE
> for 1,000-chat search, incomplete meter cuts, a UI **limited to held-message
> search**" (0.2.24) · "the feed recipe required **substantial serving
> structures**; M5 search required complete local acquisition or a **two-cursor
> server walk**, with explicit completeness obligations" (0.2.27)

## 3. The surface delta, 0.2.16 → 0.2.28

| | 0.2.16 | 0.2.28 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| guide bytes | 137,920 | **338,711** |
| distinct `N_`/`E_` codes in `snapback4-lang` | 81 | **141** |

The sixty new codes are not spread evenly. Classified by the LLP they serve:

| cluster | codes | serves |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `E_OIDC_*`, `E_ROUTE_*`, `E_STEP_*`, `E_SERVER_*` | **29 (48%)** | LLP 3000.009, the general-purpose backend |
| `E_ACTION_*` | 8 (13%) | worker actions (LLP 3000.007 §9.2) |
| `E_EFFECT_*`, `E_WRITE_*`, `E_QUARRY_*`, `E_CLOCK_*`, and singles | 23 (39%) | the declarative product |

**The Clique brief puts the first cluster explicitly out of scope.** In its own
words: *"Out of scope everywhere: email sending (so: no verification, no
password reset), SMS, **OAuth/social login**…"* It never asks for a webhook, an
HTTP route or a service account. Corroborating the brief, across all twelve
retained builder records: `oidc` appears **0 times**, `service seat` **0 times**,
and the `search_all` card **0 times**. Every raw hit for "action" and "native"
turned out to be English prose — *"per-action receipts"*, *"iOS native
execution"* — not the keywords. LLP 3000.007 §10.3 already states the same
result from the other side: *"none of these three apps used an action."*

LLP 3000.009 was accepted in direction on **2026-09-15**, the day 0.2.16
shipped, which is where the series peaks. That is a coincidence of dates, not a
demonstration of cause; it is recorded because it is checkable and because it
names the fork.

## 4. Capability is the wrong axis

A natural proposal is to split the product by capability — database, feeds,
actions. The milestone evidence refuses it. Which surfaces each builder reached
for, by milestone, counted over ten diaries:

| surface | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 | M8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| schema / rules | 2 | 1 | 7 | 3 | · | 1 | 4 |
| sync / residency | · | 7 | 7 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 4 |
| feeds recipe | · | 8 | · | · | · | 1 | 6 |
| assets / media | 4 | 8 | · | 9 | 10 | 5 | 5 |
| prediction / offline | · | 3 | 3 | 3 | 10 | 8 | 9 |
| search | · | · | · | · | 10 | 2 | 3 |
| jobs / maintains | · | 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 | · | 9 |
| effects / push | · | · | · | · | 1 | 9 | 4 |
| actions / routes / services | · | · | · | · | · | · | · |

`sync/residency` and `prediction` appear at M2, M3, M5, M6 *and* M8. `feeds`
appears at M2, goes quiet, returns at M8. **There is no feeds phase.** A
capability split cuts across every milestone rather than along one, and hands
the builder three doors they must walk through at M2. The only cluster that
separates cleanly is the one that never appears at all.

What separates is therefore not *which capability* but **whether it is on the
default path**, which is LLP 0259's axis: *first read* versus *lookup*.

## 5. Diagnosis: four causes, four owners

| # | Cause | Owner | Shape of the fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | A second deployment shape ships inside the package a chat builder installs | LLP 3000.009 | a product fence |
| C2 | The default path is a linear read of everything | this RFC | a read budget |
| C3 | `feeds.md` is 59,930 bytes — **17.7% of all guide bytes for one feature** — and 12 of 12 builders read it | LLP 3000.007 §20.22, LLP 3000.007.001 | engine work that shrinks the recipe |
| C4 | `check` asserts ceilings the builder never reaches | diagnostics | say less, and only what was measured |

C4 deserves its own line because it is invisible from the documentation. LLP
3000.007 §20.22's probe against the published binary found: **"No bound was hit
at runtime in any of the last four lanes."** No `complete: false`, no `E_BOUND`,
no cap refusal in any evidence directory. What the scorer charged was
anticipatory — notes read, cards read, designs changed in advance, limits
disclosed in the final report. The engine announces theoretical ceilings, the
builder writes them down, and the instrument charges what was written.

**Only C2 is a documentation change.** Answering all four with more or better
guidance is what the last twelve waves did.

## 6. Proposal

### 6.1 C1 — fence the second deployment shape

Routes, server handlers, OIDC, services and seats, and steps leave the chat
builder's map entirely: a separate entry point, a separate guide index, a
separate brief, and absent from `snapback4 guide`'s default listing, the default
generated types and the default error surface. Whether that is a separate
package, a capability flag at `init`, or a served index is LLP 3000.009's call,
not this RFC's — what this RFC asks is that **a builder on the declarative path
never learns these exist.**

This is the one place this RFC proposes splitting anything, and the axis is
deployment shape, not capability.

Worker actions (`E_ACTION_*`, 8 codes, `actions.rs` at 18,306 bytes) are the
boundary case: they belong to the declarative product by LLP 3000.007 §9.2, and
no builder has used one in twelve runs. Recommendation: fence with C1's cluster
and let a reference application that needs one pull it back.

### 6.2 C2 — the read budget

Three tiers, assigned from §4's milestone evidence:

- **Tier 0, before line 1** (target ≤ 20 KB): the mental model, schema, rules,
  indexes — everything M1 needs. This is LLP 0259's start packet, per template.
- **Tier 1, on demand, one page at a time**: sync/residency, reads and
  pagination, prediction and offline, assets, search, feeds, jobs, maintains,
  effects. Reached by `snapback4 guide <word>`, which is already how builders
  behave once they know what to ask for.
- **Tier 2, absent**: §6.1's cluster.

LLP 0259's rules carry over unchanged and are not re-derived here: the capability
index is *"a good registry and a bad novel"*; honesty banners and GAP fences stay
in the corpus and out of the packet; budget the bytes the carrier actually sends,
because heading-level folds save nothing when the carrier delivers whole files.

### 6.3 C3 — shrink the recipe rather than the reading of it

No reading order fixes a 60 KB recipe that everybody needs. The feeds recipe is
the paved workaround for what the engine does not yet do, and the work that
would retire it is already commissioned: §20.22's total-reads invariant, and
maintained columns (LLP 3000.007.001, READY from both families, unbuilt). This
RFC adds one obligation to that work: **a landing that removes a recipe reports
the card bytes it removed**, so the surface budget is a tracked number rather
than a side effect.

### 6.4 C4 — assert only what was measured

`check` stops asserting a ceiling the program has not been shown to reach.
N_WIDE_MERGE's static `lanes > 50` and its relatives move to `check --cost`,
which prints cost as arithmetic rather than as a warning, and `dev`/`test`
report cuts that actually happened — which §20.22 item 4 already built. The
narrower rule this RFC proposes: **a default-path note must name a measured
consequence, not a possible one.**

## 7. What this RFC does not propose

- **Splitting the product into database / feeds / actions.** §4 refuses it on
  the milestone evidence, and the record refuses it on value: authorization is
  the rising category; Convex's builders wrote scrypt by hand; Supabase's *"paid
  for four products' seams"* (LLP 3000.007 §3.4). The integration is the product.
- **Reverting to 0.2.16.** It would return `authorization` from 95 to 88 and
  reintroduce the horizon P1 — a device silently skipping rows while reporting
  `complete: true`, which that ticket says is *"older than this program"* — along
  with the merge-position P1 and the retained-history losses. The declines are
  surface-side; the engine landings are not what costs.
- **A fifth documentation wave.** C1, C3 and C4 are not documentation.
- **Any new ceiling, rule, card word or disclosure boundary.**

## 8. Acceptance

1. A cold builder on the declarative template can reach a compiling schema
   having read Tier 0 only, measured in bytes delivered, on the fixture.
2. `snapback4 guide` with no argument lists no Tier 2 word; a Tier 2 word
   prints how to opt into that shape rather than its card.
3. The generated `api.ts` and the default error surface of a declarative
   template name no Tier 2 code.
4. Guide bytes on the declarative path are reported at the landing, before and
   after, as a tracked number.
5. No correctness landing since 0.2.16 is reverted to achieve any of the above.

## 9. Predictions, stated before the next calibration

Each is falsifiable by one run of the existing instrument, and the point of
stating them here is that a wave which fails its prediction has learned
something rather than shipped something.

| # | If | then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | C1 and C2 land and the diagnosis is right | `setup_and_docs` recovers toward its 0.2.16 fit (93 → 96) within two calibrations |
| P2 | C1 and C2 land and `setup_and_docs` does **not** move | the cost is in the used surface, not the unused surface; C3 is the whole story and §6.2 was wasted |
| P3 | C4 lands | `queries_and_live` recovers *without* any engine change, which would measure how much of its −8.3 was announcement rather than obligation |
| P4 | C3 lands (a recipe retired into the engine) | `queries_and_live` and `data_modeling` both move; neither moves on C1/C2 alone |

P2 is the one worth wanting. It is the cheapest way to be told the read budget
is not the answer.

## 10. Sequence

**A — C4**, the smallest and the only one needing no product decision. It also
isolates the announcement effect before anything else moves.
**B — C1**, on LLP 3000.009's ruling about how the fence is drawn.
**C — C2**, which is cheap once B has decided what is out of the map.
**D — C3**, already commissioned elsewhere; this RFC adds only the reporting
obligation of §6.3.

A calibration runs after B and again after D, so P1–P4 are each attributable.

## 11. Questions for the author

1. **§6.1 — how is the fence drawn?** A separate package, a capability flag at
   `init`, or a served index. This RFC asks only that the fence exist.
2. **§6.1 — do worker actions go behind it?** They belong to this product by
   LLP 3000.007 §9.2 and no builder has used one in twelve runs.
3. **§6.2 — is Tier 0 per template, as LLP 0259 ruled for Snapback 1, or one
   shared packet?** Snapback 4 has fewer templates and the answer may differ.
4. **§6.4 — may a default-path note ever assert an unreached ceiling?** The
   narrow rule proposed here would retire several notes that exist today.
5. **Does the programme accept P2** — that a null result on `setup_and_docs`
   after C1 and C2 retires the read-budget theory rather than extending it?

## Non-claims

- The fitted series is N = 1 per version, read for shape. It is calibration, not
  a ranking and not a magnitude claim about any backend.
- LLP 3000.009's acceptance date coinciding with the series peak is recorded
  because it is checkable, not as a demonstration of cause.
- No causal claim is made that surface volume *produced* the decline. What is
  established is that the scorer charges volume, exonerates quality, and that
  48% of the new diagnostic surface serves a product the brief forbids. §9 is
  how that becomes a causal claim or stops being one.
- Nothing here is built, commissioned or ruled. This RFC changes no ceiling, no
  rule, no card word and no disclosure boundary.

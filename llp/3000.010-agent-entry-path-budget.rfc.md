## Summary

The Clique calibration series has now been misread three times in four days, each
time by someone doing the obvious thing with it, and each time the error made the
programme's work look worse or better than it was:

1. Twelve waves compared each run with the one before it and read a series as
   flat. (Corrected 2026-09-17, by hand, in a run record.)
2. The first revision of *this* document fitted the series but let a filename
   convention pick where it started — at the maximum. Everything downstream said
   "falling."
3. Both of the above used a "beyond noise" bar that is not a test of the slope.

Under a proper test of the slope across **all twelve runs**, almost nothing is
established: overall is flat, and no category's trend survives a correction for
having tested thirteen of them.

**What the record does show clearly is a step.** `setup_and_docs` changes
**level** at 0.2.16, from a mean of 78.5 across the two runs before it to 90.4
across the ten after: **+11.9 at t = 11.35**. Overall moves +3.6 at t = 4.59.
**These are the only results anywhere in this record that survive the
multiplicity correction**, and they are not trends — they are a one-time level
change, at the release carrying the wave the author commissioned with the words
*"solve every agent ergonomics problem with snapback4 holding back the astra
medium clique scores."* (0.2.16's own 96 is a spike 2.7 sd above the new level;
the durable gain is the +11.9, not the +17.)

**And the scorer says what changed** (§4). The two runs before the step each
carry *affirmed documentation defects* — "undocumented", "contradicted the
guide's apparent promise", "the misleading presence/prediction recipe",
"the prediction guide card was **absent**". Every run from 0.2.16 on carries at
most a discoverability gap, and several carry the phrase *"no established
misleading documentation."* Each of the four charges maps to a named landing.

And the step refutes the explanation this document previously offered. **Across
it, the guide grew 31%** — 105,076 → 137,920 bytes — **and diagnostic codes grew
21%, while the score rose 17 points.** Volume went up and the score went up, by
the largest margin in the record. Surface size is not what the instrument is
measuring.

This RFC therefore proposes no fence, no read budget and no deletion. It
proposes **rules for reading the instrument**, records what survives them, and
and asks whether closing the **current** charge list reproduces the step, which
is the one experiment this instrument is powerful enough to run.

Confidence is **0.9** that the three method failures are real and the rules
prevent them; **0.95** that the step is real; **0.75** on §5's
reading of it, which has a strong item-level mechanism and a two-run "before".

## 1. Three ways this series has been misread

**Comparing neighbours.** Each wave read its run against the previous run.
`setup_and_docs` went 85 → 89 → 92 across three runs and was written up
repeatedly as the programme's success; over a longer window it is not a trend at
all. The 2026-09-17 correction found this and stated the lesson exactly: *"The
error was comparing neighbours instead of fitting the series."*

**Letting a filename choose the window.** `score-trend.mjs` read only
`FINAL.md`. The two earlier calibrations of the same instrument, 0.2.9 and
0.2.12, write their scores to `holistic-score.md`, so the series silently began
at **0.2.16 — its maximum.** A window that starts at the peak cannot come out
anything but falling. Including them:

| | from 0.2.16 | all 12 runs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| overall | −2.7 | **+0.9** |
| setup_and_docs | −5.1 | **+5.6** |
| queries_and_live | −8.8 | **−0.0** |

Leave-one-out could not catch it, because dropping a single run never moves the
window's start. Found by the GPT family's review of this document's first
revision; both failures are now guarded in §6.

**Using a bar that is not a test.** Both readings called a category moving when
its drift exceeded one standard deviation of its own series. That statistic mixes
the trend into its own noise estimate, and it is *anti-conservative on a
low-variance series* — `media_assets` at 97 → 96 passes it because the series
barely moves. Found by the Grok family. The correct comparison is the fitted
slope against **its own standard error**, using the residual spread about the
fitted line.

## 2. What survives a proper test

Twelve runs, ordinary least squares, slope compared with its standard error
(`|t| > 2`, about p < 0.05, df = 10):

| category | drift | residual sd | \|t\| | reads |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| data_modeling | −4.7 | 1.87 | 2.73 | falling |
| offline_sync | +6.2 | 2.78 | 2.44 | rising |
| diagnostics_dev_loop | +8.7 | 4.13 | 2.29 | rising |
| authorization | +4.7 | 2.40 | 2.12 | rising |
| client_integration | −2.0 | 1.02 | 2.09 | falling |
| scale_and_limits | −10.4 | 5.87 | 1.93 | flat |
| setup_and_docs | +5.6 | 5.26 | 1.15 | flat |
| queries_and_live | −0.0 | 5.41 | 0.01 | flat |
| **overall** | **+0.9** | **2.32** | **0.43** | **flat** |
| operations, media_assets, background_work, writes_and_transactions | | | < 2 | flat |

Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters.

- **Overall is flat.** No reading of this series supports a claim that the
  product is getting better or worse on the whole.
- **More categories read rising than falling**, which no earlier reading of this
  series reported.
- **None of it survives multiplicity.** Thirteen categories tested at p < 0.05
  yields about 0.65 false positives by chance; a Bonferroni correction needs
  `|t| > 3.5` and **nothing reaches it.** The five marginal results above should
  be treated as directions to look, not findings.

`scale_and_limits` deserves one line of its own: its −10.4 is dominated by
0.2.28's 65, and that run's own record declines to call it a regression — the
54,982 ms cold feed behind it was measured on a host at load 56–70, in a window
where a 600-second suite took 3,239 seconds, and it is browser cold acquisition,
not engine reads, which were `insights p95 3.41 ms` in that same window.

## 3. The step: the only thing here that survives multiplicity

The one large, unambiguous movement in the record is not a trend:

```
setup_and_docs    78  79  96  92  90  90  92  90  85  89  92  88
                       ^^^^^^^
queries_and_live  74  80  91  89  84  91  82  78  86  82  83  81
overall           85  86  90  91  88  91  88  88  91  90  89  85
```

Treated as a level change rather than a slope — two runs before, ten after —
Welch's t on the difference of means:

| category | before (0.2.9, 0.2.12) | after (0.2.16 →) | step | t |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **setup_and_docs** | 78.5 | 90.4 | **+11.9** | **11.35** |
| **overall** | 85.5 | 89.1 | **+3.6** | **4.59** |
| queries_and_live | 77.0 | 84.7 | +7.7 | 2.33 |
| data_modeling | 88.5 | 86.1 | −2.4 | −2.66 |
| offline_sync | 86.0 | 92.6 | +6.6 | 1.64 |
| authorization | 92.5 | 94.5 | +2.0 | 1.14 |

The first two clear the corrected threshold of §2 by a wide margin; nothing
else does. **The instrument has recorded exactly one event, and it is
positive.** Everything after it — the entire period this programme has been
worrying about — is inside the noise.

Two honest limits. 0.2.16's own 96 is a spike **2.7 sd above the level that
follows it**, so the durable gain is +11.9, not +17. And the "before" group is
**two runs**, perfectly confounded with being the two earliest; a level change
that large is not plausibly noise, but two observations cannot separate "the
defects were fixed" from anything else that distinguishes the first two runs.
§4 is what makes the first reading the better one, and §8 asks for the check
that would settle it.

**Across that step the surface grew:**

| | 0.2.9 | 0.2.12 | **0.2.16** | 0.2.28 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| guide bytes | 88,689 | 105,076 | **137,920** | 338,711 |
| distinct `N_`/`E_` codes | 64 | 67 | **81** | 141 |
| setup_and_docs | 78 | 79 | **96** | 88 |

The guide grew **31%** and the codes **21%** in the release where the score rose
17 points. **The hypothesis that surface volume drives this score is refuted by
the largest movement the instrument has recorded.** The first revision of this
document argued the opposite and was wrong; §7 records what else it got wrong.

What was in 0.2.16 is on the record: it carries LLP 3000.007 §20, the wave the
author commissioned on 2026-09-14 with *"solve every agent ergonomics problem
with snapback4 holding back the astra medium clique scores"* — an inventory of
what the scorer had actually charged across four prior lanes, answered item by
item.

## 5. What this instrument appears to measure

Put §3 and §4 together and the reading is narrow: **`setup_and_docs` responds to
documentation *defects* — a document that is wrong, or absent at the point of
need — and not to documentation *size*.** That is what the rubric says it does;
it charges "documentation that misled," which is a defect category. The surface
grew 31% across the step and has grown 145% since, and volume is named in almost
every note without the score leaving the high eighties.

The step is what deliberately closing a list of observed charges looks like. The
twelve waves since have added capability against roadmaps rather than against
charges, and the series has not moved outside its noise since.

**What is solid:** the level change (t = 11.35), the change in what the notes
affirm, and the four item-level charge-to-landing matches. **What is not:** the
"before" group is two runs. A second instance would settle it, and §8 asks for
the cheapest one.

An instrument with a residual spread of 2–5 points per category can see a
12-point level change; it cannot see a 3-point drift. That asymmetry is why §9 of
the first revision was withdrawn rather than repaired — and it is also the
practical finding here: **this instrument is worth reading for steps, and not
worth reading for trends.**

## 6. Proposal: rules for reading this instrument

The only thing this RFC asks to be adopted.

1. **Fit the series; never compare adjacent runs.** Already learned once.
2. **A verdict must survive the window.** Refit from every later start; a verdict
   that changes is reported as such and not counted. *(Built.)*
3. **A verdict must survive dropping any single run.** *(Built.)*
4. **The bar is the slope against its own standard error**, from the residual
   spread about the fitted line — not the series' spread about its mean.
5. **Report multiplicity.** Thirteen categories are tested at once. State the
   corrected threshold beside the uncorrected one and do not headline a result
   that fails it.
6. **Never let file discovery choose the population.** The loader reads every
   calibration of this instrument, whatever its score file is called, and prints
   the count it found.
7. **A claim from this instrument names its window and its sensitivity.** A
   post-0.2.16 analysis may still be the right one; it has to be argued from the
   instrument rather than selected by a glob.

Rules 1–6 are implemented in `benchmarks/scripts/score-trend.mjs` and
`benchmarks/TREND.{md,html}` as of this document; rule 7 is a convention for
authors. Under all of them together, applied to the twelve runs, **no category
is counted in either direction** — which is the correct output of this
instrument at this sample size, and is what makes the step of §3 the only thing
in the record worth a wave.

## 8. Questions for the author

1. **The lever is exhausted, and that is the finding.** §4 decomposed the step:
   four affirmed charges, four landings. The run-logs classify each loop, and the
   count is exact —

   | run | Snapback-attributed defects | classified loops |
   | --- | ---: | ---: |
   | 0.2.9 | **2** | 46 |
   | 0.2.12 | **2** | 37 |
   | 0.2.26 | **0** | 33 |
   | 0.2.27 | **0** | 31 |
   | 0.2.28 | **0** | 33 |

   Those four defects are the four charges of §4, one for one. **Since the step
   there have been none.** The obvious next move — a wave that closes the current
   charge list — has no charges to close, and this document withdraws it before
   anyone spends a wave on it. What remains in the notes is volume, scatter and
   discoverability, and §3 shows those do not move the score: the surface grew
   145% after the step while it stayed in an 85–92 band.

   The loops that remain are real but not Snapback's: 20–26 per run, classified
   agent-error, and in 0.2.28 they are Expo Router and app choreography — "move
   feed tabs to persistent shell", "use Router Slot for single active route".

   So the honest reading of this instrument is that **it is not currently
   reporting a problem.** No capability regressed, `authorization` is the
   strongest dimension, and the one event it recorded was a fix that worked. A
   programme looking for its next move should look somewhere other than this
   number, and the question for the author is what that should be —
   §8.5.2. **Does the programme accept §6 as standing rules** for any claim made from
   this instrument, including in other LLPs and run records?
3. **Is a 12-run window right, or should the instrument's population be argued?**
   0.2.9 and 0.2.12 differ in scope from later runs; if they are excluded, that
   exclusion needs a reason on the record, and §6 rule 7 then applies to it.
4. **Should LLP 3000.009's surface be packaged separately?** A product question,
   not a score question, and not this RFC's to answer.
5. **What replaces this instrument as the programme's compass?** It has produced
   one event in twelve runs and its charge list is empty. It excludes by design
   the two things that still cost builders time: agent-error loops (20–26 a run)
   and anticipatory design against ceilings never reached (LLP 3000.007 §20.22:
   "no bound was hit at runtime in any of the last four lanes"). Neither is
   visible here. That is not an argument for retiring the calibration — it is a
   green light, and a green light is not a plan.

## 9. Two duplications in two days, and what they cost

Recorded here because this RFC is about what the programme can see, and it
cannot currently see this.

Twice in two days, two sessions built the same thing. **W13g**, the horizon
repair, was built in parallel by this session and the wave-13 lead; the lead
landed first (`157a80798`) with a different and better design, and a day of
work was discarded. **MC0**, the oversized-sync prerequisite, was fixed by
another session in about 80 lines (`c60751e30`, ruling 39) and again by this
session across six files and five two-family review rounds; both are now on
main, both green, and `issues/20260919-snapback4-two-implementations-of-oversized-sync-recovery.md`
carries the consolidation.

Both were checked against `origin/main` before dispatch and neither had landed
at that moment. The lanes then ran for a day. **Nothing in the repository says
who is working on what**, so the check can only ever be "has it landed", which
is the wrong question when lanes take a day.

This is the same failure the calibration cannot see (§8.5): it charges the
backend for defects and never for the programme's own coordination. A wave
spent twice is invisible to every instrument this programme has.

The cheap fix is a file — one line per active lane, naming the ticket or LLP
section and the session — updated when a lane starts and when it lands. It is
not a tracker and it does not need to be accurate about progress; it needs to
answer "is anyone already on this" in one read. That is a proposal, not a
change: this RFC commissions nothing.

## Non-claims

- This RFC establishes no trend in either direction. Its §2 table reports five
  marginal results and says plainly that none survives multiplicity.
- The step at 0.2.16 is a measurement. Attributing it to §20's wave is
  chronology, not attribution; question 1 exists because nobody has done the work.
- §5 is a hypothesis with a single supporting event and is not a basis for
  planning.
- **This RFC does change something**, and says so rather than claiming
  otherwise: §6 proposes rules that would constrain how every other document
  reads this instrument, and rules 1–4 and 6 are already implemented in the tool.
  It proposes no ceiling, language form, card word or disclosure boundary.
- N = 1 per version. These are calibration runs read for shape, not a ranking and
  not a magnitude claim about any backend.
- §9's two duplications are this session's own, reported because they are
  evidence about the programme's coordination rather than about anyone's work.
  Neither is a defect; both landed green.

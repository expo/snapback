## 1. Summary

Snapback 4 serializes writes by holding its store's head across the whole
of each mutation. On SQLite that means `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` before the
program runs; on Postgres it means `SELECT … FROM _head FOR UPDATE`.

S0 measured the cost:

- serial time is `2.05 ms + 11.98 × RTT`, because 12 round trips are made
  under the lock;
- that is 120 commits/s at a 0.5 ms round trip, against 5,700/s for raw
  Postgres with 4 writers and 89,000/s with 64;
- the serial batch recovers only the flush.

The author commissioned the optimistic committer on 2026-10-19. This RFC
designs it.

One idea organizes the design. **The committer owns one serial stream per
store. Every durable write enters that stream as a member, and each member
reaches it in one of two ways:**

- **pre-executed:** run elsewhere at a snapshot, with its writes buffered
  and every read it made recorded, then validated against everything that
  committed since;
- **in place:** run inside the stream's batch transaction, exactly as the
  reference protocol runs it today.

The stream commits members in batches, one transaction per batch: one head
update, one flush, and acknowledgement after commit. That gives two
properties at once:

- **Pre-executed members take their round trips outside the serial
  section.** This is the throughput win.
- **In-place members keep today's semantics exactly.** They cover hot keys,
  programs that are not eligible to pre-execute, and members not yet
  decomposed. They are the fallback and the correctness reference.

Snapback's schemas create contention by construction:

- a sequence column gives every insert under one parent a shared counter;
- a maintained total is updated by every write to its source;
- a cap check counts a group's occupancy.

So the design treats **sequence allocations** as their own write class
(§4.5), because otherwise every insert under a parent would conflict.
Maintained totals and caps keep ordinary value dependencies in this design,
which is safe but serializes writers to one total. A commutative class for
them is a follow-on, commissioned only if the schema-contention measurement
shows it binds (§4.5).

The two children specify the machinery pre-execution needs:

- [the write overlay](./3000.009.001.000.000.000-the-write-overlay.rfc.md);
- [read sets and validation](./3000.009.001.000.000.001-read-sets-and-validation.rfc.md).

The committer is store-neutral. On Postgres it removes the round-trip term.
On SQLite it moves execution onto parallel reader connections, and batching
amortizes the flush. Its build is gated by child C's sizing rule (S1a).

## 2. What exists

### 2.1 The reference protocol, today

## 3. The stream

### 3.1 One committer per store

- The committer lives in the process that holds the store's ownership lease
  (parent RFC §5). Other processes (P2) submit members to it.
- It owns the writer connection. Execution uses reader connections.
- `serve` stops holding `FairMutex<Engine>` around durable writes. Request
  threads submit members to a bounded queue and wait for their
  acknowledgement. Reads keep using reader connections.
- **Ephemeral operations** stay outside the stream. They make no durable
  commit and keep serializing on the hub's lock as today. They read the
  durable store through a snapshot, as they do now, with `Layered` composed
  over it. The shared id minter stays behind one lock.

### 3.2 Members

A **member** is one durable unit that commits atomically and receives a
**revision**.

| Kind | Examples | How it reaches the stream |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Program member** | A Quarry mutation | Pre-executed when eligible (§4.1), otherwise in place |
| **Engine member** | Job chunks, expiry sweeps, timeline delivery, effect claim and settle, row views | **In place** until each is decomposed into serial framing through the parent's `StoreTx` families plus a pre-executable program. They read and write raw SQL today, which neither the overlay nor the recorder can see. Engine members that run under `Ceilings::job()` (job chunks, timeline delivery) are **long** and commit alone in their batch |
| **Publication member** | Time-audience publication of one due row | In place. Submitted by the bounded per-request step, or by an execution that met that specific pending opening (§6). It never requires the whole backlog to drain first |
| **Direct operation** | Session mint, invalidation, claim, work-lease renewal, step record, settle, asset view, receipt-only report | In place by default. They are typed operations with single-key read sets. Pre-executing them is the first extension if S1a's effect-per-mutation cell shows them binding (§11) |
| **Administrative** | Activation, adoption, dev reset, seed, load, restore | In place, alone in their batch |

The store's ownership-lease renewal bypasses the stream (parent RFC §5,
§6.3).

### 3.3 Revisions and the public sequence

- Every member takes the next **revision**. That is the stream's order, and
  the serial order the store's history is equivalent to.
- A member that publishes a commit also takes the next **public sequence**
  in revision order. Public sequences stay contiguous with no gap below a
  published watermark.
- Revisions are internal. They appear in footprints, the chain hash and
  receipts.

### 3.4 Batches

The committer drains its queue into a **batch**, one store transaction:

1. **Fence.** `FOR KEY SHARE` on `_owner` on Postgres; on SQLite, the
   sidecar ownership plus an `_owner` check (parent RFC §5.1).
2. **Members in revision order.**
   - **Pre-executed:** validate it against the window and the members
     already accepted into this batch (child: read sets). If it passes,
     resolve its placeholders and allocations (§5, §4.5), then apply its
     write set. If it fails, rerun it (§4.3).
   - **In place:** under `SAVEPOINT member`, run it exactly as the reference
     does, over the batch transaction's current state.
3. **Tail.** One head update to the batch's last public sequence; the
   commits, log, change-evidence and chain rows for each member. One flight.
4. **Commit, then acknowledge.** One flush covers the batch, with the
   uncancellable COMMIT of parent RFC §5.4.1. After the commit, the
   committer runs each member's **post-commit hooks in revision order**: the
   `committed` wakeup, change answers, long waits, and follow-on enqueues
   such as the next timeline-delivery member.

**What may share a batch.** A database error rolls back the whole batch,
and the committer then reruns each member alone, **in place, with the
replay check**. A stale pre-executed write set is never re-applied without
revalidation. If the COMMIT's outcome is unknown, as in the
ambiguous-commit window (parent RFC §5.2), nothing is rerun until each
member's receipt or key settles whether it committed. Rerunning alone is
safe only for a
member whose retry is exactly-once through its receipt or an idempotency
key. That is the per-path retry table, a P1a deliverable (parent RFC §5.2).
**Until a member kind is keyed, it commits in a single-member batch.**
Batching never extends double-apply risk beyond what today's per-operation
transactions have.

**Refusals** record their receipt in the batch, like any write. A
pre-executed refusal is validated like any other result.

### 3.5 What stays fixed, and the oracle

The committer keeps every invariant of the parent RFC's §6.4:

- one total order per store;
- no gap below a published watermark;
- exact snapshots;
- whole-commit atomicity per member;
- receipts recorded once;
- the authority clock monotone in commit order;
- effects admitted only inside the commit;
- every durable transaction fenced;
- the same wire.

The property that holds them together:

- **The committed history is serializable in revision order.** Replaying
  every committed member in revision order through the **seam's `run`**
  reproduces every outcome and the final state. `run` is the member's
  program plus its post-program obligations (§6.1 of the parent), fed from
  the member's recorded **input transcript**:
  - operation, arguments, principal;
  - the effective time (§6);
  - every id it minted, drawn at admission and injected the way
    `Context::with_ids` already allows;
  - the presence observations it made (§4.6);
  - its chunk and yield decisions, such as visit counts and cursors, for
    engine members whose walks stop on wall-clock budgets;
  - placeholder and allocation resolutions.

  `run` is not `Engine::mutate` as it stands. That bundles a time-audience
  prelude which, in the committed history, belongs to separate publication
  members (§6), and replay plays those as members. **Database facts are
  re-read during replay, never replayed from the transcript.** Replaying
  them would hide exactly the validation defects the oracle exists to find.

Until every member kind's transcript is complete, the oracle covers the
kinds whose transcripts are, and says which. A kind outside the covered set
is never counted as passing.

### 3.6 Everything a member depends on is read inside its snapshot

A pre-executed member's dependencies are **all read at S, through the
recorder** (child: read sets §2). Nothing is carried in from an earlier
snapshot. That includes the facts the reference reads before its program
today:

- the session by digest and the principal's generation, which serve
  resolves under the engine lock today because "time queued must not
  preserve a session that expired or was revoked in that wait"
  (`serve_identity.rs:58–60`). **Expiry writes nothing,** so no footprint
  can catch it. The session's `expires_at`, its `cap_expires_at`, and any
  claim window are recorded as **authority deadlines**. At serial admission
  into the batch they are checked against a **fresh authority-clock
  sample**, taken by the committer at that moment, not against `at`. `at`
  can lag real time when no `not before` pin moves it (round 3: Astra N1).
  The reference does the same thing: it samples `shared.now()` behind the
  writer before `live_session` (`serve_identity.rs:58`,
  `authority.rs:207–220`). A session that expires while its member waits in
  the queue therefore refuses, as it does today;
- the receipt's absence for `(principal, op id)`;
- the asset-intent placement;
- the clock floor (§6).

Admission may check them early to refuse quickly. The check that counts is
the one inside S. Its entries carry S's label, so a revocation landing
after S is caught by validation, and one landing before S is simply read.

The batch also re-checks the receipt serially at admission into the batch.
That is a cheap guard against two copies of one operation pre-executing at
once.

The pre-execution handle exposes only `Rows`, `PartitionRows` and family
calls, so a raw-SQL read cannot compile into a pre-executed path.

### 3.7 The member protocol

K0a's review (2026-10-21; Sol and Grok, blind) found that the K0a seam
routes every producer but cannot yet batch or queue them. Producers still
own the physical transaction: a member's `tx.rollback()` or an early return
that drops its `Tx` would undo earlier members in a shared batch. Hooks run
inside the body, and several of them are the member's result (a job's
status read, the expiry sweep's continuation). The protocol the queue and
the serial batch need:

- **Member-scoped transaction.** A member receives a facade over the batch
  transaction, opened as `SAVEPOINT member`. Its `complete` releases the
  savepoint; `rollback`, `abort` and active-facade drop roll back to it. Only the
  committer issues `BEGIN` and the durable `COMMIT`. Nested savepoints
  (`program`, `job_chunk`, `timeline_chunk`, `seed_sequences`) stay valid
  inside it. Administrative members that need a raw opener (activation and
  the Following launch share one `IMMEDIATE` transaction) remain solo
  members, alone in their batch (§3.2).
- **Explicit disposition.** A member ends as **complete** (its writes stay)
  or **abort** (rolled back to its savepoint), never by inference from
  `Result::Ok`. Paths that return `Ok` without committing today, such as a
  replay rollback or a validation refusal, are aborts. Work they did before
  deciding, such as the writer-time pin, must not be persisted by a later
  member's commit.
- **Continuations, not hooks.** A member returns a **pending result**: owned
  continuation data plus what the caller is told once it runs. After the
  batch's durable `COMMIT`, the committer runs each member's continuation
  **in revision order** and only then acknowledges that member. Continuations
  include process-state changes that must never run ahead of the database:
  catalog and identity replacement, `accept_open`, ephemeral rebind,
  `expiry_after` clearing, scheduler rotation, the entropy advance, and the
  `committed` wakeup. A continuation that needs another durable write, such
  as the sweep's follow-on publication, **submits a new member**. It never
  opens a transaction itself. Caller-facing reads that a hook performs
  today, such as a job's status, run in the continuation after `COMMIT`.
  A continuation's failure is reported separately from the member's
  durable outcome. The write has committed, and the caller must be able to
  tell the two apart.
- **Owned commands against a worker-owned engine.** Member bodies today
  capture the caller's `&mut Engine`, so they cannot cross a queue. Members
  become owned commands (operation, arguments, principal, admission facts)
  that the committer thread executes against the engine it owns; worker
  state is supplied at execution, not captured. Request threads submit and
  wait for the acknowledgement, and `serve` stops holding `FairMutex<Engine>`
  around durable writes (§3.1).
- **A failed batch** rolls back every member's writes and runs no
  continuation. Each member reruns alone, as §3.4 specifies.

K0's remaining slices take this in order: the member-scoped transaction and
disposition (behavior-neutral with single-member batches), then
continuations, then owned commands and the queue, then the direct
operations named by K0a's census, then publication members, then the serial
batch. The census allowlist and the whole suite gate each slice.

## 4. Pre-execution

### 4.1 Eligibility

A program member is eligible to pre-execute unless one of these holds:

- **taint:** a sequence- or allocation-derived value flows into a branch,
  comparison, string operation, read-back or row round trip (parent RFC
  §6.3 item 5);
- **placeholder in a key:** a placeholder can reach an indexed component,
  or take part in a uniqueness check. The only exception is a **monotone
  allocation in its own parent-prefixed `$sequence` index** (§4.5). There,
  the provisional value is the counter at S plus k, the resolved value is at
  least that, and both sort above every key under that parent at S. Because
  deletes never lower the counter, that holds across gaps. If the allocated
  value reaches **any other index or unique constraint** (a user index on
  the ordinal, or one with the ordinal as a later component), the program
  is ineligible. A changed address could otherwise land on pre-existing
  data no footprint mentions (round 3: Astra N3, Grok N2);
- **time-dependent outcome:** a `now`-derived value flows into a branch, a
  `require`, a comparison, a read address, an error or a returned value.
  For example `require now - m.at <= 60000`, or `hour(now)` choosing a
  bucket. Such a program's outcome at its commit time can differ from its
  outcome at the time it ran with. It is eligible only under the strict time
  rule of §6: it is accepted only if `at = T`, and otherwise reruns (round 3:
  Grok N1, FUNDAMENTAL; Astra N2). A program that merely stores `now`
  through a placeholder is not time-dependent;
- **presence:** the program reads `connected[p]`, or emits a
  presence-guarded effect (§4.6);
- **conflict budget:** the member has exceeded it (§4.3);
- **overlay coverage:** it touches an operation family the overlay has not
  yet qualified (child: overlay §5).

`check` computes eligibility statically per operation, prints it with
`check --cost`, and states the Quarry subset its analysis covers. Anything
outside that subset is ineligible. Eligibility never changes an outcome.

### 4.2 Execution

A worker thread:

1. takes a reader connection;
2. opens snapshot S (`REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY`, or a SQLite WAL read
   transaction);
3. reads S's revision;
4. reads its admission facts at S (§3.6);
5. fixes its effective time (§6);
6. runs the program through the overlay, which records the read set and
   buffers writes.

The result is a **pre-executed member**:

- its outcome;
- its meter observations;
- its read set;
- its write set with footprint;
- its effects;
- its placeholders and allocations;
- its input transcript;
- S.

### 4.3 Conflicts and the conflict budget

A member that fails validation is rerun at a new snapshot. A member is also
rerun when its snapshot has fallen out of the window (§4.4); that counts
against the budget too, so slow programs cannot livelock between
re-execution and the window.

After `c` such reruns (default 2) it runs **in place**. A decaying score
per footprint key routes members that read a hot key straight to in-place
execution.

A rerun discards the previous attempt's effects, overlay and meter
charges.

### 4.4 The window

The committer keeps footprints of committed revisions newer than the oldest
live snapshot, bounded by age (default 2 s) and memory. Footprints never
leave the server.

### 4.5 Allocations, and why totals and caps wait

- **Sequence columns are allocations.** An insert into a table with a
  sequence column today reads the parent's counter and writes `previous + 1`
  (`engine/sequences.rs`). Under pre-execution:
  - The value is an **allocation placeholder**. The committer assigns it at
    commit, in revision order, and resolves it into the row image and its
    `$sequence` index key by the same machinery as §5. The counter read is
    not recorded as a read.
  - The taint rule decides eligibility. A program that uses the number it
    was given runs in place.
  - **Counters are durable** (round 2: Muse N2, Grok). Each allocation
    updates the parent's `_sequences` row in the batch transaction as part
    of the write set. The committer's in-memory counters are only a cache of
    those rows: seeded from the store at start and at every ownership
    acquisition, and dropped on any batch rollback.
  - The replay oracle re-derives `previous + 1` from the re-read
    `_sequences` row and compares it with the transcript. It never injects
    the value, so a diverged cache would show up (round 2: Fable).
- **Maintained totals and caps keep value dependencies in this design.**
  Round 2 showed that "the program did not read the total" is not enough to
  make a maintain commutative (Astra N2, Fable N1, Grok note 4):
  - the engine itself reads the total to decide overflow, deletion versus
    replacement, source-existence probes, charges and partition events
    (`maintains.rs:586`);
  - a maintained column is mirrored into the owner row's whole image
    (`columns.rs:78–83`), so its footprint is the owner row;
  - a recorder at `Rows` cannot tell which fields the interpreter later
    evaluates.

  So writers to one total, or to one capped group, conflict with each
  other, and after the conflict budget they run in place. That is correct
  and costs throughput only on shared totals.
- **The commutative class is a follow-on.** It needs:
  - field-level footprints for maintained columns, and an interpreter bit
    that marks evaluated maintained fields;
  - the maintain machinery's own reads excluded under its existing scope;
  - maintenance decisions re-made at commit in revision order;
  - cap and threshold predicates that keep their observed result (§4.7).

  These requirements are recorded here so the design is not lost. A child
  RFC with its own review takes it up, commissioned only if S1a's
  schema-contention cell (a maintained column on the parent, C writers)
  shows shared totals binding. Following's pending-queue tail
  (`timeline/work.rs:25`) joins it when Following is decomposed.

### 4.6 Presence runs in place, and its registry updates join the stream

Presence (`connected[p]`) is an in-process registry. Today it is ordered
against durable writes by the serving lock. A poll's entry durably marks
`_effect_presence` and then calls `presence.enter`, both under
`FairMutex<Engine>` (`serve_changes.rs:48–55`; `effects.rs:285–287`). So no
mutation can observe the registry change before the mark commits.

The committer removes that lock, so the ordering moves into the stream
(round 3: Grok N4):

- **Registry changes become stream effects.** A poll's reconnect becomes a
  direct operation: the durable mark, plus a post-commit hook that performs
  `presence.enter` in revision order. Leaving likewise updates the registry
  only from a post-commit hook, or with a direct operation where the
  reference makes a durable write.
- **Presence-reading members run in place** and read the registry inside
  the batch. In revision order the registry then reflects exactly the
  reconnects and leaves committed before them. That is the history the lock
  produces today.
- The replay transcript records each in-place presence observation, and the
  oracle checks it against the registry implied by the committed reconnect
  and leave members before it. The transcript is then checked, not just
  injected.

If the presence workload ever binds, a presence epoch that validates like a
read is the follow-on.

### 4.7 Predicates keep their observed result

A predicate entry, such as a time boundary (§6), records the **result the
execution observed** as well as the condition.

- Validation re-evaluates the condition and conflicts if the result differs
  in **either** direction (round 2: Astra N1, Grok N1, FUNDAMENTAL).
- A refusal that observed "full" is not accepted after a delete made room;
  it reruns and inserts, as serial execution would.
- A predicate is evaluated against **one source**: the batch transaction's
  current state. That state already contains every accepted member's write
  set and every in-place member's writes, so nothing is counted twice and
  nothing is missed (round 2: Muse N3). The committer may serve it from an
  in-memory mirror of the keys touched since the oldest live snapshot,
  seeded from the store, so it costs no round trip.

## 5. Placeholders and resolution

Sequence-derived values are placeholders in a pre-executed member:

- effect ids `effect-<seq>-<n>`;
- Following admission tokens;
- allocation values (§4.5);
- any value built from them.

At batch commit the committer resolves each placeholder to the member's
actual sequence or allocation, rewriting every row image, index entry,
outbox row and stored comparison that contains it. A placeholder that
reaches a key is resolved before validation, so footprints are concrete.

Engine paths that compare sequence-derived values, such as Following's
delivery-versus-backfill check (`timeline/work.rs:101, 115`), stay in place
until they are decomposed, because `check` cannot see them.

## 6. Time

Round 2 showed that a per-member effective time fixed before execution
conflicts whenever a later-admitted member overtakes it, even with no data
in common (Fable N2). Time now works this way:

- **`at` is assigned at commit.** A member executes with a provisional
  `T = max(admission now, the clock floor read at S)`. The committer assigns
  `at = max(T, pin)`, where `pin` is the previous member's `at` in revision
  order. Monotonicity in commit order holds by construction, and
  `record_commit`'s `at` is that value.
- **Two acceptance rules, by what the program did with `now`.**
  - **Time-independent programs**, which never let `now` influence control
    flow, a read address, an error or a returned value (§4.1), are accepted
    with `at = max(T, pin)` as long as `at < B`. Their outcome does not
    depend on the exact time, so committing them later than `T` changes
    nothing.
  - **Time-dependent programs** are accepted only if `at = T`. If the pin
    has moved past `T`, they rerun with a fresh `T`, and repeated time
    conflicts count toward the budget and end in place. Only these programs
    pay the overtaking cost that round 2 identified. Parent RFC §6.4's
    invariant ("execution at `t` commits only if `t` is at or after the last
    pin") holds for them literally, and for time-independent programs
    because their outcome is the same at every `t` in `[T, B)`.
- **The time boundary B** is the earliest time at which anything the
  execution observed changes meaning:
  - the lifetime edges (expiry and pending opening) of every row it
    examined or wrote. These are recorded separately, because
    `Runner::next_expiry` deliberately drops deadlines outside emitting-read
    scopes (`expiry.rs:41`) and never carries a hidden opening, so it is not
    a complete accumulator (round 3: Astra, Grok notes);
  - authority deadlines are separate, checked against a fresh sample
    (§3.6).

- **Values derived from `now`** that reach a row, an effect deadline or a
  pending index are placeholders resolved to `at` (§5). A comparison of
  `now` against stored data is a taint flow and runs in place.
- **The pin is scoped as the reference scopes it.** `pin_writer_time` pins
  only for schemas with a `not before` table (`time_audience.rs:7–21`).
  Elsewhere `at` is the request time, as today.
- **Publication follows dependencies, and keeps the reference's bounded
  per-request step.**
  - **The per-request step stays** (round 3: Grok N3; LLP 3000.036.003 §4
    and §10). Today every `/q/`, `/m/`, `/sync` and `/changes` publishes
    the one oldest due opening first, so each request shrinks the backlog
    and disclosures land below sync watermarks as they do now. Under the
    committer, a request that finds an opening due submits a **publication
    member** for the oldest one and waits for its acknowledgement before
    taking its snapshot.
    - Concurrent requests coalesce onto the same member.
    - The committer knows the next opening time, so when nothing is due the
      check costs nothing.
    - A stale or never-live opening commits its private ledger and the
      clock but takes no public sequence, as `time_audience.rs:136–140`
      does today.
  - **It stays bounded:** at most one opening per step, never a drain of the
    backlog in front of unrelated members.
  - A pre-execution that meets a due-but-pending row gets
    `TimeAudiencePending`, the store error that aborts the reference
    attempt today (`engine/read.rs:379–395`). It submits a publication
    member for **that** opening, waits for its acknowledgement, and reruns.
    That is the reference's query settle loop (`serve.rs:1258–1320`),
    moved onto the stream (round 2: Grok N3).
  - Query and sync paths do the same for the openings they meet.
- Postgres gains the same publication path and writer-time pin. That is a
  parity item for P1.

## 7. Batch sizing and latency

- **Adaptive batches.** The window adapts: the largest N whose p99 member
  latency (queue + validation + commit) stays within the write SLO
  (100 ms).
- **Long members** (job chunks, timeline delivery, administrative members)
  run alone in their batch, so they never share a batch's latency. They
  still hold the writer while they run, so members queued behind them wait.
  The SLO is measured end to end: execution, reruns, queueing and commit.
  S1a includes a mixed-length cell for this.
- **An idle store** commits a lone member immediately.

### K0a implementation note

K0a introduces the crate-private reference `Committer`, `Member` and `Kind`
in `snapback4-core/src/engine/committer.rs`. Each member runs in place and
opens its original transaction through the committer. Its body selects the
original commit point; explicit rollback and implicit drop remain distinct.
Post-commit continuations run synchronously in submission order. A
`Committed<T>` acknowledgement carries hooks across the adapter/engine
boundary for activation and reset, including ephemeral rebind and expiry
continuation clearing. There is no queue, batching, retry or persisted revision.

The sequenced producers in §2, including trusted seed deletion, now enter
that seam. Activation rebuild and Following launch retain their shared
activation transaction; `seed_sequences` remains a savepoint in seed's
member. The private time-clock pin also enters as a direct member. Mutation's
publication prelude stays in place; the evaluation Postgres `mutate_once`
path is unchanged. Host wakeups remain at their existing caller boundaries.

`crates/snapback4/tests/committer_ratchet.rs` shares the store-port ratchet's
lexer and permits only falling production opener counts outside the committer
and adapters. The base has 62 matching lines; K0a leaves 49 in 11 files.
The remaining K0b candidates cover identity/session operations, action/route
admission and lifecycle, work claims/settlement/leases/presence, action/effect
steps, asset intents/views/sweeps/receipt-only reports, job/schedule admission,
opening upgrades and development replay schedule metadata. Six of the 49
are authority snapshots conservatively counted because their handle can
write. Adapter-internal transactions are excluded, so their typed identity,
release and maintenance entry points also need K0b review. This is the first
slice of K0, not completion of the stream increment.

K0 and K1 are useful whatever S1a says. K1's read sets also feed increment
E (LLP 3000.009 §7.1).

**S1a's verdict (2026-10-20).** The predicted ceiling at 0.5 ms is 4,775
commits/s, bound by the execution stage at 32 connections (about 12 round
trips per execution). That is about 40× today's commit path, but 13.5% of
raw Postgres with 32 writers against the 25% × 2 the pre-registered rule
requires. So **K2–K5 wait**. First comes the rule's named fallback:

- reduce the round trips per execution (prefetch and batched reads on the
  execution path, which also speed the in-place path);
- or raise the connection budget.

The store is then re-sized on the Hetzner pair. K0 and K1 proceed.

**S1b's re-size (2026-10-20): the rule clears, and K2–K5 proceed.** The pair
ran over a real network (RTT p50 0.58 ms) with a separate 12-thread
database host:

- Batching same-level reads and folding setup into the first flight cut
  execution to 2 round trips: 9,544/s at C = 32.
- The full-write-set commit stage reaches 4,256/s within the SLO.
- Raw Postgres on that host reaches about 5,900/s at C = 32.

So the predicted 4,256/s clears the required 2,945/s by 45%. Two
consequences follow:

- **Round-trip reduction becomes part of K3/K4.** It also speeds in-place
  members.
- **The single committer is a structural ceiling.** Raw Postgres scales
  past it at C ≥ 64, and the ceiling is recorded in child C's ledger as a
  design-choice limit. On the 96-core host behind the proxy (S1a), the
  same rule failed, because raw Postgres there was six times faster. The
  25%-of-raw bar depends on the hardware, and the absolute per-store commit
  rate is what child C reports.

### K0b implementation note

`Committer::run(store, Member::new(kind, body))` owns the original fenced
`Store::transaction()` (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`). The body receives a borrowing
`MemberTx` facade under `SAVEPOINT member`, with the existing `Tx` surface
available through dereference. `commit(value)` releases the savepoint and
returns `Complete(value)`; explicit `rollback(value)` rolls back that frame
and returns `Abort(value)`. `abort(value)` selects a normal abort, and the
facade's drop rolls back the frame. Errors also abort. The committer alone
physically commits a Complete result; Abort/error drops the owning transaction
and issues its original rollback. `Result::Ok` never selects durability.
Nested program/chunk/sequence savepoints retain their original meaning.

Mutation replay, input/asset validation refusals, no queued job, no pending
timeline work and empty row-view scans abort. A program refusal still rolls
back only `program`, records its receipt and completes. Job/timeline refusal
parking, clock-only publication and stale/never-live opening cleanup complete,
including their original private metadata. Seed/load/restore, deletion,
expiry/orphan repair, reset and the smoke barrier complete at their original
commit points. Every aborted writer-time pin and metadata write is discarded.
Activation and Following retain `run_solo` / `Solo::open_solo_with`, explicitly
one administrative member with the original raw immediate transaction.

Job status reads, expiry continuation and follow-on audience advancement,
timeline rotation, and mutation's entropy assignment now run after `run`
returns from physical COMMIT. Reset's `run_committed` acknowledgement and
activation's solo acknowledgement retain all existing adapter/engine hooks,
including catalog/identity acceptance, narration, ephemeral rebind and expiry
continuation clearing. A sweep follow-on can still open its own transaction;
a post-commit error still reports after the sweep has committed.

The facade tests execute two members inside one physical transaction: the
first completes, and the second explicitly rolls back, drops or errors. The
first survives the final COMMIT; the second's writes, clock pin and metadata do
not. They also cover every nested frame name, lazy sequence-table rollback,
normal Abort versus Ok, a follow-on writer after COMMIT, and a deferred
constraint failure at physical COMMIT with no acknowledgement or hook.
There is no production batching, continuation protocol, queue or retry yet;
the remaining opener census and host metadata surface are unchanged.

### K0c implementation note

`MemberTx` exposes `Rows` and narrow typed methods for receipts, commit
records, publication, job chunks, effects, seed/reset and asset placement.
It exposes no owning `Tx`, dereference, raw connection, `abandon` or physical
transaction control. Nested savepoint names are restricted to `program`,
`job_chunk`, `timeline_chunk` and `seed_sequences`; neither the member frame
nor SQL fragments can be supplied through that surface.

`complete`, explicit `rollback` and normal `abort` consume the facade and
return an opaque disposition. Drop rolls back an active frame. The driver
checks its final frame state against the token before physical COMMIT, including
rollback failures. Tests forge both contradictory dispositions inside the
committer's private test module and prove they refuse before durability.
Type-checked negative surface assertions reject an owning dereference,
`AsRef<Tx>`/`Borrow<Tx>`, connection access, abandon and physical commit.

Members now return `Pending<T, D>`: an owned caller value and owned
continuation data. `Member::pending` adapts existing body results before the
physical transaction ends. `run_pending` resolves Complete/Abort only after
COMMIT/rollback, and `Committer::run_with` runs the continuation before returning
the caller value. Abort returns its existing value without running a continuation.
Plain `run` supplies an empty continuation. No transaction borrow can survive
into follow-up work. Production remains single-member; there is no queue or retry.

| Previous post-commit work | Pending data and committer continuation |
| --- | --- |
| Quarry/native job status read | Optional owned job id; `run_job_chunk_for` reads `job` after COMMIT |
| Sweep cursor and follow-on publication | Owned expiry cursor map and clock; `sweep_expired_for` installs the map, then `advance_time_audience` submits a new Publication member through the committer |
| Timeline rotation | Rotation flag, index and count; `timeline_chunk` advances the scheduler only for completed work |
| Mutation entropy | Optional entropy; `mutate` installs it only for a completed member |
| Activation store acceptance | Owned report, phase, catalog and identity; solo continuation installs the catalog/identity and calls `accept_open` |
| Activation narration and engine acceptance | Owned population facts, schema/programs and opening metadata; acknowledged continuation stages update narration, schema/generation, scheduler, expiry cursor and ephemeral binding |
| Development reset | Owned `ResetState`; continuation clears expiry, resets the scheduler, rebinds ephemeral state and constructs the asset-sweep reply |
| Host durable wake | Owned head/identity/generation and existing wake selection; `continue_durable_publication` runs the host's wake-state update and both notifications at the original caller boundary |

The solo adapter/engine stages retain a `Committed<T>` acknowledgement between
them. Its only transformation attaches owned data for another committer-run
continuation; the old `after_commit` hook surface is gone. Host publication reads
an already durable head and accepted engine identity while its existing guard is
held; its wake conditions and notification order are unchanged.

`RuntimeError::Continuation(source)` means the write committed and its follow-up
failed. Body/open/COMMIT failures retain their original error variants. The
wrapper delegates Display, code, family, refusal, retryability and busy policy to
the source, retaining existing caller behavior while Rust callers can distinguish
the durable outcome. A failed continuation never rolls back the acknowledged write.

The test-only batch driver uses the same frame execution and continuation
runner as production: two members share one physical transaction, then their
continuations run in submission order. A failed first continuation retains
both durable writes and does not suppress the second continuation. A deferred
constraint failure at physical COMMIT runs neither continuation. Other witnesses
cover abort without follow-up, follow-on submission reading the first member's
committed state, and the original error/refusal policy on follow-up failure.
The member isolation witness now preserves member 1's nonzero sequence counter
across member 2's rollback, drop or error; recorder reset starts from explicitly
unknown evidence, and every nested name still exercises lazy counter creation.

The per-member SQLite cost is inherently SAVEPOINT + RELEASE, two statements
on success (K0b Sol P2 #3 accepted). A later measured optimization may skip the
savepoint for a lone member; this slice retains it.

## 10. Alternatives

- **Keep the reference and cut `k`.** Plausibly about 500 commits/s at
  0.5 ms. It is worth doing inside the in-place path, and it misses the bar.
- **Database-native concurrency with an ordering stage.** It needs a
  contiguous-prefix publication and snapshot protocol no one has designed.
- **Postgres SSI for conflict detection.** SSI does detect phantoms through
  predicate locks. But it needs read-write transactions during execution,
  has no SQLite counterpart, works on physical index ranges of the generic
  layout rather than the interpreter's logical reads, and would conflict on
  prefetch's over-reads.
- **Run the interpreter inside Postgres.** Managed providers do not allow
  custom extensions.
- **Pure optimistic concurrency without in-place members.** Hot keys would
  degrade into retry storms, and engine and administrative members would
  need another path.

## 11. Open questions

1. **Direct operations.** An effect costs at least a claim, a step and a
   settle, each 1–2 round trips in place. S1a adds an effect-per-mutation
   cell and a mixed-batch cell. If direct operations bind, they pre-execute
   first; their read sets are single keys.
2. The conflict budget, window and batch defaults, which S1a and K3 set.
3. Whether SQLite enables pre-execution by default, which K3 measures.
4. Whether first-party clients always send operation ids. A member without
   one is unkeyed and commits alone (§3.4), costing a flush each. The client
   SDK's outbox mints them for queued writes; online calls need checking.

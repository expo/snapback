## Summary

The author, after building Clique on Snapback: *Quarry seems great except
too constraining. Its biggest value is describing data that is synced
locally and in the cloud and queried with one language. If you don't need
that, something like Convex or Supabase — more flexible, even if you lose
some nice properties — is probably easier for agents.* He asked for the
thesis to be analysed and, if it holds, for a more relaxed way to use
Snapback 4 where local data is not needed, or relaxed across other seams,
that makes it ergonomic for agents and lets the Clique benchmark run
smoothly. Then, seeing the residency draft he had already commissioned
(LLP 3000.006), he asked whether to push further.

The thesis is half right, and the half that is right points somewhere
more specific than "relax when offline is not needed."

**Right:** the constraints that cost builders time are constraints on the
*program* — a local binds once, no accumulators, no `continue`, no `else
if`, no `where`, no text `+`, no `/` — and every one of them is an
inheritance from Snapback 2's static byte-and-step pricing, not a
requirement of running on a device. LLP 2000.000 §4.1 said so when it
flipped the bound model: *"Decide the bound model, keep the language, and
re-derive its restrictions."* Snapback 4 copied the binder and never
re-derived them. The evidence that they are not device constraints is in
the code: the IR carries `Assign`, `Push`, `Continue`, `Concat` and a
scan `range`; the interpreter — one implementation on the server, in the
wasm and in the native module — runs them today; only the binder refuses
them.

**Not right:** the value that survives without local sync is larger than
"nice properties." Declarative rules that are the audience, typed
refusals with rewrites, the runtime meter, identity, typed assets with a
lifecycle, expiry, jobs on the server's clock and a process that embeds
no JavaScript are why the same builder was faster than Convex on five of
seven Clique milestones in the 0.2.6 run and why authorization is the
dimension Snapback wins. Those do not depend on `sync`. And where the
weaker builders stopped, the cause was not the program language at all:
it was two *model* frictions — spelling the audience twice (the read rule
and the `sync` line) and counters written by one principal into rows
another principal owns — which the mechanism can remove.

So the proposal is not a second, looser language for online-only apps.
It is: **one language, relaxed once for every host under the meter that
already bounds it; the sync audience inferred from the read rule; write
authority decided by the write rule; residency and prediction made
legible instead of inferred at runtime; the online-only mode paved; the
client and dev seams the lanes hit closed; two escape hatches specified
for the last ten percent; and a bar that measures exactly the ceremony
this document claims to remove.** §10 maps every correction loop the five
Clique lanes recorded to the item that removes it. A review received on
rev 1 corrected four claims; rev 3 carries the corrections in §5.0, §5.3,
§5.6, §5.9 and §6.2 and the dispositions beside the review.

## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 Quarry's constraints come from four sources, and only one is the device

| Source | Constraints it explains | Needed when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D — device execution and prediction.** A program may run on a device over the rows it holds and predict a mutation before the server judges it | reads over synced tables only for a local answer; `connected` unknown on a device; no clock beyond `now`, no randomness, no network; a mutation's fan-out must be a complete capped group so a prediction is exact | the program is meant to run locally |
| **A — authorization is static.** Rules are the audience a partition can hold; completeness is a class the prefix decides | index-prefixed reads; the rule/sync pairing; `exists` over an index; reads never raise a rule refusal | always: this is what Snapback wins on |
| **M — resources are metered.** Examined rows, probes, steps, heap, output and rows written have ceilings (LLP 2000.000 §6) | a mutation aborts `E_BOUND`, a query caps; jobs chunk | always: the process is scale-to-zero and shared |
| **S — Snapback 2's static pricing**, retired by LLP 2000.000 flip 1 but never removed from the binder | a local binds once; no accumulators; no `continue`; no `where`; `range` literals only; no text `+`; the `fn`/`proc` split; the page-to-list gap; `divmod` in place of `/` | never, under the meter |

The author's thesis attributes the constraints to D. The record attributes
the *costly* ones to S, and the ones that stopped the weaker builders to a
fifth thing: **R — restatement**, where the model asks the author to say
a fact twice (the audience, in the rule and in `sync`) or to restate an
authority it already granted (the write rule) as a read grant. LLP
2000.000 §6 named S's outcome in advance — "What it dissolves: … the
`fn`/`proc` split and the page-to-list gap, which existed to keep
collections priceable" — and §4.1 asked that the restrictions be
re-derived once the bound model was decided. They were not; the binder
came over with Snapback 2's refusals intact. R was not in the brief
because Snapback 2 had neither `sync` nor the hidden-collision ruling.

### 3.2 The interpreter already runs what the binder refuses

- `crates/snapback4-core/src/ir.rs`: `Stmt::Assign`, `Stmt::Push`,
  `Stmt::Continue`; `Builtin::Concat`, `Builtin::Len`; `Scan.range`
  with `gt`/`gte`/`lt`/`lte`.
- `crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs`: `Assign` rebinds a local
  (line 149), `Push` appends to a list local under the heap meter (157),
  `Continue` flows (252), `Concat` joins text or lists (417).
- `crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/read.rs:381`: a scan's value range
  is evaluated and applied as bounds on the scan.
- `crates/snapback4-lang/src/bind/program/control.rs:38`: "duplicate
  local"; `parser/expr.rs:178`: "`where` is not a Quarry filter";
  `parser/programs.rs:73`: a job's body is chunks and nothing else; the
  language card's "left out on purpose" list.

Because the device is the same Rust (LLP 3000 §7, 2026-09-13, "one
implementation"), a form the server interpreter runs is a form every host
runs. The relaxations of §5 are binder changes over an interpreter that
already evaluates them, plus small additive IR fields — with one
prerequisite the received review found: the meter charges `Push` and the
construction of a literal list or object (`runner.rs:159, 334, 339`) but
not `Concat`'s result (`runner.rs:417`) and not the clone every variable
read makes (`runner.rs:323`), so the accumulation the new forms invite is
unmetered today. §5.0 lands before §5.1.

### 3.3 What "not needing local data" does and does not change

Without `sync` lines an application already runs online-only today: the
online tier (`createClient`) has no store, `observe` re-runs a query
when a commit touches its tables (`packages/snapback4/src/client.ts`),
and every query answers on the server. What that mode does *not* buy is
a looser program: the binder refuses the same forms. So relaxing "for
cases where you don't need the data locally" would mean a syntax that
varies by tier — and §3.2 says there is no reason for it to. The honest
design keeps the syntax one and lets residency be a fact about the data
an operation reads, computed and displayed, not a mode the author
selects.

Two things *are* genuinely server-only and should be spelled as such:
reads of online-only tables and of `connected`, which the client already
routes to the server (`packages/snapback4/src/local.ts:608`), and a
mutation that reads beyond the device's partition, which the device
already refuses to predict (`E_PREDICT`). Both decisions exist at
runtime; neither is visible at `check`. §6 lifts them.

### 3.4 Where the thesis undersells Snapback 4

Convex's own builders paid for what Snapback carries: password identity
(scrypt by hand), non-time-reactive queries (typing, expiry and presence
filtered on the client), fan-out as a read-time scan, a second project
directory for a second instance, and the whole durable offline layer.
Supabase's paid for four products' seams. Those are the "nice
properties" of the thesis, and they are the reason the Snapback 4 lane
was faster on M1, M2, M5, M6 and M8 at first green. The gap to close is
narrow and named: the last ten percent of a feature — an aggregate, a
ranking, a digest, a notification preview, a grouped result — where an
ordinary loop with a counter and a string is what every model reaches
for; and the two restatements of §3.1 R.

### 3.5 Verdict

Agree that Quarry is too constraining; disagree that the constraint is
the price of local sync, and disagree that the answer is a looser mode
for online-only apps. The constraint is Snapback 2's pricing model wearing
Quarry syntax, plus two places the model asks for restatement. The answer
is to finish the flip: relax the language once, everywhere, under the
meter; let the engine infer what it already knows; let the write rule
decide writes; make where a program runs legible; pave the online-only
choice; close the seams the runs hit; and specify — without yet building
— the two hatches that would remain wanted.

### 3.6 What pushing further means, and where it stops

Pushing further is admitting every form the one interpreter runs
deterministically under the meter, and deleting every restatement. It
stops at: loops the meter can only abort (`while`, data-bound ranges),
floats (the value model), a mutation's unbounded fan-out (prediction and
scale), reads outside the partition on a device (D), and anything that
leaves the engine's write path. The rest — composition, accumulation,
filtering, seeking, grouping, formatting, defaults, patching — is §5.

## 4. Principles

1. **One language, one interpreter, every host.** Every form has one
   semantics, evaluated by one interpreter, on the server, in the wasm and
   in the native module. Where a form cannot be *evaluated* on a host — a
   fact only the server holds, a pass that only a non-predicting mutation
   may make (§5.9) — that is a residency class of the operation, printed
   by `check`, never a second syntax.
2. **The meter is the bound.** A form is admitted if the runtime meter
   bounds its cost; no static byte, step or closure price is reintroduced
   (LLP 2000.000 §6). A loop the meter cannot bound stays refused.
3. **The engine never asks for a fact it already holds.** The audience is
   the read rule; the write rule decides writes; a default the schema
   states is not restated at every insert.
4. **Residency is a fact, not a mode.** Where an operation runs and
   whether a mutation predicts are computed from the tables it reads and
   printed by `check`, carried in `api.ts`, explained by `why`.
5. **No silent class change.** A change that moves an operation from
   device to server, or a mutation from predictable to queued-without-a-
   row, is a note at `check`, never a runtime surprise.
6. **Nothing leaves the engine's write path.** Every relaxation is a read
   or a computation; writes keep rules, constraints, maintains, the log
   and at-most-once (LLP 3000.005's rule 2).
7. **Disclosure is decided once.** A write discloses nothing but its
   outcome; a read discloses rows the read rule admits; no relaxation
   adds a third channel (the blind-review territory of LLP 3000 decision
   4).
8. **The card grows one word at most**, and only by the author's ruling
   (LLP 2000.000 §8).

## 5. The language, re-derived

### 5.0 The meter charges what the new forms allocate (prerequisite)

The runtime meter is the bound this document leans on, and today it does
not charge the two things accumulation does most: `Builtin::Concat`
returns a new string or list without charging its size
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/runner.rs:417`), and every read of a
variable clones its value (`runner.rs:323`) without charging the copy,
while `Push` and literal construction do charge (`runner.rs:159, 334,
339`). A loop that rebinds `s = s + row.body` over 8,192 rows of 4,000
characters would allocate past the 16 MiB heap ceiling with only the step
meter watching. LLP 3000.002 lists interpreter heap among the boundaries
still needing an owner. Before any of §5.1 lands:

- `Concat` charges the result's `heap_bytes()`; `Let` and `Assign` charge
  the bound value's size when it is a string, list or object; a list or
  page produced by `group`, `sort`, `slice` or a shape charges its size,
  as scan results do today (`runner.rs:371, 376`);
- variable reads stop cloning large values, or charge the clone; the
  choice is measured on the fixture, and the cheaper one that keeps the
  ceiling honest wins;
- a stress test in the corpus: a concatenation loop and a list-growing
  loop each abort with `E_BOUND` naming the heap meter at the ceiling, on
  the server and on both device stores, and a query cut the same way
  reports `complete: false` with its `capped_at`.

This is a correction the received review made to rev 1's claim that the
interpreter "needs almost nothing"; it needs this.

### 5.1 The seven forms (rev 1)

| # | Today | Proposed | Source | Bound by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A local binds once ("duplicate local") | `x = expr` rebinds a local of the same type; a `for` may carry a counter or a running sum | S | the step meter; `Stmt::Assign` exists |
| 2 | No accumulators | `xs = []` then `xs = xs + [v]` (the binder emits `Push`); `s = s + n`; a list built in a loop is bounded by the heap meter and may be iterated | S | the heap meter (`Push` charges) and the step meter |
| 3 | `continue` refused | `continue` | S (minimalism) | `Stmt::Continue` exists |
| 4 | `else if` refused | `else if cond:` as sugar for the nesting (`elif` is a guess `check --fix` rewrites, §5.5) | S (minimalism) | nothing to bound |
| 5 | `where` refused | `t[prefix] [after c] first\|last N by idx where <predicate>`: the scan proceeds in index order; a row passing the read rule and the predicate counts toward N; examined rows and probes are metered | S + A's completeness class | the examined-rows and probe meters; §5.6 fixes completeness and cursors |
| 6 | Text `+` refused | `text + text` → `Concat`; `slice`, `tokens`, `count(text)`, `lower(text)`; no `format`, no interpolation | S (bytes) and a product discipline | the output and heap meters |
| 7 | `/` and `%` refused | `a / b` (integer quotient) and `a % b` beside `divmod`; zero refuses `E_DIV_ZERO` | S (a semantic preference) | nothing to bound |

### 5.2 Composition: helpers with bodies, jobs with preludes

**8. `fn` takes a body.** Today `fn` is one expression and `proc` is a
block that returns nothing, so the 0.1.0 builder wrote the receipt
fan-out three times (`send`, `sendMedia`, `sendPoll`) and the 0.2.6
builder wrote its receipt state as three nested one-line `fn`s. LLP
2000.000 §6 named the split as pricing residue. Proposed: one `fn`,
declared with an expression or with a block ending in `return`, callable
in expression position anywhere; expanded inline as helpers are today
(`bind/program/control.rs`), so a write inside a body is admitted exactly
when the caller may write (a query calling a writing `fn` is a placement
error at the call site, with the site named). `proc` is deleted with the
rewrite `fn` (rules: delete, don't deprecate).

```quarry
fn receiptState(cid: conversations, sender: principal, seq: int):
  unread = 0
  undelivered = 0
  for m in memberships[cid] first 50 by byConversationPrincipal:
    if m.principal = sender or m.joinedSequence >= seq:
      continue
    if m.readSequence < seq:
      unread = unread + 1
    if m.deliveredSequence < seq:
      undelivered = undelivered + 1
  return 'read' if unread = 0 else ('delivered' if undelivered = 0 else 'sent')
```

**9. A job may have a prelude.** Today a job's body is `chunk` sweeps and
nothing else (`parser/programs.rs:73`); both 0.2.6 builders hit it
("jobs must start with chunk"; "job-level binding outside a chunk").
Proposed: statements before the first `chunk` form a prelude that runs at
the start of every chunk under that chunk's meter — argument-derived
bindings, a config row, `divmod` of the period — with its bindings
visible in every chunk. Re-running it per chunk keeps a chunk wholly
done or not started; a prelude that writes is refused (a write belongs
to a chunk).

### 5.3 Data access: seeks, groups, a page is a list

**10. Range seeks in the key.** The IR and the runner already scan a
value range on the first unpinned index column (`Scan.range`,
`read.rs:381`); Quarry has no spelling for it, so a builder who needs
"messages at or after this one" or "posts in the trailing day" scans
from the start of the prefix and filters, examining everything older.
Proposed: a comparison on the first unpinned column inside the key
brackets is a seek — `t[prefix, col >= x]`, `t[prefix, col > a, col <=
b]` — priced as a prefix, continued by a cursor as any page is. A `where`
on that column alone is accepted and lowered to the same seek, with a
note naming the seek spelling so the cost is visible.

```quarry
query around(conversationId: conversations, messageId: messages):     -- M3: tapping a quote jumps to the original
  q = messages[messageId] ! else NOT_FOUND
  before = messages[conversationId, at < q.at] last 20 by byConvTime
  after = messages[conversationId, at >= q.at] first 21 by byConvTime
  return { before, after }

query trending():                                                     -- M8: the top ten of the trailing day
  day = posts[at > now - 86400000] first 8192 by byTime { id, at, likes: likeCounts[.id] }   -- the whole day, up to the examined ceiling
  return slice(sort(day, '-likes', '-at'), 0, 10)
```

A ranking is a ranking of the rows it examined. Rev 1 of this example
ranked `last 200` — the newest two hundred of the day — and the received
review caught that it could omit the day's most-liked post. The corrected
form seeks the whole day up to the examined ceiling; a day past the
ceiling is cut by the meter and the card says `complete: false`, so the
reader is told the top ten is of an incomplete set rather than shown a
plausible one. A `sort`, `group`, `sum` or `count` over a page with a
`next` cursor is over that page, and `check --cost` prints it as such.
For a population that outgrows the ceiling the maintained ranking on the
`feeds` card — `ranked`, scored by a job on a cadence — is the recipe, and
it is what the 0.2.6 lane built.

**11. `group`, `first`, `last`, `at`.** Grouping is the one shape neither
accumulators nor sorting give, and Clique asks for it three times
(global search grouped by chat, reactions counted per emoji, receipts by
stage); the value model has no dynamic map, so a builder groups on the
client. Proposed builtins, all over lists and pages, heap-metered:
`group(list, 'col')` → `[{ key, items }]` in first-seen order;
`first(list)`, `last(list)`, `at(list, i)` → the element or null;
`join(list, sep)` and `trim(text)` beside `lower(text)`, the small text
library the digest and the push preview keep asking for. A typed `map` is
considered and deferred (§11).

```quarry
query reactionCounts(messageId: messages):
  m = messages[messageId] ! else NOT_FOUND
  rows = reactions[m.conversationId, messageId] first 50 by byConvMessage
  return group(rows, 'emoji') { emoji: .key, count: count(.items), mine: exists reactions[messageId, viewer] }
```

**12. A page is a list.** `sort`, `slice`, `count`, `sum`, `group`,
`first`, `last`, `at` and `for` accept a page, a list, a shaped
collection and a list argument alike; the three types unify for every
list-valued builtin. This is LLP 2000.000 §6's page-to-list gap, closed.

### 5.4 Writes and schema: patching, defaults

**13. `upsert` patches.** Today `upsert t[key] { fields }` replaces the
row; the 0.2.6 builder lost an avatar to it, and every model expects
insert-or-update-these-fields (Postgres `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`,
Convex `patch`). Proposed: when the row exists, the fields are an
`update` patch and unnamed columns keep their values; when it does not,
the fields are an insert and a missing required column refuses `E_INPUT`
naming it. The card's "insert or replace" becomes "insert or patch".

**14. Column defaults.** `at: time default now`, `authorId: principal
default viewer`, `count: int default 0`, `role: enum('member', 'admin')
default 'member'`: an insert may omit a defaulted column; the engine
fills it on the host running the write, as it evaluates an explicit
`at: now` today, so a device's prediction and the server's judgement
agree as they do now. Defaults are literals, `now` and `viewer`; nothing
computed.

### 5.5 The guessed spellings

Every lane guessed at least one of these and paid a loop for the
diagnostic: `elif`; `cond ? a : b` for `a if cond else b`; `a?.b` for
`a.b if a != null else null`; `len(x)` for `count(x)`; `//` and `#` line
comments for `--`. Rev 2 proposed admitting them as aliases. **Ruled
(2026-09-13): the language keeps one spelling.** None is admitted; the
parser recognizes each guess and refuses it with the canonical form as
the rewrite, as `E_QUARRY_UNSUPPORTED` does for most of them today, and
`check --fix` (§8.9) applies the rewrite in place, so a guess costs a
command rather than a loop. The card stays one form per idea and
`NOT-DOING.md`'s "one spelling" stands.

### 5.6 `where`: completeness, the cursor, and reads inside it

A filtered page is `complete: true` when it holds N matches or the scan
reached the end of its range, and `complete: false` when the meter cut
it. `Scan` gains one field, `filter: Option<Expr>`; the runner applies it
inside the scan loop after the read rule; `pages.rs`'s page-plan analysis
and `N_NO_CURSOR` treat a filtered scan as a scan; on a device the same
code runs and `beyond_horizon` is unchanged.

**The cursor never positions after a hidden row.** Rev 1 said a cut page
continues after the last *examined* row, so a continuation could not
re-examine the rejected run; the received review pointed out that a
cursor is base64url over the index key values (`cursor.rs:51`) and that
the last examined row may be one the read rule hides, whose key the
viewer must not learn (LLP 3000 §7, "completeness is a class": the cut
site stays off the wire; a hidden row costs what a miss costs). The rule
is therefore: a filled page's `next` is after its last returned row; a
cut page's `next` is after the last **rule-visible** examined row —
returned or filter-rejected — and never after a hidden one. A visible
row's key is the viewer's to see whether or not the author's predicate
kept it, so nothing is disclosed, and a continuation still advances past
every filter-rejected run. On a uniformly authorized range (the rule
decided by the prefix) every examined row is visible and the two rules
coincide. On a non-uniform range the page keeps its existing class —
never `complete` (`N_NEVER_COMPLETE`), and a cut inside a hidden run ends
on an empty capped page as it does today — so `where` adds no channel
the non-uniform class did not already close.

Reads inside the predicate (`where exists likes[.id, viewer]`) are
proposed as admitted: each is a rule probe the probe meter already
counts, and `check --cost` prints the class as "probe × examined". Rev 1
deferred this; the Trending and grouped-search shapes want it, and the
meter bounds it.

### 5.7 What stays refused, and why it is not S

- `while` and recursion: a loop with no intended bound has only the step
  ceiling (1,000,000 steps, an abort) between it and a runtime failure
  for one user (LLP 0329.014.004 §3: "Refused, unchanged: `while`,
  recursion"). `range(a, b)` over int *expressions* is admitted in rev 3
  at the received review's suggestion — a range has an intended bound
  even when it is an argument's, and the step meter holds it; `check
  --cost` prints such a loop as "steps ≤ meter" rather than a literal
  count. `for` over a page, a list, a range or `tokens`, plus a job's
  `chunk`, cover the reference applications.
- A mutation's loop over a page that is not one complete capped group
  (`E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT`): D, for a mutation that predicts. A prediction
  must be exact and a write's fan-out visible; the 0.2.6 builder called
  this refusal valuable. The rewrite already names the job. §5.9 admits
  the same pass in a mutation that does not predict.
- Index-prefixed reads outside `where` and seeks; `exists` over an
  index: A.
- Floats: the value model has none; a native job or read (LLP 3000.005)
  is the hatch, and a score is rounded to an `int`.
- A typed `map` with dynamic keys: deferred (§11); `group` is the first
  answer and the rerun says whether the second is needed.

### 5.8 The language card after the change

The "left out on purpose" list loses its S entries and gains none. The

### 5.9 A non-predicting mutation may pass over a bounded page of a uniformly authorized prefix (revised after the blind review, §15)

`E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT` refuses a mutation's loop over a page unless the
page is one complete schema-capped group over a uniformly decided read
rule (`admission.rs:241–251`), because a prediction must be exact and
"for each member" must mean every member. The completeness reasons belong
to a mutation that predicts; the uniformity reason does not, and both
blind reviews showed why it must stay: a mutation runs under `Cut::Abort`,
so a pass over a non-uniform prefix whose hidden candidates exhaust the
examined ceiling aborts with `E_BOUND` where a query of the same prefix
answers one card whatever it found (`h1_a_non_uniform_scan_is_capped_
whatever_the_meter_found`). Abort versus success would be the
hidden-population bit the meter-cut review closed.

Proposed, as revised: in a mutation classified as never predicting
(§6.2 — a `server` site, or `: server` written by the author), a `for`
over a bounded page whose read rule is **uniformly decided by the
prefix** is admitted without the complete-group and cap clauses; rows
written are metered (1,024 per mutation); the pass is atomic; merges and
unknown origins keep today's refusal; a page over a non-uniform prefix
keeps `E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT` whatever the mutation's class. `check --cost`
prints "a pass over the first N rows of <prefix>, examined ceiling
<n>", so the partial claim is on the record. A predicting mutation keeps
the complete-group requirement. The variant both reviews name — ending
the pass at the ceiling the way a query does and committing the visible
prefix — is not proposed; it changes the promised semantics and needs
its own analysis. Regressions: §15's `m3_*` and `p2_*` list. Awaits the
author's ruling (§14, 15).

## 6. Residency and execution, made legible

This section absorbs the author's residency draft (LLP 3000.006) and
reduces its vocabulary to what the code already decides; §6.10 records
what was kept and what was not.

### 6.1 `online only`

A positive spelling for the table line whose absence today means
online-only:

```quarry
table auditEvents:
  actorId: principal
  action: text <=100
  at: time
  by byActorTime: actorId, at, id
  read <- exists admins[viewer]
  insert <- deny
  online only                          -- rows never enter a partition or a device
```

Optional: a table with neither `sync` nor `online only` keeps today's
meaning with no note. `check --residency` (§6.3) shows every table
either way, so the line is documentation the author can choose, not a
tax on every table. Whether it becomes required is the author's (§14).
The same line on a `maintain` opts it out of inheriting its source's
`sync` (§6.7).

### 6.2 Two words per operation, from the classes of its read sites

`check` classifies every read site, then rolls the sites up to the
operation. The received review was right that rev 1's roll-up — "every
table is synced" — was too coarse: a synced table with a horizon does not
establish that a device holds a given row or can prove its absence, and
the 0.2.6 lane's `exists messages[id]` guard is exactly a read over a
synced table that no device could decide beyond its horizon. So the site
classes are:

| Site class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `held` | a synced table with no horizon, or a maintain inherited into the partition: every row the viewer may read is on the device |
| `horizon` | a synced table with `last N`: held inside the horizon, decided by the server beyond it; a point read by a key the horizon does not order (an `id`) is `horizon` too, since absence cannot be proved locally |
| `server` | an online-only table, `connected`, or a native read on a device without the module |

and the operation's words are:

- a query **runs on the device** when every site is `held` or `horizon`
  (a `horizon` site past its horizon is `complete: false` and the client
  re-asks the server whole, `local.ts:617`, as today); otherwise it
  **runs on the server**;
- a mutation is **eligible to predict** when every read site is `held` or
  `horizon` and every write is to a synced table (LLP 2000.000 §7.3); it
  is **queued without a row** when a site is `server`. Eligibility is
  static; whether a given call *predicted* is the device's word at
  admission, and when a `horizon` site could not be decided the runtime
  refusal names the site and the key it could not establish (`E_PREDICT`
  today says only "a read it needs is not on the device"; it says which).
  `N_NOT_PREDICTABLE` (§6.4) fires when eligibility is lost, and `check`
  additionally marks each `horizon` site in an eligible mutation as "may
  queue without a row past the horizon", which is what the 0.2.6 builder
  needed to read before its offline probe.

Queueability is independent of both words: every mutation is durably
queued by the outbox whatever it reads; prediction is the only thing a
`server` site removes. A native read is `held`-class on a device with
the module and `server` on one without.

`api.ts` carries the words on `Op`: `runs: "device" | "server"` for a
query, `predicts: "eligible" | "never"` for a mutation with the `horizon`
sites listed, and a doc comment per operation with its arguments, result
and the `require` codes it can raise (§8.11). The card is unchanged;
these are facts about operations, not screen states.

### 6.3 `check --residency` and `why`

```text
residency
  profiles          public, no horizon (one row per principal)
  messages          to memberships[.conversationId] (from the read rule), last 500 by byConvTime
  auditEvents       online only
  receiptEvents     online only (no sync line)
  repostCounts      public (inherited from feedEntries) — N_MAINTAIN_SYNCED: one row per post reaches every device

operations
  thread            device; server past the horizon
  insights          server: reads activityTotals (online only)
  send              predicts
  createDm          queued without a row: reads dmPairs[other, viewer] beyond the partition
```

`why <op>` prints the site that makes an operation server-run or
non-predictable and the shortest rewrite that would change it. `why
<table>` prints its residency line and, for an inferred audience, the
rule it was inferred from.

### 6.4 Notes when a class moves

Two notes, in the family of `N_NO_HORIZON`:

- `N_NOW_SERVER` — a query that ran on the device in the previous
  generation now reads a site the device does not hold; the note names
  the site.
- `N_NOT_PREDICTABLE` — a mutation that predicted in the previous
  generation no longer does; the note names the read. This is the
  0.2.6 builder's hardest loop (a redundant `exists messages[id]` guard
  beyond the horizon) surfaced at `check` instead of in an offline probe.

`dev` compares against the generation it is serving; `check` alone
compares against `snapback/generated/` when present.

### 6.5 The offline outcome of a server-run read, and its liveness

Today a server-run read while offline is either `denied: E_OFFLINE`
(when the device refuses) or a plausible empty page (when it does not;
`local.ts:620` returns the device's data with `complete: false`). With
the class known at compile time the client does two things it cannot do
today:

- it keeps the last server answer of each observed server-run query for
  the client's lifetime, and offline returns it with `fresh: false` and
  `since` — the words already on the card — and returns `denied: {
  code: "E_OFFLINE", family: "link", retryable: true }` only when it has
  none. Never a plausible empty page;
- it re-runs an observed server-run query when a commit touches its
  tables, as the online tier does (`client.ts:276`), so a server-run
  query on the local-first client is as live as a device-run one. Whether
  the local-first client does this today is an acceptance item (§13),
  not a claim.

The residency draft's `{ unavailable: "requires-online" }` word is not
needed: `E_OFFLINE` on the card is that word.

### 6.6 The audience is the read rule

Today a synced table spells its audience twice: `read <- exists
memberships[.conversationId, viewer]` and `sync to
memberships[.conversationId]`, and `check` refuses any mismatch with a
rewrite (`E_SYNC`, `bind/schema.rs:51`). LLP 3000 §7 already binds
delivery to the rule's probe, so the second line adds no information the
engine does not derive. Muse spent six compile iterations here; Grok
named it its main friction. Proposed: `sync` with no `to` clause, with
or without a horizon, means *the audience my read rule states*:

```quarry
table messages:
  ...
  read <- exists memberships[.conversationId, viewer]
  sync last 500 by byConvTime           -- to memberships[.conversationId], from the rule

table todos:
  ...
  read <- .ownerId = viewer
  sync                                  -- to .ownerId

table notices:
  ...
  public 'notices are public'
  sync last 100 by byTime               -- public
```

The three invertible rule forms infer to the three sync forms; a target
with several principal columns still needs `by .col`, and `check` says
so; a rule that is not one of the three refuses `E_SYNC` with the rewrite
as today. `sync to …` remains valid and, when written, must equal what
the rule implies — the check that exists becomes a consistency check on
an optional clause rather than a requirement to restate.

### 6.7 A maintain that reaches every device says so

A `maintain` grouped by a public source's audience columns inherits
`sync public`: one value row per group on every device. The 0.2.6 Astra
lane's repost counter did exactly that — one row per post replicated to
every device, a cold-start timeout at the 50% checkpoint, and a
schema change mid-replay. Proposed: `N_MAINTAIN_SYNCED` names every
maintain whose inherited audience is public or a wide group, with the
row count it implies on the fixture when one is loaded, and `online
only` on the maintain opts it out of inheritance; `check --residency`
lists it (§6.3).

### 6.8 Measured residency on the fixture

`snapback4 test --residency [--fixture dir] [--as persona]` prints, per
synced table and per named persona, the rows and bytes the persona's
partition would acquire cold, and the total, beside the `N_NO_HORIZON`
and `N_MAINTAIN_SYNCED` notes. Facts (declared audiences, horizons,
caps) are printed as facts; fixture measurements as measurements; nothing
is extrapolated. This is LLP 3000.006 §9's "hub estimate," done as a
measurement on the fixture the suite already loads rather than an
estimate.

### 6.9 Two recipes, on cards, from the lanes

- **Receipts as high-water marks** (the `messages` card gains it). The
  0.1.0 lane broadcast one receipt row per message per recipient into

## 7. The online-only mode, paved

An application that wants no partition writes no `sync` line and opens
`createClient`. Both exist. What is missing is that nothing tells an
agent this is a first-class choice:

- `snapback4 init --online` writes a template with no `sync` lines, a
  `public`/`.col = viewer` schema, and a client on `createClient`;
  `init` (the default) keeps the local-first template; `init --expo`
  (§8.10) adds the native starter. The README's §1 gains a paragraph,
  "Two ways to run," and the language card's `sync` comments say "omit
  for an online-only table."
- `check --residency` on such a schema prints every operation as
  `server`, and `api.ts` says so; `useQuery` on the online tier is live
  through `/changes` as today.
- Nothing else changes: the same rules, meter, identity, assets, jobs,
  effects and diagnostics. A `sync` line added later moves an operation
  to the device and `N_NOW_SERVER`'s inverse is silent, because moving
  onto the device loses nothing.

This is the relaxed mode the author asked for, and it costs a template
and two paragraphs because the substrate already supports it.

## 8. The client, dev and rule seams the runs hit

Each item names the loop it removes (§2.2).

1. **A mutation's result reaches the client.** `Write` gains
   `result` on `sent` (the server already answers with it,
   `crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs`); `useMutation().run()` resolves with
   the `Write`. Ruled (2026-09-13): `result` on `sent` only — no
   `predicted` on `pending`; a prediction is visible as `pending` rows,
   not as an answer.
   Removes Grok's DM-reuse loop and the `dmByPair` workaround.
2. **A shape over a device row keeps `pending`.** `*` spreads device
   metadata through as it spreads columns; `api.ts` types a shaped
   object with `pending?: boolean` when its base is a row. Removes the
   0.2.6 M6 loop and the `historyRows` second query.
3. **`webDevice()` persists the wasm.** The opener keeps the module's
   bytes in IndexedDB by digest and opens on them with no network, so an
   offline reload does not depend on a URL. Removes the M3 loop the
   builder solved by hand and makes the cold-offline promise the
   opener's, not the app's.
4. **The `{ id }` asset argument predicts.** The device resolves the
   uploader's own upload facts onto the argument as the server does
   (`issues/20260913-snapback4-video-id-only-prediction-refuses.md`).
5. **Identity admission under `dev`.** Ruled (2026-09-13): on loopback,
   `snapback4 dev` lifts the per-peer and per-email minute counters to
   what the host can carry — the Argon2 slots and the connection caps,
   which are the physical bounds, remain — and prints the fact once;
   `serve` is unchanged. Removes two loops in each of the Astra and
   Grok lanes and the harness's backoff scaffolding.
6. **A hidden unique clash says what it is.** The refusal stays generic
   `E_RULE` where it must (§8.7), and its message adds the one sentence
   from `why E_RULE`: "or a unique key collided with a row you cannot
   read; to write into another principal's row use `upsert` under an
   `update` rule, or let a `maintain` keep the count."
7. **Write authority is the write rule (revised after the blind review,
   §15).** What Clique's counter tables hit is the binder: a unique-key
   `update` or `upsert` reads its target under the READ rule first
   (`bind/program.rs:603–641`), so a hidden target looks absent — an
   alternate-key update is `NOT_FOUND`, an upsert inserts and collides
   with generic `E_RULE` — while a by-id `update` already fetches without
   a read check and applies the update rule (`write.rs:66–100`). Two
   lanes opened their tables to get past it. The proposal, narrowed to
   what both reviews found defensible:
   (a) **target resolution is engine-private**: a unique-key or id write
   resolves its target without the read rule, then the table's `update`
   rule decides, as Postgres applies an `UPDATE` policy without a
   `SELECT` policy;
   (b) **one failure word for an unreadable target**: absence, an update
   rule that refuses, a failed `if` condition, an immutable-column
   mismatch and an incomplete patch on the insert path all answer
   `NOT_FOUND` — the zero-row update — with no `current`, no named index
   and no named column; a readable target keeps today's codes. The
   disclosure granted is exactly: a row the update rule let this writer
   modify exists, and the rule's predicates held of it;
   (c) **the merged image never leaves the engine**: a valued write's
   `result` and `E_CONFLICT`'s `current` are a read-rule `get` (null when
   hidden), never the merged row; `old` in `update (old, next)` is the
   engine's, bound nowhere in the author's program; asset facts on
   unchanged columns are preserved without re-validation, and the update
   rule is checked before any asset diagnostic (`write.rs:75–156`);
   (d) **withheld before recorded**: `_ops` stores exactly the body `/m`
   returns, so a replay cannot grow a row (the existing gap is
   `issues/20260913-snapback4-ops-replay-returns-cached-content-after-revocation.md`);
   (e) **the device never predicts against a target it does not hold**:
   a fetch miss on a write is queued without a row — no predicted
   `NOT_FOUND`, no predicted insert, no `pending` row — on both stores,
   and `admit` queues that class (today `local::predict` raises
   `E_PREDICT` only past a horizon, `admit` fails on any other refusal,
   and an upsert miss takes the insert path and plants a `pending` row);
   (f) **`insert … if absent` on a hidden collision stays `E_RULE`**
   (S05), and so does a collision with any *other* hidden row on another
   index: target authority does not transfer;
   (g) **a readable maintain over a hidden source is a declared read-rule
   channel** — a writer who may read the aggregate learns the delta they
   caused; `check` notes an update rule wider than the read rule under a
   maintain the writer can read;
   (h) `delete` is out of scope.
   Not claimed: constant time; that constraint outcomes disclose nothing
   — a cap counted over physical rows, `after insert` on the insert
   branch, a budget abort near a ceiling — which the GPT review lists and
   §15 records as residuals of the narrowed contract. Regressions: §15's
   `s05_*` and `w1_*` list. Awaits the author's ruling (§14, 15).
8. **`snapback4 sql "…"`, a fifth observability verb.** Read-only SQL
   over generated per-table views (`json_extract` over the store's
   `r(t, id, data)`, `crates/snapback4-core/src/store.rs:101`), as the
   developer over the dev store or a `--release` directory, bounded by a
   row limit; never an application surface and never rule-filtered,
   because it is the developer's own store. Answers the ground-truth
   complaint of two essays and the operations dimension (53 against 76);
   the 0.2.6 Astra lane backed up its SQLite by hand to get the same
   thing. Ruled (2026-09-13) as a fifth named verb beside `why`,
   `trace`, `logs` and `data`; the Moves line is in `NOT-DOING.md`.
9. **`snapback4 check --fix`.** Applies the spelling-class rewrites in
   place — an ambiguous key's `by`, a cursor index's missing `id`
   tie-break, an alias to its canonical form, `proc` to `fn` — and prints
   every other diagnostic as today. The diagnostic-and-rewrite loop
   exists so an agent can apply the rewrite; the tool applying the
   mechanical ones is the loop bar's cheapest win.
10. **`snapback4 init --expo`.** The `apps/chat-expo` starter with a
    Hermes-clean toolchain pin. Grok's seven failed iOS exports were an
    Expo/Babel version mismatch in the app, not Snapback; a starter
    removes the guess and the native artifact the grade wants.
11. **`api.ts` documents itself.** A doc comment per operation: the
    arguments, the result, `runs`/`predicts`, and the `require` codes
    the program can raise, which the binder knows statically. Agents
    read `api.ts` before the README.
12. **A server exit without a diagnostic.** The 0.2.6 Astra lane's scale
    instance exited twice during a schema change with a backfill and left
    nothing in its log; filed as
    `issues/20260913-snapback4-dev-exits-without-diagnostic-during-schema-change.md`.
    A defect, not a design item; listed because "sail through" includes
    not dying silently.
13. **An SDK-managed cache for ordinary media.** Asset responses are
    `private, no-store` by design, so every local-first builder — both
    Astra runs, and the Convex and Supabase builders for their own
    reasons — wrote a per-principal media cache by hand, with its own
    purge on revocation and its own view-once exclusion. Proposed: the
    local-first client keeps the bytes of ordinary `image`/`video`
    assets it has fetched, keyed by asset id and home row, in the
    device's own storage (IndexedDB on the web, the documents directory
    under the native module), with explicit retention (`assets: { keep:
    "viewed" | "none", maxBytes }`), purged when the home row leaves the
    partition, is cleared, expires or is deleted, and never for a `once`
    asset. `useAsset` reads the cache first offline. This is the
    residency draft's "ordinary media durability" gap and the received
    review's second-phase item; it makes the cold-offline promise the
    client's rather than the app's, as §8.3 does for the wasm. Ruled by
    the author on 2026-09-13: it lands with the language (§12, A).

Two hatches remain wanted after §5–§8, by the builders' own words:
ordinary code for integrations and SQL for reporting — in that order.
Worker actions (§9.2) are commissioned with the seams (§12, C2; ruled
2026-09-13), with Interview (LLP 3000.004), already on Snapback 4, as the
application that proves them: an
authentication provider, model calls and provider webhooks are exactly
the request-and-response integrations effects, service seats and minted
sessions stop short of, and Clique never needed one. SQL reads (§9.1)
stay specified and unbuilt until the reporting gap is measured after §5:
with seeks, `group` and the inspection SQL of §8.8 the case narrows to
familiarity, and the design as drafted has three costs the received
review named — the generated views cannot use the secondary index table,
the rule translation is a second authorization surface, and SQLite's
progress handler counts approximate virtual-machine instructions rather
than Snapback's resource contract — that a measured need has to justify.

### 9.1 SQL reads

```quarry
query insightsSql(): sql
```

with the body in `snapback/sql/insightsSql.sql`, read-only, over
per-viewer authorized views the compiler generates from the read rules
(`public` → `TRUE`; `.col = viewer` → `col = :viewer`; `exists t[.a,
viewer]` → a correlated `EXISTS`; `and`/`or`/`not` → the same). The
secondary index table is not usable by SQLite's planner, so a SQL read is
a scan bounded by SQLite's progress handler as the step meter, an
injected `LIMIT` as the output bound, and an authorizer that denies
writes, the internal tables and the raw `r`/`i`. Always server-run; typed
`unknown` in `api.ts` unless a shape is declared. It is a second query
language, which `NOT-DOING.md` refuses; it earns a Moves line only if the
rerun shows reporting still costs loops after §5.

### 9.2 Worker actions

```quarry
action sendInvite(to: text <=200, message: text <=2000)
```

An operation whose body is the application's own TypeScript in the
worker it already runs for effects (`snapback4/worker`): the client
calls `api.sendInvite` like a mutation; the server keeps the request,
a worker with a service token claims it over the wire (as it claims an
effect), performs it with a short-lived session the server minted for
the *viewer* — so its reads and writes run under the viewer's rules,
never the service's — and settles a result the server returns to the
waiting client. At-least-once, no transaction, never predicted, offline
is `E_OFFLINE`, and `dev` spawns `snapback/worker.ts` when it exists.
The engine still never calls out. This is Convex's action in Snapback's
shape and the answer to "there was no ordinary server function into
which I could move the awkward part" for the parts that need the
network.

### 9.3 Considered and not proposed

- **Native mutations** (`mutation x(): native`) for server-run
  mutations: consistent with §6 — a mutation that does not predict has
  no device twin to protect — but the Rust toolchain is the wrong
  weight for the ten percent; LLP 3000.005 stays the floats-and-vectors
  hatch.
- **A JavaScript engine in the process**: refused by `NOT-DOING.md`,
  the security review and the Snapback 2 record (Ibex's subset library
  was the effect runner's whole friction).
- **SQL writes**: bypass rules, constraints, maintains, the log and
  at-most-once. Never.

## 11. Alternatives considered

- **A relaxed grade by syntax** (`query x(): server` admits the relaxed
  forms; device programs keep the strict ones). Rejected: §3.2 shows the
  forms are device-safe, so the grade would exist only to preserve a
  restriction the meter already replaced, and every author would learn
  two languages to write one.
- **Online-only by default; `sync` as the opt-in exception.** Rejected:
  it is already the case (a table without `sync` is online-only) and the
  bet of LLP 2000.000 §7 is local-first; §7 paves the choice without
  moving the default.
- **Make the residency line mandatory now.** Deferred to the author; the
  table view gives the same legibility without a line on every table.
- **`{ unavailable: "requires-online" }` on the card.** Not needed:
  `denied: E_OFFLINE`, family `link`, retryable, is that word, and §6.5
  makes it the only offline outcome of a server-run read that has no
  last answer.
- **A typed `map`** (`m[k] = v`, `keys`, `has`; `Value::Map` exists in
  the value model). Deferred: it needs a typing rule and a `SetKey`
  statement in the IR; `group` covers every grouped shape Clique asks
  for; the ceremony bar says whether the general tool is still wanted.
- **Acquired history**: rows the server answered to a device-run query
  that fell back past its horizon are kept on the device as delivered
  history for that group, so "previously viewed" survives a cold start
  without a wide horizon. The 0.2.6 lane removed its horizons to satisfy
  the brief's "previously synced" and paid the cold sync. Deferred: the
  replica would hold a second kind of row, shaped queries cannot supply
  rows, and revocation must cover both kinds; a real feature for
  messengers, with a substrate cost this document does not price.
- **World-readable counters** as the idiom for cross-principal writes.
  Rejected: it is what two lanes did, and it is a disclosure; §8.7 is
  the mechanism.
- **A full `fn`/`proc` unification with recursion.** Recursion stays
  refused (an unbounded loop by another name); inline expansion with a
  depth of eight is kept.

## 16. The ceremony bar's "before" (2026-09-13)

The finding the bar was built to surface came on the first `check` of
every run: `N_NEVER_COMPLETE` on the membership scan behind the receipt
aggregate and the chat directory, and the same answer from every agent —
a second, private table duplicating the membership relationship keyed
the other way, kept in step by the same mutations. That is ceremony of
the §3.1 R kind: a restatement. A membership row read by the viewer's
own principal (`memberships[viewer] … by byPrincipalChat`) under a rule
`exists memberships[.chatId, viewer]` is its own witness — the row being
scanned is exactly the probe the rule would make — so the page is
complete by construction and the note should not fire. **Candidate,
not commissioned:** the binder recognizes a self-witnessing rule (a scan
whose pinned prefix binds the rule's `viewer` term on the same table)
as uniformly decided, marks the page complete, and drops the note; the
"after" run measures whether the duplicated table disappears. It
belongs with the language work of §5, after L2 lands, and needs no
ruling beyond acceptance of this document.

**2026-09-13 — commissioned as lane S and built.** In an `or`, equality
of the row's column with `viewer` (either operand order) is absorbed only

at that column and each other key term its own column's field; literals
block it. The engine and binder share the deciding fields. Server roster,

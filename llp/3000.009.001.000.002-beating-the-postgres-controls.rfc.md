## 1. Goal and decisions proposed

The author's goal, 2026-10-23, is to outperform both the Convex-on-Postgres
and self-hosted Supabase controls on every S1 W1–W5 cell, at both links.
This is a plan for that comparison, not a capacity claim. The comparison
remains the scale RFC's application, durability, authorization, offered-load
method and matched caps. It does not substitute a sharded counter for an
exact counter, or a trusted principal for a live credential.

The proposed work has three parts:

1. Land RT6's B2 flights, then allow every eligible member kind to share a
   physical commit. Give an unkeyed invocation a private server identity;
   never rerun an unknown commit. Preserve the solo boundary for long and
   administrative members.
2. Build result and authority caches over a complete per-store commit
   revision, including writes without a public sequence. A hit is a read
   at a certified committed head, not a result kept for a TTL. Zero-flight
   hits additionally require the read-ownership protocol in §4.2.
3. Complete K1–K4 pre-execution for disjoint writes. Serial in-place group
   commit is a useful first slice and a hot-key fallback, but its network
   floor cannot meet W4 or the nominal 1% mix target (§3).

This proposal refines the parent and its two existing children. It proposes
exceptions to the committer's keyed-only batching rule and the round-trip
RFC's prohibition on an authority cache, with their replacement protocols
specified here. It does not authorize implementation, publication, P2, or
SQL lowering. The author's requested Draft is the scope of this change.

Source citations below use paths relative to `snapback4/`; line numbers name
the checkout at `a025d67cd3b1a4a2aebeba014a9ecf30e2e69a25`. Historical
measurements cite their reports separately from current source. Predictions
are identified as such. The governing constraints are the store-port §5,
committer §3.5–§3.7 and round-trip §7: fenced durable writes, ordered
publication, whole-member atomicity, fresh authority and coherent snapshots.

### 2.2 Read ceilings

Use `R = 0.600 ms / 1.230 ms` as illustrative clean-link RTTs, rounded
from the matched controls' prepared-SELECT captures. The second is the
natural link **plus** delay, not an absolute 0.5 ms RTT. These are planning
constants; every new campaign substitutes its own loaded RTT distribution.
They deliberately exclude PG2/PG3's 41 ms tail. Median RTT arithmetic
cannot predict p99.

For `k` dependent exchanges, network latency is at least `kR`. With 32
connections and 128 clients the optimistic read bound is
`min(32/(kR), 128/(kR + other latency))`. The connection term binds in the
network-only model. For example, B1 W1 gives
`32/(6 × 0.000600) = 8,889/s` and
`32/(6 × 0.001230) = 4,336/s`.

| Read path | k | Network ms, natural / delayed | Pool-32 upper bound ops/s, natural / delayed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Measured B1 W1/W2 | 6 | 3.60 / 7.38 | 8,889 / 4,336 |
| RT6 B2(a) W1 | 3 | 1.80 / 3.69 | 17,778 / 8,672 |
| RT6 B2(a) W2 | 2 | 1.20 / 2.46 | 26,667 / 13,008 |
| W3, only common envelope reduced to B1, body unchanged | 136 = 153 − 17 | 81.60 / 167.28 | 392 / 191 |
| W3, even ignoring its entire envelope, unchanged 132-call body | ≥132 | ≥79.20 / 162.36 | ≤404 / 197 |
| Certified result **and** authority hit | 0 | 0 database network time | App CPU, memory and HTTP bound |

The W3 rows are extrapolations from PG2, not current RT0 measurements. RT6
alone does not batch its program reads. Its miss path needs the same-level
read composition in the round-trip RFC §8, independently of cache hits.

B1 could reach W1's historical target by this bound, but cannot reach W2
or W3. B2 W2 can exceed 16,000 naturally in this ideal model, but cannot
even tie it on the delayed link. B2 W1 has network headroom on both links;
it is the intended W1 route if its broad key distribution gives few hits.
CPU, pool queues and the 50 ms SLO can lower every bound.

For a zero-flight hit fraction `h`, miss cost `kR`, and `P` connections
available to misses, the database term becomes
`lambda(1−h)kR ≤ P`. Reserve two of the 32 total store connections for the
writer and independent lease traffic: use **P = 30** in actual sizing,
not 32 readers plus an unreported writer/lease pool. At delayed W2's
strictly higher test of 22,627/s, B2 misses need
`h ≥ 1 − 30/(22627 × 2 × 0.001230) = 46.1%`.
At 16,000/s, the analogous bound is 23.8%. An authority cache alone removes
authority reads, not program reads. A result cache without authority reuse
still pays ingress plus final validation: at two flights and P=30 its
all-hit ceiling is 25,000/12,195 ops/s, so delayed W2 still cannot tie
16,000. Combining result and authority validation into one statement does
not remove a separate pre-body ingress check. G6 removes both on qualified
hits; G5 does not claim that saving.

W2 has at most 256 × 32 = 8,192 ordinary viewer/room keys; W3 has 256
viewer keys, by the generator and schema cited in §2.1. These are plausible
warm working sets. W1 spans the million-message population with viewer
separation; assume no useful hit rate until measured. W3 with a 132-flight
miss path would need 99.2% hits to exceed 22,627/s on the delayed link;
a reset or invalidation would overwhelm it. Cache warm-up does not excuse
that miss path. At 22,627/s, 128 clients also allow only
`128/22627 = 5.66 ms` mean outstanding time before the client cap itself
binds. A zero-flight database floor does not guarantee that HTTP rate.

### 2.3 Single-member writes

The worker owns a bounded queue and writer engine
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_committer.rs:7`, `:25`, `:103`), and the
reference driver executes one member and physically commits it
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/committer.rs:198`). More request threads
do not multiply that serial stage.

A useful coarse bound is `1/(kR + execution + flush)`. Using the whole
request's k is conservative because ingress runs outside the writer. A
more optimistic bound removes that ingress flight. Show both rather than
pretending all 16 exchanges hold the writer:

| Path | Whole-request k / serial k after ingress | Whole-request reciprocal ops/s | Optimistic serial bound ops/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Measured unkeyed B1 shape | 16 / 15 | 104 / 51 | 111 / 54 |
| Measured keyed first shape | 24 / ≤23 | 69 / 34 | Preflight is also outside; needs separate stage attribution |
| RT6 B2(a) unkeyed shape | 8 / 7 | 208 / 102 | 238 / 116 |
| Exact W5 with three extra guard flights, predicted B2 | 11 / 10 | 152 / 74 | 167 / 81 |

The W5 prediction carries PG2's three extra program reads; RT0 must measure
it before use. The keyed B2(a) target is nine whole-request flights, not a
claim that RT6 has already reduced measured 24 to nine. Replay, action
credentials, cold prepares and refusals keep separate ratchets.

Even B2 cannot reach W4 or the mixed W5 targets with a single-member
committer. Exact W5 is close enough that durability decides: at a
hypothetical 5 ms flush, its ten serial flights take 11.0/17.3 ms,
or 91/58 ops/s. Natural 141/s fails; delayed 35/s might pass. C=32 writers
limits offered concurrency, not a multiplication of serial throughput.

## 3. Writes: group commit for every member kind

### 3.1 Private member identities and outcomes

Amend committer §3.4 so an unkeyed invocation can share a batch after its
kind has a qualified retry/outcome protocol. At admission mint a random
internal member id, bound to store incarnation, principal, operation/kind,
raw argument digest and immutable inputs/minted ids. Each actual attempt
records its own time, presence and chunk decisions in the replay transcript;
a rolled-back attempt does not freeze these observations for its rerun.
It is a private identity
for this invocation, not a client operation id. A new unkeyed HTTP request
still denotes a new invocation, even if its bytes match an earlier one.
Preserve public `id` absence; response normalization already omits a null
mutation id (`crates/snapback4/src/serve_head.rs:88`).

Record the internal receipt, disposition and necessary continuation data
atomically with the member's durable changes. Keep client receipts in their
existing namespace. Engine, Publication and Direct kinds need identities
too; a non-public-sequence write must not become an untracked exception.
Administrative and long engine members remain solo for latency and raw
transaction custody, even when internally identified. "Every kind" means
batch eligibility after qualification, not an obligation to co-batch every
invocation or erase committer §3.2's exclusions.

| Physical batch outcome | Treatment of every member |
| --- | --- |
| Successful COMMIT acknowledged | Publish invalidations and continuations in revision order, then acknowledge each member's result |
| Known whole-batch rollback, with the original transaction ended | Run no continuation; rerun alone, in original order, using the same internal identity and fresh authority/database facts. Revalidate or discard any speculative execution |
| COMMIT outcome unknown | Every member receives `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, with no success/head metadata. No automatic rerun, including a member whose program had returned a refusal |
| Continuation fails after a confirmed COMMIT | Preserve the durable outcome and report continuation failure separately; never classify it as a rolled-back mutation |

Only explicit database abort evidence or an acknowledged rollback of the
original transaction establishes the second row. A broken connection, an
absent receipt while the original backend can still commit, a timeout or
ownership loss does not. The adapter already distinguishes classified
COMMIT aborts from unclassified failures and keeps COMMIT uncancellable
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/ownership.rs:133`). This RFC
does not broaden a known rollback into an inference from a missing row.

For the unknown row, internal receipts permit later reconciliation after
the original transaction is known finished/fenced. They do not authorize
transparent rerun. A client with its own id can retry through the existing
bound replay protocol; an unkeyed client still cannot retry safely by
guessing. Internal receipt retention is bounded by outstanding commands and
a declared reconciliation horizon; once a receipt is retired its id can
never be retried or reused. A crash does not replay an unacknowledged queue.
Server-generated tokens or secrets in receipts require the same private
storage treatment as their existing mint outcome, not logging.

This keeps at-most-once durable application of an invocation across infrastructure
recovery, but changes failure correlation: one unknown physical commit now
makes several callers uncertain. Refusals in that batch can also become
uncertain. Latency and visibility change as well: a slow member delays its
batch peers, and readers cannot observe intermediate members before the
physical commit. These are observable differences from single-member
batches. They preserve serial outcomes and whole-member atomicity, not
identical timing or failure independence. A transaction that revokes a
session must invalidate a later member's authority inside the same batch.

### 3.2 One tail flight, serial members, then pre-execution

Use B2(a)'s ordered flights, with owner fence first and generation/receipt
checks in later commands after the head lock. Keep COMMIT a separate
flight. The batch tail carries every member's public commit/log/evidence
and internal receipt, updates head once, and verifies counts before COMMIT.
The current tail has a single `commit` slot, writes head/commit/evidence/
events/effects in one CTE and verifies one committed row
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/tail.rs:25`, `:32`, `:62`, `:85`).
Extending it to a vector of members is necessary work; wrapping today's
tail N times would retain N network waits. Rollback discards all buffered
tails, receipts, footprints and sequence allocations, and no continuation
runs before durability.

For an optimistic serial in-place B2 batch, let p be program flights/member,
one further flight/member fetch fresh authority and open its frame, and
three shared flights cover opening/fencing, tail and COMMIT. Then
`T(N) ≥ [3 + N(p+1)]R + F + N e`, where e includes local execution and
SQL work. This is a proposed schedule to ratchet, not a measured count.
For W4 p=3; exact W5 p=6; the nominal mix gives p≈3.03. With N=32 and
F=e=0:

| In-place batch | Bound natural / delayed ops/s | Can exceed the historical target? |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| W4, 32/(131R) | 407 / 199 | Neither link |
| W5 exact, 32/(227R) | 235 / 115 | Both, by throughput only; batch latency binds |
| W5 nominal 1% mix, 32/(131.96R) | 404 / 197 | Neither link |

Even eliminating all member authority/framing cost leaves the infinite-N
program floor `1/(pR)`: W4 556/271 and the mix 550/268. Neither reaches
its target on either link. Raising N cannot remove that term. An authority
cache may remove the extra member flight; it cannot remove the dependent
read/update/read program without another execution strategy.

Exact W5 is different. With p+1=7 and F=5 ms, N=4 takes
`31R+5 ms = 23.6/43.1 ms`, giving 169/93 ops/s before queueing; N=8 takes
40.4/77.6 ms, giving 198/103. N=8 still falls just short of the natural
strictly higher 200/s example. These numbers show a plausible route past
141/35, not proof of 200/50 under p99 ≤100 ms. N=10 would give 205/s
naturally in this model, but its 48.8 ms service plus up to 45 ms to collect
ten arrivals at 200/s leaves little tail margin. At 35/s, waiting for eight
arrivals alone takes about 200 ms, so a fixed batch of eight is wrong.

Follow committer §7: commit immediately when idle; drain already queued
members; adapt N and a short maximum wait from observed end-to-end p99.
Start with a 1 ms maximum collection wait and N≤32, then reduce either
when the remaining SLO budget is small. Measure batch fill, oldest-member
queue age, execution, tail and flush separately. N is limited by latency,
bytes and work, not just arrivals. A backlog of N=32 exact-W5 members
would spend 136/279 ms in network waits alone and must be split. Long
members remain solo but still delay the queue behind them.

The controls update one counter row/document for exact W5
(`experiments/scale-controls/supabase-schema.sql:103`;
`experiments/scale-controls/convex-app/convex/chat.ts:43`), so the shared
dependency serializes or conflicts; it does not become 32 independent hot
writers. In-place execution inside our
batch sees each predecessor's update without OCC retries and amortizes
durability. That is the potential exact-W5 advantage. Disjoint counters in
the raw/Supabase controls can execute concurrently, while our in-place
stream cannot; compare the scale RFC §3 H1 and the matched results rather
than assuming that advantage applies to W4.

K3/K4 therefore remain required for W4 and the mix: K1 records complete
reads/transcripts, K2 supplies the overlay, K3 validates/pre-executes on
SQLite, K4 qualifies the same mechanism on Postgres. Keep hot W5 in place
after the conflict budget rather than paying repeated optimistic failures.
This RFC moves safe group commit ahead of that work; it does not rename
serial batching as K3 or make K6 decomposition a prerequisite for W4.

A sizing example for pre-execution: at 30 execution connections and two
execution flights, `30/(2R) = 25,000/12,195 executions/s`. With N=32,
four shared commit flights and F=5 ms, a commit stage with no per-member
cost would reach `32/(4R+F) = 4,324/3,226 members/s`. To sustain the
natural strictly higher W4/mix test of 3,200/s, that model allows only
`[32/3200 − (4×0.000600+0.005)]/32 = 81 microseconds/member`
for validation, apply work and extra serial waits. A higher flush tail or
per-member flight defeats it. Measure the complete write sets; neither
two execution flights nor four commit flights is established by this RFC.
The result is the lesser stage, with conflicts and SLO headroom deducted.

## 4. Reads: exact-invalidation caches

### 4.1 The stream must cover more than public change evidence

Existing evidence is useful but insufficient. `revocation::Recorder` keeps
bounded net row images and emits table/population/column changes, widening
after 256 entries or 256 KiB (`crates/snapback4-core/src/revocation.rs:9`,
`:21`, `:58`). `preserved()` certifies **authority preservation**, not
equal query results: the regression deliberately preserves authority over
a body edit (`crates/snapback4-core/src/revocation.rs:82`, `:231`).
Postgres evidence is keyed by public seq; missing evidence widens, and
interval certification requires contiguous complete evidence, at most 500
commits (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/change_evidence.rs:25`,
`:42`; `crates/snapback4-core/src/revocation.rs:204`). A preserved table can
have changed data. It is never permission to reuse a query's bytes.

Introduce an internal durable **revision** for every committed member,
including receipt-only operations, session mints/rotation/revocation,
principal generations, worker-family markers, claims, clock pins, expiry,
publication, maintains, assets and activation. Public seq advances only
when its present semantics require it. Record revision and footprint in
the physical transaction, using the committer's already-designed revision
domain (§3.3 there), not a second competing commit order.

Ownership-lease traffic is the parent's explicit out-of-stream exception;
the queue maintains it independently
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/command.rs:258`). Handle its read-safety
effect through §4.2's grant/epoch, not a fictitious application revision.
No other out-of-stream durable producer may leave application or authority
state unversioned while these caches are enabled.

The private footprint includes family keys, old/new row identities and
index addresses, and broad invalidation for raw or unqualified operations.
It need not expose values or authority details on `/changes`. Unknown
coverage means invalidation of all potentially affected entries, never
"no change". The existing public evidence can supply a conservative
table-level invalidation when complete; typed family writes must separately
cover changes outside it. Start with whole-table dependencies for data,
whole-family dependencies for authority, and widen globally on unclassified
members. Exact invalidation means no false negatives, not zero false positives.
Refine to K1's Row/Span/family dependencies only after its completeness
census and adversary pass, including absent keys, hidden examined rows,
negative membership, scan boundaries and non-key field edits.

Add a publication gate shared by the committer and cache reads. Close it
before sending COMMIT. After confirmed COMMIT, atomically install all
invalidations, authority updates, revision/head, generation and code epoch;
then open the gate, publish wakeups and acknowledge. No cached read may
linearize in the database-commit/invalidation gap. A hit already selected
under the previous gate may complete as a read before that commit, just as
an already-open snapshot may. Keep the gate closed on an unknown outcome
until fenced reconciliation reconstructs the committed head and clears
caches; pause new writer admission during that reconciliation too. A
continuation failure must not reopen it with old evidence.

Fill from the ordinary RT4 snapshot, collecting the result and all its
dependencies at S. At installation under the gate, validate against every
revision since S; reject the fill on intersection, unknown coverage, epoch
change or a missing interval. Bound the validation window; falling behind
forces a miss/refill. Do not attach a new head to an old result without
that proof. In-batch members see the transaction's current state, while
external reads see only the final physical commit, with all member
invalidations installed together.

### 4.2 Ownership is an additional protocol, not an inference from the writer fence

In P1, complete local commit observation is possible only while this
process is the exclusive current owner and all durable producers use its
stream. Store-port §5's first-lock write fence does not itself fence a
CPU-only cache hit. A paused old owner can resume after a successor has
committed a revocation. Terminating its Postgres sessions does not stop it
from sending cached bytes. Nor does an asynchronous loss notification
close that race. The current `LeaseDeadline` starts before renewal and
uses a 50 ms/proportional margin, and queue admission observes loss
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/store/port/ownership.rs:16`, `:24`;
`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/command.rs:258`, `:335`). These are useful
mechanisms, not an existing proof of zero-flight read authority.

Propose a **bounded read grant** tied to `(store_id, owner token, holder,
cache epoch)`. It is a separate read-safety contract, not a replacement
for the write fence. The database persists the grant's expiry; every
takeover, release, offline writer and foreign activation must either wait
until it expires, or obtain an acknowledged cache-stop/drain from the
holder before proceeding. Changing a token, terminating sessions or
shortening the writer lease cannot shorten an outstanding read grant.
Local activation goes through the publication gate and clears the epoch.
A foreign activator must acquire ownership through this same protocol.
If any admitted writer can bypass it, zero-flight caches are disabled.

The grant needs a declared clock bound. Sample a suspend-aware monotonic
clock before sending its acquisition/renewal; use a local deadline earlier
than the earliest possible database expiry, allowing the qualified maximum
relative clock-rate error and database forward adjustment over the grant.
For example, if the database can advance at most `(1+rho)t + J` during t
units of the local clock, a duration-L grant permits a local deadline no
later than `send_time + (L−J)/(1+rho)`, less a rounding margin; require L>J.
The bound must survive supported lossless failover, or that deployment
cannot enable grants. A fixed empirical 50 ms margin is not that bound.
Check the deadline **synchronously on every hit at its linearization
point**, including after pauses; do not trust the renewal thread to run.
A late renewal never reopens an expired epoch. Start with grants no longer
than the existing ownership lease; renewal uses the reserved connection.

This adds an explicit clock assumption for cache reads where the write
fence previously needed none. On hosts/providers without a defensible
bound, suspend detection or grant-aware takeover, retain fresh database
validation. In an asynchronous system with arbitrary clock jumps, forced
takeover and no communication, zero-flight fresh authority is not possible.
The safe alternative is fresh database validation, potentially validating
both cached result and authority in one statement after ingress. Compare
the statement's identity/generation/revision with the cache's certified
version; a mismatch requires complete interval validation or a miss, even
if the local process has not observed the foreign writes. Do not silently
sell that as zero-flight. The panel should judge the
grant/availability tradeoff before implementation; it can reject grants
without rejecting the result-cache work.

On observed lease loss, renewal ambiguity, database reconnect/failover,
foreign epoch change or stream discontinuity, close the gate and drop both
caches. A restart begins empty. Re-enable only after new ownership/read
grant and one coherent snapshot establishes identity, generation, revision
and head. During a partition, the old owner may serve hits only until its
conservative grant deadline; a successor cannot publish newer state during
that interval. Graceful handoff first closes/drains local cache reads,
then relinquishes the grant. A paused process must check its deadline on
resume, independently of any queued callback.

P2 disables these local caches by default. Forwarding writes to one
committer does not prove that other processes have received its latest
revision. P2 would need grant-aware read barriers and a complete ordered
invalidation stream with acknowledgement, gap and reconnect handling, or
reads forwarded to the owner. Best-effort `LISTEN` notifications are a wake
signal, not that protocol. No P2 speedup is counted here.

### 4.3 Result identity, validation and response metadata

The key includes store incarnation, generation, schema/program/native-code
digest, operation and canonical typed arguments, viewer and credential
kind, authority scope, effective cap/policy version, cursor, retained-search
inputs, provenance and delivery mode, read ceilings and response-shaping
options. Include any additional context the operation can observe; an
unclassified dependency makes it ineligible. Do not put raw bearer tokens
in keys or logs. Keep principal-separated results initially; cross-viewer
sharing needs a separate proof. A generation alone is insufficient for
code identity: reader invalidation explicitly handles native code changing
without a schema-generation change
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_readers.rs:55`).

The current query opens one request snapshot, observes reader time,
captures seq, invokes authority validation and runs the interpreter over
that same snapshot (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:789`, `:820`,
`:829`, `:873`). The HTTP door borrows that snapshot for authority/identity
and supplies delivery mode according to scope
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1168`, `:1211`). Preserve this RT4 property
on misses; cache fills do not open an unrelated head snapshot.

On a hit, under one gate read: verify the live grant/epoch, re-resolve
authority from valid cached facts with a fresh clock sample, validate the
entry through the published revision, and capture the head/generation/id.
This is the hit's linearization point. Return data observationally equal
to executing at that head with those authority facts. Set query `seq` and
the response head to that certified head, not its original fill seq; use
the same `generation` and `store_id` in payload and any existing envelope.
Do not invent a new query-body `head` field: the current query reply has
`seq`, generation and store identity, whereas mutation/replay heads have
their own semantics (`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1307`, `:1332`;
`crates/snapback4/src/serve_head.rs:94`).

Initial eligibility is complete, deterministic, durable-only queries with
no output dependent on the current revision/head and no cursor/receipt/
delivery artifact bound to the fill sequence. Where a
cursor or manifest embeds seq or mutable authority, reconstruct it from
the validated dependencies at the hit head, with equivalent metering, or
bypass the cache. Never relabel an opaque old cursor. Admit W1–W3 only
after full wire/manifest equivalence, not just data equality. Request
logging, rate limits, request-worker permits, read ceilings and output
limits still apply; hits cannot become an unmetered unlimited endpoint.
Retain the logical read/cut accounting for a certified equivalent result
and measure physical cache work separately. Do not report zero logical
work as if the query had no cost.

### 4.4 Authority facts and clock boundaries

Cache the facts needed by the existing shared Rust resolver, not a
permanent `allowed` bit. Initially admit the fixed ordinary projection
only: digest/session existence including cross-table duplicate detection,
principal, family, kind, scope, session/cap expiry, principal generation,
supervisor markers, store id/generation and activation identity. Those
are represented by `SessionFacts` and the ordinary resolver
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/identity/facts.rs:5`, `:22`, `:31`). Exact
invalidation covers session deletion/rotation, family and principal
revocation, read-session changes and supervisor changes, including writes
that do not advance public head. Missing rows and default generation zero
are dependencies too. Ingress may use these facts, but a queued mutation
still validates authority at serial admission, against preceding batch
members and a fresh clock.

Do not initially cache action or arbitrary custom-kind resolution: action
authority traverses work/claim/admission dependencies and deadlines
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:192`, `:228`). A later slice may
qualify their complete dependency closure. Specialist service endpoints
retain their additional service/full-scope/principal checks, not just
ordinary bearer liveness (`crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:257`).

Expiry can happen without a commit. On every hit, the shared policy checks
session expiry, read cap expiry and captured principal generation against
a fresh authority clock, exactly as its current liveness function does
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:242`). The earliest relevant
deadline bounds reuse; a timer helps eviction but never authorizes a hit.
Authority time and the ownership grant's monotonic clock are different
clocks. Development clock changes invalidate the epoch; initially bypass
staging/dev caches, whose response helper can perform session timing reads
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_head.rs:40`). No TTL substitutes for either
invalidation or expiry checks. This is the answer to round-trip §7's
objection: a complete commit version plus exclusive read epoch and clock
checks, with fail-closed loss handling.

### 4.5 Lifetimes, delivery, ephemeral state and bounded memory

Queries can use `now` in filters, addresses and returned values; the guide
contains those forms (`crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/time.md:34`, `:37`).
Initially bypass any query or transitive rule/helper whose result depends
on sampled time, presence or ephemeral rows. Do not invent a time bucket
and call it exact. Current read rules cannot name `now`; the guide instead
uses stored lifetimes for timed authority
(`crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide.rs:518`). This proposal does not admit
clock expressions in rules. Durable time-independent maintained values can cache,
but their source, owner, maintenance and authorization dependencies must
all invalidate them; reads of maintained counts are explicit in S1 W3
(`experiments/scale-controls/snapback-app/snapback/schema.q:50`).

Initially also bypass queries whose reachable data/rule scope has expiry
or `not before`, even if no returned row currently has a deadline.
Hidden, absent and future rows can change a result. A later lifetime-aware
slice must record the earliest boundary across examined **and omitted
candidate** rows/scopes, advance/settle due publications through the
committer, and miss at that boundary. The existing engine observes reader
time and exposes pending openings; HTTP materializes an encountered opening
then reevaluates (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:824`, `:896`;
`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1276`). Keep that behavior; never turn a
pending opening into cached absence. A boundary index, not a sweep timer,
must prove that no unseen opening/expiry can change the cached page.

Do not cache delivery reports, asset grants/bytes, view-once consumption,
Following history or ephemeral/presence answers in this increment. A full
query's pure delivery manifest may be reused only with the head/authority
proof in §4.3; delivery remains a separate action. Read scope keeps its
allow-list and omits delivery manifests/meters; it cannot gain full-session
artifacts through a shared cache key. These are current guide semantics
(`crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/read_only.md:23`, `:36`). Queries touching
ephemeral tables currently use a separately versioned hub
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:833`); durable revision alone
cannot validate them.

Keep `/changes` and sync correctness independent of result-cache admission.
Current held polls share bounded answers, but entry/unheld polls read the
store and final bearer liveness is rechecked
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_changes.rs:124`, `:162`, `:192`). Its head and
evidence share a snapshot (`crates/snapback4/src/serve_changes.rs:211`).
Certified sync also reads images, receipts and evidence in one snapshot
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/sync_evidence.rs:16`). Preserve those
paths first. Publish their existing wakeups after the cache invalidation
barrier. A live-query reissue can then hit the result cache safely. No
server result hit proves a device's retained authority, supplies sync
receipts or changes public preservation certification. Sharing the new
private validation machinery later must preserve these distinctions.

Start with configurable hard per-store bounds: 128 MiB result payloads and
dependencies, 16 MiB authority facts, 32 MiB revision window, and a 512 MiB
process-wide combined cap across stores and cache bookkeeping. Count keys,
reverse indexes, retained payload references and in-flight fills, not just
serialized data. Use bounded per-principal admission and an eviction policy
that resists one-shot scans; oversize entries bypass. Cap entry count and
in-flight fill count as well as bytes. Eviction always means miss. If the
revision window fills, evict entries or drop fills needing its oldest
revision; never discard proof while retaining its claimed validity.
Internal receipts have their separate durable lifecycle (§3.1); cache
eviction never deletes a still-needed receipt. These are proposed starting
bounds to measure under the matched host RAM budget, not raised query limits.

## 5. Fair measurement

PG2/PG3 used one SSH process for all product connections; the historical
controls used one forwarded stream per connection. PG2 reports later
prepared RTT p99 near 41 ms. PG3 reports that tail before/after W1 and a
third-party Cargo job on 450a overlapping all three provisional 707/s
windows. Its report explicitly disqualifies the cell; those rates do not
establish B1 performance. See the [PG2](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261022-snapback-postgres-2/README.md)
and [PG3](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261023-snapback-postgres-b1/README.md)
records. Median idle ping agreement cannot repair this transport difference.

The claim needs a new campaign:

- Use the same database transport for every system: preferably direct TCP
  over the same app/database hosts; otherwise one independent SSH stream
  per physical connection, with a dispatcher for products accepting one
  database URI. Do not multiplex one product's pool through a single SSH
  process while distributing another's. Measure proxy/SSH CPU separately
  and charge equivalent resources to all cells.
- Use quiet app, database and load hosts, with an exclusive measurement
  window, pinned core/RAM budgets and a continuous process/resource census.
  Before each cell, take three independent prepared-SELECT RTT captures
  after warm-up. Pre-register a p99≤5 ms natural-path guard for this
  campaign, with the expected added delay applied to the delayed guard;
  any failing capture rejects the boundary. Capture loaded RTT during and
  after the cell too. Foreign builds/load, a tail recurrence or a broken
  observer invalidate the whole affected cell, retaining all raw trials.
  Do not kill unrelated processes or rerun only the winning repetition.
- A one-host emulated-link experiment is acceptable for mechanism sizing
  and, if explicitly commissioned, a comparison qualified only for that
  topology: isolated app/database/load CPU sets, non-overlapping RAM/I/O
  budgets, identical durable storage treatment, and a per-connection delay
  path applied to all app/database traffic. Verify loaded RTT and emulator
  CPU/queue limits. Direct loopback alone is not the delayed link. Shared
  memory/cache/storage contention and absence of a real host boundary must
  be reported. It cannot silently replace the scale RFC's separate-host
  comparative claim or a managed-Postgres claim.
- Rerun Supabase, Convex/PG and token-verifying raw Postgres in the same
  topology, host window, delay implementation and caps; keep the no-token
  raw row separately labelled if retained. Preserve advocate configurations
  and native caches. Keep PostgREST-only Supabase labelled as such; do not
  rename it a complete-stack or managed-service result. The historical
  figures are planning targets, not contemporary causal controls.

Keep 1M identical messages, unchanged Quarry and controls, 10 s warm-up,
20 s measured window, 128 read clients, C=32 writes, three repetitions at
every tested rate, intended-arrival latency, read p99≤50 ms/write p99≤100 ms,
errors≤0.1%, and exact write-counter equality including warm-up. Preserve
expired/time-out arrivals, unknown writes and overload errors. Unknown
outcomes disqualify a write trial. Add real HTTP quiescence before snapshots
or isolate trials so late writes cannot contaminate the next cohort; a
FIFO drain credential alone is insufficient, as PG2 records. Retain the
existing search ladder and higher failing brackets; do not interpolate.

For each cell, "outperform" means all three repetitions pass at a higher
tested offered rate than **both rerun controls'** highest all-three passing
rates, at each link. A tie, a best repetition, a lower median latency at
different loads or merely exceeding an old target is insufficient. A
campaign-wide claim needs all twelve cells. Also report common-load latency
and spread; the throughput win need not imply lower latency everywhere.

Caching is an engine feature, as it is in the matched Convex rows; it need
not be disabled for the headline. Fairness requires disclosure of bounded
memory, actual connection counts including writer/lease/background roles,
warm-up, query-key distribution, hit/miss/eviction/coalesced-fill counts,
invalidation fan-out, total hit flights including ingress, grant renewal cost and cold
recovery. Preserve Convex's caches and Supabase's configured JWT cache,
which the [matched report](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261022-fairness/README.md)
already discloses. Add cache-off and cold/reset rows and a separate mixed
read/write invalidation challenge. Do not alter S1's unchanged read-only
traffic into a friendlier hot-key workload. The headline establishes warmed
finite-window S1 capacity only; a longer run is supplementary evidence.

## 7. Alternatives, risks and what would make this wrong

- **Only RT6 and serial batching.** Rejected as the complete write plan:
  the `1/(pR)` floor remains below W4/mix even with free authority and
  infinite batches. If measured program flights disappear through access
  batching or an authoritative row mirror, redo the arithmetic; that is
  an additional mechanism, not a consequence of group commit.
- **Rerun a failed/unknown batch and rely on internal ids.** Rejected.
  Only known rollback permits the rerun row of §3.1. Internal receipts
  aid reconciliation; they do not turn missing evidence into rollback.
  Correlated unknown outcomes and receipt retention may outweigh batching
  for some kinds; retain solo eligibility where that is the better policy.
- **Per-connection authority or a TTL cache.** Rejected. A bearer is not
  connection-bound; direct revocations need not move public head, and
  expiry needs no write. The proposed authority cache fails if any durable
  producer or clock dependency is absent from its closure.
- **Assume the existing ownership lease authorizes local reads.** Rejected.
  Forced takeover fences database writes, not a paused process's cached
  responses. The read grant is the least established design choice here.
  If its bounded-clock/provider contract cannot be proved, use fresh
  database validation and report the resulting target misses. Availability delays
  during grant expiry are a real cost, even when throughput improves.
- **Use `preserved` as an equality certificate or cache whole HTTP bytes.**
  Rejected: authority preservation permits data changes, and head-bound
  cursors/manifests/clock headers can age independently of data. A stale
  result with a new head is a correctness defect, not an allowed cache
  miss. Conservative invalidation may instead destroy hit rate; that is
  a performance result requiring finer proved dependencies.
- **Depend on warm caches for every read.** Rejected. W1's broad keys and
  W3's expensive misses can defeat the plan. The G0/G5/G6 hit distributions,
  eviction and cold-reset rows may show the chosen memory bounds cannot
  sustain the required hit fraction. W7/W6 could invalidate W2/W3 faster
  than they refill even if S1's read-only cells pass.
- **Pre-execute every counter.** Rejected. Shared W5 has a real read/write
  dependency. Serial in-place execution avoids retry amplification; it
  still pays program waits. The natural 200/s example is tight and may
  need program access batching even after group commit. Maintains/caps
  do not become commutative merely because W4 does well.
- **Compile into Postgres immediately.** Deferred to §6's B3 decision.
  It introduces a second semantic executor and may not fix flush latency,
  conflict or a bad transport. Conversely, if validation/apply cost exceeds
  the 81-microsecond sizing example or cold read composition remains too
  slow, insisting on the current boundary could prevent the stated goal.
- **Treat a noisy or topology-mismatched win as evidence.** Rejected.
  Contemporary controls may rise above the historical targets, and the
  20 s windows/coarse rate ladder can reorder close results. The correct
  outcome can be "not demonstrated". This plan is wrong if a clean
  measurement attributes the limit somewhere its proposed mechanisms
  cannot remove; retain that limit rather than changing the pass rule.

The panel's directional questions are whether safe all-kind batching is
worth its correlated-failure/receipt cost, whether the new read grant is
an acceptable ownership contract, and whether measured full pre-execution
and cache miss costs leave enough margin to beat the rerun controls. Those
decisions precede tuning batch sizes or cache eviction policy.

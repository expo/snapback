## 1. Goal and decisions proposed

The author's goal, 2026-10-23, is to outperform both the Convex-on-Postgres
and self-hosted Supabase controls on every S1 W1–W5 cell, at both links.
This is a plan for that comparison, not a capacity claim. The comparison
remains the scale RFC's application, durability, authorization, offered-load
method and matched caps. It does not substitute a sharded counter for an
exact counter, or a trusted principal for a live credential.

The proposed route is:

1. Land RT6's B2 flights, then batch qualified unkeyed and keyed **program
   members**. Use an in-memory private invocation identity; rerun only
   after explicit whole-batch abort evidence, within remaining deadlines,
   never after an unknown COMMIT. Remove an identified failed member and
   rerun the remaining batch; otherwise use solo fallback. Other kinds
   stay solo.
2. Execute a batch's members in Rust under the head lock, buffering writes
   in a batch-local overlay and fetching missing reads level-synchronously.
   One composed statement serves all live members at each level; one
   apply+tail flight and COMMIT make their serial history durable (§3.3).
3. First measure G4a: non-authorizing `/q` ingress and same-level W3 read
   composition. Try raising short-request permits or queueing behind them
   when admission tails bind. Commission the complete revision stream (G4)
   and result cache (G5) only if W3's measured path or W2's permit margin
   needs them. Start G5 with immutable revision-equality hits: **one flight**
   including fresh authority, mismatch means miss. Dependency promotion and
   a COMMIT-wide gate come later only for measured benefit; consider a
   zero-flight read grant only after the cheaper route is measured (§4.2).
4. Measure the full closed-loop path and reduce per-member tail rows/bytes
   if needed, with G8 attribution alongside G3b. Natural W4 and the 1% mix
   are pre-registered as at risk with S1b's measured costs, as are delayed
   W2/W3 at 22,627/s until admission tuning is measured. Keep K3's
   inter-batch pre-execution window as a follow-on for workloads with more
   outstanding writes than N; keep B3
   after the tail/layout comparison (§6).

This proposal refines the parent and its existing children. It proposes
an exception to the committer's keyed-only batching/retry rule for program
members, a `/q` ingress admission refinement under the round-trip RFC's
open question 1, and a conditional exception to its authority-cache ban.
Section 8 records the round-1 objections and disposition (all NOT READY).
Section 9 records all three round-2 READY verdicts on revision 2 and the
lead's disposition as **revision 3, an unreviewed editorial revision**.
Those verdicts do not review revision 3. This remains a Draft design,
not authorization to implement, publish, enable P2 or lower Quarry to SQL.

Source citations below use paths relative to `snapback4/`; line numbers name
the checkout at `a025d67cd3b1a4a2aebeba014a9ecf30e2e69a25`. Historical
measurements cite their reports separately from current source. Predictions
are identified as such. The governing constraints are the store-port §5,
committer §3.5–§3.7 and round-trip §7: fenced durable writes, ordered
publication, whole-member atomicity, fresh authority and coherent snapshots.

### 2.2 Read ceilings

Use `R = 0.600 ms / 1.230 ms` as illustrative clean-link RTTs, rounded
from the matched controls' prepared-SELECT captures. The second is the
natural link **plus** delay, not an absolute 0.5 ms RTT. These are planning
constants; every new campaign substitutes its own loaded RTT distribution.
They deliberately exclude PG2/PG3's 41 ms tail. Median RTT arithmetic
cannot predict p99.

For `k` dependent exchanges, network latency is at least `kR`. Reserve
one of the 32 total store connections for the writer and one for independent
lease traffic: **P = 30** reader connections, not an additional reader pool.
The server separately admits **32 simultaneous short requests and 32
mutation requests**, retaining each class's permit throughout handling
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:352`, `:355`). These are **server constants,
not matched connection caps**. The controls' matched cap is the database
pool / Convex function cap; PostgREST queues for its pool. Request permits
are not database connections. The 128 read clients do not make 128 requests
execute inside the current short-request handler concurrently.

Admission **refuses at the class limit**, returning 503 `E_RATE_LIMIT`;
there is no extra admission queue (`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:397–413`;
`crates/snapback4/src/request_workers.rs:13`). Thus `32/mean hold time` is
not a usable capacity bound for an errors≤0.1% trial. The optimistic
database/client bound is `min(30/(kR), 128/mean client outstanding time)`,
**subject to an admission tail condition**: at offered arrivals the
probability of finding all L short permits occupied must fit within the
0.1% total error budget. Equivalently, the demanded in-flight population's
99.9th percentile must fit within L (currently 32), with allowance for
other errors. Measure arrival-sampled occupancy, refusals and hold-time
tails; a mean alone cannot establish this. CPU, HTTP and queues count too;
publication-gate stalls apply only to optional G6 hits, not G5.

The unchanged W3 rows extrapolate PG2, not current RT0. RT6 alone does not
compose its program reads. Its miss path needs the same-level composition in
the round-trip RFC §8, independently of cache hits. An unchanged W3 miss
already exceeds the 50 ms read SLO on both links before other costs.

A database-validated hit uses cached facts at ingress only as the carried
capability of round-trip §3.1: **ingress never authorizes**. Bound request
acquisition and admission work; the hit's single statement returns fresh
authority facts plus incarnation, generation, code identity, current
revision and public head in one snapshot. Rust runs the shared resolver
and compares the entry's certified identity/revision; mismatch means a
miss, never reuse with a new head. This is the fresh final check. No read
grant or bounded-clock ownership assumption is needed for that path.
Changed authority refuses or follows the existing stale-scope policy;
cached ingress facts cannot decide a success or metadata-bearing refusal.

First measure **G4a**, resolving round-trip **open question 1 for `/q`**:
replace the separate live database ingress with bounded, non-authorizing
admission, plus W3's same-level read composition as its own slice before
G4/G5. `/q` is a **POST with a JSON body**, contrary to that open question's
"no body" premise (`experiments/scale-controls/src/snapback.rs:19–20`;
`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:949–957`). Bound buffering/admission by the
existing body limit. An unknown token still costs **exactly one flight**,
the final statement, preserving today's `unknown_token: 1` bound.
A stale ingress allow can admit bounded request work before the fresh
check refuses it. Refusal ordering and resource admission need witnesses;
this draft does not silently change the parent. If the database ingress
is retained, a validated hit costs two flights, and the delayed pool bound
is 12,195/s. With the refinement, a W2 miss is already one flight, exactly
the same network cost as a hit. For **W1/W2, a hit and a fully composed,
ingress-free miss cost the same one flight**: cache benefit there is less
database CPU and shorter permit holding, not an assumed network saving.
W1's inherited row→membership schedule still takes two flights after
removing ingress; equality at one flight requires composing that closure
and measuring it, not merely RT6 or dropping ingress. Even its two-flight
pool ceiling clears both W1 target rungs, so no W1 cache saving is required.
Measure G4a independently before commissioning G4/G5 for **W3's residual
miss cost or W2's measured permit margin**.

One-flight hits have network headroom over the strict delayed W2 rung of
22,627/s, but use about 92.8% of the 30-reader pool at R=1.230 ms before
SQL and CPU. For measured total miss cost k flights and one-flight hit
fraction h, the database condition is `lambda[h + (1−h)k]R ≤ 30`.
For illustration, a measured k=2 fallback needs h≥92.2% at that rung
(47.6% at 16,000/s); ingress-free W2 misses are k=1 before any failed
validation overhead, so this is not a W2 cache-flight justification.
Count failed validation plus fallback in the measured miss cost; it may
exceed two. W3 with k=132 would need h≥99.94% at delayed 22,627/s, even
before its miss-tail failure. These are occupancy bounds, not hit promises.
Only the optional **zero-flight** grant changes the equation to
`lambda(1−h)kR ≤ 30`: for that same illustrative k=2 miss, thresholds are
46.1%/23.8%, and for unchanged W3 k=132 the threshold is 99.2%. These
are not requirements for ingress-free W2's k=1 miss; keep them distinct
from G5's one-flight path.

W2 has at most 256 × 32 = 8,192 ordinary viewer/room keys; W3 has 256
viewer keys (§2.1). These are plausible warm working sets. W1 spans the
million-message population with viewer separation; assume no useful hit
rate until measured. At 22,627/s, `32/22627 = 1.414 ms` is only a necessary
**mean-occupancy diagnostic**, tighter than the 128-client 5.66 ms budget;
at 16,000/s these are 2 ms and 8 ms. A delayed flight alone takes 1.230 ms,
leaving only ~0.18 ms before CPU/HTTP and no admission-tail margin. At 32
refusing permits, **pre-register delayed W2 and W3 at 22,627/s as at risk /
predicted misses until the cheap admission lever is measured**. W3's
historical strict example rung remains 16,000/s; 22,627/s is the higher
challenge here. Natural 22,627/s also needs measured tail margin.

The **first cheap read lever** is raising the short-request permit count
or adding bounded queueing behind it, tried and disclosed before G6.
Keep the total database cap at 32 and the reader pool at 30: once admission
is relieved, its 92.8% one-flight delayed occupancy is the next bound, not
a promise of a pass. Report queue latency, memory/CPU and refusal effects.
A zero-flight hit still holds a permit and must satisfy admission tails.

### 2.3 Single-member writes

The worker owns a bounded queue and writer engine
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_committer.rs:7`, `:25`, `:103`), and the
reference driver executes one member and physically commits it
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/committer.rs:198`). More request threads
do not multiply that serial stage.

A useful coarse bound is `1/(kR + execution + flush)`. Using the whole
request's k is conservative because ingress runs outside the writer. A
more optimistic bound removes that ingress flight. Show both rather than
pretending all 16 exchanges hold the writer:

| Path | Whole-request k / serial k after ingress | Whole-request reciprocal ops/s | Optimistic serial bound ops/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Measured unkeyed B1 shape | 16 / 15 | 104 / 51 | 111 / 54 |
| Measured keyed first shape | 24 / ≤23 | 69 / 34 | Preflight is also outside; needs separate stage attribution |
| RT6 B2(a) unkeyed shape | 8 / 7 | 208 / 102 | 238 / 116 |
| Exact W5 with three extra guard flights, predicted B2 | 11 / 10 | 152 / 74 | 167 / 81 |

The W5 prediction carries PG2's three extra program reads; RT0 must measure
it before use. The keyed B2(a) target is nine whole-request flights, not a
claim that RT6 has already reduced measured 24 to nine. Replay, action
credentials, cold prepares and refusals keep separate ratchets.

Even B2 cannot reach W4 or the mixed W5 targets with a single-member
committer. Exact W5 is close enough that durability decides: at a
hypothetical 5 ms flush, its ten serial flights take 11.0/17.3 ms,
or 91/58 ops/s. Natural 141/s fails; delayed 35/s might pass. C=32 writers
limits offered concurrency, not a multiplication of serial throughput.

## 3. Writes: program-member batches with a buffered overlay

### 3.1 Eligibility, private identities and outcomes

Amend committer §3.4 for **unkeyed and keyed program members only**, after
their outcome/transcript and touched-family protocols qualify. At admission
mint an **in-memory private identity** bound to store incarnation, principal,
operation, raw argument digest and immutable inputs/minted ids. It identifies
this invocation and its attempts, not a public operation id or a durable
HTTP deduplication promise. A new unkeyed HTTP request is a new invocation,
even if its bytes match. Preserve public `id` absence
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_head.rs:88`). Each attempt records its own time,
presence and chunk decisions; a rolled-back attempt does not freeze them
for its rerun. Do not log bearers or server-generated secrets.

Keep existing client-keyed receipts and replay binding. **Do not add a
durable internal receipt for an ordinary unkeyed HTTP program member.**
Durable internal receipts are only for kinds with server-driven
continuations, where fenced reconciliation can re-drive that continuation;
record their disposition and necessary continuation data atomically with
the member. They have a declared retention/reconciliation horizon, private
storage for secrets and no id reuse after retirement. Engine, Publication,
Direct, administrative and long kinds remain **solo** in this proposal;
future eligibility is K6 work, not an S1 prerequisite. Their durable writes
still join the complete revision/invalidation stream (§4.1). Batch
eligibility and producer coverage are different obligations.

| Physical batch outcome | Treatment of every member |
| --- | --- |
| Successful COMMIT acknowledged | Install revision state and any enabled invalidations, publish continuations and acknowledge member results in revision order |
| Known whole-batch rollback, with the original transaction ended | No continuation or reply from the aborted batch; if conclusive abort evidence names the failed member, return its failure and rerun the batch minus that member in original order; otherwise rerun members alone in order, bounded by their remaining deadlines. Use fresh authority/database facts and the same private invocation identities; keyed members re-check their bound receipts |
| COMMIT outcome unknown | **Every member receives `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`**, without success/head metadata; **no automatic rerun**, including a member that tentatively refused |
| Continuation fails after confirmed COMMIT | Preserve the durable outcome and report continuation failure separately; never call it a rolled-back mutation |

Only **explicit database abort evidence or acknowledged rollback of the
original transaction** permits rerun. A broken connection, timeout,
ownership loss or absent receipt while the original backend might commit
does not. COMMIT remains uncancellable and classified aborts remain distinct
from unclassified failures (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/ownership.rs:133`).
Discard aborted overlays, effects, meters, allocations and tails before
rerunning; do not re-apply stale write sets. A failure of that solo
attempt follows the ordinary solo outcome protocol, not an unbounded retry
loop. A crash never replays an unacknowledged in-memory queue.

Known-rollback rerun is retained for unkeyed program members by the lead's
round-1 disposition. **The server has not replied:** no abort/failure reply
is sent and then contradicted by a rerun. Explicit abort proves that the
original attempt persisted nothing, so at most one attempt of this
invocation can apply. If the client times out and sends a new unkeyed
request, both invocations may commit. There is existing precedent: the
ordinary Postgres mutation path already reruns **whole transactions, keyed
or unkeyed**, with the client unanswered, on `40001`, `40P01` and `_ops_pkey`
unique violations (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/mod.rs:313–321`,
`:490–499`). Its loop permits up to 32 retries. This is precedent for the
invocation invariant, not permission to give a batch an unbounded retry loop.

Batch fallback adds an extra window: a long serial solo-rerun backlog can
outlast a caller's timeout; a **new unkeyed request during that backlog
can double-apply across the two invocations**. N solo attempts at the
illustrative ~7.75 ms natural cost can exceed the 100 ms write SLO (32
would take ~248 ms). Bound **each solo rerun by its member's remaining
end-to-end deadline**. If it has passed, return the failure and do not
start the attempt; carry the remaining budget through cancellable work.
COMMIT remains uncancellable and any ambiguity still follows the unknown
outcome rule. This deadline rule narrows, rather than eliminates, a new
client request's overlap exposure. When constraint/row abort evidence
conclusively identifies the failed member, rerun the **batch minus that
member**, preserving order and deadline checks, instead of N solo reruns.
The private identity does not deduplicate a new client request.
A harness timeout/late commit still fails the trial and must reconcile;
this is no exemption from exact counter equality. Grok's BLOCKING objection
and this answer remain explicit in §8; round 2's acceptance and deadline
clarification are recorded in §9.

For an unknown outcome, durable client receipts or continuation receipts
may aid later fenced reconciliation where they exist. Their absence never
permits transparent rerun. An unkeyed HTTP member without a durable receipt
still returns unknown; this proposal does not promise later per-invocation
resolution to that client. A client-keyed retry keeps the existing replay
protocol, not a retry guessed from matching bytes.

A physical batch correlates uncertainty: one unknown COMMIT makes all its
callers uncertain. It also delays peers behind a slow member and hides
intermediate revisions until durability. These are observable timing and
failure-correlation changes, while whole-member atomicity and serial
outcomes remain fixed. Refusals and rule failures should be decided in
Rust **before apply**, so ordinary refusals never cause a SQL batch abort.

### 3.2 Serial group commit is an intermediate slice

First extend B2(a)'s tail from one commit slot to a vector: every member's
required commit/log/evidence/effects and keyed or continuation receipt,
one head update, one tail flight, verified counts, then separate COMMIT.
Today's single-slot CTE verifies one committed row
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/tail.rs:25`, `:32`, `:62`, `:85`).
Calling it N times is still N waits. Rollback discards every buffered tail,
footprint and allocation; no continuation precedes durability.

For **strictly serial database-backed member execution**, p program flights
plus one authority/framing flight per member and three shared flights give
`T(N) ≥ [3 + N(p+1)]R + F + Ne`. With N=32 and F=e=0:

| Serial schedule | Network bound natural / delayed ops/s | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| W4, p=3, 32/(131R) | 407 / 199 | Misses both strict example rungs |
| W5 exact, p=6, 32/(227R) | 235 / 115 | **SLO-infeasible:** 136/279 ms service before flush, already over 100 ms |
| Nominal 1% mix, average p≈3.03, 32/(131.96R) | 404 / 197 | Misses both strict example rungs |

The infinite-N floor `1/(pR)` belongs to **that schedule**, not the
architecture. W4 with p=3 gives 556/271; returning the updated image instead
of rereading it can reduce p=2 and raise the delayed program-only bound to
406/s. The 271 versus historical 282 comparison is sensitive to RTT
rounding and one eliminated access; it cannot establish an impossibility.
Neither serial schedule explains how to reach natural 3,200/s. The mix's
average p is appropriate only for this serial model; concurrent execution
pays its batch's longest dependency chain, not the average.

Serial group commit is G3's useful qualified baseline, not the complete
write route. The overlay in G3b removes per-member program network waits
and makes exact W5 an in-memory hot key (§3.3–§3.4).

### 3.3 In-batch concurrent execution under the head lock

Use K1's complete read recording and K2's qualified overlay **inside one
fenced batch transaction**, with no inter-batch validation window. The head
lock freezes application state against all admitted durable producers;
lease renewal remains separate. A family whose writers bypass this fence
cannot qualify. The Rust executor, rules, meter and reference `run` remain
the only semantic executor; SQL fetches facts and applies prepared images.

1. Fence first. After acquiring the head lock, fetch generation, identity,
   authority and bound-receipt facts for all members together. Argument-known
   first program reads may share this flight, with their statement snapshot
   taken **after** the lock grant (round-trip §3.5). Authority/framing must
   be composed across members, not one extra flight per member.
2. Give every member a private tentative overlay over the immutable batch
   base and its **accepted predecessors only**. Read own writes first, then
   predecessor writes, then the batch read cache. Later members' tentative
   writes are never visible to an earlier member, including its future
   reads. Deletes, absent keys and range/index changes merge with the same
   rules as the overlay child; they are not simple row-map substitutions.
3. Suspend live members at missing reads and fetch **one composed statement
   per dependency level for all live members** on the writer connection.
   Resume Rust on those answers. This is not N pipelined commands or a
   semicolon list of N point reads. Cache fetched base rows, absence and
   bounded range coverage for the batch. Before accepting the wave, complete
   cache coverage of the declared base dependency closure, including
   alternative paths needed for a conflict rerun, using those composed
   levels. Unproved coverage takes the serial fallback below. The database
   is unchanged by program writes until step 6.
4. Record every logical read and footprint. When an earlier-ordered member
   finishes and writes something a later member already read, discard the
   later attempt and re-execute it **after its predecessors**, through the
   overlay and complete batch read cache, with **zero database flights**
   for this qualified dependency closure. Discard its tentative effects and
   charges too. Route known hot-key colliders directly to serial execution
   in memory, avoiding quadratic reruns.
5. Accept members in revision order: fresh authority-clock/deadline checks,
   rule/refusal decisions, returned intermediate images, predicates, meters,
   effects, receipt decisions, sequence/allocation resolution and input
   transcripts all match their serial positions. A revocation or receipt
   written by a predecessor changes the successor's admission/replay result.
   Allocate public sequence/revision and head-bound artifacts in that order;
   no new durable meter write is introduced. Apply the committer §6 rules
   for effective `at`, pins and observed time predicates; unsupported
   time/presence-sensitive programs take the serial path.
6. Send **one apply+tail flight**, applying successful members' buffered
   writes and all required per-member tail records in revision order, with
   verified counts, then **COMMIT** as its own flight. The tail preserves
   intermediate images/events and required per-member receipts even when the final
   physical row is overwritten by a successor. Only confirmed durability
   releases revision state, any enabled invalidations, continuations and
   replies (§3.1, §4.1).

**Full-dependency independence rule.** Distinct mutation targets alone
prove nothing. Concurrent candidates need a conservative, finite closure
of every possible read and write: rule probes and hidden examined rows,
absent rows and ranges/scan boundaries, index and uniqueness checks,
maintained source and owner rows, cap occupancy, authority, client receipts,
effect keys and allocations. Shared read-only authority is fine; overlapping
value dependencies are ordered, not assumed commutative. The closure must
include alternative control-flow paths reachable when a predecessor changes
a value, so a qualified conflict rerun needs no new base fetch. Coarsening
may reduce concurrency but never omit a dependency. Unknown coverage,
unsupported families or dynamically expanding dependencies **fall back to
serial execution** after predecessors, fetching any required facts there;
they do not inherit the zero-flight rerun claim. Re-form the remaining wave
around that serial boundary. A late discovery discards affected speculative
successors; nothing speculative has reached the database.

**Maintains, caps and allocations.** Maintained totals remain value
dependencies: overflow, source-existence/deletion, meter decisions and the
whole mirrored owner-row image matter (committer §4.5). Writers sharing an
owner row serialize through the overlay. Caps read exact occupancy and
preserve the observed full/not-full result in either direction; uniqueness
uses the merged index including absence. For G3b's first qualification,
serialize maintained-family, capped-group and allocator members after their
predecessors; only a proved disjoint complete closure permits concurrency.
Do not turn maintains/caps into commutative deltas. Allocate under each
parent serially, buffer the durable `_sequences` update, and discard its
cache on abort. Where the existing placeholder/taint machinery qualifies,
resolve values in revision order before concrete keys, effects, footprints
and outcomes are finalized; otherwise the allocator runs serially. S1's
increment has none of these maintained writes or allocations.

**Why serial-equivalent.** By induction, the accepted overlay after member
j is the state of reference `run` after members 1…j. Its successor reads
that state or unchanged base facts; a recorded conflicting read forces a
rerun, and no successor write can reach a predecessor. Rust evaluates the
same rules, store pre-write checks and logical meter calls on that merged
view. A refused member discards its program writes and retains only its
ordinary protocol records. Each accepted outcome includes its intermediate
image, effects, transcript and applicable receipt. Step 6 makes precisely
that ordered history durable. Interleaved database writes and independent
member savepoints are **not** this design: PostgreSQL savepoints are nested,
and rolling one back could erase another member's work. SQL errors still
abort the whole batch; only §3.1's explicit abort evidence permits rerun.
Qualification requires the overlay differential and serial-history oracle,
not just aggregate counter sums.

K3's reader execution plus inter-batch window remains G7's follow-on when
outstanding writes exceed N and execution can overlap the previous flush.
With 32 synchronous writers and N=32, all writers await the same reply;
there is no next batch to prepare during that flush. K3/K4 is therefore
not required to remove the serial schedule's `1/(pR)` floor here.

### 3.4 Size the whole cycle with measured tail cost

The [S1b full-write-set commit-stage table](../snapback4/experiments/postgres-commit-path/README.md#full-write-set-commit-stage-and-re-sizing)
measures N=1/4/16/64 at 180/688/2,064/4,256 ops/s: approximately
5.56/5.81/7.75/15.04 ms per batch. A planning fit is **5.5 ms fixed plus
150 microseconds/member marginal**, not a free tail. The fixed part already
includes that model's three flights and flush/CTE overhead; do not add a
second F=5 ms to it. S1b already uses **explicit START / one CTE / COMMIT**
with roughly **five rows/member**, including `_ops` and revision/footprint
records. Its ~150 microseconds/member is server row/index/WAL work, not
N separate tail statements' overhead. A composed-versus-pipelined access
comparison alone will not remove that term. The table is a separate-stage
statement model with queue/ack included, not this engine path, and its
p99/spread remain material.
N=32 is an interpolation, not a measured table row. New revision evidence
and any required receipt rows can make the real tail cost higher.

For illustration calibrate its fixed term at R0=0.600 ms. Replacing those
three flights' RTT gives `Tstage ≈ 5.5 + 3(R−R0) + 0.150N` ms. The overlay
schedule is normally four flights:
`fence · head/generation/authority/first-reads · apply+tail · COMMIT`.
Add one R beyond the three-flight model, and d extra read levels. W4's
read-after-update is an overlay hit (d=0 if its first read is composed into
setup). Shared-counter membership may add a level (d=1). Measure the
actual schedule; no addresses discovered later are assumed argument-known.

With H for extra HTTP/ack overhead, I for ingress, Q for queue/collection,
and E for additional Rust/read work, the closed-loop planning equation is
`cycle ≈ H + I + Q + 5.5 + 3(R−R0) + 0.150N + (1+d)R + E` ms.
Only incremental overhead beyond the measured model belongs in H/Q/E;
cycle attribution must replace this fit, not double-count its included work.
The 32 synchronous write workers each await their current HTTP result
before issuing the next. Thus **throughput ≤ 32/cycle**, N≤32; also retain
the server's 32-mutation-permit hold-time bound. `min(execution, commit)`
is only an optimistic component bound unless enough requests populate both
stages. Larger N or extra execution connections cannot be assumed here.

Using H=0.3 ms, I=R and Q=E=0 only as explicit illustrative assumptions:

| Shape at N=32 | Natural cycle / upper rate | Delayed cycle / upper rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| W4, d=0 | 11.80 ms / 2,712/s | 14.95 ms / 2,140/s |
| Batch containing a shared-counter member, d=1 | 12.40 ms / 2,581/s | 16.18 ms / 1,978/s |

Even the optimistic **commit stage alone** at natural R is
`5.5+32×0.150=10.3 ms`, about 3,107/s, already below 3,200. Counting just
that stage plus ingress and H gives 11.2 ms, about 2,857/s. The extra-flight
ledger above is more conservative. Pre-register **W4 natural and nominal
1% mix natural as at risk/predicted misses at the 3,200 rung** until measured
marginal tail cost falls. Delayed rates have model headroom, not qualified
SLO passes. For the mix, use each batch's **longest dependency chain** and
observed hot-member/batch distribution, never p=3.03 as a wave depth.

Both natural 3,200 tests require a cycle ≤10 ms. If e is measured marginal
apply/tail time in ms/member, the W4 ledger permits only
`e ≤ [10−5.5−0.6 ingress−0.6 extra flight−0.3 H−Q−E]/32`:
**93.75 microseconds/member before queue/CPU margin**. Reserving 0.75 ms
for Q+E leaves about **70 microseconds/member**; a d=1 batch lowers those
budgets to 75/51.6 microseconds. Even the more optimistic three-flight
ledger permits only 112.5 microseconds before queue/CPU. S1b's ~150 misses
all these budgets. These are transparent planning allowances, not measured
CPU or a promise that slimming will reach them.

List all three remaining cycle levers beside that marginal budget:

- **Fence shares a flight with post-fence locks/reads** (round-trip §3.5),
  while retaining separate command order, a raising fence, and a fresh
  generation snapshot after the head lock. No writes follow a failed
  fence. This saves R per cycle: illustrative natural W4 11.80→11.20 ms.
- **`/m` ingress refinement**, separately qualifying round-trip open
  question 1's admission/refusal bounds, saves another R: 11.20→10.60 ms,
  about 3,019/s. Neither flight lever reaches the 10 ms budget alone or
  together under this fitted tail cost; measure both, do not assume them.
- **Tail slimming** must reduce actual server rows/indexes/WAL. Omitting
  the unkeyed-HTTP `_ops` row removes one of about five rows; reaching
  70–94 microseconds/member plausibly needs **≤3 rows/member**, subject to
  preserving required history and receipts. It is not a statement-count
  saving or a promised cost ratio. Bring G8 attribution alongside G3b.

**Exact W5 is an in-memory hot key.** Fetch the shared counter once and
its required membership/authority facts in composed levels; execute every
increment serially in the overlay, returning each distinct n+1. The controls
also update one exact counter, not a sharded substitute
(`experiments/scale-controls/supabase-schema.sql:103`;
`experiments/scale-controls/convex-app/convex/chat.ts:43`). On the d=1 fit,
N=8 takes **8.80/12.58 ms** per cycle, or **909/636 members/s**; N=32 has
the second row's bound. This replaces the old N=8 serial-database estimate
of 198/103. N=1 is about 129/87/s: batching still matters to natural 200/s.
These include the fitted durable tail cost but exclude collection, extra
Rust work and latency tails; they are not promised rates.
At 50/s, waiting for eight arrivals alone takes 140 ms and fails the SLO;
never require a full batch to acknowledge a lone request.

Commit immediately when idle, drain queued members and adapt N/byte/work
limits to end-to-end p99, starting with a maximum collection wait of 1 ms
and N≤32. Measure actual fill rather than assuming N=32 at every offered
rate. Attribute **ingress, queue age, serial section by flight, per-member
apply, flush and ack**, including read-level barriers, collision reruns,
solo fallback and permit-holding time. Long solo members still delay the
queue; slow durability can defeat every optimistic rate above.

### 3.5 Named lever: per-member tail rows and bytes

Before B3, measure and compare **per-member tail cost**, including rows,
indexes, encoded bytes, WAL and vacuum work. This is H5's write amplification:
[S0](../snapback4/experiments/postgres-commit-path/README.md) observed about
1,100 B WAL per engine increment versus raw's 115 B. The
[PG2 storage record](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261022-snapback-postgres-2/README.md)
reports **4.118×** allocated heap+indexes versus typed Postgres, crossing
scale RFC §8's **3× typed-layout reopen rule**, with its seed-history and
allocation caveats. WAL and allocated storage are different measurements;
neither identifies the exact tail bottleneck by itself. Both require the
layout/projection comparison, not an automatic typed-layout decision here.

| Candidate to slim | Potential saving | Cost/constraint to measure and preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commit/log/evidence/receipt rows and indexes | Fewer tuples, index updates and WAL records per member; omit the new unkeyed-HTTP internal receipt entirely | Preserve the complete per-member history, existing keyed replay, sync, audit/chain obligations and continuation recovery; packing records adds decode/lookup/retention work, never permits erasing required receipts |
| Private revision footprint bytes | Compact/deduplicated keys, shared encodings, conservative table/family coarsening | Coarser invalidation loses hits and increases reruns; compression adds CPU and complicates bounded interval proof; no producer or dependency may disappear |
| Row/log image encoding | Less duplicated JSON/labels or compact binary images, possible projections or typed layout | Codec/versioning, schema evolution, index parity, migration/qualification and Rust encode/decode cost; retain intermediate member images, logical meter sizes and exact wire outcomes |

Compare each change's marginal microseconds, WAL/storage bytes and read
cost under full write sets, not an empty-tail microbenchmark. Removing
ordinary refusal-triggered SQL aborts reduces wasted cycles; it does not
remove this durable per-member term. Neither the inter-batch window nor a
database-side semantic executor inherently removes required tail rows or
flush cost. G8 brings measured choices to the existing typed-layout decision
before G9/B3; this document selects none of those physical representations.

## 4. Reads: exact-invalidation caches

### 4.1 Complete revisions first; promotion only for measured benefit

Existing evidence is useful but insufficient. `revocation::Recorder` keeps
bounded net row images and emits table/population/column changes, widening
after 256 entries or 256 KiB (`crates/snapback4-core/src/revocation.rs:9`,
`:21`, `:58`). `preserved()` certifies **authority preservation**, not
equal query results: the regression deliberately preserves authority over
a body edit (`crates/snapback4-core/src/revocation.rs:82`, `:231`).
Postgres evidence is keyed by public seq; missing evidence widens, and
interval certification requires contiguous complete evidence, at most 500
commits (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/change_evidence.rs:25`,
`:42`; `crates/snapback4-core/src/revocation.rs:204`). A preserved table can
have changed data. It is never permission to reuse a query's bytes.

Introduce an internal durable **revision** for every committed member,
including receipt-only operations, session mints/rotation/revocation,
principal generations, worker-family markers, claims, clock pins, expiry,
publication, maintains, assets and activation. Public seq advances only
when its present semantics require it. Record the revision in the physical
transaction, using the committer's already-designed revision
domain (§3.3 there), not a second competing commit order.

**G5 starts with a revision-equality baseline.** Fill an immutable entry
at revision S from one coherent ordinary query snapshot, retaining its
identity/code and result. Reuse it only if the fresh validation statement
returns **exactly S**, matching identity/code and fresh authority facts
from that same statement. The shared Rust resolver with a fresh clock
still authorizes. **Every revision mismatch misses**, including an
unrelated local commit; never promote implicitly. Complete producer
revision coverage, coherent fills, expiry checks and §4.3–§4.5's identity,
metering and eligibility exclusions are mandatory. This baseline needs
neither dependency-based promotion across revisions nor a COMMIT-wide
publication gate or additional durable footprint rows. Measure its benefit
and write-induced false misses before commissioning those mechanisms.

Ownership-lease traffic is the parent's explicit out-of-stream exception;
the queue maintains it independently
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/command.rs:258`). G5 handles foreign
ownership/state changes through its fresh database statement. Only optional
G6 relies on §4.2's grant/epoch; do not invent an application revision for
lease traffic. No other out-of-stream durable producer may leave
application or authority state unversioned while these caches are enabled.

**Only if measured benefit warrants cross-revision promotion**, add complete
footprints in the same physical transaction and a bounded validation window.
The private footprint includes family keys, old/new row identities and
index addresses, and broad invalidation for raw or unqualified operations.
It need not expose values or authority details on `/changes`. Unknown
coverage means invalidation of all potentially affected entries, never
"no change". The existing public evidence can supply a conservative
table-level invalidation when complete; typed family writes must separately
cover changes outside it. Start with whole-table dependencies for data,
whole-family dependencies for authority, and widen globally on unclassified
members, including kinds that always execute solo. Exact invalidation means
no false negatives, not zero false positives.
Refine to K1's Row/Span/family dependencies only after its completeness
census and adversary pass, including absent keys, hidden examined rows,
negative membership, scan boundaries and non-key field edits.

For that later promotion route, add a **publication/install gate**. Close
it before COMMIT; after confirmed COMMIT atomically install invalidations,
authority updates, revision/head, generation and code epoch, then open it,
publish wakeups and acknowledge in revision order. A non-intersecting
install **bumps each surviving entry's certified revision to the published
head under the gate**, retaining its immutable fill revision and payload.
An intersecting or unproved entry is evicted. A promoted G5 hit requires
**statement revision = entry certified revision = published head**, plus
matching identity/code and fresh authority from the statement. Advancing
certification requires complete non-intersection proof; never relabel old
bytes or head-bound artifacts as though they had been recomputed.

**G5 hits never wait on the publication gate**, including this promoted
variant. With `synchronous_commit=on`, a statement that sees r while r+1
is flushing linearizes before that commit, or conservatively misses. In
the durable-commit/install gap a newer statement revision fails the
comparison and misses. Use nonblocking capture/comparison of an immutable
candidate and published state; unavailable or racing proof means miss,
not a flush-length wait. The revision-equality baseline simply compares
the statement with S and needs no local install proof.

**G6 requires the gate at its CPU-only read point** (§4.2–§4.3); it must
exclude the commit/install gap and may stall for COMMIT plus flush. Record
gate-closed fraction, G5 gate-race misses and **G6 hit stall time**
separately. On unknown COMMIT, keep any promotion/G6 gate closed until
fenced reconciliation reconstructs the committed head and clears caches;
pause new writer admission too. A continuation failure cannot reopen it
with old evidence. G5's ordinary fresh-statement path remains subject to
the existing ownership/authority policy, not a local zero-flight promise.

For promoted fills, collect the result and all dependencies from the
ordinary RT4 snapshot at S. At installation under the gate, validate every
revision since S; reject on intersection, unknown coverage, epoch change
or missing interval. Bound the window; falling behind forces a miss/refill.
The baseline instead retains S and misses on any changed revision, without
interval proof. In-batch members see their own tentative writes over the
accepted predecessor overlay and batch base; external reads see only the final
commit, with all member invalidations installed together.

### 4.2 Database validation first; a read grant only if needed

**G4a is the first read slice; G4/G5 are conditional** on its measured W3
cost and W2 permit margin (§2.2, §6). G5, if commissioned, starts with
§4.1's revision-equality baseline: one statement supplies fresh authority,
incarnation, generation, code identity, revision and public head. Rust
compares identity/code and **statement revision = immutable fill S**;
any mismatch misses even before local observation of a foreign write.
No read lease, bounded-clock ownership promise or publication-gate wait is
needed. Ordinary expiry checks and all cache exclusions remain. For W1/W2
the comparison with an equally composed ingress-free miss is CPU/permit
margin, not fewer flights; W3's composed miss may still cost two flights.

Try and disclose raising short-request permits or bounded admission
queueing **before G6**, retaining the 30-reader/32-total connection cap.
Measure G4a and any G5 path against read targets, refusal tails, pool
occupancy and total permit holds. Consider a grant **only if the measured
cheaper path still misses a read target**; neither hit-rate optimism nor
the old two-flight W2 model suffices. Dependency promotion is independently
conditional on measured benefit; neither it nor a grant is a mandatory
next step after a revision-equality cache.

Complete local observation alone is insufficient for zero-flight hits.
Store-port §5's first-lock write fence does not fence a CPU-only response:
a paused old owner can resume after a successor's revocation. Terminating
its database sessions or notifying it asynchronously cannot prevent that.
The current `LeaseDeadline` margin and queue loss observation
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/store/port/ownership.rs:16`, `:24`;
`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/command.rs:258`, `:335`) are not an
existing proof of zero-flight authority.

If G6 is needed, propose a separate **bounded read grant** tied to
`(store_id, owner token, holder, cache epoch)`, with expiry persisted in the
database. Every takeover, release, offline writer and foreign activation
must honor its expiry or an acknowledged holder cache-stop/drain before
publishing newer application state. Token changes, killed sessions and a
shorter writer lease cannot shorten it. Local activation closes the gate
and clears the epoch. Any bypassing writer disables zero-flight hits.

**Claim must precede takeover action.** An expiry poll is only a hint. The
successor's first act toward replacing the epoch is a **conditional claim
that conflicts with renewal**, in the store-port §5.2/W11c ordering. Match
store, token, holder, epoch and the required expiry (or acknowledged-drain
state), taking the conflicting row lock before termination, replacement
or application writes. Zero rows or a timed-out claim means abandon without
those actions. If renewal won, the claim sees the extension and fails; if
the claim won, renewal waits and then fails its token/epoch condition after
replacement. Hold that claim through the handoff decision; no unlocked
expiry re-read can authorize takeover. A lost, ambiguous or late renewal
**cannot reopen an epoch**. This is the race the old re-read protocol failed
in W11c. Its exact combined owner/grant representation and lock order need
qualification if G6 is selected, including the acknowledged-drain path.

The clock assumption must also be explicit. Sample a suspend-aware
monotonic clock before acquiring/renewing. If the database can advance at
most `(1+rho)t + J` during t local units, a duration-L grant permits a local
deadline no later than `send_time + (L−J)/(1+rho)`, less rounding margin;
require L>J. Bound relative clock rate and forward adjustment across every
supported lossless failover; an empirical 50 ms margin is not a bound.
Check that deadline **synchronously at every hit's linearization point**,
including after pauses. No renewal thread or callback substitutes for the
check. Start with grants no longer than the ownership lease and use the
reserved lease connection for renewals.

This adds a clock assumption where write fencing needs none. Arbitrary
clock jumps, unsupported failover, or unqualified suspend detection keep
the deployment on G5. During a partition the old owner may hit only until
its conservative deadline, while the successor cannot publish past the
outstanding grant. Graceful handoff first stops/drains cache reads, then
relinquishes the grant. Availability lost waiting for expiry is a measured
cost. On ownership loss, renewal ambiguity, reconnect/failover, foreign
epoch change or stream discontinuity, close the gate and drop the caches;
a restart begins empty. Re-enable G6 only with a new qualified epoch/grant
and coherent identity/generation/revision/head snapshot. G5 may instead
rebuild entries and validate them with fresh statements.

P2 disables zero-flight local caches by default. Forwarded writes and
best-effort `LISTEN` wakeups do not prove ordered invalidation. P2 would
need complete read barriers and invalidation acknowledgement/gap/reconnect
handling, grant-aware owners or reads forwarded to the owner. No P2
speedup is counted here.

### 4.3 Result identity, validation and response metadata

The key includes store incarnation, generation, schema/program/native-code
digest, operation and canonical typed arguments, viewer and credential
kind, authority scope, effective cap/policy version, cursor, retained-search
inputs, provenance and delivery mode, read ceilings and response-shaping
options. Include any additional context the operation can observe; an
unclassified dependency makes it ineligible. Do not put raw bearer tokens
in keys or logs. Keep principal-separated results initially; cross-viewer
sharing needs a separate proof. A generation alone is insufficient for
code identity: reader invalidation explicitly handles native code changing
without a schema-generation change
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_readers.rs:55`).

The current query opens one request snapshot, observes reader time,
captures seq, invokes authority validation and runs the interpreter over
that same snapshot (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:789`, `:820`,
`:829`, `:873`). The HTTP door borrows that snapshot for authority/identity
and supplies delivery mode according to scope
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1168`, `:1211`). Preserve this RT4 property
on misses; cache fills do not open an unrelated head snapshot.

For a **baseline G5 hit**, select an immutable entry filled at S without
waiting on a publication gate, then run the single validation statement
(§2.2). Require matching identity/code and **statement revision = S**.
Fresh authority comes from the same statement and the shared Rust resolver
with a fresh authority clock; a mismatch, missing fact or expiry means
miss/refusal under ordinary policy. The statement snapshot is the read
point, so an in-flight later commit can linearize after the hit. Coherent
fills and complete producer revisions make equality sufficient without a
commit/install gate. Do not alter the entry's immutable fill revision.

If promotion later qualifies, use the nonblocking comparison in §4.1:
**statement revision = certified revision = published head**, with each
survivor's certification advanced under the install gate only after proved
non-intersection. A closed/racing gate may cause a miss, never a G5 wait.
Never relabel old payload bytes or an opaque cursor with a newer head.
No second SQL command is hidden in response construction. A fallback uses
the ordinary coherent miss snapshot and pays its full measured flights.

Only a **G6 hit**, under one gate read, verifies the live read grant/epoch,
re-resolves authority from valid cached facts with a fresh clock sample,
validates the entry through the published revision and captures identity
and head. That gate read is its linearization point. A grant makes this
CPU-only point safe against foreign takeover; G5 uses the fresh statement
instead. Both return data equal to executing at their certified head with
their authority facts. Set query `seq` and response head to that head, not
the original fill seq; use the same generation/store id throughout. Do not
invent a query-body `head`: query replies carry `seq`, generation and store
identity, while mutation/replay heads have their own semantics
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1307`, `:1332`;
`crates/snapback4/src/serve_head.rs:94`).

Initial eligibility is complete, deterministic, durable-only queries with
no output dependent on the current revision/head and no cursor/receipt/
delivery artifact bound to the fill sequence. Where a
cursor or manifest embeds seq or mutable authority, reconstruct it from
the validated dependencies at the hit head, with equivalent metering, or
bypass the cache. Never relabel an opaque old cursor. Admit W1–W3 only
after full wire/manifest equivalence, not just data equality. Request
logging, rate limits, request-worker permits, read ceilings and output
limits still apply; hits cannot become an unmetered unlimited endpoint.
Retain the logical read/cut accounting for a certified equivalent result
and measure physical cache work separately. Do not report zero logical
work as if the query had no cost.

### 4.4 Authority facts and clock boundaries

For G5, facts cached at ingress are only non-authorizing carried context;
the final statement supplies live facts to the existing shared Rust resolver.
For optional G6, cache those resolver facts, never a permanent `allowed`
bit. Initially admit the fixed ordinary projection only: digest/session
existence including cross-table duplicate detection,
principal, family, kind, scope, session/cap expiry, principal generation,
supervisor markers, store id/generation and activation identity. Those
are represented by `SessionFacts` and the ordinary resolver
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/identity/facts.rs:5`, `:22`, `:31`). Complete
revision coverage includes session deletion/rotation, family and principal
revocation, read-session changes and supervisor changes, including writes
that do not advance public head. Later dependency promotion must invalidate
these too; missing rows and default generation zero are dependencies.
Ingress may use these facts only for bounded
admission. A queued mutation still validates authority at serial admission
against preceding batch members and a fresh clock; G5 reads always fetch
fresh facts at the final statement, regardless of ingress reuse.

Do not initially cache action or arbitrary custom-kind resolution: action
authority traverses work/claim/admission dependencies and deadlines
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:192`, `:228`). A later slice may
qualify their complete dependency closure. Specialist service endpoints
retain their additional service/full-scope/principal checks, not just
ordinary bearer liveness (`crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:257`).

Expiry can happen without a commit. On every hit, the shared policy checks
session expiry, read cap expiry and captured principal generation against
a fresh authority clock, exactly as its current liveness function does
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/authority.rs:242`). The earliest relevant
deadline bounds reuse; a timer helps eviction but never authorizes a hit.
Authority time and the ownership grant's monotonic clock are different
clocks. Development clock changes invalidate the epoch; initially bypass
staging/dev caches, whose response helper can perform session timing reads
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_head.rs:40`). No TTL substitutes for either
invalidation or expiry checks. G5 answers round-trip §7's freshness
requirement with a fresh final statement; only G6 substitutes a complete
commit version plus exclusive read epoch and qualified clock checks,
failing closed on loss.

### 4.5 Lifetimes, delivery, ephemeral state and bounded memory

Queries can use `now` in filters, addresses and returned values; the guide
contains those forms (`crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/time.md:34`, `:37`).
Initially bypass any query or transitive rule/helper whose result depends
on sampled time, presence or ephemeral rows. Do not invent a time bucket
and call it exact. Current read rules cannot name `now`; the guide instead
uses stored lifetimes for timed authority
(`crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide.rs:518`). This proposal does not admit
clock expressions in rules. Durable time-independent maintained values can cache,
but their source, owner, maintenance and authorization dependencies must
all invalidate them; reads of maintained counts are explicit in S1 W3
(`experiments/scale-controls/snapback-app/snapback/schema.q:50`).

Initially also bypass queries whose reachable data/rule scope has expiry
or `not before`, even if no returned row currently has a deadline.
Hidden, absent and future rows can change a result. A later lifetime-aware
slice must record the earliest boundary across examined **and omitted
candidate** rows/scopes, advance/settle due publications through the
committer, and miss at that boundary. The existing engine observes reader
time and exposes pending openings; HTTP materializes an encountered opening
then reevaluates (`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:824`, `:896`;
`crates/snapback4/src/serve.rs:1276`). Keep that behavior; never turn a
pending opening into cached absence. A boundary index, not a sweep timer,
must prove that no unseen opening/expiry can change the cached page.

Do not cache delivery reports, asset grants/bytes, view-once consumption,
Following history or ephemeral/presence answers in this increment. A full
query's pure delivery manifest may be reused only with the head/authority
proof in §4.3; delivery remains a separate action. Read scope keeps its
allow-list and omits delivery manifests/meters; it cannot gain full-session
artifacts through a shared cache key. These are current guide semantics
(`crates/snapback4-lang/src/guide/read_only.md:23`, `:36`). Queries touching
ephemeral tables currently use a separately versioned hub
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/mod.rs:833`); durable revision alone
cannot validate them.

Keep `/changes` and sync correctness independent of result-cache admission.
Current held polls share bounded answers, but entry/unheld polls read the
store and final bearer liveness is rechecked
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_changes.rs:124`, `:162`, `:192`). Its head and
evidence share a snapshot (`crates/snapback4/src/serve_changes.rs:211`).
Certified sync also reads images, receipts and evidence in one snapshot
(`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/sync_evidence.rs:16`). Preserve those
paths first. Publish their existing wakeups after installing revision state
and any enabled cache invalidations. A live-query reissue can then hit
the result cache safely. No
server result hit proves a device's retained authority, supplies sync
receipts or changes public preservation certification. Sharing the new
private validation machinery later must preserve these distinctions.

Start with configurable hard per-store bounds: 128 MiB result payloads and
dependencies, 16 MiB authority facts, 32 MiB revision window if promotion
is enabled, and a 512 MiB process-wide combined cap across stores and cache
bookkeeping. Count keys,
reverse indexes, retained payload references and in-flight fills, not just
serialized data. Use bounded per-principal admission and an eviction policy
that resists one-shot scans; oversize entries bypass. Cap entry count and
in-flight fill count as well as bytes. Eviction always means miss. If the
optional revision window fills, evict entries or drop fills needing its
oldest revision; never discard proof while retaining its claimed validity.
Keyed receipts and the internal receipts required for server-driven
continuations have their separate durable lifecycle (§3.1); cache eviction
never deletes a still-needed receipt. Ordinary unkeyed HTTP program
identities are in memory, not new retained durable rows. These are proposed
starting bounds to measure under the matched host RAM budget, not raised query limits.

## 7. Alternatives, risks and remaining questions

- **Only RT6 and strictly serial group commit.** Useful intermediate
  measurements, insufficient for natural W4/mix under the stated schedule.
  Its `1/(pR)` floor disappears with the buffered, level-synchronous route;
  it does not establish a need for K3/K4. Read-back elimination can change
  delayed W4's serial result and must be counted in the measured schedule.
- **Interleave database writes across member savepoints.** Rejected:
  nested rollback can erase peers and reverse visibility can change earlier
  members' reads. Private overlays and ordered acceptance are required.
  Unknown/dynamically expanding dependencies lose concurrent eligibility;
  the serial fallback can cost flights and throughput.
- **Rerun an unknown batch using private identities.** Rejected. Only
  explicit abort evidence permits §3.1's deadline-bounded rerun path. A
  new unkeyed HTTP request is distinct; backlog can extend its double-apply
  window. Correlated unknown outcomes can still disqualify a trial.
  Section 8 preserves the round-1 disagreement; §9 records round-2
  acceptance and the added deadline and named-member constraints.
- **Batch every durable kind or add an internal receipt to every HTTP
  member.** Deferred: S1 needs program members; all-kind invalidation
  needs a complete stream, not co-batching. Required client and continuation
  receipts stay; their bytes and other tail work remain a potential limit.
- **Treat result preservation as equality, or use a TTL.** Rejected:
  `preserved()` only proves authority preservation, and direct revocations
  may not move public head. Any missing producer, range, hidden/absent row
  or authority dependency breaks correctness; conservative coverage can
  instead destroy hit rate. Reconstruct head-bound artifacts or bypass.
- **Require a grant to reach the read goal.** Deferred until measured
  G4a/any needed G5 and cheap permit/queue tuning miss. Does `/q`'s
  body-limited ingress preserve bounded admission and refusal ordering,
  and what admission-tail margin remains at 22,627/s? The 1.414 ms mean
  diagnostic cannot answer that. A grant requires qualified claim/renewal
  ordering, failover clock bounds and takeover availability cost; unsupported
  deployments keep fresh validation. P2 has no local zero-flight claim.
- **Rely on warm locality for every read.** W1's broad population and W3's
  expensive misses can defeat it. Report bounded-memory evictions and
  adjacent cold/cache-off failures. Mixed writes can invalidate W2/W3 or
  cause revision-equality misses, or keep a promotion/G6 gate closed;
  G5 never waits on it. Read-only S1 does not answer that.
- **Pre-execution or lowering alone fixes natural writes.** Unsupported:
  the measured ~150 microseconds/member already exceeds the natural budget,
  before a useful inter-batch pipeline exists. Can G8 lower the marginal
  term while preserving log/evidence/receipt obligations, and which part
  comes from generic image layout versus protocol rows? The typed-layout
  question is reopened, not decided here.
- **A noisy or topology-mismatched win establishes the goal.** Rejected.
  Controls may rise; product-induced loaded tails are failed capacity,
  not environmental exclusions. Keep finite-window and path labels,
  three repetitions, failing brackets and exact counter reconciliation.
  W4 natural/mix natural may remain **not demonstrated** after this route.

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Accepted

**Author:** Codex / GPT-6 (for Charlie Cheever)
**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Revised:** 2026-09-22 (selected scope after independent reviews; parent §7; measured stop and bounded atomic recipe in §6)
**Related:** [Problem](./3000.011.001-reporting-and-job-complexity.research.md), [LLP 3000.007.002](./3000.007.002-common-application-shapes.rfc.md), [LLP 3000.009](./3000.009-general-purpose-backend.rfc.md)

## 1. Selected recommendation

Preserve a recipient's exact live counts and effect admission in one transaction.
Measure a narrowly recognized count consumer that avoids retaining a full scan
array solely to compute its length. Reuse the existing interpreter and read
machinery, without lowering Quarry to SQL or changing resource ceilings.
This replaces the first draft's message-paged accumulator after the independent
reviews and the delegated selection in the parent §7.

The observed control is the 0.2.31 whole-chat count: both the original and a
narrow projection parked at the 16 MiB heap ceiling. Its replacement completed
33,949 chunks and 580,996 writes. Retained original source and receipts exist
in the calibration-15 packet. Use those shapes, including stored-row body size,
rather than claiming that a synthetic tiny-row example fixes the diary.

## 2. Exact count implementation boundary

Recognize only a supported count-of-scan form. Count the same selected rows,
with the same limits, authorization, expiry, tombstones, filters, ordering,
read dependencies and acquisition coverage that the array-producing path has.
Unsupported forms continue through the existing path. Do not add a second
interpreter, special application authority or aggregate cache.

Avoid allocations the scalar consumer does not need; meter the allocations
and work it still performs. Preserve examined/index/rule/step charges, the
existing heap ceiling and total-read refusal. A capped or unknown domain is
not a successful scalar total. `count(first N ...)` still counts that page;
it is not silently promoted to a whole-domain count. The jobs example must
state its supported domain and refuse overflow before emitting a purported
total. Do not weaken the meter merely to make a fixture green.

Use the same implementation on memory, SQLite, device and any affected store
adapter. Keep expiry dependencies and live invalidation intact even though
the consumer returns only an integer. Do not optimize a search/merge form by
assuming its continuation semantics equal an ordinary scan.

## 3. Digest composition

Keep the direct period-seeking recipe as the default. For each recipient,
re-prove membership because jobs have system authority; compute all required
message, follower and Following-post components under the declared product
scope, then admit the effect and a recipient/period emission marker atomically.
The business delivery identity is independent of an implementation-policy
version: changing code must not create another notification for the same
completed recipient/period. A replacement run observes the marker.

Markers hold no private bodies, are unreadable by ordinary principals and do
not sync. Keep them for the life of the store in this increment; accepting an
old rerun after deleting its marker would undo the idempotence contract.
Provider retries use the same business key. Atomic effect admission is not
exactly-once external delivery.

Replace or clearly narrow the current `digestLarge` card example in place.
Its membership chunks and small inner count cannot be advertised as a
complete busy-chat solution; deleting totals after emission is not a durable
replacement-run marker. Preserve useful direct guidance and the actual parked
run recovery commands. Do not install a new digest generator.

## 4. Rejected live accumulator

The initial proposal counted a message in an early chunk, retained that count,
and rechecked membership at emission. A later expiry, tombstone or read-cursor
advance could therefore leave a stale positive count. Clique M8 requires those
live facts to affect its answer. Another non-atomic reconciliation pass merely
moves that race. Grok's contribution-time policy is coherent for another
product, but is rejected as a fix for this one.

The first draft also left membership incarnation, late backdated inserts,
replacement upper bounds, recipient fanout and policy-version delivery identity
unsettled. No period-owner table or job-state inspection primitive is added to
paper over those decisions. Paging finalized immutable input could be a later
separate design; it is not implemented or claimed here.

## 5. Validation and success

Capture the failing control first. Compare the selected count against the
ordinary materialized result and an independent oracle for many quiet chats,
a 10,000-message busy chat, both together, and large stored bodies. Record heap,
examined rows, writes, chunks, duration and marker bytes for equivalent work.
Check whether the whole recipient transaction, rather than just one count,
fits its existing budget. A useful narrower improvement may ship with a stated
remaining envelope, but not with an unproved claim to solve every busy digest.

Exercise authorization, expiry, deletion, read advance, leave/rejoin, empty
periods, overflow, cuts, unknown device coverage, repeated period and crash
before/after the atomic emission transaction. For advancing-time cases use
separate scheduled chunks or transactions: one `run_job` invocation pins its
clock and cannot alone prove time advancing between chunks. Preserve rollback
and provider ambiguity. Ship the count change only with measured benefit and
no semantic weakening; if it is ineffective, retain the reproduction and
correct guidance and record the remaining product boundary honestly.

## 6. Implementation and measured stop (2026-09-22)

**The count-retention optimization is not shipped.** The premise was tested
before changing the interpreter. Removing the result vector does not remove
SQLite's decoded row images or their message bodies. The store allocation
experiment still exceeds the existing heap ceiling before adding the
interpreter's authorization, filter, expiry and dependency work. Reducing
the charge for allocations that remain would not be an optimization.
No interpreter, store adapter, resource ceiling or read semantics changed.

The exact original calibration-15 failure was reproduced with the installed
`snapback4-darwin-arm64@0.2.31`, retained original Quarry source and a disposable
APFS clone of the retained scale store. Only `digestContributions` and
`digestMarkers` were cleared through a cleanup job, maintaining their derived
tables; otherwise the completed workaround's bookkeeping would skip the
original work. Original arguments were `periodStart=1789900080000`,
`periodEnd=1789900200000`, with `--now 1789900626466`. The run took 1.451 s
and parked after **5 chunks / 5 writes** with
`E_BOUND: heap bytes reached the ceiling 16777216`, matching the original
receipt. This does not qualify the original accumulator's live semantics.

Read-only inspection corrected the causal hypothesis: the original failing
chat does **not** have large bodies. The store has 100,015 messages, with mean
body length 27.28 UTF-8 bytes and maximum 42. The sixth membership reaches a
10,000-message chat whose bodies average 30.992 bytes (maximum 31), while
every row carries **50 recipient IDs**. The inspected sender and recipient
IDs are 31 bytes, the conversation ID five bytes and message ID twelve.
The inspected row omits nullable media columns, materialized as null on
read. The stored JSON averages 2,361.223 B per row (maximum 2,539 B).
The original two-minute range selects **5,000 candidates**, all unexpired;
**4,900** qualify after the unread/sender/tombstone filter (`readAt=0`).
The 10,000-row stress fixture is not the original range cardinality.
The maximum-body test below is a separate worst case; it must not be cited
as the explanation of the original failure. A representative recipient-heavy
fixture now measures that actual shape as well.

`snapback4/crates/snapback4-sim/tests/digest_count.rs` retains the original
count expression and message-column shape, including optional media,
recipients and a body of up to 2,000 characters. Unrelated operations are
removed; the matched period has 100 own messages that the filter excludes.
The initial smaller-recipient fixtures below let every message pass. The control uses the
existing query interpreter with the unchanged job ceilings and pinned time;
queries are legal over expiry tables, unlike a mutation page. A test-only
store pass compares fetching a full batch against batches of 32, dropping
each row after counting. It is an allocation lower-bound experiment, not
another interpreter or an equivalence claim about authorization.

The final matched-period fixture uses 5,000 candidates, 4,900 qualifying
messages, 31-byte bodies, 50 recipient IDs of 31 bytes each, the observed
ID lengths and omitted media columns. The ordinary interpreter examines
5,000 rows and reaches **24,060,341 B** metered heap / **55,714,442 B** Rust
allocations, returning null/incomplete. Even the decode-only pass that drops
results still decodes row images with a summed heap footprint of
**22,940,000 B**, above 16,777,216 B. Rust allocations total **45,303,495 B** with batches of
32 and **45,537,915 B** with one batch of 5,001; both count the same 4,900
eligible rows. This is the decisive stop evidence for the original workload:
removing result retention leaves the actual stored-row decoding work.

These measurements ran on the isolated M5 workspace with four Cargo build
jobs and no concurrent build. The busy large-body control took 58,694 µs;
the decode-only batch-of-32 pass took 36,564 µs. The latter omits interpreter
work, so their timing difference is **not** an implementation speedup.
Rust allocations are cumulative allocator requests, excluding SQLite's
native allocations; neither column is peak memory or RSS. The meter's heap
is its existing approximate allocation ledger. Ten thousand six-byte rows
return a page count, which does not establish an unbounded total. The
whole-recipient transaction remains bounded even where one count fits.

The jobs card now keeps one exact live recipient transaction, tests a
101st matching message and refuses `DIGEST_CHAT_BOUND` above its declared
100-message per-chat envelope. This retains `count(first N ...)` page
meaning. It adds an unreadable, unsynced, job-written recipient/period
marker in the same transaction as effect admission, including a marker for
zero-count recipients. Replacement runs, restarts and implementation changes
share that business identity; markers are retained for the life of the store.
Provider deduplication remains separate. The misleading `digestLarge`
accumulator was removed in place, with its live-state race and the remaining
whole-recipient meter limit explained. No digest generator was added.

The revised engine cases pass **11/11**. They cover overflow, atomic rollback
before/after effect and marker, durable restart, duplicate queued runs,
replacement after a parked run, private markers, empty periods, and expiry,
deletion, tombstone, read-cursor and leave/rejoin changes between recipient
transactions. The three-profile oracle fixture commits **3 chunks / 3 writes /
2 effects / 3 markers**. Marker canonical row payloads total **307 B**, excluding
indexes and SQLite overhead. The 1,000-chat fixture with five period messages
per chat uses **7,004 examined rows**, one chunk and one marker write; its
ordinal-seeking control parks on attempted examination 16,385. Under the
same unchanged 16,384-row job ceiling, 1,000 memberships plus **14,380**
period messages fit; one more message rolls back the whole recipient. These
are the small-body fixture's bounds, not a large-body capability claim.

The compiled-guide suite passes **49/49**, including the replacement of the
old `digestLarge` expectation and `DIGEST_CHAT_BOUND`'s `why`/jobs routing.
The standalone allocation target passes its complete matrix: small and
maximum-body rows, the actual recipient-array shape at 1,000 and 10,000
rows, the matched 5,000-candidate period, 999 empty quiet chats, and quiet
chats combined with each busy stress shape. Those mixed fixtures process
the quiet chats before the busy one. The original full-store control remains
the historical workload reproduction; the generated fixtures isolate its
allocation costs without committing application IDs or messages.
The 1,000 empty-chat control returns zero with 264,760 B heap and 1,000
examined rows. With 999 quiet chats followed by a 10,000-message busy chat,
the maximum-body case reaches 33,534,760 B heap and the recipient-heavy case
49,354,788 B, both at 11,000 examined rows, returning null/incomplete.

Affected commands, run serially on M5 with `CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4`:

```
cargo test -p snapback4-sim --test clique digest:: -- --nocapture
cargo test -p snapback4-sim --test digest_count -- --nocapture
cargo test -p snapback4-lang --test guide
```

All pass. An initial stale shared-target artifact ran the old compiled jobs
card; rebuilding the workspace crates superseded that invalid run. No
optimization or expanded busy-chat capability is claimed. The shipped
improvement is the truthful bounded recipe, atomic durable deduplication,
its refusal guidance and the retained failure/measurement evidence.

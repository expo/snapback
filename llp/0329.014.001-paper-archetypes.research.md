## Summary

Six applications — the four LLP 0329.015 archetypes (booking, workspace,
auction, ledger), the Clique M1–M8 benchmark, and the shipped `coterie` app —
were each written *completely* in the v0 language of
[LLP 0329.014.000](./0329.014.000-bounded-programs-language-v0.spec.md) by a
model family different from the one that designed them, and scored operation by
operation with the spec's §11 rubric. Every report is retained verbatim under
`llp/reviews/0329.014.001-paper-archetypes.*`.

**Result.** The language's core — typed, bounded, traced reads and writes with
real control flow; rules in the language; `maintain`; constraints; `schedule`;
`channel` — carries the large majority of operations in every application, and
**no application needed a second query class or an opaque handler**:

| Double-entry ledger | 12 | 5 plan + 5 maintained | 11; 1 refused (exact unbounded reconcile) | codex |

The residue is **not more operators**. Every report retired the operator
demands of the earlier stress test (`sum`, fan-out patch, range scans, stable
ordering, `appendOnly`, `immutable`, maintained balances, intervals, `ctx.now`
— "v0 already contains them"), and what remained sorted into six *dimensions of
the model* (§2). The most valuable findings are the five places where a spec
rule as written **admits a wrong program** (§3); four of them are the same
defect — a fact escaping its visibility — seen from a new side each time.

The thesis this leaves for the ideal design: **a small language and a rich
schema**. The language was never the bottleneck once control flow between
typed bounded reads is admitted; the schema must grow the six dimensions as
first-class declarations the engine enforces and the certificate reports.
"Refuse, do not degrade" then has one failure mode left — the application
needs a fact the schema cannot yet declare — and that is what every residue
was. Wave 2 ([LLP 0329.014.002](./0329.014.002-paper-archetypes-wave-2.research.md))
was designed to break exactly this thesis.

## 1. What the core carried

- **Bounded set writes** are loops: workspace trash/restore, auction settlement
  over holds and wallets, ledger fan-out postings, Clique receipt fan-out —
  `for (r of db.scan(…, { take })) db.update(…)`, bound = the scan's `take`,
  `hasMore` at the bound = `E_FANOUT`.
- **Aggregates**: exact over a page or maintained over the population — follow
  counts, card counts, tallies, unread, split balances, lifetime totals,
  descendant counts; `countCapped` for "99+".
- **Find-or-create** under a unique key (`startDm`, DM keys, `(lo, hi)` pairs)
  with prediction `hydrate` or `partial` by key provenance.
- **Time windows in mutations** via intent-bound `ctx.now()` (edit/delete
  windows, holds), server-validated and reused.
- **Schedules** with a system principal for closes, sweeps, expiry, digests.
- **Channels** for typing, slot intent, cursors.
- **Prediction**: most mutations `closed` or `conditional`; `partial` only at
  computed keys (mentions, search tokens) and at sites that depend on rows the
  caller cannot hold (waitlist promotion).
- **Multi-device**: client-minted ids, OCC on the traced version, monotone
  merges (`max`) where the product wants convergence, no CRDT.

## 2. The residue, as six model dimensions

**A. Structural facts versus caller facts — K2 generalized.** Every report
found a count, rank, "is it full", or "who wins" whose *filtered* version is a
confidently wrong integer: waitlist depth, auction occupancy, highest sealed
bid, last-owner count, a link's `useCount`, a bootstrap seat. Auction extends
K2 from counts to **every reduction** (`sort … [0]`, `take 1` as a maximum);
workspace extends it to **writes** — a subtree update under caller visibility
silently skips descendants the writer cannot see. One family suffices —
*a reduction or structural write must not escape its visibility* — with two
admitted routes: a maintained aggregate whose `readable` is a declared
disclosure, or system-visibility computation by a `schedule` written to a new
table at the transition. Loop tallies are in the family (booking) or it is
laundered. Adopted as v0.1 §9.1(8); the write side (`E_STRUCTURAL_WRITE`) is
new.

**B. Time is state.** "Live", "future", "last 90 days", "sealed until close",
"expires at 5:00" are statuses flipped by schedules, never comparisons;
`maintain … where` is time-independent by design, so time-windowed aggregates
are maintained over a flipped status; visibility transitions are *data*
transitions (the sealed winner is written to a public table at close, never
exposed by a time-dependent `readable`). Schedules need: live `at` with
reschedule-on-change, `if` guards, args from the trigger row, no enqueue on an
absent `at`, a schedule generation distinct from schema succession, and
**system-only callables** (a schedule's target must not be client-callable by
default).

**C. Constraints that bind every writer.** Partial `unique`/`cap`/`exclusion`
(`{ where }`), empty-interval semantics, self-row exemption and two-phase
deferral for `exclusion`, cross-column checks (`bookings.orgId ===
resources.orgId`), conditional `immutable(fields, { unless })`, `(old, new)`
update predicates with per-field grants (`immutableExcept`), and — the ledger's
demand — **transaction-scoped aggregate constraints** (`balanced(postings, …)`,
`sumEquals(child.share, parent.amount)`). These are what turn invariants from
program-level `require`s into engine facts; K6 holds only because there is no
write path outside the language.

**D. Missing kinds for a complete application** (Clique's fifteen refusals):
an **identity** kind (credentials, sessions, tokens, `ctx.viewer` projection);
an **asset** sublanguage (intent/finalize, ordinary versus one-use delivery,
revocation, purge receipts); a **commit-triggered effect outbox** (durable job
identity, connection-state-at-commit, provider idempotency); a **calendar** seam
(zoned recurrence is `E_OPAQUE` without it); **in-transaction composition**
(`db.run(namedMutation)` or inlined private transaction functions); and
**bearer capabilities** (a `unique(…, { bearer: true })` index whose full-key
`get` is admitted to any principal and whose possession becomes a rule input —
share links, invites, magic links, resets — without which `get` by token
returns `null` to exactly the people the link exists for).

**E. Hierarchy needs a primitive, not just a closure.** The spec's claim that
`tree` turns recursive ACL into "one indexed read" is false for the rule
grammar: "nearest self-or-ancestor grant wins, a denial stops the walk" is a
**`nearest`** primitive in rules and programs (point-probe lowering ≤
`maxDepth + 1`, or a hoisted scan with O(depth) pure steps under a companion
index and cap), plus engine-propagated **inherited columns**
(`tree({ inherit })`, authorized by the root row's rule — the write-side K2
fix), `maxSubtree` with `engineWrites` in the certificate, and per-node
descendant counts. Twenty of the workspace's twenty-nine operations hinge on
this one dimension.

**F. Authorization ≠ retention ≠ disclosure.** Three policies the spec
collapsed into one read rule: what the server serves *now* (the rule); what a
client may *keep* after authorization ends (Clique's expelled member retains
delivered history while future rows are denied and content revocations still
purge — the spec's "evict rows held by no range" is wrong for that product;
the ledger's former member sees interval rows plus own postings); and which
*aggregates* are disclosed beyond their sources (`readable` on `maintain`;
workspace's coarse `nodeCount` versus refused fine counts). Each is a separate
declaration with its own security item.

Plus spec completeness the reports all asked for: `optional(t)`, enums with
declared transitions, `int({ min })`, bounded-list arguments, cardinality
propagation through `filter`/`map`/`sort`/`Map`/`Set`, normative money
arithmetic (Euclidean `divmod`, widened `mulDiv`), published default ceilings,
directional cursors, settlement semantics (OCC retry versus arrival-order LWW).

## 3. Spec rules that admitted a wrong program

These are the results that matter most, because each is the silent-degradation
class this family exists to eliminate, arriving through the text written to
prevent it:

1. **§4.4's "highest bid wins" route** (`bids.byAuctionAmount desc take 1`) is
   an admitted wrong program under a sealed-bid rule: it returns the *caller's*
   highest bid and can be written back as the winner. (auction) Fixed in v0.1
   §9.1(8): reductions escape their visibility; sealed facts are computed by a
   schedule under system visibility and written at the transition.
2. **K3's "bound = the scan's `take`"** admits `for (r of db.scan(nodes.byPath…))
   db.update(…)` with a clean certificate while descendants the trasher cannot
   see stay live. (workspace) New family `E_STRUCTURAL_WRITE`; rewrite: an
   inherited column or a system schedule.
3. **§2's `tree` claim** ("turns recursive ACL into one indexed read") is
   false for §3's rule grammar. (workspace) Needs `nearest`.
4. **`E_STRUCTURAL_COUNT` as first written** covered `page.count()` and could be
   laundered as `let n = 0; for … n++`. (booking) Fixed in v0.1 §9.1(8).
5. **§7's "read elevation is never needed to settle"** is false for purge and
   for scheduled closes that must read rows the system principal's rules do not
   otherwise admit; and `schedule` targets were client-callable by default.
   (workspace, auction) Needs `callable: 'system'` and an explicit statement of
   which reads a system principal may make.
6. **`maintain … where`** cannot express "future" or "last 90 days"; the honest
   form is a schedule-flipped status. (booking) Clarified in v0.1 §9.1(11).
7. **K2's "the compiler proves readability or refuses" false-positives when
   readability is inherited through a foreign key** — receipts, post media,
   and feed events carry a retyped copy of the parent's rule over denormalized
   columns, so `maintain.count(receipts, { by: ['messageId'] })` is refused or
   forced to declare a `readable`, emitting a security item for a number that
   discloses nothing. (coterie) Needs an `inherits(messages, 'messageId')` rule
   form the prover understands.
8. **Prediction ignores constraints the client cannot evaluate.** `openDm` and
   `sendMessage` certify `closed` while the server refuses at a `cap` the
   client cannot count — and the operation-exact envelope code the acceptance
   suite expects needs the very third-party count K2 refuses. (coterie) The
   two amendments collide on coterie's most-tested behavior; prediction must be
   `conditional(constraint)` for any write subject to a `cap`/`unique` the
   client cannot evaluate, and constraints need declarable refusal codes.
9. **The bound calculus has no fan-in shape.** The following feed — the
   archetypal social read — is admitted only as a 1,025-prefix loop certified
   at 21,785 examined entries against 2,330 for a k-way merge; under
   `G = 10,240` the only in-language rewrite is fan-out-on-write, which is
   unbounded (followers are uncapped) and pushes toward capping followers, the
   wrong product change. (coterie, Clique) Needs a `merge` scan over several
   prefixes of one index with a shared `order` and `take`.

A pattern worth naming: in four of the six, **writing the design in a strict
language found bugs in the design itself** — two reversed money signs and a
temporal-visibility self-contradiction (ledger), a false waitlist-standing
formula (booking), a share link that keeps working after the node moves
(workspace), and an anti-snipe patch that lets a losing bidder stall a close
forever (auction). The language's refusals are a design review.

## 4. Product changes the reports recommend, and where they are cheats

Consistent across applications: show *depth* never *rank* over hidden rows
(booking, auction); reveal at the close *commit*, never at the clock
(auction, workspace expiry as scheduled revocation); cap what must be bounded
and expose the cap in the product (follows, members, custom splits, subtree
size); materialize recurrence to a horizon (booking); forbid retroactive
correction only if "fix the dinner" was never promised (ledger); occupancy
public only when the number is not itself secret (auction). Every report drew
the same boundary: a product change is legitimate when the simpler product is
genuinely wanted, and a cheat when it is adopted to protect the backend model
— especially while still claiming offline retention or exact money.

## 5. What the ideal design must include that v0 lacks

Ranked by how many applications needed it and how wrong the answer is
without it. "Kind" is the §11 taxonomy.

| # | Addition | Kind | Needed by | Smallest form |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | `(old, new)` update predicates; per-field grants (`immutableExcept`) | schema | auction, workspace, booking, ledger | v0.1 §9.1(4) |

| 4 | Schedule semantics: live `at`, reschedule, `if`, trigger args, absent-`at`, system-only callables, schedule generation | runtime | auction, booking, Clique | §2 B |
| 5 | Retention policy separate from the read rule (keep-delivered-history; content revocation overrides) | policy | Clique, ledger | per-table `retain` declaration |
| 6 | Partial constraints (`where`) on `unique`/`cap`/`exclusion`; empty intervals; self-row exemption; cross-column checks | schema | booking | §2 C |
| 7 | Transaction-scoped aggregate constraints (`balanced`, `sumEquals`) | schema | ledger | §2 C |
| 8 | Identity kind; asset sublanguage; commit-triggered effect outbox | kinds | Clique (auction, workspace for assets/outbox) | §2 D |
| 9 | Bearer capabilities (`unique(…, { bearer })`, `principal.bearerOf`) | schema + principal | workspace (invites, links, resets everywhere) | §2 D |

| 15 | Normalized prefix/token indexes with identical local derivation | schema | Clique, booking | §2 |
| 16 | Fan-in `merge` scan (k-way over prefixes of one index, shared order and `take`) with its own bound rule | construct | coterie, Clique | §4.2, §5 |

Nothing in this table is a second query class, a handler, or an author-written
optimistic reducer. Items 1, 2, 4(part), 6(part) are already v0.1 rulings; the
rest amend the schema, the kinds, or the principal model.

## Confidence

- **High** that the six reports say what is quoted (verbatim artifacts) and
  that no application needed a second query class — the strongest cross-family
  agreement in the family.
- **High** on the admitted-wrong-program findings (§3): each has a one-line
  counterexample checkable by inspection, and three were found independently
  by different families on different apps.
- **Medium-high** on the six-dimension framing: it is the orchestrator's
  taxonomy over the reports' deltas, corroborated by each report's own
  "not needed here / already in v0" lists, not a measurement.
- **Medium** on the per-app counts (self-classified, paper) and on the claim
  that the additions in §5 are *sufficient* — wave 2 exists to test that.
- **Low** on cost: `nearest`'s hoisted lowering, `tree.inherit` write
  amplification, and per-member fan-out on SQLite are named but unmeasured.

## Open questions

1. Is `nearest` a rule-grammar extension or a schema-declared derived column
   (`tree.inherit` of the grant itself)? The workspace report gives both
   lowerings; the ideal design should pick one and state its certificate.
2. What is the retention-policy declaration's exact shape — per table, per
   rule, or per subscription — and how does content revocation compose with
   it?
3. Bearer capabilities change the principal model (`principal.bearerOf`). Is
   that one addition or the start of a capability system?
4. Which system-principal reads are admitted for schedules, stated once?
5. Do the wave-2 archetypes (vector, graph, health, payments, game, editor,
   BI, realtime) reduce to the same six dimensions, or does one of them force
   a second read-site class — the pre-registered question of LLP 0329.014.002?

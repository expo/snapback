## Summary

[LLP 0329.014.007](./0329.014.007-competitive-and-local-first-assessment.research.md)
found that local-first is the axis on which the bounded-data-programs design
([LLP 0329.014.004](./0329.014.004-bounded-data-programs-v1-design.rfc.md))
most clearly improves on Snapback as built, and listed eight candidate
improvements in one paragraph each. The founder asked for them written up.
This document works each candidate through at the level the v0 spec
([LLP 0329.014.000](./0329.014.000-bounded-programs-language-v0.spec.md))
is written at: the stated gap, the proposal as declarations, certificate
lines, states, and diagnostics, a worked Clique example, interactions with
the design's dimensions, the security argument, cost, and the evidence that
would settle it. It is Research: proposals for the author, not amendments to
the spec, and it authorizes nothing.

The eight, ranked:

| # | Candidate | What it turns into | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hold sets and `offline: 'required'`** | `partial` prediction becomes a `check`-time refusal with a rewrite | certificate `holds[]`, `E_OFFLINE_PARTIAL` |
| 2 | **Dependency-aware outbox** | multi-intent offline sessions get a stated rejection cascade, derived from the local trace, and rejections carry the re-read closure | intent `dependsOn`, `rejected: { reason }` |
| 3 | **Delta resume by range version** | reconnect is a per-range diff, not a full resync | coverage manifest, `_commitSeq`, tombstone horizon |
| 4 | **Asset bytes under the row's retention lease** | offline images that obey purge, revocation, sign-out, and the fence | `retain({ assets })`, byte purge receipts |
| 5 | **Per-field `unheld` in result types** | an unheld author row no longer makes the whole thread `partial` | `{ unheld: 'allow' }`, `Held<T>` |
| 6 | **Local replay of token indexes** | search over downloaded rows with no replica | completeness `held`, tokenizer in the twin |
| 7 | **Store budget with eviction priority** | quota behavior stated once; eviction shrinks coverage, never invents | `store.maxBytes`, `retain({ priority })`, `partialSites[].reason` |
| 8 | **Cancel a queued intent; `asOf` on read handles** | unsend-while-offline; "as of three minutes ago" | `cancelled`, `handle.asOf` |

Two principles organize them. First, the design's own rule — *a static
certificate is the gate; refusal carries a rewrite; local replay is exact or
`partial`, never invented* — is applied to offline-ness itself (1, 2, 5, 7).
Second, the client stays what LLP 0008 DK-9 says it is, "a result cache +
cursor/epoch state + outbox, never a relational replica": nothing here adds a
local query the model did not name (3, 4, 6, 8 are all projections of the
receipt or the outbox). One correction to 0329.014.007's summary is recorded
in §7: eviction needs no ninth state, because it only shrinks coverage and
the existing exact-or-`partial` rule already covers it.

Interactions (§Interactions), a proposed split between v1 and post-v1
(§Sequencing), the deliberately not-proposed alternatives, and an evidence
plan against the S1 corpus close the document.

## The design's local-first contract, as the baseline

From 0329.014.000 §5–§6 and the RFC §7, restated so each candidate can name
what it changes:

- The **certificate** carries `predictionSites[{ transition, reads, mode:
  closed | conditional | partial }]`, `completeness: page | capped |
  maintained | all` (v1.1 adds `replica`, v1.2 `frontier(vector)`),
  `maxClosureBytes`, and `haloRows`.
- The **closure receipt** delivers authorized rows, visible ranges with a
  stop reason, absences, the N+1 row and N+2 key for delete-capable pages,
  and scoped verdicts; never hidden range extents.
- **Local re-execution is exact while `Reads(P, D₀ ⊕ Δ) ⊆ Coverage(receipt
  ⊕ Δ)`; otherwise `partial`, never invented.** Read states `hydrating |
  fresh | stale | partial`; intent states `predicted | queued | committed |
  rejected`.
- Prediction is `closed` when every read on the prediction path is keyed
  from arguments, the viewer, constants, or fields of traced rows the client
  holds; `conditional(constraint)` when the write is subject to a `cap` or
  `unique` the client cannot evaluate (RFC §5); `partial` at a computed-key
  site or an unheld read (S1: `@mention` is `partial` only at
  `thread.mentionUser`).
- **Reconnect** re-authorizes before outbox replay and evicts rows held by
  no range. One store-wide `maxOfflineAge`; `maxIntentAge` decoupled (v1.2).
  Sign-out destroys the per-principal store key.
- The **client** is a TypeScript twin of the interpreter (S3: 7.8 KB gzip)
  over a per-principal encrypted range store at one version, a verdict cache,
  and an outbox; "it cannot issue a query the model did not name."

## 1. Hold sets, and `offline: 'required'` as a gate

### Gap

The RFC names the cost in its own §10: "the `partial` state will surface in
offline UIs and its diagnostic must be excellent." Its §3.2 gives the
canonical instance: "an offline `send` whose author join needs
`users[viewer]` is `partial` after the fact." The design tells the author
*that* a site is partial, at `check`; it does not tell the client to *hold*
what would make it closed, and it gives the author no way to say "this
mutation must predict offline."

### Proposal

**(a) Derive the hold set.** For each mutation `M` and each query `Q` that
`M`'s write sites can affect (their write tables intersect `Q`'s read
tables), consider `Q`'s local replay over `D₀ ⊕ Δ_M`. Every read site of `Q`
whose key is a shape expression over `Δ_M`'s new rows — `users.byId([msg.
authorId])` where `authorId = ctx.viewer` — is a **hold candidate**. `check`
classifies each by *trigger*:

| Trigger | Key derivable from | Client behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `always` | `viewer` or a constant | subscribe at sign-in; keep for the session |
| `onHeld(table, key)` | a field of a row the client holds under some receipt | subscribe when the trigger row is delivered; release when it is evicted |
| `onArg` | a mutation argument only | cannot be prefetched; the site is `closed` only if the client happens to hold the row (it usually does — the user navigated there) |
| `computed` | a value computed inside the program | cannot be held; remains `partial` by nature |

The certificate gains `holds[{ site, prefixTemplate, trigger, bytesMax }]`
and a ceiling `maxHoldBytes`. Hold subscriptions are ordinary point
subscriptions (`get`s) — live, receipt-fed, rule-mediated, evicted like any
range — so they add no mechanism, only a default.

**(b) Report prediction mode against the hold set.** `predictionSites[].mode`
is computed over the union of the application's declared subscriptions and
the derived holds, and each `partial` site names its trigger class. The
agent-facing report is the certificate's prediction column read this way:
"`send`: closed given holds `users.byId([viewer])` (always); `@mention`:
partial at `thread.mentionUser` (computed)."

**(c) The gate.** A mutation may declare `offline: 'required'`; an
application may declare `offline: { required: [send, markRead] }`. For a
required mutation, `check` refuses with `E_OFFLINE_PARTIAL` any prediction
site that is `partial` with trigger `onArg` or `computed`, and the refusal
pastes the rewrite: for `onArg`, name the row and the query that would hold
it; for `computed`, the *restructuring* — move the computed key to the
client's compose step and pass it as an argument (a mention is resolved to a
user id by autocomplete before `send` is called, so `send({ mentionedUserIds:
id[] })` is `closed`), or admit the site as `unheld: 'allow'` (§5) if its
value only decorates the result. `predict: false` and `offline: 'required'`
on the same mutation is a contradiction and is refused.

### Worked example — Clique

```ts
export const send = mutation({ conversationId: id(conversations), body: text({ max: 4000 }),
                               mentionedUserIds: list(id(users), { max: 20 }) },
  ({ db, ctx, args }) => { /* insert message; bump memberships; insert mentions */ },
  { offline: 'required' });
// certificate (excerpt):
// holds: [{ site: thread.author, prefix: users.byId([viewer]), trigger: always, bytesMax: 512 },
//         { site: inbox.conversation, prefix: conversations.byId([membership.conversationId]),
//           trigger: onHeld(memberships.byUserActivity), bytesMax: 50 × 1 KiB }]
// predictionSites: [{ transition: send, mode: closed }]   // mentions are arguments, not lookups
```

The first attempt an agent writes — `const user = db.get(users.byHandle,
[handle])` inside `send` — is refused: `E_OFFLINE_PARTIAL at
send.mentionUser (computed key). Rewrite: resolve the handle before calling
send and pass mentionedUserIds; or drop offline: 'required'.`

### Interactions

- **Dimension A (structural facts).** A hold is a `get` under the table's
  rule; it can hold only what the principal may read, and the receipt's
  hidden-versus-absent indistinguishability applies. Holds widen nothing.
- **Dimension F (retention).** Held rows retain under their table's policy;
  an `always` hold of `users[viewer]` is the viewer's own row.
- **§7 (budget).** Holds are `pinned` priority; the budget must admit
  `maxHoldBytes` or `check` refuses the application's budget declaration.
- **§5 (`unheld`).** The gate accepts an `unheld: 'allow'` site as
  satisfied; the two are the same decision from two sides.

### Security

The hold set is a function of the schema and the program, not of data; its
bytes ceiling is a compile-time fact. A hold keyed from a held row reveals
only what a program over that row could already read. No hold may be
keyed from a hidden probe (`getHidden`) — refused, since that would make the
hold's presence an oracle.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: a certificate section, a diagnostic family, and hold bytes bounded by
`maxHoldBytes`. Evidence: enumerate prediction sites for the S1 corpus's
`send`, `markRead`, `startDm`, `addExpense`, `reserve`; compute each hold
set; report the fraction of formerly `partial` sites that become `closed`
and the hold bytes for a fifty-conversation inbox. Open: whether the hold
set is Clique-shaped (booking's `reserve` needs the slot row it is writing,
which is `onArg`; ledger's `addExpense` needs the group's member list, which
is `onHeld`).

## 2. A dependency-aware outbox

### Gap

The spec settles intents `committed | rejected` and defines local replay over
`D₀ ⊕ Δ`, but says nothing about `Δ₂` when it read rows `Δ₁` wrote and `Δ₁`
is rejected on replay. A reply to a message whose send was rejected, a
`markRead` over a membership row a rejected `join` created, an
`addExpense` after a rejected `addMember` — every offline session longer
than one intent has this shape. Today the behavior lives in per-arm code
(`packages/snapback-client/optimistic-rebase.mjs`, LLP 0218) and is not a
stated contract.

### Proposal

**Local traces.** The twin already runs the same interpreter for local
mutations (S1). Each local execution of an intent `Δᵢ` yields a local trace
`Tᵢ` — points, ranges, absences read; rows written — in the same form as the
authoritative trace. Define `dependsOn(Δⱼ, Δᵢ)` for `i < j` iff `Tⱼ.reads ∩
Tᵢ.writes ≠ ∅`. The outbox is a DAG in outbox order; in practice a chain.

**Replay.** Intents are dispatched in outbox order and settle in order. On
each settlement the baseline advances:

- `committed`: replace the predicted `Δᵢ` with the authoritative delta from
  the receipt. If they differ (an engine-assigned `sequence` number, a
  `commitTime`, a `maintain`ed counter the server computed over rows the
  client did not hold), every dependent is **re-predicted** over the new
  baseline, deterministically, in outbox order.
- `rejected`: remove `Δᵢ` from the overlay; re-predict dependents over the
  baseline without it. A dependent whose re-prediction itself fails — a
  `require` that no longer holds, a `ref` that now dangles — settles locally
  as `rejected: { reason: 'dependency', on: Δᵢ }` **without being sent**.
  Independent intents are unaffected.

**The re-read closure.** A server rejection carries, beside its code, the
authorized current rows the OCC compared — the rows of the authoritative
trace, filtered by the receipt rules (readable rows only, no hidden extents,
no absences the principal may not learn). Re-prediction then needs no round
trip. Its size is a certificate line, `maxRejectionBytes`, defaulting to
`maxClosureBytes`.

**States.** No new top-level state: `rejected` gains a `reason` (`server |
dependency | expired | cancelled`) and `on`. Re-prediction happens while the
dependent stays `predicted`.

### Worked example

Offline: `send(m1)` → `reply(m2, to: m1)` → `markRead(conv)`. On reconnect
the membership had been revoked and restored between the two, so `send(m1)`
is rejected by the rule at its commit time (dimension G: the security window
compares `commitTime`). `T_reply` read `messages[m1]` (the `ref` check), so
`reply` depends on `send`; re-predicted without `m1` its `require` fails and
it settles `rejected: { reason: 'dependency', on: send }` locally.
`T_markRead` read only `memberships[conv, viewer]`, which `send` did not
write; it is dispatched and commits. The UI shows two rejections with
reasons and one committed read marker — the same result the server would
have produced had the three been sent one at a time, which is the
determinism claim.

### Interactions

- **Dimension G.** Re-prediction rebinds nothing: the intent's `now` and
  `newId` were bound at intent time and stay bound; only the baseline moves.
- **`serialBy`.** Server-side per-key order is orthogonal; the outbox is
  sequential per client, so pipelining is not proposed.
- **§3 (delta resume).** Resume establishes the baseline *before* replay, in
  the spec's existing order (re-authorize, then replay).
- **§8 (cancel).** Cancelling `Δᵢ` is the rejection path with reason
  `cancelled`; dependents cascade identically.

### Security

The re-read closure is a receipt and obeys every receipt rule. For programs
declared `padTo: 'certificate'` (v1.2, dimension L) rejection detail is
already a private bit, and no closure ships — the cascade then runs over the
client's own baseline only. `insertIfAbsent`'s `already_acted` stays opaque.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: local traces (already produced), a dependency edge per intent pair, a
certificate line. Evidence: a corpus test — the three-intent chain above,
reject the first, assert both dependents' states and that Rust and the twin
settle byte-identically; a second chain where `committed` differs from
prediction (an engine `sequence`) and the dependent's re-prediction is
checked.

## 3. Delta resume by range version

### Gap

Today "a non-empty outbox withholds resume … full resync first"
(`guide/sync/offline-and-retention.md`). The design says reconnect
re-authorizes and evicts rows held by no range, and its store is "at one
version," but it does not say whether re-authorization re-delivers every held
range. On a phone after an hour offline, a fifty-conversation inbox with new
messages in five of them should cost five diffs, not fifty pages.

### Proposal

- Every row carries an engine-maintained `_commitSeq` (the design already
  has `commitSeq` watermarks for jobs and standing queries). The engine keeps
  a **tombstone log** `(table, key, commitSeq)` for deletes, retained for a
  `tombstoneHorizon` ≥ `maxOfflineAge`, so any client inside the fence can
  always delta-resume.
- The store records, per held range, `(readSite, prefix, lo, hi, version)`,
  where `version` is the `commitSeq` the range was delivered at.
- On reconnect the client sends its **coverage manifest** `[{ site, prefix,
  version }]` plus its auth epoch. In one transaction the server re-executes
  each subscription (it must, for re-authorization), and for each range
  returns rows with `_commitSeq > version`, tombstones in the range since
  `version`, and fresh verdicts. A range whose `version` is older than the
  horizon, or whose rule inputs' epoch changed, is returned in full.
- The client applies the whole response as **one generation swap**, so the
  store stays at one version. Until the swap, reads over affected ranges are
  `stale` with an age (§8), not `hydrating`; `hydrating` is scoped to ranges
  the server returned in full.

### Interactions

- **Dimension F.** Eviction of rows no longer in any range is the same step;
  a tombstone in a held range is the receipt's delete.
- **Halo.** The N+1 row and N+2 key are re-issued with the diff; a diff that
  crosses a page boundary returns the page in full.
- **§2.** Resume completes before outbox replay, establishing the baseline.
- **Dimension P.** The delegated regime's `atLeast(frontiers)` and the
  vault's `frontier(vector)` are the multi-authority analogues; this proposal
  is the single-authority case and does not touch them.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: eight bytes per row, a tombstone log with a retention window (one more
product parameter), manifest bytes linear in held ranges. Evidence: bytes
and time-to-`fresh` for the fifty-conversation inbox after one hour offline
with two hundred new messages in five conversations, delta versus full; the
same after a `purge-on-block` in one conversation, asserting the tombstones
evict.

## 4. Asset bytes under the row's retention lease

### Gap

The guide, verbatim: "Retained rows persist asset references and text, but
the first-party asset client does not persist rendition bytes … Snapback
currently provides no privacy- or revocation-aware offline image-byte
cache, so do not describe that workaround as platform retention." The
design's `assets` kind has upload intent/finalize, ordinary versus one-use
delivery, revocation, and purge receipts (RFC §6), and dimension F has
per-table retention — the two declarations exist and are not joined.

### Proposal

- Per table: `retain({ assets: { renditions: ['thumb', 'display'], maxBytes,
  policy: 'inherit' } })`. Default `assets: 'none'`.
- When a retained row carrying an asset reference is delivered, the asset
  client fetches the declared renditions **under the row's lease** and stores
  them keyed `(assetId, rendition)` in a byte cache wrapped by the
  per-principal store key.
- Lifecycle binds to the row: eviction, `purge-on-block`, content
  revocation, `propagate`d revokes (dimension E, E2EE), the `maxOfflineAge`
  fence, and sign-out all purge the bytes, and the **purge receipt covers
  bytes**.
- Assets delivered through `audited(into)` with a fenced delivery guard
  (v1.2, zero-trust) are never cached: caching would defeat the commit-time
  audit. `retain({ assets })` on such a table is refused,
  `E_ASSET_RETAIN_AUDITED`. One-use delivery likewise.
- Byte budget is the second bucket of §7's store budget (`maxAssetBytes`),
  LRU by the owning row's recency; never originals, renditions only.

### Interactions

- **Receipt.** The receipt already lists delivered asset references; the
  byte cache is a projection of the receipt, so nothing is cached that the
  receipt did not authorize.
- **§3.** A delta resume that tombstones a row purges its bytes in the same
  generation swap.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: a second budget, rendition fetches on delivery (bounded by
`maxBytes`), purge-receipt scope. Evidence: the Clique media milestone with
the device offline; a `purge-on-block` test asserting the blocked
conversation's bytes are gone before the next render; sign-out asserting
the cache is unreadable.

## 5. Per-field `unheld` in result types

### Gap

S1 "reports exact partial site ids," but the read state is whole-query:
one unheld author row makes the thread `partial`. For decorative reads that
is the wrong grain — the messages are exact; only the author's display name
is missing.

### Proposal

- A read site may be declared `db.get(users, id, { unheld: 'allow' })`.
  Its result field is typed `Held<T> = T | null | { unheld: true }`. On the
  server the sentinel never occurs (the server holds everything); locally an
  unheld site yields `{ unheld: true }`, distinct from `null` (absent).
- The query's state stays `fresh | stale`; `partialSites` lists nothing for
  an allowed site. A query is `partial` only when a read it could not take
  is on a **control-flow** path.
- `check` refuses `unheld: 'allow'` on any site whose value reaches an
  `if`, a `require`, a loop bound, a write, or another read's key —
  `E_UNHELD_CONTROL_FLOW` — using the same dataflow the v1.1 negative and
  flow claims already run. Branching on an unheld value would be inventing.

### Interactions

- **§1.** The gate accepts an allowed-unheld site; a site is either held by
  the hold set or allowed unheld, and the author chooses.
- **Dimension A.** `{ unheld: true }` is local-only and never crosses to the
  server or into a receipt; it cannot become a hidden-versus-absent signal.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: type noise, hence opt-in per site with whole-query `partial` as the
default. Evidence: the Clique thread with the viewer's own `users` row not
yet held — before: `partial`; after: `fresh` with a placeholder author, and
the hook re-renders once the hold (§1) arrives.

## 6. Local replay of token indexes

### Gap

`byToken` indexes "with declared normalization and prefix match" are
ordinary index scans (`db.scan(table.byToken, [term], { take })`), and a
range over `[term]` is replayable locally only if the same term was queried
online and the receipt covers it. Searching downloaded messages for a new
term while offline is therefore impossible, although every row it would
match is already held. The ranked-index site's "declared local alternative"
(dimension M, photos `all-owned`) covers the vector/FTS case by exception;
LLP 0329.013's argument for SQLite as the client store was largely that
local search would come free, and the panel judged that store year-two.

### Proposal

- Declared normalizations are a closed, small set pinned in the value model
  the twin is held to: `ascii-fold`, `unicode-simple` (case fold plus
  strip combining marks), `none`. Full NFKC is refused for `byToken`
  (table size against a 7.8 KB twin).
- The twin includes the tokenizer and maintains a **local token index over
  held rows** for each table with a `byToken` index, counted against the
  store budget (§7).
- A `scan(table.byToken, [term])` with no receipt coverage for `[term]` is
  answered locally over the held set with a new completeness label,
  **`held`**: exact over the rows the client holds, never a population claim.
  The certificate line for the site says `localReplay: 'held'`. Online, the
  same program gets server coverage and completeness `page`.
- A reduction over a `held` scan is a fact about the held set, which is the
  caller's own readable rows — admitted under dimension A as a caller-scoped
  computation, labeled ("3 results in downloaded messages"), never a
  population fact.

### Interactions

- **§7.** Local index bytes count against the budget; evicting a range
  drops its postings.
- **Dimension M.** This does not change the ranked-index site's
  `localReplay: false`; it makes the *ordinary* token index locally exact
  over held rows.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: tokenizer bytes in the twin (the only candidate that plausibly moves
the D-A measurement), a normalization conformance corpus run under Node and
JavaScriptCore, index maintenance on delivery. Evidence: the twin size delta
per normalization option; the corpus; a Clique offline-search test asserting
the `held` label and exactness over held rows.

## 7. A store budget with declared eviction priority

### Gap

The certificate bounds each program's closure (`maxClosureBytes`) and
`replica` has `maxBytes`, but nothing states what the device store does when
its total exceeds what the device will give it. Unstated behavior here
becomes either unbounded growth or silent loss, and the guide's
persist-debounce note shows how a loss reads as a retention bug.

### Proposal

- Application level: `store: { maxBytes, maxAssetBytes }`.
- Per table: `retain({ priority: 'pinned' | 'high' | 'normal' | 'low' })`,
  default `normal`. Holds (§1) are `pinned`.
- The eviction unit is a held **range** — the receipt's own unit. Within a
  priority class, least-recently-read first. Never evicted: ranges a mounted
  subscription holds, `pinned` ranges, and rows referenced by `predicted` or
  `queued` intents or their dependencies (§2) until settlement.
- Eviction **shrinks coverage and nothing else**. There is no new state: a
  program whose read site falls in an evicted range replays `partial` at
  that site under the existing rule (`Reads ⊆ Coverage` fails), and the
  next online read is `hydrating`. What is added is the *reason*:
  `partialSites[{ site, reason: 'evicted' | 'never-held' | 'computed-key' }]`,
  so the diagnostic and the UI can distinguish "you scrolled past this a
  week ago" from "this needs a network."
- Optionally, `maxOfflineAge` per priority — `pinned` at the store fence,
  `low` shorter — which both frees space and bounds staleness.

This corrects LLP 0329.014.007 §7, which proposed a "ninth transition": the
design's exact-or-`partial` rule already covers eviction, and adding a state
for it would put a second mechanism beside the one that suffices.

### Cost and evidence

Cost: two budget numbers, one priority field, a reason on `partialSites`.
Evidence: Clique with ten thousand messages across fifty conversations under
a 50 MB budget, asserting pinned, hold, and outbox-referenced rows survive,
that evicted ranges report `reason: 'evicted'`, and that the next online
read of an evicted range rehydrates it.

## 8. Cancel a queued intent, and `asOf` on every read handle

### Proposal

- **Cancel.** A `queued` intent (never dispatched) may be cancelled →
  `cancelled`; its `Δ` leaves the overlay and dependents cascade (§2) with
  reason `cancelled`. A `predicted` intent that has been dispatched is not
  cancellable — `E_INTENT_IN_FLIGHT` — and needs an app-authored
  compensating mutation, which is honest: the server may already have
  committed it. `maxIntentAge` expiry takes the same path with reason
  `expired`. Without this primitive an agent building "unsend" reaches for a
  `delete` that fails offline or, worse, a client-side hack around the
  outbox.
- **`asOf`.** Every read handle exposes `{ version: commitSeq, deliveredAt
  }`. The store is at one version, so `version` is one number per store and
  `deliveredAt` one per range. `stale` therefore carries an age and "as of
  three minutes ago" surfaces need no device clock arithmetic against server
  time (dimension G's ban on client-clock security decisions is untouched:
  this is display).

### Cost and evidence

Trivial; a corpus case each.

## Interactions, summarized

```
§1 holds ──pins──▶ §7 budget ◀──second bucket── §4 asset bytes
   │                  ▲
   │ satisfied-by     │ postings count
   ▼                  │
§5 unheld          §6 local token index
§3 delta resume ──baseline-before-replay──▶ §2 dependency cascade ◀── §8 cancel (reason: cancelled)
§8 asOf ◀── §3 (stale-with-age until the generation swap)
```

None of the eight introduces a client capability the receipt or the outbox
does not already project, and none adds a query the model did not name.

## Sequencing

Candidates that change **semantics the certificate or the state machine
must carry** are hard to add later and belong in v1: §1 (a) and (b) —
hold derivation and mode-against-holds; §2; §5; §8. §1 (c), the
`offline: 'required'` gate, is a diagnostic over (a) and (b) and can ship
with them at no extra semantic cost — it is also the agent-facing artifact
that makes "offline-first" a compile-time claim, which is the product
reason to have it early.

Candidates that are **runtime or product parameters** and can be added
without changing what an admitted program means belong post-v1: §3
(resume), §4 (asset bytes), §7 (budget), §6 (local token replay — the only
one that touches the twin's size, and so the one to measure before deciding).

## Not proposed, and why

- **A replica with ad-hoc local queries** (Zero/Electric/PowerSync-shaped).
  It re-opens the client as the complexity sink (LLP 0329 F3) and puts
  hidden range extents on the device, which is the cardinality oracle
  dimension A closed. Everything above is a projection of the receipt.
- **CRDT-everything.** Table `merge` modes and the host `sequence` kind
  already cover the editor and collaborative-cart cases; `occ` with
  conflict-as-evidence was defended by the wave-4 cash design.
- **Automatic merge for `occ` rejections.** §2's re-read closure gives the
  application the rows to decide with; deciding for it would be inventing.
- **Pipelined outbox replay.** Sequential settlement keeps §2 deterministic
  and matches today's contract; the latency case is not yet evidenced.

## Open questions

1. Is the hold set Clique-shaped? Booking's `reserve` and ledger's
   `addExpense` are the tests (§1, evidence).
2. Should `dependsOn` include *absence* reads — `Δ₂` relied on a row *not*
   existing that `Δ₁` then inserted? The trace records absences, so it is
   derivable; whether to cascade on it is a ruling.
3. `maxRejectionBytes`: its own line, or `maxClosureBytes` reused?
4. Is `held` a completeness label or a scope label beside `page`? It is
   neither a page of the population nor the population; the structural-fact
   rule needs to know which.
5. Does the tombstone horizon have to be ≥ `maxOfflineAge`, or may an
   application accept full rehydration past a shorter horizon?
6. Which normalization set is small enough for the twin and large enough
   for non-Latin scripts? Measure before ruling.
7. Which of §1–§8 does the author want in v1? §Sequencing is a proposal.

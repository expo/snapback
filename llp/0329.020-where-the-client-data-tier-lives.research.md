## Summary

The founder proposed that **every Exact 2 app use Snapback 2 on the client**, with
that client able to talk to a Snapback 2 server *or* to a foreign backend (Rails,
GraphQL) through a bridge — replacing Aquifer and unifying the moving parts.

The orchestrator counter-proposed keeping Aquifer as the tier, making Snapback 2's
client a **rich provider**, and widening Aquifer's provider seam with a completeness
field so a Rails bridge could be a **thin provider** reporting `unknown`.

Two rounds — one blind, one open — across three families. **Both proposals were
rejected, including the orchestrator's, and the panel converged.**

> **Snapback 2's client is the store and execution tier for receipt-backed facts.
> Foreign backends are a separately declared resource family with non-coercible
> result types — not a lower grade of the same thing. Aquifer keeps coordination
> and loses the store.**

## What the panel converged on

### C1 — The tier for receipt-backed facts is Snapback's client

Not a taste judgement; a structural one. `ClientStore::hydrate(schema, receipt)`
seeds rows **and** calls `install_verdicts`, retaining range and absence evidence.
Grok: *"Those are not cache features. They are the store."*

Codex, which opened by defending Aquifer-as-public-tier, **withdrew that position
in round 2**: "I withdraw my round-one formulation that 'Aquifer stays the public
data tier' if *data tier* means one public query surface and one state machine for
all server-derived facts… That is composition only after a substantial shrink. It
is closer to Grok's position than my round-one label suggested."

### C2 — A per-backend capability grade is wrong; capability is per operation

Unanimous, and it kills the orchestrator's rich/thin framing directly. A
receipt-backed execution can still return `Partial`; one carefully mirrored Rails
resource can be stronger than its neighbours. Codex: *"A single 'rich backend'
badge will eventually hide exceptions."* Grok, adopting the sharper form:
**"Proof is a property of a result, not of a vendor."**

### C3 — Three layers that must stay distinct

Codex's formulation, which grok adopted, and the cleanest result of the panel:

1. the **authoring declaration** states the semantic contract it requires;
2. **admission** checks whether an implementation can supply that contract;
3. **runtime evidence** reports what happened in this particular execution.

> **"The third cannot substitute for the first two."**

This is precisely where the orchestrator's completeness field failed: it was layer
3 doing layer 1's job.

### C4 — The mechanism is non-coercible types, not a field and not ugliness

A receipt-backed declaration and a remote declaration need **different constructors
and non-coercible result types**. The remote result "must not have Snapback's
`Fresh`, `Predicted`, verdict, or covered-absence cases" — not as a field set to
`unknown`, but as cases that do not exist to be read.

And grok **moved** on its own round-1 mechanism. Codex: *"Ergonomic asymmetry is a
legitimate warning device, but 'worse' is not a durable correctness mechanism.
Teams under delivery pressure will wrap an ugly API, alias the convenient hook, or
add a default that hides `unknown`. **Taste loses to the first important Rails
customer.**"* Grok accepted this: "feel worse" → "type split and non-coercion;
ugliness is incidental."

### C5 — "Receipt-backed", not "proved"

Both families independently objected to the orchestrator's vocabulary. A closure
receipt is protocol evidence under a schema, verdict set, and authorization epoch —
not a cryptographic proof and not eternally current. Grok: *"that word is doing
harm if it inflates the claim."* **The corpus should use "receipt-backed" or
"trace-certified".**

### C6 — Aquifer shrinks but does not die

Grok's round-1 "shrink or die" was overstated and it moved. The split both families
accept:

| Aquifer keeps | Snapback owns |
|---|---|
| identity and principal isolation | the receipt-seeded store |
| dependency scheduling | program re-execution and replay |
| request lifecycle, cancellation | prediction and optimistic overlay |
| route/server integration, view binding | coverage and absence evidence |
| non-server-derived and foreign-fetch facts | local authorization verdicts, OCC |

Grok: *"I will not fight a coordination layer. I will fight a coordination layer
that owns the resource type."*

## Where they still disagree

One narrow point, worth recording because it decides an implementation.

**Codex:** separately named nominal contracts *may* share a compiler, scheduler,
subscription mechanism, and a generic cell container. "A generic container is
harmless if it carries `SnapbackResourceState<T>` unchanged; a generic data API
that projects both paths into `Rows<T> + completeness` is not."

**Grok:** that is the projection creeping back. *"Separate axes on a shared cell
are still cell state. Cell state is how the last defect hid."*

The test between them is whether the container is *parametric* (carries the
Snapback state type opaquely) or *normalizing* (projects into a common shape).
Codex's version is safe under that test; grok's worry is that the distinction
erodes under maintenance pressure. Both are plausible and the panel did not settle
it.

## Consequences for LLP 0329.019

The orchestrator's four proposed Aquifer amendments need rework:

- **A1 (completeness field on `rows-arrived`) is withdrawn** as the mechanism. C3
  says evidence cannot carry a contract. What survives is that the *evidence* is
  still needed at layer 3 — but it must not be the thing distinguishing the paths.
- **A2 (split partially-arrived from partially-held) survives and strengthens.**
  Under C4 those are different *types*, not two values of one status.
- **A3 (store ownership) survives intact and both families endorsed it.** Codex:
  Aquifer "must not independently evict rows while a Snapback receipt still claims
  their range is held." This is now the least contested item in the family.
- **A4 (record the reconnect mechanism)** unaffected.

The bigger consequence: LLP 0329.019 was framed as amendments *to* Aquifer's
provider seam. Under C1/C6 the more important conversation is about Aquifer's
**scope**, not its seam. A widened seam is the thing the panel most warns against.

## Confidence

High that the panel said what is quoted; the four documents are committed
verbatim.

Medium-high on the convergence being meaningful rather than an artifact of the
brief. The brief stated the orchestrator's position and invited attack, which
biases toward rejecting it — but codex opened by *agreeing* with the orchestrator's
direction and moved away only after seeing grok, which is movement under argument
rather than under instruction.

Low on the durability of C6's split. It is a list drawn in one panel round by
models that have not read most of Aquifer's 1,046 lines. The orchestrator has read
roughly a third; neither external family was given more than the brief plus
optional repository access.

**Declared bias:** the orchestrator wrote the proposal the panel rejected, ran the
panel, and wrote this synthesis. The two-round structure and verbatim artifacts are
the mitigation; they are not a substitute for someone else reading it.

## Open questions

1. Parametric container or separate stacks — the one live disagreement (§Where they
   still disagree). Deciding it needs someone who knows Exact's runtime.
2. Does C6's split survive contact with the parts of LLP 0498 nobody in this panel
   read — §4.1–4.3, §4.5, §5–7?
3. If Snapback's client owns the store for receipt-backed facts, what happens to
   Aquifer's `exact-sqlite` cell persistence for those facts specifically? A3
   answers ownership but not migration.
4. Grok proposes "foreign storage behind a Snapback *server*" as the honest version
   of the founder's unification — one local-first story, with the adapter on the
   server side where it can mint real receipts. Nobody costed it, and it may be the
   most useful idea in the panel.

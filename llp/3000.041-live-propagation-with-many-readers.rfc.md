## 0. Summary

A client that holds several live reads does not see other people's writes
promptly. This is not one defect. Three layers each lose time, and which
ones an app crosses depends on its client:

1. **exact2 apps have no push.** exact2 hosts Exact apps on Snapback 4
   through its own Rust device and TypeScript driver, not Snapback 4's
   TypeScript client. The driver exposes a long poll, but its README tells
   apps not to hold one inside a resource, so every farm app and Harmony
   re-read the world on a Contract timer. The designed fix, exact2 LLP
   1110's `Snapback.changes()` iterator, is on a branch with fourteen open
   review findings, and exact2 pins a Snapback 4 twenty releases behind
   `main`. exact2 apps benefit from the server work below once their own
   client adopts its interest protocol (W3) and from W4; the Snapback 4
   client work (W2) does not reach them.
2. **Snapback 4 wakes everyone on every commit.** One store-wide counter
   wakes every held `/changes` poll of every principal. Woken polls at the
   same cursor share one answer, so the cost is no longer one read per
   poll, but every client still answers, re-polls and (on a device) syncs
   for commits it cannot see, and a bystander learns which tables someone
   else wrote. Work per commit scales with connected clients, not with the
   commit's audience.
3. **The Snapback 4 client re-evaluates by table.** A wake re-runs every
   watch that shares a table with the commit, so a message in one room
   re-runs the reads of every other room. Under queue pressure a
   replacement that outlives its one-second episode withdraws to
   `loading` when known evidence says a row could have been revoked. One
   lost notification intermittently never starts the sync.

Separately, the classic Snapback server (LLP 0200) serves one subscription
per global-lock turn, on the blocking pool while holding the lock, and
re-runs non-delta queries in a fresh Hermes VM every turn. Twelve
subscriptions across two principals converge in tens of seconds. That is
diagnosed in §3.4 and handed to its own proposal (§4.5); it is not on this
RFC's critical path because no exact2 or Snapback 4 app uses it.

This RFC sets measurable targets (§2) and proposes four workstreams (§4)
that run as parallel tracks where their inputs allow (§6): a publication
contract so the server answers by audience (W1), precise re-evaluation and
a notification-to-sync liveness invariant on the client (W2), exact2's
adoption of the protocol, its iterator and its pin (W3), and streams that
cost no thread per idle client (W4). It records proposed rulings on the
open questions (§7) and the obligations it deliberately leaves to later
work (§8).

## 1. Why now

- Harmony, the exact2 chat demo, re-reads on a three-second timer. A
  message, typing mark or reaction from one person reaches the others in
  up to three seconds plus a full re-read. The demo target is about 200 ms
  on web and iOS. (Harmony's source is not in the exact2 checkout read for
  this RFC; its timer is reported by its builder. The farm census below is
  the durable evidence.)
- The app farm's client arm shows this is the rule, not Harmony's choice:
  in LLP 3000.036 §6's census, nearly every client-arm app drove
  freshness from a timer, median five seconds, and none held the long
  poll, because a held answer stalls Exact's test settlement.
- The classic contention ticket shows the same shape on the other server:
  independent subscriptions degrade together, and the recommended
  workaround (fold everything into one relational query) pushes the cost
  onto every author.

### 3.1 exact2's device

`snapback4/ts/snapback.ts` offers `poll(wait = 20)`, one long poll shared
per partition. exact2's Rust client (`client/src/client.rs`, `changes`)
builds the poll URL itself: `/changes?since=…&wait=…` plus `store_id`,
nothing else; its `changed` test returns true only for a durable head
move, a store replacement or a generation change, and ephemeral epochs are
not consulted. `snapback4/README.md` ("Live updates") says: do not hold a

### 3.2 Snapback 4's HTTP host (option A, as built)

- **One wake for the whole store.** `serve_changes.rs` parks a held poll
  on one condvar, `shared.polls`, and every durable publication calls
  `notify_all` (`serve_ephemeral.rs`, two sites). The wake predicate is
  the global `wake.seq != observed`, so any commit wakes any waiter.
- **Woken polls share an answer.** `Answers` in `serve_changes.rs` caches
  the checked answer by (cursor, `wake.seq`, generation, store identity)
  for one second; a commit costs one `check` read per distinct cursor, not
  one per held poll. Entry and unheld polls read for themselves. `check`
  has no viewer.
- **The body is global.** `check` computes four interval-derived fields
  without a viewer, from `since` to the head: `tables` (the union of
  touched tables, filtered only by schema visibility, saturating to every
  table past 500 commits), `preserved` (the tables whose complete interval
  footprint preserved every row, or null when evidence is missing or
  unknown; `revocation.rs`), and `changes.writes` (per visible table, null
  for population or deletion changes, else the changed columns, from
  `settlement_changes`; LLP 3000.023 §11 certifies the whole interval
  including server-only inputs through it). None is filtered by what the
  poller may read. A sleeping bystander learns those names at its
  deadline.
- **Two threads per held wait.** `tiny_http` runs one OS thread per
  connection; a held wait adds a pool worker. Defaults in
  `host_limits.rs`: 1,024 viewer long waits per host, 16 held change polls
  per principal (refused at once with `E_STREAMS`), a separate pool of 32
  worker claim polls. This is LLP 3000.036 §6's option A, implemented.
- **The time-audience alarm is sampled per poll.** Each held poll reads
  the store's next `not before` opening when it parks and keeps that
  sampled deadline while asleep; it re-samples only when a wake returns it
  to the loop. A mutation that inserts a not-yet-open row publishes no
  partition event for it (unopened rows are excluded from publication) but
  updates the private opening ledger. Today the store-wide wake makes
  every poll re-sample; the opening is advanced by whichever poll's
  deadline fires, one due row per advance, after which the publication
  wakes everyone again and the next due row follows. Every `/changes`,
  `/sync`, `/q/` and `/m/` request also advances at most one due opening
  at entry.
- **Ephemeral rides the same poll.** Ephemeral interest is by table name
  (`ephTables=`), with epoch and incarnation rings; an ephemeral-only wake
  is paced to 100 ms. Durable wakes are not paced. Typing in one room wakes
  every waiter interested in the typing table.
- **Presence.** A held poll enters `connected[p]` for its duration and
  counts for a 35 s grace after it answers (LLP 3000.018). A commit that
  emits an effect to a principal with an unconfirmed presence dependency
  records that principal (`_effect_presence`); its next poll entry
  confirms presence and cancels the deferred push. Today that confirmation
  arrives within milliseconds because every commit wakes every poll; the
  2026-09-17 ticket names this as an obligation any filtered wake must
  keep.

The measured cost of the store-wide wake, from the 2026-09-17 ticket (on
0.2.27, before the shared answer cache and the 1,024 pool; a baseline on
the current host is T2's first step):

| condition | writer p50 | writer p95 | poll answers per 160 commits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| writer alone | 2.07 ms | 2.55 ms | 0 |
| 49 unrelated polls held | 4.00 ms | 7.18 ms | 6,439 |

### 3.3 Snapback 4's client

- **Re-evaluation is by table.** After a `/changes` answer the client
  syncs (device tier) and re-runs every watch whose recorded `tables`
  overlap the answer's (`rerunLocalTables` in `local-reads.ts`;
  `evidenceTouches` in `change-evidence.ts` for the online tier). A watch
  with no recorded tables always re-runs. Each watch also records
  `scopes` (table → audience-group prefixes, built in
  `serve_read_scopes.rs` from the audience columns the public query inputs
  bind, so a group may name fewer keys than the table's audience), used
  today only for scope-loss withdrawal (`overlapsScope`, which compares by
  prefix). An uncovered sync table is recorded as `[{}]`, which matches
  every group. The client's copy of the schema (`backend.ts`) carries each
  synced table's audience, so the audience's arity is known on the client.
- **Queue and priority.** `read-work.ts` runs at most eight reads in
  flight per client with an urgent lane. Online wake-triggered reads
  already call `prioritize`; local wake-triggered reads (`scheduleRead` in
  `local.ts`) do not. `notifyReady` coalesces ready notifications under
  pressure by 50 ms in a document. The client's poll (`changes.ts`) sends
  `since`, `wait`, `heartbeat`, `store_id` and ephemeral interest; nothing
  about durable interest.
- **Retention.** LLP 3000.023 §10 bounds every change-evidence
  replacement to a one-second episode (queue time excluded on the local
  tier, included on the online tier). At the deadline the answer withdraws
  to `loading` only when §12's revocation classifier, given **known**
  preservation evidence (`preserved`), finds a watch table it does not
  cover; absent or null evidence never starts a revocation withdrawal
  (`canRevoke` returns false, Integration 20). One-shot reads settle on
  `changes.writes` (§11). The LM report's empty-list reproduction (a
  history table with a `once` column) was the case the ON2 ruling closed
  on 2026-10-03: `once` columns now qualify. The remaining withdrawals are
  real possible-revocation cases and unknown dependencies, which is the
  correct conservative boundary. Bounded subscription inspection is
  implemented (`observation-work.ts`, `inspection.ts`) and promises no
  arguments, values or identities.
- **A lost notification.** `issues/20260930-…notification-sync-intermittent.md`:
  with a frozen clock and a committed `/changes` answer, the notification
  intermittently does not start the sync. The live loop is `waitForChange`
  then `network.once()`, and `once` can join a round already in flight
  that started before the notified head; `explicitSync` chains a follow-up
  round in that case, the live loop does not. Root cause is unresolved.

### 3.4 The classic Snapback scheduler

`spawn_live_fanout_backstop` in `crates/snapback-server/src/server.rs`
serves one subscription per global-lock turn, serially across every
connection, deferring up to `max_hold / 4` before each turn while a writer
is visible. The turn runs on `tokio::task::spawn_blocking` while holding
the instance lock. `run_pending_live_subscription_with_path` in
`crates/snapback-engine/src/local/engine.rs` either ships an op-log delta
(single-table HostPlan queries) or re-runs the query in a VM. The VM is
fresh per invocation: `crates/snapback-runtime/src/warm_vm.rs` is a
permanent no-op because current Ibex runtimes bind creation, evaluation,
inspection, value release and destruction to their creating OS thread,
and the blocking pool changes threads (LLP 0009, DK-4 retired
2026-07-27). From the retained contention run (two principals, six
subscriptions each, twelve in all; the ticket is shelved at
`issues/shelved/20260820-familiar-live-subscription-contention.md`):

| fact | twelve independent references | two relational references |
|---|---:|---:|
| post-settlement convergence | 18.9–33.9 s | 1.0–1.7 s |
| finished scheduler turns | 72 | 12 |
| cumulative lock-held turn time | 32.3 s | 4.5 s |
| overload conversions | 44 | 0 |

That is about 450 ms of lock-held evaluation per turn on trivial tables;
twelve such turns exceed the 5 s `max_hold`, and the resulting refresh
obligation costs what it replaces. Whether the 450 ms is VM creation or
query, authorization and publication work is not measured; LLP 0009
records a fresh VM at a few milliseconds, so the profile is the first
question (§4.5). The ticket's classification, "(b) documented composition
bound", is accurate to LLP 0200's text; whether it is the right product
posture is Q1.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 W1. A publication contract, and the server answers by audience

**The record.** Every durable publication carries a bounded **publication
record** beside `(seq, store_id, generation)`:

| field | content | source |
|---|---|---|
| `kind` | `attributed` or `global` | the write path (table below) |
| `partitions` | the sync partitions whose rows changed (owner principal, group, `public`), per table, including the principal partitions that received membership events (join, scope) for that table | `runner.events`, which already stamps `Event.partition` for every synced row change and logs join and scope events on the principal's own partition |
| `tables` | every visible table touched, including online-only tables (which log no partition events) and the content tables named by membership events, so the table a join or scope concerns is in the set even when the commit wrote only a membership row | `_commits.touched` as recorded today, united with the event tables |
| `presence` | principals named as unconfirmed presence dependencies by effects in this commit (`_effect_presence`) | `engine/effects.rs`, the same set Postgres already inserts in its tail |
| `openings` | whether this commit mutated the private opening ledger in any direction (insert, reschedule earlier or later, delete, rebuild) | `store/time_audience.rs` |

The record is internal routing state. What crosses the wire is the
per-viewer projection below; raw partition strings (`p:…`, `g:…`) never
do.

**Write-path coverage.** A path either attributes completely or publishes
`global`; an empty event list never means "irrelevant".

| path | record |
|---|---|
| mutations, actions, jobs, effects, seeds and loads | `attributed` from `runner.events` plus touched tables |
| time-audience openings, expiry sweeps | `attributed` (both append row events; openings are published by the host-owned alarm or the request-entry advance, below) |
| live-parent policy writes (LLP 3000.014.000) | `attributed`: child copies are ordinary row changes in the same commit, so child partitions are touched; sound because copy maintenance is atomic by that LLP's contract |
| online-only tables (no `sync`) | `attributed` with `tables` only; no partitions |
| a commit that inserts, reschedules or deletes a not-yet-open row | `attributed` for whatever else it touched, plus `openings` |
| effect-only or receipt-only commits with no row events | `attributed` with empty partitions and tables, plus `presence` |
| schema adoption (including column activation, which is event-free, and the opening-ledger rebuild it performs), generation change, store identity reset, dev reset, restore, saturation or log overflow, any path not in this table | `global`, plus `openings` when the ledger was rebuilt |
| ephemeral tables | not durable; the existing ephemeral change snapshot (table names, epoch, incarnation) is unchanged in this RFC |
| device `clear_rows` | a client boundary, not a host publication |

**The alarm becomes host-owned.** Held polls stop sampling the opening
ledger. The host keeps one alarm whose armed deadline is always the
ledger's earliest pending opening (`next_time_audience`, the read each
poll makes today), re-read:

- after every durable publication whose record says `openings`, and
  after every `global` publication, whichever direction the earliest
  opening moved;
- after every fire of the alarm, including a fire that found nothing due
  (a rescheduled or deleted entry) and published nothing;
- at startup, and on every development clock jump (which already routes
  through a control request).

When it fires, the host advances time audiences exactly as a poll's
deadline does today: one due row per advance, published as an ordinary
`attributed` record that wakes the waiters whose partitions the opened
row belongs to. If the ledger's next opening is already due after that
advance (several rows due at one instant, or a stale entry followed by a
live one), the alarm re-arms with zero delay and advances again until the
next opening is in the future or the ledger is empty; a backlog after a
long clock jump is therefore published one commit per due row, as today,
with the alarm yielding the engine between advances so request serving is
never starved (the pacing is implementation work). If the re-read
deadline is already past when the alarm is armed, it fires immediately.
The request-entry advance (every `/changes`, `/sync`, `/q/` and `/m/`
advances at most one due opening before it is served, as today) stays as
it is; the alarm is what covers a held wait when no request arrives and
what exhausts a backlog. The `openings` bit is set where the ledger is
mutated (`store/time_audience.rs`), not at each caller, so a ledger
mutation added later cannot forget it. Which thread owns the alarm is
implementation work; that openings are published on time by the host
rather than sampled by polls is the decision, and §6 states it as the
refusal condition.

**Interest.** A held poll registers interest once at entry, under the
same mutex that guards the wake predicate (no lost wake between
registration and the initial read):

- the viewer's own partition, always. Membership events (join, scope)
  are logged only on the affected principal's own partition, so a
  principal learns of its own join or removal there; everyone else in the
  group learns of the group's row changes on the group partition;
- the groups the engine currently places the viewer in, and `public`;
- the online-only tables the client declares with a new bounded `tables=`
  parameter (Q4). An omitted `tables=` means no online-only interest; the
  client re-issues the poll when its watch set changes, as it does for
  `ephTables=` today. A client that sends neither `tables=` nor the
  `interest=1` marker keeps today's wake-on-everything and the
  unprojected body;
- its ephemeral interest, unchanged (`ephTables=`).

**Wake rule.** A publication wakes a waiter when any disjunct holds:

1. `kind = global`;
2. a touched partition is in the waiter's interest (this includes a join
   or scope event on the waiter's own partition, and the opened rows the
   alarm publishes);
3. a touched table is in the waiter's declared online-only interest;
4. the waiter's principal is in `presence` (the `_effect_presence`
   obligation: a connected recipient of a row-less effect confirms within
   milliseconds, as today);
5. the ephemeral snapshot changed for the waiter's ephemeral interest
   (existing rule, existing 100 ms pacing);
6. the waiter's own deadline fired (existing rule).

The wake predicate becomes a per-waiter stamp ("my interest's publication
sequence advanced") instead of the global `wake.seq`, so a heartbeat
wake-up does not answer for an unrelated commit. Predicate inspection and
the condvar wait keep sharing one mutex, and the engine → hub → wake lock
order is unchanged. In this RFC the condvar itself stays store-wide
(`notify_all`): every waiter's thread still wakes to test its stamp and
goes back to sleep. Answers scale with audience; thread wake-ups still
scale with waiters. T2's writer-cost alert measures the residue, and
per-interest signaling is O7, after W4 decides what a parked waiter is.

**What stays correct without a wake.** Captured `read through` authority
is captured; parent writes do not change it. Presence-reading queries keep
LLP 3000.018's five-second client refresh; presence changes are not
commits. Revocation evidence, settlement changes and the saturation rule
are delivered to the woken viewer from the whole interval, because `check`
still computes them from `since` to the head; a waiter that is not woken
is one for which nothing it can read, and no opening it can see, has
changed, and its next answer covers the whole interval anyway.

**The body is projected per viewer.** `check` and the `Answers` cache are
unchanged in shape and keyed as today by (cursor, the store's publication
sequence at the wake, generation, store identity); the per-waiter stamp
replaces the wake predicate, not the cache key, and the global
publication sequence keeps counting for it. The cached union, now
carrying the interval's
per-table changed partitions beside its tables, preservation evidence and
settlement changes, is computed once per cursor without a viewer. Every
interval-derived field is then **projected per viewer after the cache**,
using the identity the poll resolved at entry. The viewer's **interest
tables** are the synced tables it currently holds partitions of (own,
current groups, public) plus its declared online-only tables; T8's
accepted residue and the wake rule use the same current-holding sense.

- `tables`: the union intersected with the interest tables.
- `partitions`: for each remaining table, the audience groups the viewer
  holds whose partitions changed, plus a per-table `own` bit set when the
  viewer's own partition changed for that table (a row the viewer owns,
  or a join or scope event for the viewer), plus `*` when the interval is
  saturated, coalesced past retention, or includes a `global`
  publication.
- `interest`: the viewer's own interest tables (bounded like `tables=`;
  `*` past the bound). It is the viewer's own information and discloses
  nothing. Three states: a list (a recorded table is outside the
  interest iff absent from it; the empty list means every recorded table
  is outside), `*` (the client **cannot classify** any recorded table and
  treats the whole body's evidence as unknown: the unknown-evidence row of
  the truth table, every watch re-runs, every one-shot re-settles, no
  revocation withdrawal; never "everything is in interest", because the
  evidence fields stay projected to the real interest and a hidden
  dependency's write would otherwise vanish from settlement), and absent
  (an unprojected body). Under `*` or a missing `others` bit the client
  substitutes null for `preserved` and `changes` **before** calling the
  existing classifier and interval functions, which otherwise read a
  stripped name as unpreserved and an empty `writes` map as "no writes".
- `preserved`: the preserved list intersected with the interest tables
  (the §12 classifier walks every visible table against the interval's
  footprint, written or not, so this lists every preserved interest
  table), plus one aggregate bit, `preserved.others`, true only when
  **every visible table outside the interest is in the unprojected
  preserved list**. It is the classifier's outcome for the outside set,
  not a write-set test: an outside table whose hidden dependency was
  written drops out of the preserved list and clears the bit. Null stays
  null (unknown evidence certifies nothing, as today).
- `changes.writes`: the per-table entries for interest tables, plus one
  aggregate bit, `changes.others`, true when any table outside the
  interest had writes in the interval. Null stays null.

The client classifies a recorded table exactly: it is outside the interest
iff it is absent from the answer's `interest` list. The rule for the two
aggregate bits is conservative, by this truth table:

| recorded table `t` | preservation | settlement | W2a |
|---|---|---|---|
| in interest, in projected `preserved` | preserved | per-table `writes` entry as today | may skip, subject to the `partitions` test |
| in interest, not in projected `preserved` | not preserved: the classifier bounds the replacement as today | as today | re-runs |
| outside interest, `preserved.others = true` | preserved | `changes.others = false`: no outside write; `true`: possibly a write affecting it, re-settle | re-runs (W2a never skips a watch that recorded an outside-interest table; the bits decide preservation and settlement only) |
| outside interest, `preserved.others = false` | not preserved: bounded replacement | re-settle | re-runs |
| `preserved` null; or `interest` is `*`; or a projected body (one carrying `interest`) lacks an `others` bit; or a projected body's `changes` is null | unknown: no revocation withdrawal (Integration 20) | unknown to the query cuts: re-settle, with no fallback to the projected `tables` as a write set | re-runs |
| body without `interest` (an unprojected answer, old server or old client) | today's rules on the unprojected fields | today's rules | today's table overlap |

Degenerate classifier output is conservative by construction: a schema
past the classifier's table limit yields an empty preserved list, so
every in-interest table reads as not preserved and `preserved.others` is
false.

LLP 3000.023 §11 and §12 therefore still certify the whole interval,
including server-only inputs; what changes is that the names of tables the
viewer cannot read no longer travel. The projection is bound to the
interest the poll registered at entry; a client whose watch set changes
re-issues the poll (as for `ephTables=`), and an answer to the old poll
certifies only the old interest. A client that did not send `interest=1`
receives the unprojected body. T8 inspects all four fields.

**Multi-process.** The registry and the alarm are per process. The
publication record is the payload the cross-process commit feed of LLP
3000.009 §7.1 will carry; that feed, its durable replay on reconnect, its
startup race and the election or sharing of the alarm are an obligation
(§8), and this RFC qualifies one process.

Touches: `serve.rs` (`Shared.wake`, the waiter registry, the host-owned
alarm replacing the per-poll `next_time_audience` sampling, the
request-entry advance unchanged), `serve_changes.rs` (interest at entry,
per-waiter predicate, per-viewer projection of the whole body after the
cache), `serve_ephemeral.rs` (both `notify_all` sites),
`engine/committer.rs` (`continue_durable_publication` carries the
record), `store/time_audience.rs` (the `openings` signal), and every
`record_commit` caller in the coverage table. The record, its coverage and
the alarm are core and go through the lead session (LLP 3000 §7,
decision 4).

### 4.2 W2. The client re-evaluates precisely and never loses a notification

**W2a. Dependency disjointness before re-run.** The `/changes` answer's
per-viewer `partitions` projection (§4.1) is the footprint. It covers the
whole interval from the answer's basis through the notified head and is
bound to store identity and generation, exactly like `tables`.

A watch may skip its re-run only when the answer is a projected body
whose evidence is known (an `interest` list, not `*`; `preserved`
non-null; the `others` bits present), every table it recorded is in the
`interest` list, and, for every table `t` it recorded:

- `t` is not in the answer's `tables` **and** `t` is in the projected
  `preserved` list (a non-null list; a table dropped by the classifier
  because a dependency it does not record was written is absent from
  `tables` too, and must re-run); or
- `t` is a synced table in the projected `preserved` list, the watch's
  `scopes[t]` is known and non-empty,
  every group in `scopes[t]` names as many keys as the table's audience
  (a full group, not a prefix; the arity comes from the client's schema
  copy, `backend.schema.tables[t].sync.audience`, and an arity the client
  cannot determine is unknown), the projection for `t` is not `*`, its
  `own` bit is clear, and no group in `scopes[t]` prefix-overlaps any
  group in the projection (the comparison `overlapsScope` already makes).

Everything else re-runs: a missing `scopes`, an empty `[{}]` scope, a
partial prefix group (fewer keys than the audience, the same class as
`[{}]`), an unknown arity, an opaque or native plan, an online-only table
(table overlap decides, as today), a `*` projection, a set `own` bit (so a
join or scope event for the viewer never skips a watch that recorded
`t`), a dependency outside the interest tables (the aggregate bits of
§4.1 decide), and every protected answer still displayed
(`protectedReads`). The rule is applied on both tiers, in
`rerunLocalTables`, `evidenceTouches` and the `local.ts` notification
path. It changes which watches re-run; it never changes what a re-run
does, so LLP 3000.023 §10 is untouched. The case it wins is the one §3.3
loses: a message in room A no longer re-runs room B's reads.

**W2b. Wake reads go first on both tiers.** Local wake-triggered reads
call `prioritize`, as online ones already do. No new ordering; §10's
fairness is unchanged.

**W2c. Per-watch preservation: an obligation, not a workstream.** r0
proposed a footprint-based certificate past the one-second episode. The
motivating case (`once` columns) is already closed by the ON2 ruling, and
a partition footprint cannot certify server-only authorization
(`local-reads.ts` says so). W2a removes the re-runs that were competing
for the queue. If T3's measurement after W2a still shows `loading`
publications on reads whose dependencies were provably untouched, a
per-watch refinement is proposed inside LLP 3000.023 §12's classifier,
with unknown scopes never certifying and existing revocation episodes
never erased (§8, O3; Q2).

**W2d. A liveness invariant from notification to publication.** The poll
answer's head becomes a goal the sync loop must reach:

- the client records `wantedSeq` (bound to store identity and
  generation) **before** any fallible work on the answer;
- a round completes for live purposes only when the published watermark
  (device tier) or the accepted server-answer cut (online tier) reaches
  the goal;
- after every round completes and after every ownership release, the
  goal is rechecked; if unmet, another round is reserved (the chaining
  `explicitSync` already does, applied to the live loop), independently of
  any future notification, **at most three times per goal**; after that
  the unmet goal is surfaced through inspection and the loop waits for
  the next notification or its ordinary retry;
- a reset, generation or store-identity change clears the goal with the
  rest of the client's fences.

This repairs the coalescing race (`once()` joining a round that started
before the notified head). It is claimed as a fix for that race and as
observability for any other cause: a goal that stays unmet is reported
through the bounded inspection surface. Before the change lands, the
2026-09-30 fixture is extended with controlled interleavings (answer
arrives while a round is in flight; answer arrives between a round's
completion and ownership release; timer frozen) to reproduce the loss
or show the swallow is elsewhere in the poll loop's early returns.

**W2e. Inspection.** The bounded inspection adds, per client, the current
goal versus watermark as a boolean and age, and per query the last wake's
disposition (`skipped-disjoint`, `rerun`, `withdrawn`) with counts. No
partition names, groups, arguments, values or identities, honoring the
existing promise.

**Wire and API additions (declared).** `GET /changes` request, sent by
the Snapback 4 client's own poll in `changes.ts` from the release that
carries W2: `interest=1` and an optional bounded `tables=` (at most 256
names of at most 128 bytes, filled from the watched operations' online-
only tables the way `ephTables=` is filled today). `GET /changes` answer,
for a client that sent `interest=1`: the viewer's `interest` list
(bounded like `tables=`, `*` past the bound, never a prefix), the
projected `tables`, the optional `partitions` projection (bounded: at
most 256 entries of at most 128 bytes, mirroring §12's publication fence;
`*` on overflow), and the projected `preserved` and `changes` with their
`others` bits (carried beside `writes`, never as a key inside it, so
today's footprint parsing is untouched).
`ReadInspection`: the additive fields above. All additive; older clients
and servers interoperate with today's behavior.

Touches: `changes.ts`, `client.ts`, `local.ts`, `local-sync.ts`,
`local-network.ts`, `read-work.ts`, `local-reads.ts`, `change-evidence.ts`,
`query-settlement.ts`, `observation-work.ts`, `inspection.ts`. W2a needs
the server's per-viewer projection (the interval's changed partitions in
`check`'s union, projected in `serve_changes` after the cache; this needs
the poll's identity, which exists today, not W1's registry). W2b, W2d and
W2e need nothing from W1.

### 4.4 W4. Streams that cost no thread per idle client

Adopt LLP 3000.036 §6 as written. Option A is landed. Build the §6
measurement first (T6), run B (evented parking inside the vendored
`tiny_http`, which parks the connection thread) against C (a second async
listener behind the same TLS contract, which has none) under identical
admission, credentials, presence, backpressure and resource budgets, in
direct development serving and behind a proxy, then build the winner on
the thread, RSS, descriptor and latency rows. Ship `GET /changes?stream`
(`Accept: text/event-stream`) behind the wake-hint contract: each event
carries a **resumable cursor** composed of the durable head, store
identity, generation and, when the stream carries ephemeral interest, the
ephemeral epoch and incarnation; the client fetches `GET /changes?since`
for the interval (so the full projected body, revocation evidence and
settlement changes stay behind the hint); heartbeats between; the same
credentials, re-checked before each event and on expiry; the same
admission and `E_STREAMS` bounds; presence entered for the stream's life
and left on write failure, read-side close and idle timeout, keeping the
five-second presence refresh; replay gaps answered by the `since` fetch;
slow readers bounded by non-blocking writes with a write timeout. LLP
1110 D9 moves `Snapback.changes()` onto it with no Contract change.

W4 follows W1 because a stream per client multiplies the store-wide wake;
after W1 a stream answers only for its own interest.

### 4.5 W5. The classic scheduler moves to its own proposal

The classic server's remedy is a separate, security-sensitive proposal
under LLP 0200, opened only if Q1 is answered yes. Its shape, so that the
decision is informed:

- **Profile first.** Decompose the ~450 ms turn into VM creation and
  setup-source evaluation, query evaluation, authorization resolution and
  publication, on the retained fixture. LLP 0009 records a fresh VM at a
  few milliseconds; if creation is not the cost, VM reuse is not the fix.
- **W5a, an owner-thread actor.** Replace `spawn_blocking` for the live
  evaluation path with one dedicated OS thread (or one per module source,
  bounded) that creates, evaluates, inspects, releases, resets and
  destroys the runtime, on every path including timeout, reload and
  shutdown. Rebuild DK-4's obligations on that actor: baseline-restore
  reset with the full root set, invocation-epoch stale-continuation guard,
  fail-closed burn on unhandled rejection, pool generation invalidation,
  local-only posture. Query and mutation handlers stay fresh per
  invocation on the blocking pool.
- **W5b, off-lock evaluation,** only if W5a misses the 2 s bar: an
  explicit LLP 0200 §2.2 and §2.5 revision (a short-lived read snapshot
  at the cut, one evaluation per subscription in flight, delivery-side
  version gating, authority fence and shrink-obligation transfer before
  any publication).
- **The contention ticket** keeps its separate Wanted #4 diagnostic work;
  reclassification waits for the profile.

Four tracks run in parallel where their inputs allow; the only hard edges

| track | step | estimate | needs |

| server | W1 publication record, coverage, host-owned alarm, registry, per-viewer projection of the whole body | one to two weeks, core, lead session | nothing |
| client | W2b, W2d (with the reproduction first), W2e | days | nothing |

What could refuse this:

- **Presence.** `connected[p]` counts held polls (LLP 3000.018). W1 does
  not change when a poll enters or leaves presence, keeps the
  `_effect_presence` confirmation path by waking those principals, and
  leaves the five-second client refresh alone; W4 must keep all three, as
  LLP 3000.036 §6 states.
- **Openings.** With polls no longer sampling, `not before` rows open on
  time only if the host alarm is re-armed from the ledger after every
  publication that mutates the ledger in any direction (including the
  adoption rebuild), after every fire including a no-op fire, at startup
  and on clock jumps, fires immediately when the re-read deadline is past,
  and re-arms with zero delay while rows remain due; the §5 alarm writer
  is the regression case for each of those.
- **"No broadcast event stream"** (NOT-DOING). A per-viewer cursor stream
  woken by interest is not a broadcast; LLP 3000.036 §6 already draws
  that line.
- **LLP 3000.023's rules.** W2a, W2b, W2d and W2e are inside §10. The
  projected `preserved` and `changes` keep §11's and §12's whole-interval
  certification through the aggregate bits and the client's conservative
  rule. A per-watch certificate (O3) would be a §12 revision and waits
  for Q2 and T3's measurement.
- **One owner on the core** (LLP 3000 §7, decision 4). The publication
  record, its coverage and the alarm are core and go through the lead
  session.
- **LLP 0200's text.** Untouched by this RFC; W5 is its own proposal.

## Summary

The author observed on 2026-09-13 that a great many applications have an
algorithmic feed as a crucial part of their backend, that Convex and Supabase
address it with primitives only, and asked whether Snapback 4 could be a good
solution — and whether Interview, the Quora successor being built on Exact2
and Snapback 2, needs one.

The answer this document records: **yes for the serving half of a feed, no
for the model half, and the line between them is the design.** A feed's
delivery, sync, read state, freshness and bounds are exactly what Snapback 4's
two flips already make declarative — a per-reader partition, a shared scored
partition plus the viewer's own, maintains delivered as local facts, jobs and
schedules under ceilings, `check` naming the wide-merge cliff at compile time.
Candidate generation and learned scoring are not in the data plane and should
not be; they are an external ranker writing through mutations under a rule,
the same jobs-table-and-worker pattern Interview already runs for its model
answers.

The evidence is §5: Interview's feed-relevant schema written for Snapback 4
(twelve tables, ten operations), compiled clean by `snapback4 check` at 0.1.14
and run against a local store — a scheduled `rescore` job writing a shared
`ranked` partition from maintained vote sums and comment counts, a `home`
query reading one page of it with the viewer's own `seen` and vote beside each
row, and the order moving after new votes. §6 records what is missing, §8
proposes Interview as the second reference application LLP 2000 §4 asks for,
with a bounded spike, and §9 lists the decisions the author is asked to make.
Nothing here moves anything off `NOT-DOING.md`: ranked and vector search stay
after the clock.

## 2. What an algorithmic feed is

Every production feed has three stages, whatever the product calls them.

1. **Candidate generation.** Which items are eligible for this reader: the
   accounts they follow, the groups they belong to, everything public in a
   window, or a model's guess over the whole corpus.
2. **Scoring.** An order over the candidates: recency, engagement counts,
   affinity to the reader, a learned model, or a hand-written combination.
3. **Serving.** Getting the ordered items to the reader's device and keeping
   them there: delivery, pagination, dedupe, read state, freshness, revocation,
   and the bounds that keep one reader from costing a corpus scan.

Three shapes cover most products. A **chronological following feed** (early
Twitter, Instagram) is candidates from the follow graph, scored by time,
served from a per-reader inbox written on the way in. A **ranked following
feed** (Facebook, Twitter "For You" at first) is the same inbox with a score
beside each entry. A **discovery feed** (TikTok, Quora's home) draws candidates
from the whole corpus by a model and ranks them per reader.

In every one of them the serving stage is the larger engineering surface, and
it is the one platform vendors leave to the application. Convex gives tables,
functions and cron; Supabase gives Postgres, RLS and edge functions. Both are
adequate primitives for fan-out and for a score column, and neither says a
word about which rows live on which device, how a reader's inbox stays bounded,
or what happens when a thousand-lane merge meets a real follow graph. The
Snapback 2 record shows the cost of leaving that to the app: LLP 1017.000 and
LLP 1018 spent a program on the following feed at the 50-lane merge cap, and
the Snapback 4 full-brief Clique run's worst number was the same feed — the
lane merged 1,000 follow lanes on read, 21,000 rows per page, 19.2× the
five-lane read against the brief's 3× (the record's O5).

## 3. What Snapback 4 already has, stage by stage

| Stage | What the substrate gives | Where it is proven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serving: the reader's feed on the reader's device | `sync to .readerId last N by index` on a per-reader table — the inbox is a partition, one watermark, read locally, `complete` inside the horizon; `unique` by reader and item for dedupe; a `seenAt` column for read state; `expire at` for freshness; the card's `fresh`, `pending`, `complete` words for every screen state | the `feeds` card; `crates/snapback4-sim/tests/clique.rs::a_feed_fans_out_on_write` (250 followers: 201 rows from the mutation, 251 after the job, none twice, a far follower's device holding exactly its own entry) |
| Serving: a shared candidate set on every device | `sync public last N by index` — the top N by a score index held by every device | §5 below |
| Serving: signals as local facts | `maintain … count/sum … by …` delivered into the partition as a value when it groups by the audience | the chat app's `messageCounts`; the Clique run's M7 backfilled count |
| Serving: the cliff, named at compile time | `N_WIDE_MERGE` on a merge whose lanes may exceed fifty, with the row count and the fan-out-on-write shape; the meter cutting the page past 8,192 examined rows | 0.1.8; the record's O5 row |
| Scoring: the score as a column | integer arithmetic, `divmod`, `a if c else b`, maintains as inputs, `upsert` into a scored table with an index on the score | §5 |
| Scoring: on a cadence, under ceilings | `job` with `chunk … first N by index` (one page per commit, resumable), `schedule … every 1m` and up, parked on refusal, `doctor` listing runs | 0.1.0; `tests/clique.rs` |
| Scoring: a fan-out bounded by the mutation and finished by the job | the card's 200 near followers in the mutation (1,024 rows written per mutation), the rest by `fanOut` five posts a chunk, `insert … if absent` making the pass idempotent, an account past the job's bound being the one lane its readers merge | the `feeds` card |
| Candidate generation beyond the graph | **nothing in the data plane, by decision**: no JavaScript in the data plane, no effects host until measured, ranked and vector search after the clock (`NOT-DOING.md`) | — |

The last row is the boundary. What lives outside it is an **external
ranker**: a process with a service principal that reads the corpus through
queries and writes candidates and scores through mutations, admitted by a
rule (`insert <- exists services[viewer]`), on whatever cadence and with
whatever model it likes. Interview runs exactly this pattern today for its
three model answers — a durable `aiJobs` table pumped by a Node worker — and
nothing about it is Snapback-specific.

## 4. Two shapes

**4.1 Per-reader fan-out (the `feeds` card).** One row per reader per item,
written by the mutation for the near followers and by a job for the rest,
synced to the reader, read as one local page. This is the shape for a follow
graph at scale, and it is already on the card, compiled and run. A score
column and a `byReaderScore` index turn it from chronological into ranked; a
re-score job over the reader's window keeps the order current.

**4.2 Shared candidates, device personalization.** For a community — a few
hundred to a few thousand members reading a common pool — the per-reader
table is waste. Score the pool once on the server into a public `ranked`
partition every device holds; sync the viewer's *own* signals (their votes,
what they have seen, a ranker's affinity for them) to the viewer; let the
device compose the two. The server does one job per cadence, not one per
reader; the device holds a few hundred rows; personalization is a sort over a
page in the host. Convex and Supabase can materialize a ranked table; what
they cannot express is that the ranked table and the viewer's signals *live on
the viewer's device with a watermark* and that a Home read is local.

Move from 4.2 to 4.1 when personalization needs data the viewer cannot hold
(other readers' private signals, a model's embedding), or when the candidate
pool exceeds what a public horizon should put on every device.

## 5. The Interview sketch, compiled and run

Interview today: Snapback 2 0.0.27 behind a Node adapter that owns sessions
and phone sign-in; a Home feed of `questions[] last 100 by byCreated`; the
adapter reads every vote and every answer and sorts in JavaScript, which its
own `rules/NOT-DOING.md` records as "reads current collections rather than a
maintained ranking index." The author's ruling of 2026-09-12 makes Home *posts
to read* and Prompts *conversations to contribute to*; Marc Bodnick's outline
(Interview LLP 0001) leaves "Feed / discovery" as "to discuss."

The feed-relevant subset of Interview, written for Snapback 4 in shape 4.2.
This is the text that compiled; it is a sketch of the authoring surface, not
Interview's schema.

```q
use identity
use personas alice, bob, marc, ranker

table people:
  principal: principal
  name: text 1..60
  bio: text <=240
  initials: text 1..4
  unique byPrincipal: principal
  immutable principal
  read <- allow
  insert <- .principal = viewer
  update <- .principal = viewer
  delete <- deny
  sync public last 2000 by byPrincipal

table services:                      -- the ranker's seat: a principal the adapter holds
  principal: principal
  unique byPrincipal: principal
  read <- deny
  insert <- deny
  update <- deny
  delete <- deny

table prompts:
  title: text 1..300
  details: text <=2000
  authorId: principal
  at: time
  by byTime: at, id
  immutable authorId, at
  read <- allow
  insert <- .authorId = viewer
  update <- .authorId = viewer
  delete <- deny
  sync public last 500 by byTime

table posts:
  promptId: prompts
  authorId: principal
  provider: text <=30 ?               -- null for a person; the model's name for a model
  summary: text <=1200
  body: text <=6000
  at: time
  by byTime: at, id
  by byPromptTime: promptId, at, id
  immutable promptId, authorId, at
  read <- allow
  insert <- .authorId = viewer
  update <- .authorId = viewer
  delete <- deny
  sync public last 2000 by byTime

table votes:
  postId: posts
  principal: principal
  direction: int
  unique byPostPrincipal: postId, principal
  by byPrincipal: principal, id
  read <- .principal = viewer
  insert <- .principal = viewer
  update <- .principal = viewer
  delete <- .principal = viewer
  sync to .principal last 1000 by byPrincipal

table comments:
  postId: posts
  authorId: principal
  body: text 1..1500
  at: time
  by byTime: at, id
  by byPostTime: postId, at, id
  immutable postId, authorId, at
  read <- allow
  insert <- .authorId = viewer
  update <- .authorId = viewer
  delete <- deny
  sync public last 2000 by byTime

maintain postScore: sum votes of direction by postId
  read <- allow

maintain commentCount: count comments by postId
  read <- allow

maintain postCount: count posts by promptId
  read <- allow

-- Layer 1: one shared candidate set, scored on the server by a job, on every device.
table ranked:
  postId: posts
  score: int
  at: time
  unique byPost: postId
  by byScore: score, id
  read <- allow
  insert <- deny
  update <- deny
  delete <- deny
  sync public last 500 by byScore

-- Layer 2: the viewer's own signals, on the viewer's device.
table seen:
  readerId: principal
  postId: posts
  at: time
  unique byReaderPost: readerId, postId
  by byReaderTime: readerId, at, id
  read <- .readerId = viewer
  insert <- .readerId = viewer
  update <- deny
  delete <- deny
  sync to .readerId last 1000 by byReaderTime

-- The external ranker's word, per reader: written by a service, read by the reader.
table affinity:
  readerId: principal
  postId: posts
  score: int
  at: time
  unique byReaderPost: readerId, postId
  by byReaderScore: readerId, score, id
  read <- .readerId = viewer
  insert <- exists services[viewer]
  update <- exists services[viewer]
  delete <- deny
  sync to .readerId last 200 by byReaderScore

job rescore():
  chunk q in posts first 100 by byId:
    (ageHours, rem) = divmod(now - q.at, 3600000)
    score = postScore[q.id] * 10 + commentCount[q.id] * 2 - ageHours
    upsert ranked[q.id] { postId: q.id, score, at: q.at }

schedule rescoreEvery every 5m: rescore

query home(c: cursor ?):
  return ranked[] after c last 50 by byScore { *, post: posts[.postId], seen: exists seen[viewer, .postId], mine: votes[.postId, viewer] }

query prompts(c: cursor ?):
  return prompts[] after c last 50 by byTime { *, posts: postCount[.id] }

query thread(promptId: prompts, c: cursor ?):
  return posts[promptId] after c first 30 by byPromptTime { *, score: postScore[.id], comments: commentCount[.id] }

mutation createPrompt(title: text 1..300, details: text <=2000):
  return insert prompts { title, details, authorId: viewer, at: now }

mutation writePost(promptId: prompts, summary: text <=1200, body: text <=6000, provider: text <=30 ?):
  require exists prompts[promptId] else PROMPT_NOT_FOUND
  return insert posts { promptId, authorId: viewer, provider, summary, body, at: now }

mutation vote(postId: posts, direction: int):
  require exists posts[postId] else POST_NOT_FOUND
  require direction = -1 or direction = 0 or direction = 1 else INVALID_VOTE
  upsert votes[postId, viewer] { direction }

mutation comment(postId: posts, body: text 1..1500):
  require exists posts[postId] else POST_NOT_FOUND
  return insert comments { postId, authorId: viewer, body, at: now }

mutation markSeen(postId: posts):
  require exists posts[postId] else POST_NOT_FOUND
  return insert seen { readerId: viewer, postId, at: now } if absent

mutation setAffinity(readerId: principal, postId: posts, score: int):
  require exists posts[postId] else POST_NOT_FOUND
  upsert affinity[readerId, postId] { score, at: now }
```

**The run** (binary `snapback4 0.1.14` built from `origin/main` at
`292c42c42`, a scratch project, the local SQLite store, `snapback4 run` with
personas; the transcript is reproduced from the session, not summarized):

- `check`: `ok — 12 tables (10 synced), 10 operations, no problems, 1 note,
  12.9 ms`. The note is `N_NO_CURSOR` on the job's `chunk`, which is a
  misfire recorded in §6.
- `check --cost` for `home`: one scan of `ranked.byScore first 50` with the
  rule decided by the prefix and no probes, then three point reads per row
  (`votes.byPostPrincipal`, `posts.byId`, `seen.byReaderPost`), one rule
  check each. For `rescore`: one scan of `posts.byId first 100`, two maintain
  reads and one point read per row, an `upsert ranked` that "fans out to
  everyone."
- alice creates a prompt; bob, marc and alice (as a model, `provider:
  "Claude"`) each write a post; alice and bob upvote marc's, marc downvotes
  bob's, bob comments on the model's. Every write `sent`.
- `run rescore`: `{"state": "done", "chunks": 1, "rowsWritten": 3}`. The
  `ranked` rows: marc's post 20, the model's 2, bob's −10 — two votes × 10,
  one comment × 2, one downvote × 10, all at age 0 hours.
- `run home --as bob`: `complete: true`; the page in score order — marc's
  post (score 20, `seen: false`, `mine: 1`, bob's own upvote), the model's
  (2), bob's (−10, `mine: null`). Meters: 12 rows examined, 0 probes, 69
  steps, 1,444 output bytes.
- `run markSeen` on marc's post, then `home` again: the same order, `seen:
  true` on that row alone.
- alice, bob and marc upvote bob's post; `run rescore` again writes 3 rows;
  `home` now leads with bob's post at 30, then marc's at 20, then the model's
  at 2. The order moved on the cadence, as designed.
- `run thread` for the prompt: the maintained score beside each post (3, 2,
  0) and the comment count (0, 0, 1).
- `run setAffinity --as ranker` with no `services` row: `failed`, `E_RULE`,
  "affinity's insert rule does not admit this viewer." The ranker's seat is a
  rule, not a bypass.
- `test --as bob`: `home` complete in 0.6 ms (12 examined, 69 steps), `prompts`
  0.1 ms, `thread` 0.2 ms, all far under the ceilings the line prints.

Three posts prove the shape, not the performance; the spike in §8 measures
`rescore` at a thousand and ten thousand posts.

What the device does with it: Home reads `home` from the replica, then sorts
the page in the host by the viewer's own facts — an `affinity` row lifts a
post, `seen` sinks it, the viewer's own posts and votes are known without a
request — and paginates on the cursor. That sort is application code over a
few hundred rows, and it is where personalization lives in shape 4.2.

## 6. What is missing, honestly

1. **Aggregates are `count` and `sum` only.** No `max`, no `latest`, no
   top-N, no expression index. A score is a column a job rewrites. The
   position here is to keep it: a job on a cadence is the honest shape for a
   time-decayed score, and it is what production feed systems do. A `max`
   maintain would be a post-clock addition made for an app that needs it.
2. **Time decay is a cadence, not an index.** Rules cannot see time and an
   index cannot hold a value that changes with the clock, so the order
   refreshes when the job runs. The one-minute schedule floor is fine for a
   feed. The spike should verify whether a `chunk` can take a computed
   prefix (a day bucket from `divmod(now, 86400000)`) so a re-score sweeps
   the candidate window rather than the corpus; the sketch sweeps `posts`
   whole.
3. **A maintain that groups by something other than its source's audience
   is not delivered.** `postScore` sums votes only their casters may read
   and groups by post, so it never reaches a device as a value; the job's
   materialization into `ranked` is what puts the score on the device. That
   is why layer 1 exists, and the `feeds` card should say so in one line.
4. **A maintain over private rows is disclosed by its own `read` rule.**
   `postScore` is `read <- allow` over per-principal votes. That is the tool
   working as intended — the sum is the disclosed fact, the rows are not —
   but the sketch leans on it and the partition review should see it. The
   maintain grammar's `readable` word exists for non-uniform disclosure.
5. **The external ranker has no seat in a deployed backend yet.** Personas
   are loopback-only; `serve` takes bearer tokens; a `services` row admits
   the ranker by rule, but nothing seeds that row in a release except `/seed`
   in `dev` and `test --fixture`. How a deployed backend admits a service
   principal is a small H0 question the spike must answer.
6. **Named upvotes, anonymous downvotes.** Marc's outline wants upvoter
   names visible and downvoters hidden. The sketch keeps every vote private
   to its caster. The product rule is a read rule with a disjunct (`.principal
   = viewer or .direction = 1`), which `sync` refuses by decision (union
   audiences, `NOT-DOING.md`). The honest shapes are a separate public
   `upvotes` projection written by the `vote` mutation, or an online-only
   `voters` query. A real design question for the spike, not for this
   document.
7. **Two diagnostics found on the way**, filed as tickets (observations at 0.1.14):
   `N_NO_CURSOR` fires on `for x in t[..] first N` inside a mutation and on
   `chunk` in a job — the `feeds` card's own recipe compiles with two such
   notes and the sketch with one — and a reference column accepts an id that
   names no row (a `vote` on `postId: "null"` was stored, `sent`). The
   2026-09-13 corrections restrict the cursor note to returned query pages
   and document references as typed IDs without implicit existence checks.
   The sketch above now guards vote, comment, markSeen and setAffinity with
   `require exists posts[postId] else POST_NOT_FOUND`, as writePost already
   guarded its prompt. These corrections do not accept the proposed spike.

## 7. The claim

What Snapback 4 can say about feeds, on the evidence above: **it owns the
feed's state** — who sees what, in what order, on which device, with what
freshness, under what bounds — and the application owns the scoring function.
That is a coherent line against Convex and Supabase, which leave the serving
half to the app, and it is provable on the Clique instrument (O5) and on
Interview.

What it must not say: "recommendation engine," "ranked search," anything
that implies a model in the data plane. `NOT-DOING.md` keeps ranked and
vector search after the clock, and this document does not move them.

## 8. Interview as the second reference application

LLP 2000 §4 asks for a second reference application to test generality once
the first slice works, and names a hypothetical field-work app. Interview is
the better second app:

- It is real, has an author with product intent, and is Exact2-hosted, which
  the native module (LLP 3000 §7, 2026-09-13) already targets.
- Its feed is the crucial part of its backend and is unranked today; its
  product outline leaves discovery open; its adapter's sort-everything posture
  is exactly the community-scale shape 4.2 replaces.
- It is AI-native — three model posts per prompt — so an LLM-judged affinity
  ranker is a natural, cheap product move, and it exercises the external
  ranker seat (§6.5) as a real requirement.
- Its feed exercises the meter, partitions, maintains, jobs and schedules
  together, which no scenario in LLP 2000 §4's list does.

**The costs, stated.** Interview pins Snapback 2 0.0.27. Its adapter owns
sessions, invitation codes and Twilio phone sign-in; Snapback 4's identity is
guests and password accounts with bearer sessions, and personas are
loopback-only. The schema is a re-authoring in the same Quarry (§5 is most of
it); the adapter shrinks to the model-answer and ranker workers over a
service token; the Exact2 host calls the native module instead of the adapter
for reads. Phone sign-in either stays in the adapter as a token-minting front
or waits. None of this is a swap.

**The spike, bounded.**

1. The ranked variant lands on the `feeds` card and the README — shape 4.2
   beside 4.1, with the one-line rule of §6.3 — compiled and run in
   `tests/clique.rs` as the chronological recipe is, and published as 0.1.x.
2. Interview's schema on Snapback 4 in a branch of the Interview repository:
   Home ranked by layers 1 and 2, Prompts and thread as in §5, the adapter
   reduced to its two workers.
3. A device witness: Home opens from the replica with the server unreachable
   and shows the ranked page.
4. The numbers, recorded in the journal: `home` meters; `rescore` wall time
   and rows written at 1,000 and 10,000 posts; client bytes unchanged.
5. The §6 questions answered in the journal: the computed chunk prefix, the
   service seat in a release, the named-upvotes shape.

**The bar.** Interview's Home on Snapback 4 with a ranked feed and the app's
own tests green, in at most two sessions; no ceiling raised; no `NOT-DOING.md`
move. If the spike misses, the record says which of §6's items blocked it.

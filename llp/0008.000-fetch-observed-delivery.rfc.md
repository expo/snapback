## Summary

Design the smallest server-observed fact that can honestly say a row was
served to a principal's query. This is a delivery fact, not a read fact: the
row may have reached a cache that no person opened. Human-visible "read"
remains the client-ack pattern already paved in
`guide/recipes/read-receipts.md`.

## Motivation

The server already witnesses which authorized rows it supplies, but Snapback
does not expose that witness. Applications therefore ask the client to echo a
delivery acknowledgement or incorrectly mark a recipient delivered from the
sender's mutation. Neither represents the stronger fact the server actually
knows. LLP 0317 O43 records the repeated correction loop and the 2026-08-28
author ruling to split delivered from read.

## Questions the design must settle

1. Which serving cuts count: initial replacement, live delta, poll response,
   resumed materialization, or another closed set?
2. Is identity per `(principal, row)`, per device/client store, or another
   bounded key, and how are multi-device observations combined?
3. Is the fact durable, monotone, expiring, or derived? What retention and
   write-amplification ceilings apply, and what exact refusal occurs at them?
4. How do authorization loss, redaction, row deletion, and replay affect an
   earlier delivery fact?
5. What authoring and client surface exposes the fact in both Explicit and
   Familiar profiles without turning reads into durable client mutations?
6. What protocol/versioning addition is required, if any, and how does an old
   client behave?

## Required acceptance evidence

An acceptable design names exact producers, storage/custody, consumers,
limits, compatibility behavior, and diagnostics, then requires executable
witnesses for:

- initial, delta, reconnect/resume, and poll delivery cuts;
- two devices for one principal and two principals for one row;
- authorization loss and deletion without fabricating human read;
- bounded retention and fail-closed saturation;
- Explicit/Familiar generated-surface parity; and
- a published-package messaging journey distinguishing `delivered` from
  client-acked `read`.

## Non-goals

- Inferring that the user saw, opened, or understood content.
- Replacing the client-ack read recipe.
- Treating connectedness or `hasFreshClient` as delivery.
- Selecting a wire or storage design in this commission.

## Open questions

All questions in §Questions the design must settle remain open. Review should
reject any proposal that makes per-row read traffic an unbounded hidden write
or that collapses delivery back into human read.

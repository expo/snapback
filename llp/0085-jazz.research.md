# LLP 0085: Jazz — the counterpoint that conceded

**Type:** Research
**Status:** Draft
**Systems:** Sync, Auth, Storage, Realtime, Competitive analysis
**Author:** Charlie Cheever / Claude
**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Related:** LLP 0000, LLP 0001, LLP 0008, LLP 0011, LLP 0013, LLP 0022, LLP 0037, LLP 0053, LLP 0082, LLP 0084, https://jazz.tools/blog/what-we-learned-from-classic-jazz, https://github.com/garden-co/jazz

## Summary

Jazz (jazz.tools, Garden Computing, MIT) earned this pass as the sharpest
counterpoint to Snapback's server-authoritative model: a local-first
framework where sync, storage, permissions, and auth all *fall out of the
data structure* — CRDT CoValues owned by cryptographic Groups, write
permission enforced by signature verification, read permission by
encryption, with the server an untrusted, unprivileged peer. The pass was
commissioned to steelman that bet.

**The steelman resolved itself: Jazz abandoned the bet.** On 2026-04-18,
founder Anselm Eickhoff published a three-post retrospective announcing
Jazz v2 — a ground-up rewrite (Rust engine, relational model, RocksDB
server storage) that retreats from both pillars in his own words:
"Permissions moved away from cryptographic enforcement around the whole
system toward a trusted server applying richer policies at sync time," and
"Concurrent history moved away from CRDT replay and toward a more git-like
snapshot model." The retrospective's diagnosis: adopters valued the
*experience*, not the cryptography; permission mistakes were "hard to
evolve once baked into the system"; and every serious team needed "at least
a semi-trusted authority somewhere."

This is the second first-party retreat of its kind on record — ElectricSQL
walked back generic CRDT sync in 2024
([LLP 0022](./0022-electric-powersync-turso.research.md)); Jazz walked
back cryptographic-structural permissions in 2026. And the destination is
striking: **Jazz v2 is architecturally convergent with Snapback** —
declared relational schema, server-enforced row-level policies as queries,
a server-side live query graph pushing deltas to subscribed clients,
field-level last-write-wins. The differences that remain (embedded local
replica with offline writes, git-like branching and deep row history,
lens-based migrations, no server functions, no ephemeral presence) are now
the interesting part.

## Findings

### Classic Jazz — the pure form of the bet

**The model.** Applications are graphs of **CoValues** — CoMap, CoList,
CoFeed (per-session append-only feeds), FileStream — declared with a
Zod-hybrid schema and owned by **Groups**. Under the hood (cojson):
per-account sessions are append-only logs; every mutation is a signed
transaction (Ed25519 over BLAKE3 hash chains), encrypted with the group's
current symmetric read key (XSalsa20); current state is derived by CRDT
replay of full edit history. Create/edit/share works entirely client-side
and offline; the sync server stores and forwards ciphertext it cannot read.
[confirmed — classic.jazz.tools/docs/react/core-concepts/covalues/overview,
/reference/encryption]

**Permissions as cryptography.** Roles (`admin`, `manager`, `writer`,
`writeOnly`, `reader`) are enforced without any trusted server: write
permission is signature verification (unauthorized transactions are
dropped by every peer), read permission is key possession (admins reveal
the read key to readers-and-up). Revocation is key rotation — with the
honest limitation stated in their own docs: "removed members can't read
new data, but existing data they already had access to remains readable to
them." Invites are secret links, and per their docs, "Invites do not
expire and cannot be revoked." [confirmed — /reference/encryption,
/permissions-and-sharing/sharing]

**Server logic without server authority.** Classic's doctrine: "server
code doesn't have any special or privileged access to the user data." A
"Server Worker" is just another account the client must explicitly share
CoValues with; single-instance recommended; authoritative flows (payments,
bookings) route through it by convention, not by architecture. [confirmed —
/server-side/setup]

**No queries.** There is no query, filter, or aggregation layer — you load
by ID and traverse references, bounding depth with resolve queries;
filtering is application-side; there are no secondary indexes. Presence has
no ephemeral primitive: the recommended mechanism is CoFeeds — meaning
**every cursor twitch persists into permanent, synced, encrypted history**.
Deletion is tombstones ("total erasure... coming soon," 2024); the docs are
silent on history GC in both generations. [confirmed —
/core-concepts/subscription-and-loading, /core-concepts/covalues/cofeeds;
HN 41748912]

**The critiques that were live before the pivot.** From the 500-point
2024-10-05 HN thread: a concurrent-admin revocation race (two partitions
each rotating keys known to a removed member — unanswered in-thread);
membership transparency (you cannot hide a group's member list from its
members); per-field permissions requiring CoValue splitting; and the
founder's own admission that validation was weak ("we basically rely on
all clients having the same schema or backwards-compatible schemas").
[confirmed — HN 41748912]

### The retreat — in their own words

The 2026-04-18 trilogy ("What is Jazz?", "What we learned from classic
Jazz", "Four fresh ideas behind Jazz") is the definitive self-assessment.
[confirmed — jazz.tools/blog/*, all 2026-04-18]

- What adopters actually valued: instant local reads, sync for free,
  permissions *shaping what syncs*, deep history — "the experience," not
  the cryptography.
- What broke: "public-key complexity, the unusual permission APIs, or the
  fact that permission mistakes were hard to evolve once baked into the
  system"; granular reference-loading became "a leaky abstraction with
  lots of custom caveats"; teams needed "at least a semi-trusted authority
  somewhere" regardless.
- The mechanics of the retreat: the CoValues-era repo is renamed
  **classic-jazz** (2,536 stars, maintenance/security-patches only); the
  v2 repo is fresh (created 2026-01-04, ~63% Rust, alpha through July
  2026). `jazz-tools` npm history tells the version story: ~2.5 years of
  0.x, **never a 1.0**, then `2.0.0-alpha.*`. [confirmed — GitHub API,
  npm registry]

**[inferred]** The evidentiary weight is high precisely because it is
first-party and costly: a funded company with 2.5 years invested rewrote
from scratch rather than continue the crypto-structural model. Combined
with Electric's 2024 pivot (LLP 0022) and the corroborating pattern in
every other close peer ([LLP 0082](./0082-instantdb.research.md),
[LLP 0084](./0084-triplit-ditto.research.md)), the corpus can now cite
two independent, dated, first-party retreats from "the data structure is
the authority" — with the narrow exception Ditto defines (CRDTs where the
mesh is the product, LLP 0084).

### Jazz v2 — the convergent replacement

What v2 ships (alpha): declared relational schema; **server-enforced
policies authored in TypeScript as queries** (`allowRead`/`allowInsert`/
`allowUpdate`/`allowDelete` with `.where()`, inheritance through foreign
keys, `whereOld`/`whereNew` update guards) — "Clients that fail a policy
check have their writes rejected and their reads filtered," with a
compiled policy bundle also enforced locally; a query builder
(`where`/`orderBy`/`limit`/`include`/recursive) with **live subscriptions
fed by a server-side "live query graph"** pushing membership deltas;
three-tier topology (local embedded DB / edge / global) with per-operation
durability-tier options; **field-level last-writer-wins** over a git-like
per-row snapshot DAG that retains losing versions; first-class
branch/merge with per-type merge strategies; Cambria-style bidirectional
schema lenses for migrations; E2EE demoted to optional encrypted columns;
privileged backend contexts (`context.asBackend()`) — the thing classic
refused on principle. MIT; usage-priced cloud; llms.txt and an MCP docs
server. [confirmed — jazz.tools/docs/auth/permissions, /docs/reading/queries,
/docs/concepts/how-sync-works, /docs/concepts/local-first-data-model,
/blog/four-fresh-ideas-behind-jazz, /docs/getting-started/server-setup]

**[inferred]** Read against Snapback: schema, server-evaluated
filter-permissions feeding reads *and* writes, live query subscriptions
with delta push, LWW — that is Snapback's shape, arrived at by retreat
rather than by thesis. Where v2 still genuinely differs:

- **It keeps a full local replica with optimistic offline writes** —
  Snapback deliberately holds authorized query results, not a replica of
  record ([LLP 0008](./0008-local-first-sync-and-realtime.rfc.md)).
- **"Permissions must determine what is allowed to be present locally, not
  only what can be returned from a server roundtrip"** — their restatement
  of why RLS gets *harder* under local-first; the compiled
  client-side policy bundle is their answer, and a design worth watching
  against [LLP 0053](./0053-authorization-invalidation.rfc.md).
- **Branching, deep per-row history, and lens migrations** are real
  capabilities Snapback has no counterpart for — the surviving residue of
  the classic vision, now decoupled from CRDTs.
- **Still missing on their side:** server functions/actions/cron (backend
  contexts are DB-access modes, not a code-hosting story), any ephemeral
  presence primitive, and any history-GC/compaction story.

**Reception, honestly noted:** the classic launch drew 500 HN points; the
v2 announcement drew 4 points and zero comments. Funding exists but is
unquantified in primary sources (founder confirmed "as of recently I have
funding," 2024; investors named only by aggregators). Production users are
small-scale showcase apps. [confirmed — hn.algolia.com; HN 41748912;
observed — investor names]

## Synthesis — cross-cutting

1. **The strong form of the counterpoint is now empirically closed.**
   [inferred] "Auth/permissions/sync fall out of the data structure" has
   been tried in its purest form by a capable, funded team and withdrawn
   first-party. What survives of the idea — permissions shaping what syncs
   locally; history as a first-class database concern — is exactly the
   part compatible with server authority, and Snapback already holds the
   first ([LLP 0008](./0008-local-first-sync-and-realtime.rfc.md) +
   [LLP 0011](./0011-auth-and-permissions.rfc.md)); the second is a
   genuine open idea to weigh.
2. **Cryptographic revocation semantics are a warning label, not just a
   limitation.** [inferred] Forward-only revocation, unrevocable invites,
   member-list transparency, and the unresolved concurrent-admin rotation
   race are all *structural* consequences of serverless enforcement — the
   same lesson as Ditto's `_id`-only permissions
   ([LLP 0084](./0084-triplit-ditto.research.md)) from the encryption side
   instead of the mesh side. Server authority is what buys revocation that
   means what users think it means
   ([LLP 0053](./0053-authorization-invalidation.rfc.md)).
3. **Ephemeral state keeps proving it must be a separate lane.** [inferred]
   Classic Jazz persisting presence into permanent encrypted CRDT history
   is the reductio of "one primitive for everything" —
   [LLP 0013](./0013-ephemeral-realtime-state.rfc.md)'s "ephemeral state is
   not faster durable data" rule, demonstrated by counterexample. Neither
   Jazz generation has an answer; Snapback and InstantDB
   ([LLP 0082](./0082-instantdb.research.md)) both do.
4. **A rewrite-shaped competitor is a weak competitor for years.**
   [inferred] Never-1.0 followed by a breaking full rewrite (npm history
   makes this citable fact, not spin) resets Jazz's ecosystem, and the v2
   launch's non-reception suggests the local-first audience hasn't
   followed. The *ideas* in v2 matter to Snapback more than the product
   currently does.

## What to learn — actionable, mapped to owning LLPs

**Adopt / strongly consider:**

- **Cite the retreat.** The 2026-04-18 trilogy joins Electric's pivot post
  as primary-source evidence for Snapback's server-authoritative
  foundation — quote it in competitive material and in
  [LLP 0008](./0008-local-first-sync-and-realtime.rfc.md)/
  [LLP 0011](./0011-auth-and-permissions.rfc.md) rationale sections.
- **Evaluate row-history and branching as product surface.** Deep
  per-record history with retained losing versions, and branch/merge for
  environments and drafts, survived Jazz's rewrite because users wanted
  them. Snapback's changeset machinery (LLP 0008) and PITR-style bookmarks
  ([LLP 0083](./0083-cloudflare-do-d1-prior-art.research.md)) are partial
  ingredients; whether to expose history/branching as API is a real
  product question worth an RFC-level look. →
  [LLP 0007](./0007-storage-portability.spec.md), LLP 0008.
- **Watch the compiled client-side policy bundle.** v2 enforcing the same
  policy queries locally (to gate what may exist locally) is a concrete
  mechanism for the local half of authorization-aware sync — compare
  against [LLP 0053](./0053-authorization-invalidation.rfc.md)'s
  server-directed model as v2 matures.
- **Look at Cambria-style lens migrations** as prior art for
  schema-evolution across deployed clients. →
  [LLP 0037](./0037-schema-source-of-truth.decision.md).

**Treat as a deliberate decision, not a default:**

- **Optional E2EE as encrypted columns** — v2's landing spot shows E2EE
  can be a *feature* on a server-authoritative base rather than a
  foundation. If hosted-privacy demand ever materializes, this, not the
  Evolu corner (LLP 0001 §Evolu), is the shape to study. →
  [LLP 0011](./0011-auth-and-permissions.rfc.md).

**Avoid / they hit this wall:**

- **Authorization semantics baked into data structures** — "hard to evolve
  once baked in" is the founder's own epitaph for it; permission systems
  need to be revisable without rewriting history. →
  [LLP 0011](./0011-auth-and-permissions.rfc.md).
- **Unbounded history as the state representation** with no GC story —
  both Jazz generations are silent on retention cost; LLP 0008's
  retention rule and bounded change-log stance already guard this; keep
  them. → [LLP 0008](./0008-local-first-sync-and-realtime.rfc.md).
- **Presence over a persistence primitive.** →
  [LLP 0013](./0013-ephemeral-realtime-state.rfc.md).

## Open questions

- Should Snapback expose record history/branching as product surface, or
  keep history internal to sync? (The one classic-Jazz idea users
  demonstrably loved that Snapback lacks.)
- Does v2's compiled local policy bundle handle revocation eviction
  correctly offline — and is there anything in its mechanism
  [LLP 0053](./0053-authorization-invalidation.rfc.md) should borrow or
  explicitly reject?
- Watch items: Jazz v2 leaving alpha; any funding announcement; whether
  the "durable streams / real-time queues" on the v2 homepage grow into an
  ephemeral-adjacent primitive.

## 0. Why

**One benchmark shape.** Snapback 4 has more than thirty recorded Clique runs:
calibrations and comparatives of the LLP 0201 social messenger. The series is a
good regression instrument, but it now drives most product decisions, and it
has one shape: conversations, feeds, memberships, media, expiry and
replication. A product tuned against one benchmark learns that benchmark.
Clique scores are N=1 narrative assessments. The 0.4.12 comparative (Astra:
Snapback 4 92, Convex 80, Supabase 72) is recorded in
`benchmarks/runs/2026-10-04-clique-cmp-0.4.12/README.md`.

**One contrary data point, unverified here.** Exact2's `QUEUE.md`
(2026-10-05) records an equipment-lending app built on Snapback 4, Convex and
Supabase through Exact2. All three polled and needed hand-written adapters. A
fuller local record of that run (a scored comparison at near parity) exists
only as uncommitted files in one checkout, and its diary number collides with
an existing diary. This plan therefore treats "Snapback 4's Clique lead does
not carry to other shapes through Exact2" as a **hypothesis to test**, not a
finding.

**The seam today.** Snapback 4 and Exact2 meet in four places:
- **Packages.** Exact2's README says TypeScript "can't import npm packages
  yet. `app.ts` imports only its own local files."
- **Data-module lifecycle.** A data module runs inside one answer, and Exact2
  rejects ambient clocks and timers there (`js/src/prelude.js`). A client
  that owns a socket, a refresh timer or storage has nowhere to live. Snapback
  4's current client uses timers (`client.ts`), so npm resolution alone would
  not admit it.
- **Streams.** `fetch` with `exactStream` is receive-only (`docs/reference.md`).
  It cannot speak a protocol that sends a subscribe frame.
- **An existing adapter.** `exact-snapback4` is a Rust `DataSource` named
  `snapback4` over `snapback4_device::ffi`. It is used by the legacy Messages
  app on Apple, Linux and the browser. It is pinned to Snapback 4 **0.2.30**
  through a private-source dependency, the README does not mention it, and
  `exact new` does not wire it.

So a builder on Exact2 today hand-writes HTTP adapters and polls. Snapback 4's
long-poll presence (`/changes`), live queries, replica and offline client go
unexercised.

**Charlie's ruling (2026-10-07):** optimize for **Exact2** as the surface apps
are built on. This plan measures Snapback 4 *as an Exact2 backend* across many
application shapes. It produces a ranked, evidence-backed list of what to build
next in Snapback 4, in Exact2 and at the seam.

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals:**
1. Find what is good, bad or missing when building on Exact2 + Snapback 4,
   across a deliberately diverse corpus whose stresses are actually exercised.
2. Attribute each issue to an owner, with stated confidence: Snapback 4,
   Exact2, the seam, the builder model, the brief, the environment or harness,
   or unknown.
3. Test whether an issue is Snapback 4–specific, using matched controls and
   targeted reproductions.
4. Turn findings into ranked work in both repositories.
5. Measure improvements on a holdout that never shaped them, under a fixed
   comparison condition.

**Non-goals:**
- A leaderboard or a claim-grade ranking.
- Replacing Clique: it becomes a lower-cadence regression series (§9).
- Native-device behavior at scale. Builds are web-first, with a stratified
  native subset (§4.4); the native instrument (LLP 0201.000) stays the device
  measure.
- Rediscovering the seam a hundred times. §6 measures it once, at small
  scale, then studies it directly.

## 2. The corpus

### 2.1 Taxonomy

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Data shape | records/CRUD, ledger/append-only, time series, graph/relations, documents/trees, files/media, geospatial, search |
| Concurrency | single-writer, multi-user editing, scarce-resource allocation, queues/workflows, counters/aggregates, realtime presence |
| Authority | single user, shared lists, multi-tenant orgs, roles/approvals, public plus private, delegated/service actors |
| Time | none, schedules/recurrence, deadlines/expiry, background jobs, digests/reports |
| Connectivity | always-online, intermittent, offline-first |
| Scale | tens, thousands, hundreds of thousands of rows per user or org |
| Audience | consumer, prosumer, internal tool, B2B SaaS, game/toy |

**Coverage:** every axis value appears in at least 3% of archetypes, and no
value exceeds 25% of its axis.

**Known absences:** capabilities listed in `snapback4/NOT-DOING.md`, such as
ranked or vector search, are tagged when an archetype needs them. They are
reported as known absences and kept out of the build-next ranking unless the
program finds them blocking a broad class of apps.

### 2.2 Generation, clustering and briefs

1. Generate about 2,000 two-line pitches with axis tags, using several
   generator models, prompted per taxonomy cell.
2. Deduplicate and cluster them into about 150–200 archetypes. Each archetype
   keeps one representative pitch; its alternates stay with it, and alternates
   are never split across development and holdout.
3. Write a **slice brief** per archetype:
   - a product paragraph and the data hints a user would give;
   - acceptance checks, in two kinds:
     - **UI checks** use Exact2's agent operations (`tree`, `screenshot`, `tap`,
       `type`, `state`, `layout`, `logs`, `clock`, `prefer`, `perf`, plus
       `fail fetch`) and Contract tests;
     - **Stress oracles** are run by the operator, never rendered by the
       builder's app. **Every stress tag maps to at least one concrete fixture
       and assertion:**

| Stress tag | Required oracle (operator-run) |
|---|---|
| Authority | a second, independently authenticated principal, plus a direct backend read, showing that the forbidden row or action is refused by the server, not merely hidden by the client |
| Scarce allocation or concurrency | two independent clients, submissions released together, then a fresh backend read showing exactly one winner |
| Time, deadlines or jobs | the backend's own clock advanced (Snapback 4's dev clock and each control's equivalent), with the outcome observed while the UI is closed. Exact2's `clock` alone does not advance the backend |
| Connectivity | `fail fetch` or backend stop/restart, followed by recovery checks, including restart while offline where the archetype is offline-first |
| Realtime | two viewers, a write by one, convergence on the other |
| Scale | operator-seeded populations (the builder does not load 100k rows inside a slice), with a timing or meter check |
| Media | operator-seeded files, upload and fetch through the app |

**Oracle validity:** each oracle comes with a deliberately broken reference
implementation that the oracle must catch. An example is a booking that checks
availability in the client before inserting.

**Unreached stresses:** a stress the build never reached, because the slice
ended or the feature is missing, is recorded as **blocked**, not as "no
friction".

Briefs say what to build, never how; they name no backend APIs. Briefs, checks,
oracles, fixtures and tags are frozen under a manifest hash.

### 2.3 Holdout, under custody

- Split by archetype, 80% development and 20% holdout, stratified across axes.
  The split uses a **committed random seed**.
- The holdout is held by a **custodian** (an operator script and storage
  location named in phase 0). Builders, the extractor, synthesis and anyone
  writing phase-3 changes see only the development manifest.
- The holdout is unsealed once, in phase 4. A holdout batch exposed by any
  other use is retired to development.
- Phase 4 pre-registers one primary outcome: oracle-and-check pass rate per
  build. Friction count per build is secondary. Per-axis numbers are
  descriptive only; about 35 holdout archetypes cannot support per-axis
  claims.

## 3. Builders

**Two model families (Charlie, 2026-10-07):**
- **Muse Contributor**, a variant of Meta's Muse 1.3 Spark, run with the `muse`
  CLI;
- **GPT-6.1-sol at high reasoning effort**, run with the `codex` CLI.

Phase 0 pins both model IDs and probes each one on every host and account that
will run it. A model missing on a host fails preflight there.

**Assignment:**
- Each archetype gets one model, balanced across the taxonomy.
- An **overlap set** is built by both models. It has a minimum of 20
  archetypes in the pilot, stratified.
- Both models get the same prompt; only the CLI invocation differs.

**Session rules:**
- one cold build per session;
- published Snapback 4 at a pinned npm version, plus its served guide;
- Exact2 at a pinned `main` commit.

**Pre-provisioned lanes.** Following Exact2's fleet rules, the Exact2 checkout,
`target/`, Hermes, Bun and Chrome are warmed before a build starts.
Provisioning is outside the slice clock; it has its own clock.

**Slice budget:**
- 60 minutes of builder wall time;
- a non-cached-token cap set provisionally for the pilot (§7). Cache reads are
  recorded, not capped;
- stopping at the cap is an outcome.

The builder's exit note is structured: good, bad, missing, worked around.

## 4. Backends, surfaces and hosts

### 4.1 Primary arm

The primary arm is Exact2 + Snapback 4, built however the builder can manage
with the published packages. The integration cost is part of what is measured.

### 4.2 Matched controls

About 30% of development archetypes are **paired** with one control backend,
Convex or Supabase, assigned in balanced blocks. The paired subset is
stratified onto concurrency, connectivity and time. Each control build is
matched to its primary build on model, brief, budget, Exact2 pin, provisioning
and host class.
- **Specificity** is computed only on paired archetypes. Unpaired frequency
  is reported separately, with specificity marked *unknown*.
- A control difference is an **attribution hypothesis**. An issue is promoted
  to the top of the ranking only after a **targeted reproduction**: a minimal
  matched case that shows the difference directly.

### 4.3 Standalone reference

About 10% of archetypes are also built on Snapback 4 with its own client.
React/web changes the UI framework, starter and test workflow, so the
reference is reported as a **whole-stack difference**. It is not a measured
seam cost. Seam costs are counted only from separately evidenced integration
work: adapters, polling, missing lifecycle, lost presence.

### 4.4 Hosts and the native subset

- **Web builds** run on the Macs and eb1000.
- **The native subset**, about 10%, runs only on Macs, each on its dedicated
  simulator. It is stratified onto offline, replication, media and lifecycle
  archetypes. Each native brief carries host-sensitive checks for auth
  persistence, storage and outbox restart, reconnect, and media.
- **Coverage is not claimed** for every host Exact2 supports.
- **Charlie's laptop (the black M5) is excluded**, by his 2026-10-04
  instruction that it is overloaded.

## 5. Signals, extraction and synthesis

### 5.1 Per-build signals

| Signal | Source |
|---|---|
| Checks and oracles passed, and stresses reached or blocked | Operator execution: the driver, oracles and fixtures, never the builder |
| Builder wall time, non-cached and cached tokens, tool calls | Harness metering |
| Setup and verification clocks | Separate from the builder clock |
| Termination reason | Completed, cap, provider or usage failure, host failure, harness failure |
| Stuck points, documentation misses, workarounds, abandoned work | Transcript and source mining |
| Builder exit note | §3 |

### 5.2 Extraction and blind audit

**The extractor.** A pinned, versioned model pass emits issue records. Each
record has:
- a category and a description;
- an evidence pointer;
- a severity: a lost check or oracle, or a cost in minutes or tokens. *Unknown*
  is allowed; costs are never guessed;
- an owner: `snapback4`, `exact2`, `seam`, `model`, `brief`,
  `environment/harness`, `unknown`, or a set of owners;
- a confidence level.

Each issue is counted once per build.

**The blind audit.** For a stratified 10% of builds, an auditor from a
different family (**Grok**, which is independent of both builders and of the
extractor) does three things:
1. annotates the complete transcript and source *without* seeing the
   extractor's output;
2. reruns the operator checks;
3. reconciles its annotation with the extractor's records under a fixed
   matching rule: the same evidence location and the same category.

From this the program computes **precision and recall**, together with
agreement on category, severity and owner.

### 5.3 Synthesis

1. **Cluster** the issue records across builds. Clusters are adjudicated by
   rule, so a merge or split is decided by the record's root cause and
   evidence, not by its wording.
2. **Rank** clusters within each stress family, never by raw frequency alone.
   Each cluster carries:
   - **eligible** and **reached** denominators;
   - **severity**;
   - **specificity on paired archetypes, or *unknown***;
   - **model agreement** on the overlap set. Single-model issues are flagged.
3. **Promote** an issue to the top of the ranking only with a targeted
   reproduction (§4.2).
4. **Report** to the owners: tickets or LLP proposals in this repository; and
   for Exact2, issues in `ccheever/exact2`'s `issues/` under its issue policy,
   plus a `QUEUE.md` line. Seam issues name one coordinating owner and are
   cross-linked, never tracked live in both places.

## 6. Phases

**Phase 0: preflight.**
- Pin models, hosts, accounts and concurrency limits.
- Warm and provision the lanes.
- Build the harness: brief rendering, launch, metering, the operator runner for
  checks, oracles and fixtures, broken-reference validation of every oracle,
  evidence capture, off-host supervision, and the custodian.
- Commit the corpus generation and split seed.
- Do an end-to-end dry run on five archetypes.

**Phase 1a: a matched baseline and a seam spike, in parallel.**
- **Baseline:** about 12 varied development archetypes, each built by both
  models, with controls, under the frozen **baseline condition**: the model
  IDs, Snapback 4 and Exact2 pins, checks, oracles, extractor version and
  budgets.
- **Seam spike:** a bounded compatibility study of a supported, current,
  cross-host Snapback 4 integration for Exact2. It covers lifecycle,
  scheduling, authentication, subscriptions and durable writes, and whether
  `exact-snapback4` can be brought from 0.2.30 to current and published. The
  spike is design evidence; it ships nothing.

**Phase 1b: pilot.** About 100 development archetypes, still under the
baseline condition, rotated across the two models. They include the overlap
set, the paired controls and the standalone references (§7).

**Gate to phase 2: instrument validity, not issue count.**
- Extractor precision of at least 0.8 and recall of at least 0.6 on the blind
  audit.
- Oracles reached in at least 70% of eligible stresses.
- Model agreement measured on at least 20 overlap archetypes.
- Actionable evidence for at least the top ten issues: each carries a targeted
  reproduction or an explicit *unknown*.

**"Few new issues" is a legitimate result:** it stops the program. Pilot
repair is bounded to one protocol revision. A repeat pilot draws only on the
remaining development archetypes; if too few remain, the program stops and
reports.

**Phase 2: scale.**
- Build the remaining development archetypes, plus repeats, under the **same
  baseline condition**.
- Stop at saturation: adjudicated new clusters per 50 builds fall below a
  threshold set in phase 1b, and the top-20 ranking is stable across two
  successive batches.
- Builds made after any intervention (for example, a seam fix) form a
  separate, labeled arm and are never pooled with baseline builds.

**Phase 3: act.** The top issues become LLPs and tickets in both projects,
reviewed as usual, and ship through normal releases.

**Phase 4: holdout.**
- Freeze both comparison conditions completely: the baseline condition from
  phase 1, and the post-intervention condition, with the same checks, oracles,
  extractor, models and budgets.
- The custodian unseals the holdout. Each holdout archetype is built in both
  arms, in fresh sessions, in balanced order and on balanced hosts.
- Report the pre-registered primary outcome, then the secondary outcome, then
  descriptive per-axis numbers. This is the program's only improvement claim.

## 7. Budget and capacity

| Item (pilot, phase 1b) | Builds |
|---|---|
| Primary arm (development archetypes) | 100 |
| Overlap: the second model on stratified archetypes | 20 |
| Paired controls (about 30%) | 30 |
| Standalone references (about 10%) | 10 |
| **Pilot total** | **about 160 builds**, plus native executions, audits and retries |

Phase 1a adds about 36 builds: 12 archetypes × 2 models, plus about 12 controls.

**Clocks:** provisioning, the builder slice (capped at 60 minutes), operator
verification, and audit. Each is recorded separately.

**Tokens:** the pilot's non-cached cap per slice is set provisionally at 400k.
Full eight-milestone Clique builds used about 0.8–1.5M. Phase 0 revises the cap
from dry-run measurements.

**Capacity:** concurrency is capped per host (for example, two web builds on
the mini, two on the office M1 and four on eb1000, subject to disk) and per
model account. The calendar estimate is **conditional on throughput measured
in phase 0** and on available usage quota. It is stated as N builds per day
per host, not as a fixed date.

## 8. Operations and failure policy

- **Every attempt is preserved** with its termination reason (§5.1).
  Infrastructure outcomes (provider or usage limits, host crash, harness fault)
  are reported separately from product outcomes and never count as product
  failures.
- **Retries:** an infrastructure failure gets at most two retries, each in a
  fresh session. Product failures are never retried.
- **Selective reruns:** builds are never rerun because their result looked bad.
  Every rerun is listed.
- **Supervision runs off the build host,** with liveness and log-progress
  checks, not just exit files. Evidence is copied off-host periodically. The
  stated loss bound is the in-flight builds on a crashed host, plus anything
  since its last off-host copy (at most 15 minutes).
- **Shared hosts:** one dedicated simulator per Mac, the concurrency caps in
  §7, disk guards, and no deletion of other sessions' files.
- **Evidence:** structured records are committed. Transcripts and large
  artifacts are archived off-git, with committed indexes. Credentials stay in
  host-private configuration.

- **Clique** continues as a regression series, for example one calibration

- **The native instrument** (native.2) remains the device measure. Its Phase-2

- **This program** provides breadth and the holdout improvement claim.

## 10. Resolved questions

- **Q1 (seam first or baseline first).** Measure a small matched baseline
  first (phase 1a, about 12 archetypes), and in parallel run a bounded seam
  spike. Do not spend 100 builds rediscovering one blocker. Intervention builds
  stay a separate arm; the holdout judges the fix.
- **Q2 (auditor family).** Grok, because it is independent of Muse, of
  GPT-6.1-sol and of the extractor. Astra shares a family with GPT-6.1-sol.
  Blind annotation before reconciliation is required.
- **Q3 (native share).** 10%, stratified onto offline, storage and outbox
  restart, reconnect, media and lifecycle, with host-sensitive checks. No
  cross-host claim is made from it. Revisit after the pilot.
- **Q4 (where Exact2 issues go).** `ccheever/exact2`'s `issues/`, under its
  issue policy, plus a `QUEUE.md` line, cross-linked with one coordinating
  owner per seam issue.

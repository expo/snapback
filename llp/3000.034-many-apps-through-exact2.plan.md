# LLP 3000.034: Many apps through Exact2

**Type:** Plan
**Status:** Draft
**Systems:** Snapback 4 (published packages and guide), Exact2 (`github.com/ccheever/exact2`: Contract UI, TypeScript data modules, agent driver), Convex and Supabase as controls, the machine fleet, two builder model families, a friction-mining and synthesis pipeline
**Author:** Claude Opus 5.5 for Charlie Cheever
**Date:** 2026-10-07
**Revised:** 2026-10-07 (draft)
**Related:** [LLP 3000](./3000-snapback4.explainer.md), [LLP 0201](./0201-social-messenger-canonical-benchmark.plan.md) (Clique), [LLP 0201.000](./0201.000-cross-lane-native-app-protocol.rfc.md) (native instrument), [LLP 3000.032](./3000.032-client-contract-builder-costs.research.md) (calibration pre-registration), [Depot run](../benchmarks/runs/2026-10-04-depot-exact2/README.md), [Diary 0123](../diaries/0123-depot-exact2-three-backends.diary.md)

## 0. Why

Snapback 4 has been measured more than thirty times against one application:
Clique, the social messenger of LLP 0201. The series is valuable as a
regression instrument, but it now drives most product decisions, and it has
one shape: conversations, feeds, memberships, media, expiry and replication.
A product tuned against a single benchmark learns that benchmark.

The one exception so far points the same way. **Depot** (2026-10-04) was an
equipment-lending desk built through Exact2 on three backends: interval
exclusivity, FIFO waitlists, lending workflows, staff/tenant authority and
grouped time reports.
- All three backends reached 100/100 supervisor-verified fidelity.
- Builder experience was 72 (Snapback 4), 72 (Convex) and 68 (Supabase).
- Snapback 4's Clique lead (Astra 92 / 80 / 72) did not appear.

Diary 0123 gives the reason: Exact2's integration layer dominated. All three
builders wrote HTTP adapters by hand and polled, so Snapback 4's live queries,
replication and offline client were never exercised. Exact2's README states
the cause directly: *"TypeScript can't import npm packages yet. `app.ts`
imports only its own local files."* An Exact2 app cannot use the Snapback 4
SDK today.

**Charlie's ruling (2026-10-07):** optimize for **Exact2**, the framework in
`ccheever/exact2`, as the surface that apps are built on. This plan therefore
measures Snapback 4 *as an Exact2 backend*, across many application shapes
rather than one. The output is a ranked, evidence-backed list of what to build
next in Snapback 4, in Exact2 and at the seam between them.

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals:**
1. Find where building on Exact2 + Snapback 4 is good, bad or missing, across a
   broad, deliberately diverse corpus of application shapes.
2. Attribute each friction to its owner: Snapback 4, Exact2, the seam between
   them, the builder model, or the brief.
3. Separate "Snapback 4 is weak here" from "this app is hard" with competitor
   controls.
4. Turn the findings into ranked work: LLPs and tickets in both repositories.
5. Keep a sealed holdout, so improvements are measured on apps that did not
   shape them.

**Non-goals:**
- A leaderboard or a claim-grade statistical ranking.
- Replacing Clique. Clique continues as a regression series at a lower cadence
  (§9).
- Native-device behavior at scale. Builds are web-first; a small native subset
  runs on Macs (§4.4). The native instrument (LLP 0201.000) stays the device
  measure.
- Fixing anything before the baseline. Phase 1 measures the stack as it is
  today, including the npm-import gap.

## 2. The corpus

### 2.1 Taxonomy

Ideas are generated against explicit axes, so coverage is designed rather
than hoped for. Each axis value must appear in at least 3% of archetypes:

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Data shape | records/CRUD, ledger/append-only, time series, graph/relations, documents/trees, files/media, geospatial, search-heavy |
| Concurrency | single-writer, multi-user editing, scarce-resource allocation (booking, inventory), queues/workflows, counters/aggregates, realtime presence |
| Authority | single user, shared lists, multi-tenant orgs, roles/approvals, public plus private, delegated/service actors |
| Time | none, schedules/recurrence, deadlines/expiry, background jobs, digests/reports |
| Connectivity | always-online, intermittent, offline-first field use |
| Scale | tens, thousands, hundreds of thousands of rows per user/org |
| Audience | consumer, prosumer, internal tool, B2B SaaS, game/toy |

### 2.2 Generation and clustering

1. **Generate about 2,000 ideas** from several generator models, prompted per
   taxonomy cell. Each idea is a two-line product pitch plus its axis tags.
2. **Deduplicate and cluster** into about **150–200 archetypes**. Each
   archetype is a distinct combination of stresses; near-duplicate pitches
   collapse into one. Each archetype keeps one representative idea, chosen as
   the clearest pitch, and its alternates.
3. **Write a slice brief per archetype**, using a fixed template:
   - a product paragraph;
   - the data model hints a user would give;
   - **three to six acceptance checks**, executable with Exact2's agent
     operations (`tree`, `tap`, `type`, `state` and friends) and Contract
     tests, plus at least one check of backend authority and one of
     concurrency or time where the archetype has them;
   - a stress tag naming what the archetype is meant to exercise.

   Briefs say what to build, never how; they name no backend APIs.
4. **Freeze** the corpus: briefs, checks, tags and a manifest hash.

### 2.3 Development and holdout split

Split by archetype: **80% development, 20% holdout**, stratified so the holdout
covers every axis value. The holdout briefs are sealed: kept off disks the
builders can see, and never read by anyone working on product changes. They
are run only at measurement milestones (§6, phase 4).

## 3. Builders

**Two model families, for variety (Charlie, 2026-10-07):**
- **Muse Contributor**, a variant of Meta's Muse 1.3 Spark, through the `muse`
  CLI. The exact model ID is pinned in the phase-0 preflight.
- **GPT-6.1-sol at high reasoning effort**, through the `codex` CLI. A
  2026-10-07 probe succeeded on eb1000's account. The 2026-10-04 Depot run found
  it unsupported on another host's account, so the preflight checks every host
  that will run it.

**Assignment:**
- **Rotated, not crossed.** Each archetype in a phase gets one model, balanced
  across the taxonomy.
- **About 15% overlap.** Those archetypes are built by both models, to tell
  model idiosyncrasy from product friction.
- **The same prompt for both models,** except for the CLI invocation.

**Session rules:**
- Cold context, one build per session.
- Published packages only:
  - Snapback 4 at the current npm version, with its served guide;
  - Exact2 at a pinned commit of `main`, with its own setup and docs.

  The pins are recorded per phase.
- **Slice budget:** 60 minutes of wall time and a fixed non-cached-token cap,
  set from the pilot (the LLP 0201 D7 lesson: count non-cached tokens; record
  cache reads). Stopping at the cap is an outcome, not an error.
- The builder writes a short structured exit note: what was good, bad and
  missing, and what it worked around.

## 4. Backends, surfaces and hosts

### 4.1 Snapback 4 through Exact2: primary

Every archetype is built on Exact2 with Snapback 4 as the backend. The Exact2
data module talks to Snapback 4 however the builder can manage today: HTTP
adapters, polling or a local cache. **That integration cost is part of what
is measured.**

### 4.2 Controls

About 20% of development archetypes, stratified, are also built on Exact2
with Convex or Supabase, alternating. A friction that appears equally on a
control is an Exact2 or app-shape cost, not a Snapback 4 one.

### 4.3 Standalone Snapback 4 reference

A small subset (about 10%) is also built with Snapback 4 directly, using its
own React/web client. This measures what the Exact2 seam costs, and which
Snapback 4 strengths it currently hides (live queries, offline, replication).

### 4.4 Hosts

- **Web builds** (most of them) run on the Macs and on eb1000.
- **The native subset** (about 10%: macOS and the iOS Simulator through
  Exact2's agent driver) runs only on the Macs, on dedicated simulators.
- **The black M5 is excluded,** as on every program. Co-tenancy, disk and
  liveness rules follow §8.

## 5. Signals and synthesis

### 5.1 Per-build signals, collected automatically

| Signal | Source |
|---|---|
| Built? Checks passed (each, and in total)? | The Exact2 agent driver running the brief's checks, executed by the operator, not by the builder |
| Wall time, non-cached and cached tokens, tool calls | Harness metering |
| Stuck points | Transcript mining: repeated failing commands, error codes (Snapback 4 `E_*`, Exact2 diagnostics, compiler errors), correction loops |
| Documentation misses | Guide and README lookups that found nothing; searches repeated with new wording |
| Workarounds | Hand-written adapters, polling loops, client-side caches, re-implemented features, disabled checks |
| Abandoned features | Checks the builder skipped or declared out of scope |
| Builder exit note | §3 |

### 5.2 Friction extraction

A **friction extractor**, a model pass over each transcript and exit note,
emits structured records. Each record carries a category, a description, an
evidence pointer (transcript line, file or command), a severity (cost in
minutes or tokens, and whether a check was lost) and a provisional owner:
Snapback 4, Exact2, the seam, the model or the brief.
- The extractor is pinned and versioned, and its prompts are frozen per phase.
- Ten percent of builds also get a **deep review**: a separate-family model
  reruns the checks, reads the source and audits the extractor's records for
  that build. This measures extractor precision and recall, and catches
  claimed passes that do not hold up.

### 5.3 Synthesis

1. **Cluster** friction records across builds into issues: the same
   underlying gap, seen in many apps.
2. **Rank** each issue by frequency, severity and Snapback 4 specificity.
   Specificity is the rate on Snapback 4 compared with the controls on the
   same archetypes.
   - Weight model agreement on overlap archetypes.
   - Flag issues seen with only one model.
3. **Attribute** each issue to its owner, with the evidence. Seam issues are
   reported to both projects.
4. **Write the output:** a ranked report and, for the top issues, a filesystem
   ticket or LLP proposal in this repository or in `ccheever/exact2`. Each
   cites example builds.

The expected first headline is the seam itself. Without npm imports there is
no supported way for an Exact2 app to use Snapback 4's client. Phase 1 should
quantify that cost across many shapes, instead of inferring it from one.

## 6. Phases

**Phase 0: preflight, about half a day.**
- Pin the models: verify that Muse Contributor's ID and GPT-6.1-sol high run on
  every host that will use them.
- Pin Exact2 and Snapback 4.
- Build the harness: brief rendering, launch, metering, operator check
  execution, transcript capture, and liveness-based watchers (§8).
- Do a **dry run on five archetypes**, one per stress family, end to end
  through extraction.

**Phase 1: pilot, one to two days.** Generate and freeze the corpus. Then build
the following, rotated across both models:
- about **100 development archetypes** on Exact2 + Snapback 4;
- **20 controls**;
- **10 standalone references**.

Run extraction and deep review, then a first synthesis.

**Go/no-go for phase 2:**
- the pilot yields at least 25 distinct friction clusters with evidence;
- extractor precision is at least 80% on the deep-reviewed sample;
- on overlap archetypes, the two models agree on at least half of the
  high-severity clusters.

Otherwise, revise the slice template, the extractor or the budget and repeat
the pilot on fresh development archetypes.

**Phase 2: scale.** Build the rest of the development set, plus repeats so
every development archetype has at least two builds, about 300–400 builds.
Grow it to the thousands only if the cluster count keeps rising at the
margin. Stop when new builds stop producing new clusters (a saturation
curve). Synthesis runs at the end of phase 2.

**Phase 3: act.** The top issues become LLPs and tickets in both projects,
reviewed as usual. Fixes ship through normal releases.

**Phase 4: measure on the holdout.**
- Unseal the holdout and build it on the pinned *pre-intervention* stack and on
  the post-intervention stack, the same models and budgets for both.
- Report the before/after difference in check pass rate, friction counts and
  cost, by axis.

This is the only place this program claims improvement.

## 7. Budget

- **Per build** (60-minute slice): wall time is bounded by the slice. Tokens
  are set by the pilot. Recent full Clique builds used roughly 0.6–2M
  non-cached tokens over eight milestones, so a slice is expected to use well
  under that.
- **Pilot:** about 130 builds, plus extraction and deep reviews. That's a day or
  two across three or four hosts running several builds each, within the
  co-tenancy rules.
- **Capacity limits:**
  - Codex account usage: the eb1000 account hit its limit on 2026-10-06. The
    harness spreads GPT-6.1-sol work across accounts and backs off on usage
    errors.
  - Muse provider outages: the harness probes before relaunching.
  - eb1000's disk and stability: two hard crashes on 2026-10-06.

  The plan therefore runs at a sustainable rate, not a burst.

## 8. Operations

- **Liveness, not just exit files.** On 2026-10-06 two hosts' monitors missed
  processes killed by a crash, because they waited only for exit files. Every
  watcher here also checks process liveness and log progress.
- Every build checkpoints its state and evidence. A crash costs one build,
  never a phase.
- **Shared-host etiquette:**
  - one simulator per Mac, the dedicated one;
  - a bounded number of concurrent builds per host;
  - no deletion of other sessions' files;
  - disk guards on eb1000.
- **Evidence:** structured records are committed; transcripts and large
  artifacts are archived off-git with committed indexes (the same pattern as the
  native instrument).
- **Credentials** stay in host-private config and never enter briefs, records or
  logs.

## 9. Relationship to existing measurement

- **Clique** calibrations continue, but as a regression series (for example,
  one Astra run per release) rather than the main driver of direction.
- **The native instrument** (native.2, Phase 2 rerun in progress) stays the
  on-device measure for offline and restart behavior.
- **This program** supplies breadth, and its holdout supplies the improvement
  claim.

## 10. Open questions

- **Q1. Fix the seam before the baseline?** A supported Snapback 4 data source
  for Exact2 data modules, via npm imports or a first-class Exact2 binding, is
  likely the top lever. The leaning is to measure first (phase 1 as is), so
  the seam's cost is known across shapes and the fix is judged on the
  holdout. If the seam dominates the pilot as clearly as it did Depot, it can
  start in parallel with phase 2.
- **Q2. Which family deep-reviews?** Claude is the orchestrator's family.
  Grok or Astra is preferred for independence.
- **Q3. Native share.** Is 10% enough, given Exact2's cross-host promise?
  Revisit after the pilot.
- **Q4. Where do Exact2 tickets live?** The leaning is `ccheever/exact2`'s own
  `issues/`, cross-linked from this program's report.

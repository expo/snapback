## 1. Finding and proposed author ruling

The recurrent cost is composing truthful reads and writes into a screen.
The platform already supplies a replica, custody, existence classification,
retention and receipt machinery. Builders repeatedly assemble the boundaries
between them. This research recommends **R, a generated receipt binding and
seen-reporting hook, then B, a pending-intent presentation helper**, for
Charlie's ruling. Neither is authorized for implementation by this document.
The follow-history alternatives remain unselected.

Across the requested Astra #16–#29 sample, availability handling is cited in
13/14 builds, receipt or retained-history composition in 13/14, subscription
and interaction lifetimes in 11/14, and custody without prediction in 7/14.
The explicit 1,000-event follow-history limit occurs in 2/14, rather than in
every recent narrative. These are minimum **documented mentions of work or
constraints**, not a failure rate. Categories overlap; they must not be added.

The measured code also changes the prioritization. Cal29's text-send fallback
is only two compressed source lines (280 bytes); its author still reports it
as demanding. G47's adapted visible-receipt hook occupies 37 lines without
imports. Following's missing older history produces **zero workaround lines**
in Cal29: it is an accepted capability limit, not hidden client code to delete.
LOC alone would mis-rank both problems.

The lowest mean category in this sample is actually `scale_and_limits` (84.00),
followed by `client_integration` (85.57) and `queries_and_live` (86.71).
Client friction is persistent; the record does not establish that it caused
all of these scores, or that deleting a helper would buy a specified number
of points. Cal29 itself is above the series means for both client categories.

## 2. Evidence, scope and method

The [evidence ledger](../snapback4/reports/20261003-cx-client-contract/EVIDENCE.md)
contains verbatim quotations, source lines, the scorer's actual category
wording, independent grader counts and actual builder code. The
[measurement data](../snapback4/reports/20261003-cx-client-contract/measurements.json)
retains scores, coding decisions, file hashes, source spans and fitted values.
The [corpus inventory](../snapback4/reports/20261003-cx-client-contract/CORPUS.md)
covers every `benchmarks/runs/*snapback4-clique-*` directory, the differently
named Grok 0.4.7 comparative, and the external Cal29 bundle.

The primary series is 14 cold Astra builds, #16–#29: 0.2.32, 0.2.33,
0.3.0, 0.4.0–0.4.4, 0.4.6–0.4.11. There is no invented 0.4.5 run.
Cal29's diary, essay and score are retained as documentary copies beside the
ledger; its client source was located on the original M1 host and checked
against the bundle's matching narrative and schema hashes. Earlier Astra
#1–#15 are included separately and all 29 are fitted as a sensitivity check.
Pre-calibration/multimodel records have their own inventory and quotations;
their changed brief and scoring custody do not become extra Astra trials.

G47 is a Grok comparative score based on three backend lanes. G28 is a Grok
single-backend calibration. They corroborate costs but are not pooled with
Astra, or fitted as a two-point release trend. A two-observation line would
fit perfectly and explain nothing. Different hosts, application choices,
changing package versions, workload failures and narrative-only holistic
scoring prevent causal claims even within the Astra series. Hermes exports
are not native-runtime evidence. Grader assertions and holistic scores remain
different measurements.

Coding is narrow and auditable:

- **A — availability:** explicit work distinguishing acquisition, missing,
  partial, stale or withdrawn reads; not a generic spinner or slow build.
- **B — custody without prediction:** explicit lack of a predicted row or
  a nonpredicting write requiring UI treatment; not every pending write.
- **R — receipts/history:** receipt vocabulary/accounting or composition of
  retained content with current metadata. A quotation that only praises the
  feature is not counted.
- **F — follow history:** the newest 1,000 events per followed author, not
  1,000 conversations, an inbox acquisition floor, or asynchronous delivery.
- **L — lifetimes:** keeping interaction owners alive while authorized read
  observations withdraw, or controlling subscription ownership/pressure.

`—` means no qualifying citation was selected. It does not prove absence of
work. A broad R count combines manual receipt code on older versions with
mapping/composition on newer ones; it is not 13 complaints about the new
declarative receipt clause. Agent errors are retained as evidence of learning
work, never silently relabeled platform defects. One researcher coded this
sample; no inter-rater reliability or blinded experiment is claimed.

### 2.2 Fit the series

For category c and calibration i, fit ordinary least squares
`score(i,c) = mean(c) + slope(c) × (i − 22.5) + residual(i,c)` on all
14 primary observations with equal weight. Calibration number represents
successive trials, not equal time or equal implementation scope. The ledger
shows both Δ from the category mean and the residual from this fit. The model
is descriptive: no treatment variable, causal adjustment or point-per-cost
coefficient is estimated. Repeated citations in the same build do not add
observations. All 12 category means are retained in the data, avoiding
selection of only low categories.

| Category | #16–#29 mean | Slope / trial | R² | All #1–#29 mean | All-series slope |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `queries_and_live` | 86.71 | -0.233 | 0.127 | 85.07 | +0.149 |
| `client_integration` | 85.57 | -0.101 | 0.020 | 87.48 | -0.200 |
| `offline_sync` | 92.14 | -0.070 | 0.014 | 92.28 | +0.058 |
| `data_modeling` | 87.50 | +0.231 | 0.138 | 86.72 | +0.051 |
| `scale_and_limits` | 84.00 | -0.905 | 0.197 | 83.52 | -0.112 |
| `overall` | 89.36 | -0.068 | 0.021 | 89.07 | +0.041 |

The sign of the query slope reverses in the full-series sensitivity fit. This is a reason to avoid treating the recent negative slope as an effect of a particular release.

scale category's low mean and Cal28's 60 are not attributed to pending UI.

## 4. Five proposals, each with a zero-product-change option

§4.1, §4.2 and §4.3 are **implemented**, as recorded below; the other API sketches
remain unimplemented. Costs are engineering person-days
for one experienced implementer, including focused tests, docs and review
repair, excluding publication/fleet queues. They are planning estimates,
not timing measurements. No runtime/wasm budget increase is assumed.

### 4.1 A — presentation from the existing read contract

Add a small pure `readPresentation(read)` helper and one complete React/Expo
screen recipe. It derives display flags from `existence` plus the unchanged
metadata; it neither owns a subscription nor saves a result.

```tsx
const status = readPresentation(page.read); // implemented; see the record below
// { existence, pending, incomplete, stale, revalidating, why?, since? }
return <ScreenShell>
  <Results items={page.items} />
  <ReadNotice status={status} />
</ScreenShell>;
```

`incomplete` remains meaningful for nonempty `present` data. `stale` and
`revalidating` remain independent. No “safe to mutate” Boolean is inferred;
the current composer recipe only blocks on denied or fresh, non-revalidating
proven absence, and still relies on server authorization. Shell state belongs
to the account/resource key, not the read's loading arm.

**Expected reduction:** A1's two hand-selected widget predicates become one
adapter call, about 0–1 compressed physical lines; a shared notice may remove
2–4 repeated branches per screen. A2's three state/latch lines can leave the
screen when the stable-shell recipe is followed. The helper cannot remove
the product's choice of labels or make unknown data available. No app-wide
LOC extrapolation from one gate.

**Correctness risk:** low if purely derived; medium if the helper is used as
an authority gate. Keep .031's pending/cut/continuation predicates, .023's
hard withdrawals and freshness distinction. Verify a nonempty incomplete page,
empty continuation, pending insertion, stale empty read and denied read. No
new cache or alternate `existence` algorithm.

**Estimate:** 2–4 days; recipe only 0.5–1 day. **Do nothing/document better:**
retain `existence` and publish a complete screen test/example using it. This
is a credible first choice because the classifier already exists; reject the
extra helper if it merely renames those fields. Ticket:
[A](../issues/closed/20261003-snapback4-read-presentation.md).

### 4.2 B — show a durable intent without inventing a row

**Implementation, 2026-10-03:** Charlie’s 2026-10-02 ruling authorizes Lane B.
The decided API and lifetime rules are in [LLP 3000.019 §3.3.1](./3000.019-what-the-builder-still-wrote.rfc.md#331-pending-item-presentation-lane-b-2026-10-03);
[the pilot](../snapback4/reports/20261003-b-pending-intents/REPORT.md) records
zero physical-line savings and the added settlement/media behavior. The following
proposal and estimate are preserved as the pre-implementation baseline.

Offer `usePendingItems` over current custody with explicit application
mapping and a discriminated item type. It can compose several operations,
such as text and media sends, but never writes those items into query data.

```tsx
const items = usePendingItems({ // proposed; descriptor spellings illustrative
  writes: [api.send], scope: { conversation: id }, rows: page.items,
  rowId: row => row.id, intentRowId: write => write.args.id,
});
// Each item is { kind: "row", row } or { kind: "intent", write, phase }.
// Render args.text only from the intent arm; no fabricated ordinal/receipt.
```

The SDK can own filtering, stable write keys, duplicate suppression against
the exact predicted/observed row identity, and presentation through the
terminal-receipt-to-observed-row interval. The app supplies the scope/key
mapping; mutation ID is never guessed to be row ID. A confirmed receipt can
be shown as “Sent; updating view” while its row is unavailable. It cannot
claim that the row must exist: idempotent/no-op writes and subsequent deletion
can produce a success without a visible row. Retirement requires the existing
observed-write evidence or a terminal refusal, not an arbitrary timer.

The public pending list currently filters settled IDs; implementing this last
interval therefore needs a presentation view of existing outbox/receipt facts,
with the same scope fences, not just `.map(usePendingWrites())`. Pre-admission
calls remain “saving/not yet admitted”; restored unknown outcomes remain
unknown. Optional failure cards use saved intent references and receipt lookup,
never a resend loop. Ordinary once-media handles cannot be synthesized from
arguments. A set-state selector over the same source can replace B3's repeated
filter/last-intent spelling without changing the mutation.

**Expected reduction:** B1+B2 are just 2 physical lines / 280 B. A reusable
mapping call plus renderer may save **zero lines in this compressed builder**;
roughly one filtering/deduplication expression moves into the SDK per list.
B3 contributes another 281 B of repeated selection. A realistic initial target
is 100–250 B of app-specific selection removed across these examples, not a
claimed multi-hundred-line saving. The stronger value is one tested treatment
of the custody/row/receipt race. Measure actual replacement before proceeding
beyond the pilot.

**Correctness risk:** medium. Preserve .019 §3.3's sole outbox, ordering and
retirement; .022 §14's provisional-accounting exclusions; .023's account/store
fences. A pending item is neither a prediction nor authorization. Test no
prediction, prediction withdrawn and reappearing, reply before sync, receipt
before hook admission completion, offline restart, refused tail, media send,
and account/backend switch. Expose zero arguments from a retired scope.

**Estimate:** 5–8 days for observed-settlement composition; 2–3 days for a
list-only helper that explicitly omits that interval. **Do nothing/document
better:** keep `usePendingWrites` and give text/media/toggle recipes including
the terminal-to-observed gap. **Alternative predicted-row rule:** relaxing
prediction to guarantee a row would need known authority, reads, unique keys
and receipt accounting on every call; unknown coverage makes it unsound.
Do not choose that alternative. Ticket:
[B](../issues/closed/20261003-snapback4-pending-intent-presentation.md).

### 4.3 R — bind receipt families once; package visible-seen reporting

### 4.4 F — older follow history: keep the window

**Charlie ruled on 2026-10-03:** keep the newest 1,000 events at follow time
plus new events afterward; older posts are on the actor's profile. F measured
99,000 extra copies / about 286 MB for one reader of a 100,000-event actor
(about 9.9M copies for 100 readers by arithmetic), whole-feed withholding during
backfill, and growing catch-up traffic despite the unchanged device floor;
2/14 mentions and zero workaround lines show no builder demand. The
[version-3 prototype and evidence](./3000.016.000-older-following-history.rfc.md#0-ruling)
are shelved, with code parked at `origin/sb5/f-follow-history-prototype-parked`
(0936b660f0), not landed. If needed later, prefer older pages on demand under
an independent design. The options below preserve the original comparison.

Current follow copies 50 newest pre-follow events in the transaction and
schedules at most 950 additional copies. Later live births and own copies
are different populations. The fixed device inbox floor is also a separate
boundary. Increasing one number does not solve all three.

| Design option | API sketch (proposed) | Fixed domain, paging and fairness | Tradeoffs / estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep 1,000; document/profile escape | `home` unchanged; UI links to author's paged profile | Current bounded window; no new task obligation | 0 workaround LOC saved; no older Following guarantee. 0.5–1 day documentation. |
| Freeze all pre-follow history, materialize in chunks | `view following: ... history all at follow` | Pin a birth watermark/edge epoch at admission; enumerate event identities durably in metered chunks; removals skip without refill; fair turns across declarations, readers, delivery, cleanup and backfill | Finite work per admission but potentially huge O(sum of retained events per edge) storage. Quiet actors cannot starve behind hot ones. Existing follows need explicit upgrade policy. 15–25 days design/prototype, then 20–40+ implementation/qualification if chosen. |
| Page older history on demand, keep current window | `olderFollowing({ cursor, asOfFollow })` | Server cursor binds viewer, schema, edge incarnation and frozen domain; keyset merge across actors with metered persisted frontier; interactive work cannot starve background delivery/cleanup | Lower initial copy/storage cost; network-dependent older history, removal-aware continuation and honest per-page completeness. Must say whether acquired pages become retained offline data. 10–15 days design/prototype, then 15–30+ implementation. |
| Planned current-membership read over sources | Existing `activity where ... exists follows ...` query, or a compiled indexed multi-actor merge | Define a finite source watermark for one traversal; schedule bounded per-actor work fairly; continuation must not omit quiet actors or confuse backdated births | Avoids per-edge copies but changes historical-delivery semantics. Existing sparse-filter plan is not an unbounded scalable replacement. 5–10 days measurement/design; new merge implementation 10–25+ days. |

For either expanded-history design, enumerate `(event identity, birth token,
edge epoch)` rather than mutable timestamps alone. New backdated events
cannot extend an allegedly finite traversal indefinitely; removed items
advance it without refilling a frozen window. A resume token is not proof of
completion. Scope every pending witness to its reader, report per-page
completeness honestly, and preserve order across quiet/hot authors. Explicitly
budget discovery/frontier work before the first copy as well as copy work.

**Expected builder-code reduction:** zero established. F1/F2 already use the
declaration; none of their six lines is a cap workaround. The gain would be
older Following history and fewer product caveats, at server/storage/device
cost, not automatic LOC removal. A configurable larger constant is another
fixed-window trade, not a full-history design.

**Correctness risk:** high. .016 §3 explicitly requires a fixed enumeration
domain, fairness, page completeness, existing-follow upgrade and storage
cost. .026 §§2–4 prevents reconstructing admission/delivery history from
current sources; §8 is future online-adoption design, not an implemented
fairness mechanism. Preserve event slots, removal tombstones, edge epochs,
device floors and publication fences. Test continuous backdated births,
deletions, refollow with old cleanup, skewed actors, >16,384 frontier heads,
restart and a cold device halfway through copying. Meter limits stay fixed.

Ticket [F](../issues/closed/20261003-snapback4-follow-history-options.md) is closed
by the ruling above; the history window remains the product contract.

const feed = useQuery(api.home, {}, { active: routeIsFeed }); // proposed

unmounts observations; add a hook option only if the pilot demonstrates value.

Cal29's key-only history list is useful evidence but not an SDK algorithm:
it grows without bound and creates a point observation per remembered ID.

backend, query arguments and generation, and obtain each protected body from
its live read. An identity placeholder never means the row exists. Do not
retain withdrawn cursors, authorize media from cached metadata, or accumulate
unbounded offscreen observations to prevent a flash.

**Expected reduction:** L1/L2 are only 2 physical lines / 484 B, so extracting
them does not establish a large LOC saving. L3's foreground/cleanup logic is

repeated lifetime debugging and unnecessary observations; measure mounted
query count and owner resets, not just deletion of a ref. No additive savings
claim across A/R/L.

**Correctness risk:** medium to high for a hook; low for a tested recipe.
Keep .023 §§3.4,12 and .019 §3.3, cursor ownership in .028, and the app's
principal/store fences. Do not revive Shelved .029 or a per-query cache.
Test Strict Mode unsubscribe/remount, account change while inactive, late
responses, expiry, denied rows, page-boundary edits and active-sibling sharing.

**Estimate:** 3–5 days for an explicit inactive hook with types/tests; 1–2 days
for the component recipe. **Do nothing/document better:** keep the existing
hooks and teach route-scoped child components, owner keys and inspection.
The current guide already explains these rules; the new value must be one
working composition, not another warning paragraph. Ticket:

### 8.4 Supported conclusion and limits

**Supported:** Astra used R/B/A in the running app, describes a concrete
benefit of durable intent presentation, and exceeds the registered
client/query bands. Grok used R, describes reduced receipt bookkeeping,
and meets the literal two-call source criterion only with dead starter A.
Both meet M4's narrow narrative condition. Neither reports the cal29
returned-shape failure; Grok reaches the scale envelope on its copied
schema, while Astra's completed aggregate model is qualified only after
replay and adoption. M3 has report-absence support in Grok and unresolved
revalidation evidence in Astra. These are mixed mechanism results, not an
all-predictions-pass result or a measured deletion of a particular number
of builder lines.

**Not established:** a causal score, wall-time or token saving from this
program. The release also contains the office-session lanes named in §7
(SJ, BG, RV, CT, NX, FDL, FIM, QF), plus the other release work documented
in the Astra record. There is no helper-disabled or otherwise matched
counterfactual. N=1 per family, different Mac hosts and contention,
builder choices, nonidentical scale programs, and narrative-only scoring
all remain. The comparative's unchanged Convex/Supabase lanes move by up to
9 overall points between scoring passes (Grok Convex 80 → 71), illustrating
assessment variation without a new control build. The
[wall/token table](../benchmarks/runs/2026-10-04-clique-cmp-0.4.12/README.md#builder-wall-time-and-tokens)
is useful resource evidence, not a controlled backend comparison: those
September 16 control builds ran on eb1000, while Snapback 4 ran on Macs.

Finally, the grades remain independent measurements, not validation of the
holistic numbers. Astra passes 101/119 assertions with 18 not-run; Grok
passes 98 with 2 fails and 19 not-run, and its coverage claims are judged
dishonest in four milestones. Both graders are uncalibrated, no holdout
probe set exists, native proof is absent, and O3 is null. Both O5 records
are band 75 with incomplete degradation evidence; Grok has none and Astra
omits four pinned comparisons. None of this is erased by the favorable
Astra scores or the comparative rank.

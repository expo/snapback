## 1. Summary

On the one-host S1 runner Snapback's group committer runs one batch at a
time. A batch averages N≈16 of its 32-member cap, makes five (G5) or six
(G6) serial PG flights and pays a ~2.6 ms durable WAL sync before the next
batch may begin. That caps W4 near 1.1k writes/s, and the final comparison
qualifies 800/s on both links against the Supabase advocate's 3,200 (A) and
1,600 (B). Supabase gets there because roughly ten independent backends
share PostgreSQL's group commit. Strictly beating the controls needs the next
rungs: 4,525/s on A and 2,262/s on B.

This RFC designs **G7, a pipelined group commit for the Postgres store**.
While batch k's final message (apply, tail, COMMIT) is in flight and its WAL
flush is running, batch k+1 is collected, fetches its facts and executes in
Rust on a second writer connection. It reads through k's frozen, uncommitted
overlay. k+1's commit is made conditional on k by a **commit ticket**, a
transaction advisory lock that k takes in its first flight and k+1 waits on
in its final message, followed by an exact **compare-and-set of `_head`**
against the values k leaves. Pipelining is bounded at depth 2, with depth 3
conditional. Only buffered G3b waves pipeline. Every other producer is a
**barrier** that drains the pipeline and runs today's protocol unchanged.
Results, receipts, continuations and publication are released strictly in
revision order.

Two findings shape what this design can promise:

1. **The flushes of ordered batches cannot merge.** PostgreSQL releases a
   transaction's locks only after its commit record is flushed and visible.
   Any primitive that makes k+1 wait for k therefore wakes k+1 after k's
   flush, so k+1 needs its own. Committing k+1 without waiting would let it
   become durable and visible while k aborts. COMMITs overlap in flight, and
   execution overlaps the flush, but there is one serialized WAL sync per
   batch (§2.3). That chain still supports about N/2.8 ms, roughly 5.7k/s at
   N=16, so **the writer stops being the throughput limit**.
2. **With 32 closed-loop clients, latency then binds.** By Little's law, the
   rate cannot exceed 32 divided by the mean end-to-end write latency L. The
   diagnosis states its targets as "an N=32 cycle ≤ 7.07 / 14.15 ms", and
   those are exactly the bounds L ≤ 7.07 ms on A and L ≤ 14.15 ms on B
   (§2.2). A pipeline removes the wait for the previous batch, but it cannot
   shorten a request's own two flights, its sync or its share of queueing.

Planning arithmetic, not a measurement (§5): G7 alone roughly doubles W4 to
about 2.1–2.2k (A) and 1.9–2.1k (B). With the two-flight final message,
pre-wait disjoint apply and two levers already named elsewhere
(non-authorizing `/m` ingress, and committer CPU near 100 µs/member), it
projects about 2.5–2.6k on B. That makes **the B rungs (W4/B and W5 mix/B at
2,262) plausible**. On A it projects about 2.6–2.7k, and **this design's own
floor is about 7.8 ms (≈4.1k/s), above A's 7.07 ms bound**. G7 is therefore
not predicted to win W4/A, or even to reach the 3,200 tie. A W4/A win needs
an author decision beyond this RFC (§10, ruling 7).

Decisions proposed:

1. Build G7 for Postgres program batches in the slices of §8. First
   restructure the batch flights at depth 1 (G7.1): fused opener, commit
   ticket, head/revision CAS, disjoint prune and folded publication, which
   takes flights from 5/6 to 3. Then go to depth 2 with a three-flight final
   (G7.2), and only then the two-flight final (G7.3, needing §10.7's parent
   amendments).
2. Replace the batch-opening `_head FOR UPDATE` with the ticket plus CAS for
   pipelined batches only. Barriers keep the current opener.
3. Pipeline only buffered waves under G3b's existing selector. The selector
   widens only when a fold rule for the new family exists (§3.3).
4. Keep SQLite serial (§6).

Rulings the author must make are collected in §10. Source citations use
paths relative to `snapback4/` at `25ee876966`. Bare file names refer to
`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/postgres/` (store, batch, group_tail,
revision, flight, ownership, read_grant),
`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/` (command_batch, program_batch) or
`crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/committer/` (wave, overlay, outcome).
Every number in §5 is labelled as measured or as an assumption.

### 2.1 One batch at a time

The diagnosis traced S1 writes at offered 1,131/s. The table shows mean wall

| Parallel interpretation (G3b-par) | 0.62 | 0.73 | 0 |

A PG flight costs R ≈ 0.55 ms on A and 1.10 ms on B: the diagnosis prices
two saved flights at 1.1/2.2 ms. Charging the two opener flights at R+0.1 ms
each leaves **K ≈ 3.1 ms per batch of unattributed committer CPU** on both
links, about 195 µs/member. It covers prepare, bind, closure, finish and
acknowledgement. The G3b-par loopback profile measured 99.5 µs/member of
committer CPU at N≈32 without HTTP. K is an inference from means that the
diagnosis warns are not generally additive, and G7.0 measures it directly
(§8).

The writer is busy for the whole cycle. Two complementary groups of about 16
clients alternate. One batch's members wait for the other's whole cycle, and
then for their own: the diagnosis's 3,422.8×(15.98/31.90)×(9.32/16.19) ≈
987/s on B. Collection takes microseconds and conflict reruns are zero, so
the serial schedule, not fill policy or contention, sets the rate.

### 2.2 What 7.07 ms and 14.15 ms mean

S1 drives 32 write clients, each with at most one request outstanding.
Arrivals that find all 32 busy wait in the generator. **The sustained rate
is therefore at most 32/L, where L is the mean time from send to response.**
4,525/s needs L ≤ 7.07 ms (A) and 2,262/s needs L ≤ 14.15 ms (B). These are
mean bounds with no allowance for the 100 ms p99. At about 90% client
occupancy, a rough allowance for that tail, they become 6.4 and 12.7 ms.
With all 32 clients in every batch, L equals the cycle, so this is the same
bound the diagnosis states as "N=32 cycle". Under any schedule,

`L = H + I_ing + Q + (batch execution) + (commit) + (release)`

H is HTTP and generator time outside the server. I_ing is the warm
independent authority statement that `ingress_of`
(`crates/snapback4/src/serve_identity.rs:8`) makes per mutation, outside the
batch but on the request's path. Q is the wait to be collected. A pipeline
changes how these terms compose. It cannot relax 32/L.

### 2.3 What a pipeline removes, and what it cannot

Today Q is about one cycle, because a member waits for the batch already
running. A pipeline cuts Q to about half a batch interval and overlaps one
batch's flights and CPU with another's flush. That is the whole gain.

What it cannot do is merge flushes. PostgreSQL's commit inserts the commit
record, flushes WAL to it, marks the transaction visible and only then
releases its locks. Whatever makes k+1 wait for k, whether a row lock, an
advisory lock or the head tuple, wakes k+1 after k's flush. k+1's commit
record is written after that and needs a later flush. No PostgreSQL primitive
says "commit k+1 only if k commits" while letting k+1 join k's flush. If
k+1 commits without waiting, PostgreSQL can make it durable and visible while
k later aborts. That is a visible k+1 without k, a gap below head, and
`/changes` or G5 evidence for a prefix that never existed. Two-phase commit
adds a flush; it does not remove one (§7). Independent group-commit sharing,
as Supabase's backends get, is available only to transactions with no order
between them. That is option C, with its publication protocol (§7).

So **the commit chain carries one serialized WAL sync per batch**. Its
capacity, N/(sync+δ), is about 5.7k/s at N=16 and about 11k/s at N=32. It
does not bind. What binds is that every request still pays its own batch's
facts flight, final flight and sync, plus about half a chain interval of
queueing (Q ≥ sync/2). §5 turns this into a floor.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Shape, eligibility and barriers

- **Depth.** At most d batches are in flight. A batch is in flight from its
  first flight until its results are released. The baseline is **d = 2**:
  one batch executing while one commits. **d = 3** adds look-ahead and is
  conditional on §5.3.
- **Pipelined batches.** A batch pipelines only if it is a buffered G3b wave
  (`program_batch.rs:268–273`). Its members must be selected by the bounded
  closure: existing rows, unchanged index keys, no inserts, deletes,
  allocations, maintained or capped writes, and no time- or
  presence-dependent programs (controls §6, G3b note). Its physical write set
  must lie within `r` images of those rows, the tail tables (`_commits`,
  `_change_evidence`, `_log`, `_outbox`, `_effect_presence`), `_ops` and
  `_head`. A late unknown family that would take G3b's serial recovery
  aborts the attempt before dispatch and reruns as a barrier.
- **Barriers.** Every other producer is a barrier: Engine, Publication,
  Direct, administrative and long members, non-buffered program groups and
  activation or adoption. A barrier waits until every in-flight batch is
  released. It then runs at depth 1 through today's opener
  (`store.rs:314–322`, `:359`, with `_head FOR UPDATE`) and today's tail.
  The next pipelined batch starts after it is released. Session mints and
  revocations are Direct members, so **authority changes are always
  barriers**, ordered after every batch already in flight.
- **N = 1.** With an empty pipeline a lone member keeps today's solo route
  (`program_batch.rs:55–57`), so the idle RT0 budgets do not change. With a
  non-empty pipeline, an eligible lone member prepares a one-member wave and
  pipelines. Otherwise it would drain the pipeline like a barrier.
- **Collection.** This is unchanged (`command_batch.rs:206–237`). Collection
  drains only work already queued, under the 32/1 ms caps, and an idle
  committer never waits. A new batch starts when the committer has
  dispatched its predecessor's final message and a writer slot is free.

### 3.2 The flights of a pipelined batch

**P1, one message** (replacing today's three flights: BEGIN+fence,
head/generation/authority, then facts):

1. `BEGIN ISOLATION LEVEL READ COMMITTED READ WRITE` and the settings guard
   (`flight.rs:35–61`).
2. The owner fence, `SELECT store_id, token FROM _owner … FOR KEY SHARE`,
   as its own first command (`store.rs:183`).
3. **The ticket**: `pg_advisory_xact_lock(<store ticket key>, k mod 64)`
   (§3.4).
4. Generation, plus `_head` seq and revision read **without** a lock (the
   authority SQL already selects them unlocked, `batch.rs:28`).
5. The composed authority, receipt/placement and level-0 statements
   (`batch.rs:18–41`, `:64–84`). Authority is read once; the opener does not
   read it a second time, as today's does.

The fence check stays client-side. Every command after it is a lock or a
read, never a write, so a refused fence leaves nothing behind (round-trip
§3.5). Dependent levels (the W5 mix's membership level) remain composed level
flights, as today.

**CPU:** bind, closure, the chain fold (§3.3), parallel interpretation,
acceptance in revision order, and building the tail payload.

**Final message.** In G7.2, three-flight, it is one message, then COMMIT:

1. `SET LOCAL statement_timeout` and `lock_timeout` to the earliest
   member's remaining deadline (§4.4).
2. Wait on the predecessor's ticket.
3. The head/revision CAS, plus a generation and identity check that also
   captures publication. Each is its own command after the wait, so each
   takes a fresh READ COMMITTED snapshot (round-trip §3.5's reason for the
   generation command).
4. Bulk apply plus the vector tail, with the existing counts
   (`group_tail.rs:222–227`).

The client verifies the counts. Then a separate message sends
`SET LOCAL statement_timeout=0; SET LOCAL lock_timeout=0; COMMIT`, as today
(`store.rs:746–756`).

In G7.3, two-flight, one message runs the same commands through apply, then
raising count assertions, then `SET LOCAL statement_timeout=0; SET LOCAL
lock_timeout=0; COMMIT`. This is exactly controls §10.7's qualified
two-flight variant, with its M2 witnesses and parent amendments.

That is **3 flights per batch (G7.2) or 2 (G7.3), independent of N**,
against 5 (G5) and 6 (G6) today. The ticket wait is a server-side lock
wait, not a flight.

### 3.3 Reading through uncommitted predecessors

When batch j has accepted its last member, the committer freezes a
**pending overlay P_j**. It holds:

- the final image of every row j writes (the `accepted` map,
  `wave.rs:293–301`);
- every receipt and refusal receipt j records, keyed by
  `(principal, id)`;
- the `_head` seq and revision j will leave;
- the effective-time pin, entropy and private-identity state after j's last
  member.

The **chain** of batch k is the set of batches not yet released when k's P1
is dispatched. It holds at most d−1 batches.

Before k interprets, the committer **folds** the chain, in revision order,
into k's batch base and receipt facts. Chain images replace fetched images,
and chain receipts replace fetched receipt rows. Fetching a key the chain
already covers may be skipped. The existing lookup, own writes then accepted
predecessors then base (`overlay.rs:71–100`), and the wave's conflict
detection (`wave.rs:262–292`) then run unchanged.

**Why the fold is exact.** Between barriers, under the fence, only pipelined
batches write. Any statement snapshot P1 takes therefore sees the committed
state after some prefix of k's chain. A key written by a chain member gets
its latest pending value from the fold. Every committed state in that range
agrees on any other key. The folded base is therefore the state after k−1,
however the commits actually interleave with P1. The controls RFC's
induction (§3.3, "Why serial-equivalent") then extends verbatim: k's members
are members N+1, N+2, … of a hypothetical merged batch k−1 ∪ k. Rules,
refusals, meters, intermediate images, receipts, effective times and
sequence positions are those of serial reference `run` in revision order.

The first slice relies on G3b's selector: no absence, range or index entry
can change. The fold is therefore a pure image and receipt replacement, and
`covers_span` (`wave.rs:274`) is unaffected. A later family joins the
pipeline only after its fold rule for absence, ranges and index entries
qualifies under the same differential. Until then it is a barrier.

**Authority stays fresh.** Program members cannot write session, identity,
generation or `_meta` state, because those producers are barriers. The
authority statement in k's P1 is therefore the final authority read inside
k's fenced transaction (round-trip §3.1, §7). Authority deadlines are checked
against a fresh authority-clock sample when members are admitted to k
(committer §3.6), and again immediately before the final message is
dispatched.

Pending images stay within each batch's existing 32 MiB fact reservation,
so in-flight memory is at most d times that.

### 3.4 The commit ticket and the head CAS

**Why `_head FOR UPDATE` must leave the opener.** Today every writer takes
the head lock first (`store.rs:315`, `:359`). k+1's P1 would wait for k's
COMMIT, which is the serial schedule.

**Why a head CAS alone is not enough.** Two connections are not ordered on
the server. k+1's final message can reach `_head` before k's tail has updated
it (`group_tail.rs:7`). The CAS would see k's start values and fail, a
spurious cascade on the normal path. Or k+1 could lock a row that k is about
to update, and deadlock (§3.5).

**The ticket.** Each batch takes a transaction advisory lock T_k in P1. It
lives in memory, writes no WAL and is released only when the transaction
ends, whether by commit, abort or backend termination. The committer never
dispatches k+1's final message before k's P1 has returned, because k+1's
interpretation needs P_k. So k already holds T_k when k+1's final message
waits on it, and k+1 proceeds only after k has ended. Keys are
`(hashtext('sb4-commit-ticket:' || current_schema()), k mod 64)`, with the
schema-namespaced bootstrap lock (`store.rs:173`) as precedent. A hash
collision with another store can only add a wait; the CAS decides
correctness. Slot reuse is safe, because batch k+64 cannot start until k has
ended (d ≤ 3).

**The CAS.** k+1 updates `_head` with
`SET seq = $s1, revision = $r1 WHERE singleton AND seq = $s0 AND revision =
$r0 RETURNING …`, where (s0, r0) are the values k leaves. It runs as a fresh
statement after the ticket wait, so it sees k's head if and only if k
committed. Zero rows means k did not commit, or something unexpected moved
the head. k+1 then aborts, by raising in G7.3 or through the client's count
check in G7.2. The raising assertion reports the observed (seq, revision)
(§4.3). The CAS replaces both today's seq-only compare
(`group_tail.rs:7`) and the relative `revision = revision + n`
(`revision.rs:80–101`). Both values become exact.

### 3.5 Other serializing rows, and why the chain cannot deadlock

| Row or table | Today | Under G7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `_head` | `FOR UPDATE` at open; seq CAS and revision bump in the tail | Unlocked read in P1; one exact CAS after the ticket wait; never contended |
| `_change_evidence` prune | `DELETE … WHERE seq <= last−1000` (`group_tail.rs:22`), so ranges overlap between batches | `seq > s0−1000 AND seq <= s1−1000`: disjoint, and no batch touches a tuple another in-flight batch deletes |
| `_commits`, `_log`, `_outbox`, `_effect_presence` | Inserts | Unchanged; seq ranges are disjoint per batch |
| `_ops` | Insert of the bound receipt | Duplicates across batches resolve in memory through the fold; a key collision is a 23505 known abort, as a backstop |
| `r` images | Bulk update of final images | A row written by both k and k+1 (W5's shared counter, any hot key) is updated by k+1 only after the ticket wait, so it never waits on k's row lock |
| `_owner` | Key share; renewal `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` | Unchanged and compatible (store-port §5.1) |
| `_meta`, `_sequences`, maintained owner rows, cap rows | Written by some families | Not written by first-slice members; their writers stay barriers until a slice adds them to the after-wait set |

**Deadlock freedom.** Before its ticket wait, a pipelined batch holds only
the fence's key share and its own ticket. After the wait, every lock of
every lower batch has been released. Waits therefore point only at the
immediate predecessor, so the wait graph is acyclic and 40P01 cannot occur.
With G7.4's **pre-wait disjoint apply**, rows provably absent from every
chain member's physical write set may be applied before the wait. That
shrinks the serialized chain to roughly the sync. Rows in the overlap set
never are, and the argument still holds, because pre-wait writes touch no
row that any in-flight batch writes. The witness is a zero delta in
`pg_stat_database.deadlocks` (§8.2).

### 3.6 Sequencer, in-order release and the publication gate

**The sequencer.** The committer assigns each batch its public seq range
and revision range in memory, starting at its predecessor's end. The durable
truth is the CAS'd head. The in-memory values are a cache, like the
`_sequences` counters (committer §4.5). They are re-seeded from `_head` at
every barrier and after any abort or unknown outcome. The effective-time pin
`at = max(T, pin)` chains the same way (committer §6), so the authority
clock stays monotone in commit order. So do entropy, which today is
installed in `finish_programs` (`program_batch.rs:76–84`), and the G2
private identities. Each is provisional until release and discarded on
abort.

**In-order release.** Confirmations can arrive out of order on two sockets.
Continuations, replies, `committed` and `/changes` wakeups, G5 cache epochs,
`BatchStats` and published state are released **strictly in revision
order**. k+1's confirmation is buffered until k is resolved. A confirmed k+1
implies a committed k (§3.4), but k's own classification still governs k's
members (§4.3). In the database, visibility order is revision order, because
k becomes visible before it releases T_k. Reader snapshots, `partition::stream`
and effect claimers therefore only ever see contiguous prefixes.

**The G6 gate.** Today the grant's state write lock is held across one
COMMIT (`read_grant.rs:189–240`). With overlapping COMMITs it becomes an
**in-flight count**: the gate is closed while any final message is
unresolved, and `published` for k is installed only when k is confirmed and
every lower batch has been released. The sticky uncertainty rule is
unchanged (`read_grant.rs:222–227`). Any unknown outcome closes the gate and
pauses writer admission until fenced reacquisition with a fresh epoch.
Publication capture moves into the final message's identity check, replacing
the separate `PUBLICATION` query (`store.rs:744`). No G6 hit can observe
k+1 before k is confirmed.

Under write saturation the union of the closed intervals covers most of the
time; today the gate is closed for about 23–24% of G6 write time. G6 entries
are revision-equality entries without promotion, so under sustained writes
they already miss. The cost that remains is the hit path's one-second
bounded wait (`read_grant.rs:155–175`). Under G7 a hit that finds the gate
closed should go to the G5 path after a short bound (ruling 5). S1's write
cells have no G6 hits. Its read cells have no writes and are unaffected.

**Adaptive N** (`command_batch.rs:172–203`) keeps its queue-through-COMMIT
p99 controller; ticket waits are part of that latency.

### 3.7 Writer slots, driver and the connection budget

- **Slots.** There are d writer connections, each tagged
  `sb:<store>:<holder>:<token>` as today, so takeover terminates all of them
  (store-port §5.2). Under the matched cap of 32 connections, that is
  1 owner/lease + d writers + (31−d) readers: **29 readers at d=2, 28 at
  d=3**, against store-port §8's 30 (ruling 1).
- **Driver.** Each slot keeps the synchronous `postgres` client, and its
  flights are run on a slot thread. The committer thread never blocks on a
  socket. It waits on the mailbox and on slot completions, and it alone owns
  `&mut Engine` for interpretation, acceptance and continuations. This amends
  store-port §8 ("the writer … keeps synchronous `postgres`"). The amendment
  is per connection: no writer joins the PIPE runtime (ruling 6). Today's
  `Tx<'a>` borrows the store mutably (`store.rs:401–429`); slot transactions
  become owned and `Send`.
- **CPU.** The committer thread's busy time per batch, about
  K + interpretation wait + payload/decode, must stay below the batch
  interval (§5.1). At the projected intervals this is 60–80% busy, so K is
  the next binding term at d=3.

## 4. Outcomes and faults

### 4.1 Outcome table

The rules of controls §3.1, the store-port §5.2 rows and §10.7's two-flight
table apply to each batch on its own connection. G7 adds the cross-batch
rows.

| k's outcome | Successor k+1 not yet dispatched | Successor k+1 dispatched (waiting on T_k, or later) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed | k+1 proceeds | k+1 is classified on its own connection |
| Known abort (explicit error, acknowledged ROLLBACK) | Roll k+1 back: a known abort with no failed member (§4.2) | T_k is released, the CAS fails and k+1 raises; COMMIT is provably skipped (the transaction is in the failed state) and ROLLBACK is acknowledged: a known abort (§4.2) |
| Unknown (lost reply, timeout, terminated during COMMIT, unacknowledged rollback) | Roll k+1 back (known). Pause; re-seed at a barrier (§4.3) | k+1 resolves on its own connection: committed ⇒ k committed; CAS failure observing k's start ⇒ k's transaction ended without committing; k+1 unknown ⇒ both unknown (§4.3) |
| Ownership lost | Do not dispatch; roll back; `E_STORE_OWNERSHIP` | Actual result; the fence orders it before takeover; termination after dispatch is unknown |

### 4.2 Cascades and reruns

A known abort of k aborts every chain successor. k's own members follow
controls §3.1: remove a named failed member and rerun the rest in order, or
rerun each alone, bounded by its deadline. **Successors are innocent known
aborts.** No member of theirs is implicated, and their facts were only
conditional on k. This RFC proposes that each successor rerun **once, as a
whole batch**, in original order, after k's survivors. The rerun uses fresh
facts against the re-seeded head and counts as that invocation's one
fallback attempt. A second failure falls to §3.1's solo rule. All pending
overlays, provisional entropy and sequencer state from the aborted chain are
discarded. No reply precedes a rerun. G2's "otherwise rerun members alone"
was written without innocent co-batches, so this needs ruling 2.

### 4.3 Unknown k with k+1 in flight

If k+1 has not been dispatched, it rolls back. The pipeline pauses. Under G6
it stays paused until fenced reacquisition (the existing sticky rule). Under
G5 the next batch is a barrier. Its `_head FOR UPDATE` waits until k's
backend has ended and then reads the real head, which re-seeds the sequencer
whichever way k went. k's members keep `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`. k+1's
members rerun within their deadlines.

If k+1 has been dispatched, its connection resolves it independently, and
**its evidence also decides k**. A committed k+1 CAS'd against k's end
values. A failed CAS that observed k's start values means T_k was released,
so k's transaction ended, and head was not advanced, so k did not commit.
That is conclusive database evidence about k, obtained on another
connection. This RFC's default records it as a **successor witness** for
diagnostics and re-seeding only. k's members still receive unknown, no
automatic rerun follows and the G6 gate stays closed. Whether the witness
may resolve k's members, acknowledging them as committed or permitting a
§3.1 rerun, is ruling 3. A dispatched k+1 also widens its own unknown window
by the ticket wait, at most one predecessor flush, because a transport loss
during that wait leaves k+1 unknown.

### 4.4 Ownership, deadlines and crash recovery

- **Ownership loss.** The K0-Q watchdog latches loss and refuses queued work
  that has not started. Before dispatching any final message the committer
  checks the lease deadline. If the lease is lost it rolls back undispatched
  batches, whose members are started commands and receive their actual
  result, `E_STORE_OWNERSHIP`. Dispatched batches deliver their real
  outcome. Takeover's `FOR UPDATE` on `_owner` waits for both writers' key
  shares, classifies them as blockers with the old token and terminates them
  (store-port §5.2). Ticket locks vanish with the backends. Renewal stays on
  its own connection and never touches the head or ticket. An unacknowledged
  rollback stays unknown, exactly as today (`outcome.rs:22–36`).
- **Deadlines.** The ticket wait is bounded by `lock_timeout` set to the
  earliest member's remaining end-to-end deadline. This is a new enforcement
  point for first attempts, which today carry no statement budget
  (`program_batch.rs:239`). A predecessor that is stuck, whether behind a
  slow sync or a dead socket, therefore cannot hold k+1 indefinitely. A
  55P03 is a known abort with COMMIT skipped, and its members return deadline
  failures without rerun. Once COMMIT itself runs it is uncancellable with
  timeouts set to zero (`ownership.rs:141–167`), and in G7.3 that is
  preceded by §10.7's `SET LOCAL` reset. Deadline checks before dispatch are
  unchanged (`program_batch.rs:457–494`).
- **Crash.** If the process dies with k and k+1 dispatched, each backend
  either commits a COMMIT it has received or aborts. k+1 can commit only
  after T_k is released and the head equals k's end, so the database holds
  nothing, k alone, or k then k+1. **There is never a k+1 without k.** Seq
  stays gap-free because head advances only by an exact CAS in the same
  transaction as that batch's `_commits` rows. No in-memory queue is
  replayed. A new owner reads `_head` and continues. With a synchronous
  standby, the "canceling wait" warning still fails closed until the
  standby's flush position passes the LSN. k+1's LSN is later than k's, so
  waiting for it covers both.

### 4.5 The guarantees, one by one

| Guarantee | How it holds under G7 |
| --- | --- |
| Single owner, fence, lease | Fence is the first lock of every pipelined transaction; key shares are compatible; takeover terminates every tagged writer; renewal is untouched |
| Serial-equivalent outcomes in revision order | The fold makes k's base equal to the state after k−1 (§3.3); results, receipts, sequences, change evidence, meters and refusals follow G3b's induction |
| G2/G3 outcome table | Per-batch classification unchanged; cascades are known aborts; unknown stays unknown by default (§4.3); COMMIT uncancellable |

## 5. Budget: flights, CPU and latency

### 5.1 Stage model

For one batch:

- X is collection to dispatch of the final message:
  `X = K_pre + P1 + interpretation (+ dependent levels)`.
- C is dispatch to confirmation:
  `C = R + wait + apply + CAS + COMMIT_server`, plus a further R between the
  apply result and COMMIT in three-flight.
- The chain is the server-serialized time from the predecessor's end to the
  batch's own end: `chain = apply + COMMIT_server (+R three-flight)`. With
  G7.4 it is about `COMMIT_server + δ`.

At depth d the batch interval is `I = max(X, (X + C)/d, chain, CPU)`.
Collection waits `Q ≈ I/2` for uniformly phased returns; phase-locking is
discussed in §5.4. Then

`L = H + I_ing + Q + X + C + release`, and `rate ≤ 32/L`.

Inputs. "Measured" means the §2.1 means, at N≈16.

- R = 0.55 / 1.10 ms (measured, A / B).
- P1 = facts + 0.15 ms of fused-opener server work: 1.94 / 2.38 ms.
- Interpretation: 0.62 / 0.73 ms (measured).
- Apply server = apply+tail span − R: 2.11 / 1.68 ms.
- COMMIT server = COMMIT span − R: 2.90 / 2.75 ms.
- K = 3.17 / 2.97 ms, split evenly before dispatch and after confirmation
  (inferred).
- Release, the mean wait for a member's reply after confirmation: 0.8 ms
  (assumed).
- H = 0.6 ms: the generator↔app link is an undelayed Unix socket, but this
  term is not measured (assumed).
- I_ing = R + 0.15 ms (assumed).
- Publication folded into the final message: +0.1 ms (assumed).

None of this is iterated to self-consistency. Projected N = rate × I comes
out near 11–12, so the per-member parts of apply and K shrink somewhat.
Read every projection as ±15%.

### 5.3 Depth

Depth 2 suffices when C ≤ X plus a little; then `(X+C)/2 ≈ max(X, chain)`.
At the projected values (X+C)/2 still binds on both links. Depth 3 buys
about 0.5–0.6 ms of L by overlapping a third batch's P1. Depth beyond 3 buys
nothing once I = max(X, chain, CPU), and each extra unit costs a reader
connection. **Build d=2; build d=3 only if G7.2/G7.3 measurements show
(X+C)/2 binding** (G7.5).

### 5.4 Verdict on 7.07 ms and 14.15 ms

- **B (14.15 ms) is reachable** with G7.3, G7.4 and the two levers:
  projected L ≈ 12.4–12.9 ms, about 88–91% client occupancy at 2,262/s.
  That is plausible, not assured. It would flip **W4/B and W5 mix/B**. G7.2
  alone projects about 1.9k and passes 1,600, which ties the controls but
  does not beat them.
- **A (7.07 ms) is not reachable by this design.** One facts flight, one
  final flight, a serialized sync per batch and half a chain interval of
  queueing already cost about 7.8 ms with everything else near zero. The
  levered projection, about 11.7–12.3 ms, does not reach Supabase's 3,200
  either.
- **The only term that moves the floor is Q.** Timing batch starts to the
  clients' return burst could cut Q below I/2. That is M3's bounded
  collection policy, and nothing yet shows it is achievable. Pre-register
  **W4/A as a predicted loss under G7**. W5 mix/A (1,131 against 800) and
  exact W5 on both links already win, and must not regress.

## 6. SQLite

SQLite does not get G7. Its write lock is held from `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`
through the WAL fsync, so a second writer transaction cannot apply, let alone
commit, while k flushes. It has no network flights to hide either: its
serial section is local apply plus fsync. The S1 SQLite W4 diagnosis shows
about 7.9 ms per ordinary WAL fsync and an engine plateau of 118–122/s, so
batching (G3) dominates. All a pipeline could overlap is k+1's in-process
interpretation with k's fsync, a gain of at most interpretation/(apply +
fsync), which is small at these fsyncs. G5 reuse is also disabled on SQLite.
The store-neutral pieces, the fold, in-order release and the sequencer
cache, run at d=1 on SQLite. The paired differential therefore exercises
them on both stores. A SQLite overlap of interpretation with fsync is not
proposed.

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **C: concurrent writer transactions** (controls §10.4). Independent
  transactions share flushes and need no batch queue, so C's floor is the
  only evaluated route below 7.07 ms on A. The S1b raw durable disjoint
  control, 5,890/s at C=32, implies L ≈ 5.4 ms. C needs a coherent
  publication protocol: private intents plus a publisher, or versioned rows
  plus a commit-order map and watermark reads. Without one, a committed but
  unpublished member breaks head, `/changes` and receipt coherence. That is
  very high effort and a new visibility layer. G7 is the smaller structural
  step. If W4/A matters, C, or sync latency, is the decision, not a deeper
  G7 (ruling 7).
- **Larger or fuller batches.** There are only 32 clients. Under G7, N
  self-sizes to rate × I. Under the serial schedule, N=32 makes the writer
  idle during H. The diagnosis's fill-only forecast was 1.9–2.1k (A) and
  1.5–1.7k (B). A coalescing wait adds to Q. Fill is useful under G7 only as
  phase-locking (§5.4).
- **`commit_delay`, `synchronous_commit`, fsync.** `commit_delay` only
  merges the flushes of concurrent committers, and the chain has none.
  Turning off `synchronous_commit` or fsync is forbidden: durability stays.
  `wal_sync_method` or storage tuning preserves durability, but it is a
  matched-configuration change to every system under S1's fairness rules,
  so it is for the author.
- **Same-connection protocol pipelining.** k+1's P1 could be queued behind
  k's COMMIT on one backend. That saves an R, but the backend runs P1 only
  after k's flush, so execution does not overlap the flush. The two-slot
  design subsumes it.
- **Two-phase commit** (`PREPARE TRANSACTION`, then ordered `COMMIT
  PREPARED`). That is two flushes per batch, and it needs
  `max_prepared_transactions`. Rejected.
- **Head CAS without a ticket.** The cross-connection race of §3.4.
  Rejected.
- **No `_head` row, with head derived from `max(_commits.seq)`.** That
  removes one HOT update per batch but changes every reader's head
  statement and the G5/G6 stamps, and no cost has been measured. Rejected.
- **Pre-execution on readers with validation (K3/K4, "G7c").** Member facts
  would be fetched at ingress, in the statement that already runs, and
  validated against a footprint window. This removes P1 from X, shrinking I
  and Q. Each request still pays two R and a sync, so it gains about 1–2 ms
  of L and does not move A past its floor. It needs read-set windows and a
  fresh-authority amendment (round-trip §7). It stays conditional, behind
  measured G7.3.

### 8.2 Witnesses

1. **Randomized serial-equivalence differential against N=1.** This extends
   G3b's paired serial-history oracle: seeds, 192 members, both stores. It
   randomizes batch boundaries, d ∈ {1, 2, 3}, adversarial completion order
   of slot replies, hot keys spanning batches, keyed duplicates across
   batches, refusals and barriers interleaved. It compares full outcomes,
   meters, receipts, rows, sequences, change evidence, intermediate log
   images, outbox, head and revision. Under injected failures, the committer
   §3.5 replay oracle checks the committed history.
2. **Fault matrix between k and k+1**, each case with k+1 both dispatched
   and not dispatched:
   - a planted apply failure in k (the suppressed-image trigger);
   - a CAS mismatch;
   - termination of k's backend before and after COMMIT is received;
   - a socket drop after dispatch;
   - a ticket timeout, holding k with a deferred constraint trigger that
     sleeps;
   - an adoption between P1 and final;
   - takeover with both in flight;
   - SIGKILL of the process with both dispatched, then restart.
   Each case asserts the §4.1 classifications, no reply before rerun,
   contiguous head and exact counters.
3. **Visibility hammer.** Concurrent REPEATABLE READ readers run during
   pipelined load. In every snapshot, `_head.seq` equals the maximum
   contiguous `_commits.seq`, and each batch's marker images are present if
   and only if its commits are. `/changes` pages are contiguous. G6 hits
   never serve outside confirmed in-order publication, and any unknown
   closes the gate.
4. **Lock witnesses.** `pg_stat_database.deadlocks` delta is zero. Sampled
   `pg_locks` waits occur only on the advisory ticket, with no `_head` or `r`
   row waits on the disjoint path.
5. **RT0 ratchets.** There are 3 (G7.2) or 2 (G7.3) flights per pipelined
   batch for every N. The ticket adds no flight. Idle N=1 budgets are
   unchanged.
6. **Release order** equals revision order under every injected completion
   order.

### 8.3 Measurement on the one-host S1 runner and stop rules

Use the unchanged one-host runner (`scale-controls/scripts/s1-onehost.py`)
and matched method: 10 s warm-up, 20 s measurement, three repetitions,
32 writers and whole-cohort counter equality.

- **Cells:** W4, exact W5 and W5 mix, on A and B, under G5 and G6. The
  ordinary search plus the pre-registered rungs 1,600, 2,262, 3,200 and
  4,525.
- **Arms:** d=1 (G7.1), d=2 and, if built, d=3.
- **Probe:** the diagnosis's lightweight out-of-tree probe records the N
  histogram, X, C, chain, ticket wait, I, Q and the L components;
  `pg_stat_wal` syncs per batch; committer, slot-thread, proxy and PG CPU.
- **Regression:** W1/B and W2/A at their passing rungs with 29 readers; G6
  gate closure fraction.

The §5.2 rows are the pre-registered predictions.

Stop rules:

- the G7.0 chain exceeds sync + 1 ms;
- G7.2 does not pass 1,600 on B;
- any deadlock, out-of-order release or visibility violation;
- read rungs regress at 29 readers, in which case revisit ruling 1.

Failed arms are retained.

## 9. Amendments this RFC would make

- **Committer §3.4 and §7.** Pipelined batches replace "one store
  transaction at a time" with the ticket chain. Their head update moves
  from step 1 to the final message's CAS. Barriers keep §3.4 verbatim.
  Adaptive sizing counts ticket waits.
- **Controls §3.3.** "Under the head lock" becomes "under the commit
  ticket" for pipelined batches. Controls §6's G7 row points here.
- **Round-trip §3.5.** For pipelined batches, the generation and receipt
  re-checks move from "after the head lock" to "after the ticket wait, in
  the final message"; receipt copies resolve through the fold with the `_ops`
  key as backstop. "COMMIT is its own flight" holds through G7.2. G7.3
  adopts §10.7's amendment.
- **Store-port §5.2.** The retry table gains the cascade rows of §4.1.
- **Store-port §8.** d writer slots, each with a synchronous client on its
  own thread; readers become 31−d.
- **Store-port §7.2.** The head is CAS'd once per pipelined batch.

## 10. Open questions and author rulings

1. **Connection budget.** Take the second writer, and the third at d=3,
   from the 30 readers, keeping the matched 32? Default: yes, with the W1/W2
   regression check of §8.3.
2. **Cascade victims rerun once as a whole batch** in original order,
   counting as their one fallback attempt, instead of §3.1's solo reruns.
   Default: yes (§4.2).
3. **Successor witness.** May a dispatched k+1's committed CAS, or its
   failed CAS that observed k's start values, resolve k's members from
   unknown to committed or known-aborted? Default: no. It is recorded and
   re-seeds the sequencer, but k's members stay unknown and the G6 gate stays
   sticky (§4.3).
4. **Two-flight final (G7.3).** Adopt §10.7's wider unknown window for
   pipelined batches once M2's witnesses pass. The ticket wait widens it by
   up to one predecessor flush.
5. **G6 hit wait under pipelining.** Replace the one-second hit wait with a
   short bound, about two COMMIT p50s, followed by G5 validation, because
   the gate is closed most of the time under write saturation (§3.6).
6. **Writer driver.** Amend store-port §8 to allow per-slot threads around
   the synchronous client, rather than moving writers to the async PIPE
   runtime.
7. **W4/A.** G7 is not predicted to pass 4,525, or 3,200, on A (§5.4).
   Choose between: accept the W4/A loss and pursue B; commission C's
   publication protocol (§7); investigate sync latency through a
   matched-configuration change applied to every system; or test Q
   phase-locking in M3 first.

Questions that need no ruling, which G7.0 answers by measurement:

- the actual split of K and H;
- whether N=1 should join a non-empty pipeline (§3.1);
- whether the 64-slot ticket namespace needs a store-unique key rather than
  a schema hash on shared databases.

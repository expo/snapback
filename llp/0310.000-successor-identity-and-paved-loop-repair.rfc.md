## Summary

LLP 0310's 0.1.27 Clique diaries show Snapback is now harder to *operate*
than to *author*. This RFC repairs the paved Expo loop without weakening
LLP 0302's fail-closed identity gate.

The product rule: **a legal schema successor in the same `snapback dev`
owner must leave the web client talking to that owner.** Adding tables is
not "you pointed at a different app." Restarting Metro because generate
deleted a watched tmp file is not an acceptable recovery. Supervised Expo
web must not mint a session that hides the password UI.

**What r3 changed, and why it matters to a reader of r2.** The r2 text
was written on three premises the source audit did not support:

1. That a same-owner recook did not exist. A remint *primitive* does —
   `rotateBindingAfterLoad` (`packages/snapback-cli/expo-dev.mjs`) remints
   the binding on `generated-and-loaded`, and
   `guide/clients/react-expo.md#know-what-the-supervisor-owns` documents
   it. **r4 correction:** that primitive is not this RFC's recook, and r3's
   conclusion that the fault was therefore unlocated was itself wrong. Both
   round-3 reviewers found the fault statically — see
   [The ordering defect](#the-ordering-defect). Track A is a repair, but to the
   *ordering* around that primitive and to the client's retry behaviour, and
   it is no longer gated on a reproduction.
2. That an agent-launched owner has no control channel. It has one —
   `POST /_snapback/expo/v1/control`, operator-token authenticated, no
   TTY required. Track E is a **verb addition**, not a second control
   plane.
3. That injecting `EXPO_PUBLIC_SNAPBACK_URL` would fix the supervised
   password-UI failure. Under a completed rendezvous LLP 0302 §4 makes
   `url` **ignored**, so the injection is a no-op. Track B is the **mint
   lifecycle**.

Five tracks. Two are design (A, B). One is design plus an open
verification (D). Two are straightforward product fixes filed as tickets
and only specified here (C, E). Guide drift is tickets, not a sixth
track.

## The ordering defect

r3 said the root cause was unlocated and gated Track A on reproducing it.
Round 3 refuted that. Round 4 then corrected r4's own account of it, so this
section states carefully what the source does and does not establish.

**What the source establishes — a supervisor-side ordering defect.** In
`regenerateAndLoad` (`packages/snapback-cli/index.mjs`), in this order:

| # | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `generateApp` | the successor generated graph is published into `generated/` — codegen renames its `.generated.tmp-*` staging directory into place, so the successor becomes **Metro-visible here**, not later |
| 2 | `loadGenerated` | the backend activates the successor and begins serving the successor identity |
| 3 | `generated-and-loaded` emitted | only now does `rotateBindingAfterLoad` (`packages/snapback-cli/expo-dev.mjs`, reached through `runDev`'s `onEvent`) remint the binding the bootstrap exchange serves |

Both exposure events — graph publication and the serving-identity flip —
precede the remint. The supervised Metro child is a generated-client watch
custodian, so from step 1 onward a live arm may load a successor `app` while
the exchange still serves the predecessor binding. That is the defect, and it
is established from source without reference to any run.

**What the source does not establish — the browser-side interleaving.** Metro
HMR and the provider re-boot are asynchronous and unawaited; nothing orders
them against step 3. The failure is therefore a **race**, not a deterministic
trace. Its consequence for a live arm is what makes the race fatal rather than
transient: `SnapbackExpoProvider` (`packages/snapback-expo/provider.mjs`)
disposes and re-boots on any `app` change, performs **one** bootstrap exchange
and **one** admission attempt, and on mismatch `admitAppBindingV1` *refuses* —
nothing is admitted. The boot settles on `status: 'error'` (surfaced through
the `fallback` prop; `fallback` is not itself a boot status), and `app` does
not change again, so the arm never re-exchanges even after the remint lands.

Whether that interleaving is what the 0.1.27 lane hit is what
[A0](#acceptance) corroborates. r4 called this "located" too strongly; the
ordering defect is located, the run's interleaving is inferred.

Two independent consequences follow, and Track A owns both:

- **Window narrowing (supervisor side).** The gap between the remint and the
  arm's re-exchange must be bounded by the supervisor rather than left to
  whenever Metro notices. **Not** by reordering around `loadGenerated`: rounds
  4 and 5 killed both the naive and the "single safe order" forms of that idea,
  and r6 withdrew it. See I1.
- **Retry (client side).** Even with correct ordering, a pull-only exchange
  plus a single-shot admission is fragile. The provider must re-exchange on a
  supervised-dev identity mismatch **at boot admission** rather than fail
  closed forever.

Two corollaries retire r3 text. The "schema successor is untested" hypothesis
was the wrong first question — the rotation predicate is
`successor.identity.descriptor !== descriptor.identity.descriptor`, which a
functions edit changes just as a schema edit does. And the existing dev-loop
test does more than r3 credited: it waits for `binding-rotated`, fetches the
next bootstrap exchange, waits for Metro to serve the successor bundle, and
drives a **fresh** successor boot. What no test exercises is the
**already-running arm** across a successor. That is the coverage hole.

**This section does not amend LLP 0181.** r5 asserted here that it conflicted
with 0181's supervised baseline — which specifies the remint "after each
successor graph reaches the backend's `generated-and-loaded` compatible-cut
event" — and that Track A carried a dated remint-timing amendment. **Both
claims are retracted at r7**, at their origin rather than only in Ruling 1:
r6 withdrew the reordering that would have created the conflict, so the
compatible-cut event stays the eligibility fact and 0181's baseline stands
unchanged. (r5 also claimed such a move would leave that eligibility fact
"unchanged"; round 5 correctly said it would not. Moot, and recorded so it is
not re-derived.)

## Motivation

The same explicit prompt that greened M1 activated an additive successor
with preserved accounts, then blanked the web app with
`SNAPBACK_APP_BINDING_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — the imported successor descriptor
and the delivered binding named different identities, and admission *refused*. Familiar greened M1–M3 only after
Metro died twice and the owner was restarted.

**This is not framed as a regression.** LLP 0310 r3 withdrew that claim:
each earlier Clique "explicit green" used a different topology (Diary
0096, Diary 0113) or restarted the owner after a descriptor change
(Diary 0106, Diary 0113). **None of the cited baselines** exercised the
supervised same-owner successor, so there is no baseline here to regress from.
That is a claim about those three runs, not an exhaustive history of every
Clique run ever (round-3 review). That changes the argument's shape
but not its force: the paved path is claimed in the guide, is partly
implemented, and does not survive the one clock we measure it on.

LLP 0300 wanted Snapback to be more like Convex: a URL, a client, a
boring reload. LLP 0302 then locked identity so a client cannot send
credentials to the wrong backend. Both are correct. Together with a
remint that does not reach the web arm on this successor shape, they
made "I added a table" look like "I swapped the app."

Convex and Supabase in the same wave had Expo/Playwright papercuts and
still reached M2 green. Only Snapback explicit stopped on identity.

## Non-goals

- Weakening `SNAPBACK_APP_BINDING_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` against a *different*
  backend, a stale supervisor, or a hosted-target swap.
- Merging explicit and familiar into one authoring profile.
- Removing `reads`/`writes`/`refusals` or `defineAuth`.
- Changing Clique's frozen typing clock (instrument; LLP 0201 / 0281).
- Multi-asset `defineCommand` (already owned by
  `issues/shelved/20260815-finish-media-happy-path.md` / LLP 0239).
- Making Node `memory` platform grow durable custody. Document the
  `expo-web` + fake-indexeddb requirement; do not invent a second store.
- Rewriting the supervised binding publisher
  (`packages/snapback-cli/expo-binding-refresh.mjs`). Its monotone
  publication and same-generation remint are the mechanism Track A
  repairs, not replaces.

## Design

This RFC does **not** change the LLP 0302 mismatch predicate. It makes
the declaration that predicate reads reach the client on a proven
same-owner successor.

### Ruling 1 — same-owner successor is not a foreign app

A legal successor of the live `snapback dev` incarnation MUST leave
every supervised Expo web arm talking to that incarnation under one
coherent identity. Split-brain (backend on successor, delivered binding
on predecessor, or the reverse) is a product bug.

Eligibility is a trusted lineage, not “we saw an activation event”:

- live owner incarnation id
- currently committed predecessor identity
- candidate successor identity (generated `app` + backend)
- monotonic transition epoch

Stale, reordered, unmatched-predecessor, foreign-backend, and
stale-supervisor events remain `SNAPBACK_APP_BINDING_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.
They never authorize a recook.

**The identity-bearing artifact is the delivered `AppBinding`.**
`admitAppBindingV1` (`packages/snapback-expo/app-binding.mjs`) compares
the imported generated `AppDescriptor` against the binding the client
received from `GET /_snapback/expo/v1/bootstrap`. The config plugin's
`expo.extra.snapback.integrationReceipt` is a build-invalidation and
compatibility record with **no runtime reader**; it is not part of this
transition and MUST NOT be named as the thing to recook. Any
implementation note, ticket, or guide sentence that says "recook the Expo
extra" is describing the wrong artifact.

Two invariants make that concrete, and they are what Track A implements
(round-3 review: the r2/r3 freeze/drain/ack table had no carrier — the
browser talks directly to `binding.target.origin` and the supervisor's
browser-facing seam is a *pull* bootstrap exchange, so the supervisor cannot
hold handshakes or collect acks):

- **I1 — narrow the exposure window; do not reorder around it.** The
  supervisor MUST publish the successor binding as early as its eligibility
  fact allows, and MUST NOT leave the arm to discover the successor on its own:
  after publishing, it MUST drive a client reload (Track E's `reload-metro`) so
  the arm re-imports the successor `app` and re-exchanges promptly rather than
  whenever Metro happens to notice. Today that eligibility fact is the
  backend's `generated-and-loaded` compatible-cut event, and I1 does not move
  it. **Track E is therefore load-bearing for Track A**, not an independent
  convenience.

  **r6 withdrawal — r5's reordering was wrong twice over.** r5 ruled a single
  order: publish the binding while the successor graph is unpublished, then
  `loadGenerated`, then expose the graph. Round 5 refuted it on both
  feasibility and safety. Recorded rather than quietly dropped:

  1. **Unrunnable.** Both the remint and `loadGenerated` read
     `backendRoot/generated` — the tree Metro watches. Holding it unpublished
     starves the two steps that must run first, and performing `loadGenerated`
     against `generated/` *is* the exposure r5 tried to defer. Sharing a
     staging mechanism with Track C does not resolve it: Track C commits into
     `generated/` at `generateApp` and C1 then requires Metro to serve the
     updated graph, so one mechanism cannot satisfy both — and Track C landing
     independently would lock in the publish r5's I1 forbade.
  2. **Harmful.** Between activation and graph exposure the backend serves the
     successor while the arm still holds the predecessor `app` and binding.
     That is a live-episode mismatch against LLP 0302 §3's standing gate, which
     is fail-closed and which I2 is explicitly forbidden to retry. r5's order
     converted a *racy* boot-admission failure into a *deterministic* kill of
     the arm A1 requires to stay up.

  The shipping order plus I2 already covers the path the 0.1.27 lane hit:
  dispose on `app` change, retry admission until the remint lands. I1 is a
  narrowing requirement, not a reordering one.

  **The LLP 0181 remint-timing amendment r5 recorded is withdrawn with it.**
  With the compatible-cut event retained as the eligibility fact, 0181's
  supervised baseline is unchanged and needs no amendment.

- **Open obligation — the activation window (W2).** Between `loadGenerated`
  and the arm's reload there is a window in which the backend serves the
  successor and a live arm still holds the predecessor. This window **exists in
  the shipping code today**; r5's ordering widened it into a certainty, and r6's
  withdrawal returns it to its present size but does not close it. A live
  episode that transacts in that window meets LLP 0302 §3's standing gate and
  dies.

  Two candidate closures, neither ruled here:

  1. **Collapse it operationally.** I1's mandatory post-publish `reload-metro`
     makes the window short and bounded rather than "whenever Metro notices."
     Short is not the same as correct, but it may be sufficient for a
     development loop.
  2. **Admit a narrow re-verification.** LLP 0302 §3 could permit one
     re-verification cycle — rather than terminal refusal — when the mismatch
     is against the *same supervised owner incarnation* and a successor
     transition is in flight. That is an amendment to 0302's fail-closed gate
     and belongs to 0302's owner.

  This RFC deliberately does not choose. Four consecutive revisions of this
  document have shipped a repair that a later round refuted, and three of those
  were cases of ruling past the evidence. W2 is recorded as an obligation with
  its options named, and A1 must report which window it is measuring.

- **I2 — a supervised-dev identity mismatch is retryable at boot admission.**
  On `SNAPBACK_APP_BINDING_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` raised while admitting a
  candidate whose `source` is `supervised-dev`, the provider MUST re-exchange
  (bounded attempts, backoff, the same rendezvous origin) rather than settle on
  `status: 'error'`. Today the arm dies permanently because `app` does not
  change a second time.

  Three scope limits, each load-bearing:

  1. **Boot admission only.** LLP 0302 §3's standing discovery gate raises the
     *same* diagnostic code for a live episode against a replaced backend.
     That path MUST NOT retry — it is the fail-closed gate 0302 exists to
     provide. The retry decision therefore lives at boot admission in
     `SnapbackExpoProvider`, not in a shared handler keyed on the code.
  2. **Decided from the candidate, not the error.** `admitAppBindingV1` raises
     the identity mismatch *before* its source-specific arms, so the thrown
     error does not carry the source. The provider holds the resolved candidate
     from `resolveBinding` and MUST gate on that candidate's `source`;
     `declared-dev`, hosted, and release admissions keep today's terminal
     refusal.
  3. **Every attempt re-runs the full predicate.** Retry re-admits nothing by
     itself. No credentials exist at boot admission, so a bounded retry against
     a mismatched listener discloses nothing.

  The bound MUST tolerate the whole exposure window: with I1 correct the window
  is short, but a bound tuned to a fixed small delay would reproduce the
  original failure if any future change reopens it. Exhausting the bound yields
  a typed diagnostic, not a hang.

Recovery is in-process. Owner restart is a fallback, not the paved
path. `pkill` is never the recovery.

**Caller-owned 0300 path:** generate/successor rewrites the on-disk
generated app; a client/Metro reload is the recovery. Track A is only the
owner-managed supervised binding delivery.

**Sessions:** same-owner recook preserves account and session rows. An
already-authenticated principal remains authenticated after commit. It
must not be dumped onto `SNAPBACK_AUTH_SESSION_EXPIRED` as the only
path. Re-login is not the paved M2 path.

### Ruling 2 — supervised `--expo` remains a paved path

LLP 0300's caller-owned `expo start` + URL-first loop stays canonical.
This RFC does **not** amend LLP 0302 §4: a completed supervised
rendezvous still wins source selection; `url` does not become a second
binding source.

Supervised `snapback dev --expo --expo-platform web` remains a supported
convenience owner (LLP 0181 / 0299). Independent choices — decided here except
where the row says otherwise:

| Choice | Decision |
|---|---|
| Binding source | Supervised rendezvous remains selected (0302 §4 unchanged) |
| Data/auth origin | The supervised binding's own `target.origin`. It already *is* the backend origin; no environment variable is involved |
| `EXPO_PUBLIC_SNAPBACK_URL` under `--expo` | **Not injected.** See below |
| Mint creation | On demand, not boot-eager |
| Mint adoption | **Proposed**, not decided here: explicit opt-in rather than the default first session. The precedence this changes is owned by LLP 0258 D5, so this row is a request to that owner — see Track B item 2. If LLP 0258 declines, this row gives |
| Sign-out | Returns to anonymous/password UI. Must not crash the provider |

**Why the URL row is a refusal, not a fix (r3).** r2 proposed injecting
`EXPO_PUBLIC_SNAPBACK_URL` as the same backend origin. Under a completed
rendezvous LLP 0302 §4 says `url` is *ignored* — and §5 says the library
reads no environment at all; only the template passes `url` to the
provider. So the injection cannot change client behavior, and an
acceptance test asserting "signup round-trips against the injected
origin" cannot distinguish it from the binding origin it already uses.
Injecting a variable the paved path ignores is a discoverability trap,
not a repair. If a future design wants the variable present under
`--expo` for hand-rolled clients, it must amend LLP 0302 §4/§5 in that
design and say what reads it.

Two standing decisions bound this area and are named here so a reviewer
does not have to rediscover them: LLP 0223's "auto-inject, do not
auto-write" (no durable `.env` copy of supervisor facts) and LLP 0262 D2
(the dev-identity bearer never enters `EXPO_PUBLIC_*`). Neither is
amended: D2 fences the *bearer*, and Ruling 2 injects nothing.

This RFC **amends LLP 0300 P4** (which says the supervised `--expo` path
"retains its existing issuance behavior unchanged") for **mint creation only**:
creation becomes on-demand rather than boot-eager. The *adoption* half is not
amended here — that precedence belongs to LLP 0258 D5 and is a recorded
obligation on its owner (Track B item 2), not a decision this RFC makes.

### Ruling 3 — one typing episode is one presence generation

Generation fencing stays, against overlapping *logical* presence rows.
One typing episode MUST reuse the generation admitted by the first
`touch()` until `withdraw`, idle expiry, unmount, routing-key change, or
session change. Those events MUST fence earlier in-flight publishes so a
delayed completion cannot resurrect presence. Per-keystroke `touch()`
MUST NOT start a new generation or cancel the in-flight publish of that
episode.

`touch()` today (`packages/snapback-react/implementation.mjs`) uses the
generation bump as the fence for two other things. Removing the bump
without replacing them is a regression, so this ruling also requires:

- **Timer fencing is mostly already there.** `scheduleActivityDeadline` and
  `expireActivity` fence on `activity.deadlineMs` *as well as*
  `activity.generation`, so extending the deadline with a new `deadlineMs`
  already invalidates the predecessor timer without any generation change
  (round-4 review; r4 overstated the need for a new epoch here). An
  implementation MUST keep a monotone per-episode identifier for the coalescing
  rule below, but it does not need to reproduce the deadline fence.
  `attemptActivityWithdrawal` does **not** fence the delete — it always issues
  it and uses the generation only to clear errors afterwards, with its callers
  doing the pre-call checks (r3 misdescribed this). **The episode identifier
  MUST NOT be placed in front of that delete** — moving the fence there can
  drop a required withdrawal.
- **Deadline still extends.** Every `touch()` MUST push the idle deadline
  to `now + idleMs`, exactly as today. Reusing a generation must not make
  an episode expire on the first keystroke's clock.
- **Mid-episode value change.** If the captured value changes within an
  episode, the change MUST be coalesced and published after the in-flight
  publish settles, under the same generation. It MUST NOT cancel the
  in-flight publish, and it MUST NOT be silently dropped. (The guide's
  stability rule already tells authors to keep volatile text out of
  presence values; that is guidance, not a licence for the runtime to
  lose an update.)
- **Every episode boundary discards pending work.** This is the load-bearing
  half of the ruling. A queued coalesced publish MUST be abandoned at **every**
  boundary — `withdraw`, **idle expiry**, unmount, routing-key change, session
  change — not only at explicit `withdraw`. Without it: V1 in flight, V2
  queued, the episode expires on idle, V1 settles, V2 publishes — presence
  resurrected after the episode ended. `expireActivity` ends an episode
  **without** bumping the generation, so the discard cannot rely on a
  generation change to fence it; it needs the episode identifier.

**Amendment to LLP 0257.** LLP 0257 currently requires a per-`touch()`
generation increment. This ruling reverses that for the within-episode case
and is a **dated amendment to LLP 0257**, recorded here rather than left
implicit — r3 claimed 0257 stood unamended while reversing its rule (round-3 review). LLP 0257's *boundary* fencing and its background-withdrawal
requirement are untouched; only the per-keystroke increment changes.

**The package boundary is held open.** r2 ruled that the
`@expo/snapback-expo` facade hook is for `SnapbackExpoProvider` trees
only and named `@expo/snapback-react` as the URL-first import. The audit
found that ruling rests on an unverified premise: the facade resolves
`lifecycleContext?.source ?? await resolveSnapbackExpoLifecycleSource(...)`,
so the Expo provider context is optional, and on web the resolver reaches
`react-native-web`'s `AppState`. LLP 0257 *requires* that bare-provider
fallback, and
`issues/20260825-ruling-3-vs-llp-0257-background-withdrawal.md` records
that the shipped facade follows it. The originating lane never observed a
refusal; it inferred the rule from type declarations and labeled the row
`agent-error`.

Therefore: **no package boundary is ruled here.** LLP 0310 P20
(`issues/closed/20260826-probe-expo-presence-facade-bare-provider.md`) re-tested
the facade under a bare URL-first `SnapbackProvider` on web.

**P20 ran on 2026-08-26 and the answer is `works`**
(`tests/probes/expo-presence-bare-provider-20260826/`, retained
`observation.json` and standing regression `probe.test.mjs`): no
`SNAPBACK_EXPO_ACTIVITY_LIFECYCLE_UNAVAILABLE`, the gated admission opened, and
`touch()` published once, with no lifecycle context in the tree. Only
`react-native-web`'s `AppState` module was substituted — it is not a workspace
dependency — and `--conditions=browser` stood in for Metro's web condition
resolution; both limits are recorded with the artifact, and neither touches the
claim under test, which was that the *Snapback* context is required.

Consequently, and as this ruling pre-committed: LLP 0257 stands unamended, the
conflict ticket is closed as "no conflict"
(`issues/closed/20260825-ruling-3-vs-llp-0257-background-withdrawal.md`), and
the guide keeps both imports with their gated/ungated difference documented.
The guide MUST describe the observed behavior of both hooks and MUST NOT teach
that the facade is unsupported under a bare provider — that correction landed
on `guide/realtime/ephemeral-state.md`, `guide/recipes/activity-presence.md`,
and `guide/backend/familiar-profile.md`, with retrieval case 100 aligned.

### Track A — let the client retry, and bound the window

**Owns P1, P19.** Tickets:
`issues/20260825-successor-expo-receipt-stale.md` (repair),
`issues/20260826-probe-successor-binding-rotation-json-stream.md` (A0).

**A1 — narrow the window (I1).** Keep the shipping order. After
`rotateBindingAfterLoad` (`packages/snapback-cli/expo-dev.mjs`) publishes the
successor binding, the supervisor drives a client reload through Track E's
`reload-metro` so the arm re-imports the successor `app` and re-exchanges
promptly. Do **not** reorder around `loadGenerated`, and do not stage the
generated tree away from Metro for this purpose — r6 withdrew both.

The eligibility lineage of Ruling 1 is evaluated where it is today, at the
compatible-cut event. If it fails, the successor is refused and the predecessor
keeps serving; emit `recook-failed-successor-refused`.

**A transition that publishes and then fails still needs a rollback rule.**
`loadGenerated` activates through `ensureApp` and then performs separately
fallible seed and cleanup work, so "activation failed" does not imply "the
predecessor is still serving." Any rollback Track A implements MUST establish
which of the two it is before restoring anything, and MUST enter a typed stop
rather than pair a restored predecessor binding with a successor backend.

**Forbidden:** a runtime identity setter on the client; a second binding
channel beside the bootstrap exchange; treating the config-plugin `extra`
receipt as the artifact to rewrite; weakening `admitAppBindingV1`; or a
supervisor→browser push plane (there is none, and r2/r3's freeze/drain/ack
table wrongly assumed one).

**A2 — client retry (I2).** In `SnapbackExpoProvider`
(`packages/snapback-expo/provider.mjs`), a `supervised-dev` identity mismatch
at boot admission becomes a retrying boot state rather than a terminal
`status: 'error'`: re-run the bootstrap exchange with bounded attempts and
backoff, re-running the full `admitAppBindingV1` predicate each time, gating on
the resolved candidate's `source` rather than on the thrown error, and
surfacing a typed diagnostic when the bound is exhausted. Leave LLP 0302 §3's
standing-gate mismatch terminal. This is the half that does not depend on the
supervisor at all.

The publisher's existing monotone guard
(`SNAPBACK_EXPO_BINDING_PUBLICATION_STALE`) is the floor, not the contract: it
prevents an older binding replacing a newer one, but says nothing about the arm
that already holds the older one. A2 closes that half.

**A3 — coverage for the live arm.** No existing test exercises an
already-running browser arm across a successor: the dev-loop rotation test
verifies the rotated binding in the next exchange and then drives a *fresh*
boot. Add a test that holds an arm live across a successor — schema and
functions — and asserts no terminal mismatch, a successful post-successor
authenticated call, and a preserved session.

**A0 — corroborating evidence (non-blocking).** Reproduce a schema successor
under `snapback dev --expo --expo-platform web` with the `--json` stream
retained, recording whether `binding-rotated` fired, its descriptor identity,
what the next `GET /_snapback/expo/v1/bootstrap` returned, and what the client
admitted. r3 made this a **gate**; round 3 refuted that framing — the fault is
already located from source, so A0 corroborates and closes
[OQ6](#open-questions), and A1–A3 do not wait on it. It also settles the one
thing the retained lane evidence cannot: **which side of the predicate was
stale.** The lane recorded the M1 identity from the Expo `extra`, not from the
bootstrap response, so "the delivered binding was stale" remains an inference
(round-3 review) — consistent with the located fault, but not independently
established.

Guide: extend `#diagnose-a-binding-identity-mismatch` with this table,
and cross-link it to `#know-what-the-supervisor-owns`, which already
documents the remint. The two sections not referencing each other is part
of why the lane read the refusal as unrecoverable. Do not tell agents to
restart unless the event is `recook-failed-successor-refused`.

### Track E — add the missing verb to the control route that already exists

**Owns P2.** Ticket:
`issues/20260825-dev-control-channel-requires-stdin.md`.

The supervised bootstrap listener already serves
`POST /_snapback/expo/v1/control` (`SUPERVISED_CONTROL_PATH`,
`handleBootstrapControl` in `packages/snapback-cli/expo-dev.mjs`),
authenticated by constant-time comparison against the managed admin
token, advertised to the owner as `controlPath` in the supervisor
descriptor, and covered by `packages/snapback-cli/reset-dev-fresh.test.mjs`
including its unauthorized and unknown-verb arms. It requires no TTY and
no stdin. r2's premise that an agent-launched owner has no control path
was wrong.

What is missing:

1. **A non-destructive verb.** The supported set is `['dev-fresh']`, and
   `dev-fresh` is destructive — exactly what the Clique prompt forbids.
   Add `reload-metro` (and `stop-children`) to this route, with the same
   operator-token gate, each returning an acknowledgement the caller can
   wait on. Recook is Track A's automatic transition, not a
   client-invented identity patch.
   **`reload-metro` is not the stdin `reload` verb.** The existing `reload`
   (`packages/snapback-cli/index.mjs`) is `forceRegenerateAndLoad` — it
   regenerates and hot-loads the *backend*. `reload-metro` must act on the
   supervised Metro child and must be specified by its effect, not just its
   acknowledgement (round-3 review).
2. **Documentation, including how a caller authenticates.** `controlPath` and
   its verbs have zero occurrences under `guide/`. The guide must also name the
   token carrier: the operator token is
   `managedAdminToken(readReadyDescriptor(readyFile), database)` — the
   owner-only `<database>.admin-token` sibling that the shipped
   `snapback reset --dev-fresh` caller already reads non-interactively. An
   agent running as the lifecycle owner can therefore authenticate today; it
   simply has no way to learn that. Note the bootstrap listener's CORS
   advertises `GET, OPTIONS` only, so this route serves the owner process, not
   a browser-originated caller (round-3 review).

**A second control plane requires justification.** r2 proposed a `0600`
unix socket in the owner state directory. That may still be wanted — for
callers that cannot reach the bootstrap listener, or to avoid handing an
admin token to a launcher — but it introduces a second authority model
(uid + file mode) over the same lifecycle. If a socket lands, this RFC
requires that it (a) name the concrete case the HTTP route cannot serve,
(b) bind to the owner's incarnation id / lifecycle receipt and refuse
foreign, stale, or other-uid endpoints, and (c) share one verb
implementation with the HTTP route so the two planes cannot diverge.
Absent (a), the HTTP route is the answer and no socket is built. Fifo
remains a non-goal either way.

## Closed questions

- **OQ1 (r2, amended r3, amended r4).** Recook in-process; refuse the
  successor on failure. r3 said a recook mechanism already existed and made A0
  answer why it missed. r4 corrects both halves: the shipped
  `rotateBindingAfterLoad` is a remint *primitive*, not this recook, and the
  fault is [located](#the-ordering-defect) — exposure precedes publication, and
  the provider never re-exchanges. The repair is invariants I1 + I2, not a
  coordination protocol, and it does not wait on A0.
- **OQ2 (r2, amended r3).** Binding source stays supervised. The data and
  auth origin is the binding's own `target.origin`; **no
  `EXPO_PUBLIC_SNAPBACK_URL` is injected under `--expo`**, because 0302
  §4 ignores it. Mint is on-demand and explicitly adopted.
- **OQ3 (r2, amended r3).** Non-destructive verbs go on the **existing**
  `POST /_snapback/expo/v1/control` route. A socket is admitted only with
  a named case the HTTP route cannot serve, and must share the verb
  implementation.
- **OQ4.** 0302 identity includes the generated `app` descriptor the
  delivered binding names. An additive successor that changes that
  descriptor MUST remint. This RFC does not loosen foreign-backend
  mismatch; it also does not invent a cheaper “additive schema is not
  a new app” carve-out.
- **OQ5.** Sessions remain valid across same-owner commit.

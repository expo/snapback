## Summary

Wave 1 ([LLP 0329.014.001](./0329.014.001-paper-archetypes.research.md)) wrote
six applications in the v0 language and found, in every one, that the residue
after the language's core is a **schema or model dimension** (structural versus
caller facts, time as state, richer constraints, missing kinds, authorization ≠
retention ≠ disclosure) rather than a missing operator. That is the thesis this
wave tries to break: six archetypes chosen because their reads are not
range-decomposable, their authority is not row-shaped, or their semantics are
not serializable-transaction-shaped.

Method as in wave 1, in two phases: one family **designs** each application deep
(product contract, invariants, traps, data model, hard operations, scale) with
no Snapback vocabulary; a *different* family **writes it in v0** and scores it
with the §11 rubric of [LLP 0329.014.000](./0329.014.000-bounded-programs-language-v0.spec.md).
The orchestrator's predictions are **pre-registered below, before any design
existed**, so the results can be read against them. Wave-1 results were not
shown to the designers.

## Pre-registered predictions (orchestrator, 2026-08-29 01:05 PDT, before any design existed)

1. **Payments.** Every operation is expressible; what breaks is *cost*, not
   class: hot-key maintained aggregates serialize on one balance row; card
   capture and a SQLite commit cannot be one transaction (an intent/saga is the
   honest form, as the auction found); close-of-books over unbounded rows needs a
   chunked, resumable **`job` kind** that wave 1's ledger reconcile and Clique
   backfill already wanted. Optimistic prediction of money is a product hazard
   the certificate should make visible, not a language gap.
2. **Semantic notes.** The one most likely to force a *principled second
   capability class*: a top-k over a vector or ranked-FTS index has the whole
   index as its read set, is approximate and implementation-dependent (the
   hydration replay check fails by construction), and cannot be locally
   re-executed. Either the spec admits an **opaque-index read site** — server-only,
   coarse invalidation, retained as last result, never predicted, marked in the
   certificate — or "admitted ⇒ every capability" is false for a real category.
   Embedding computation is an effect.
3. **Graph.** Depth-2 fits `recurse`; depth ≥ 3 × fan-out 50 exceeds `G`; DAG
   acyclicity on edge insert and reachability-based visibility need a maintained
   transitive closure (`tree` generalized with a fan-out cap); reachability rules
   re-enter the two-hop rule-affordability trap (0329.014 §5 D-F).
4. **Health.** Three dimensions the spec lacks: **column-level visibility**,
   **reads that must write** (break-glass audit), and **row versioning / legal
   hold** overriding delete. All schema declarations, all new.
5. **Game.** The spec's ban on randomness is wrong for this category: server
   randomness must be a modeled input bound at *settlement* (prediction honestly
   `unknown`, like commit time); hidden information exercises verdicts at scale;
   a leaderboard is the structural-rank problem at scale.
6. **Editor.** Reorder works (fractional keys); rich text does not without
   OT/CRDT positions; "no CRDT" becomes a stated product boundary
   (last-writer-wins per block), and undo across devices is a product question.

7. **Analytics / BI** (added 01:12 at the founder's request). The design refuses
   ad-hoc queries and unbounded aggregation by construction, so the honest
   result is a *boundary*, not coverage: dashboards over **maintained**
   aggregates and schedule-built cohort/funnel tables are expressible; ad-hoc
   slice-and-dice is not and should not be. The candidate kind is a declared
   **change-stream export** to an OLAP replica (a kind, not a hatch); the `G`
   ceiling is the wrong instrument for analytical reads. K2 at scale: an
   analyst's "users by region" summarizes rows the analyst may not read
   row-wise, so `readable` on maintained aggregates generalizes to an analyst
   role — an explicit disclosure, never a filtered count.
8. **Realtime multiplayer game** (added 01:12). The transaction-per-mutation
   model is wrong for a 20–60 Hz simulation. Prediction: inputs and positions are
   `channel` (ephemeral, untraced); the authoritative simulation is a **long-lived
   stateful process** the spec has no kind for — a `room`/`session` with in-memory
   state, a tick loop, channel I/O, and periodic durable checkpoints to rows;
   durable outcomes (match results, inventory, currency) are ordinary mutations.
   Client-side prediction here is netcode (reconciliation against server
   snapshots), outside the sync model entirely. The question the app settles is
   whether `room` is a reasonable kind or a different product; the surrounding
   services (matchmaking, lobbies, persistence, leaderboards) should be ordinary.

Cross-cutting prediction: no archetype needs a second *query* class; at most
one (semantic) needs a second *read-site* class with declared weaker
guarantees; two (BI, realtime) end at a declared boundary with a new kind
(export stream, room) rather than at a language change; every other residue is
a schema dimension or a kind.

### The cross-cutting prediction

*No archetype needs a second query class; at most one needs a second
read-site class; two end at a declared boundary kind.* Five of eight in: true
so far. Semantic is the second read-site class, exactly as pre-registered; BI
ends at `exportStream`; payments ends at a `job`; nothing needed an opaque
handler, a per-query flag, or an app-authored optimistic reducer. **The
schema-dimension thesis survived its first wave of paradigm-breakers — but it
grew.** Wave 1's six dimensions (structural facts, time as state, richer
constraints, missing kinds, hierarchy, authorization ≠ retention ≠
disclosure) are joined by seven more:

- **G. Authority of modeled inputs.** The spec's blanket bans on time and
  randomness were wrong in *form*. The right form is *modeled inputs with
  declared authority*: intent-bound (`ctx.now` for prediction), commit-bound
  (`commitTime` — **security windows must use it**, since a replayed offline
  intent keeps its old `now`; payments), ingress-bound (`ctx.receivedAt` with
  `delivery: 'online-only'` for intents whose meaning expires; game), and
  system-random (a committed seed, hashed with nonces, drawn once; game).
- **H. Merge semantics as a declaration.** Table `merge: 'append' | 'lww' |
  'occ'`, and a host `sequence` kind for identity-addressed ordered text
  (editor). OCC-reject is the wrong default for commuting writes.
- **I. Jobs.** Chunked, resumable, sealed-input, generation-fenced bulk work
  — close of books, vacuum below a proved horizon, compaction, purge, backfill,
  rank rebuild — asked for by five applications across both waves. It is a
  kind, with a commit-sequence watermark (`commitSeq`) as its as-of primitive.
- **J. Disclosure has a rate.** `readable` on an aggregate discloses a *live*
  number; over hidden rows its derivative is a per-subject event log.
  `disclose: { cadence: 'epoch' | 'live', minGroup }` (BI).
- **K. Rank as a maintained structure.** `maintain.rank` / indexes over
  maintained rows for leaderboards and top-groups without a capped domain
  (game, BI); never a time-independent `readable` over sealed sources.
- **L. Hidden probes.** Ruling 8 (reductions must not escape their
  visibility) is too strong for atomic cross-principal money: a transfer must
  branch on the destination's debt without returning it. `db.getHidden` —
  usable in control flow and writes, forbidden in `return` and in
  caller-visible columns, certificate-marked, prediction `partial` (payments).
- **M. Declared local alternatives.** A non-replayable site may name a
  different bounded device-only function and must label its scope, model, and
  version instead of claiming replay identity (semantic).

### Spec rules that admitted a wrong program, wave 2

1. **v0.1 rulings 2–3** (hidden rows consume `take`; no extents under
   non-uniform rules) starve a guest's page of a mixed-visibility append-only
   log — the rendered document is missing characters, not just comments.
   (editor) A completeness-sensitive log must be uniform-read
   (`E_SEQUENCE_VISIBILITY`); do not add skip-hidden-`take`, which leaks
   cardinality.
2. **Intent-bound `ctx.now` used for security windows** passes a 60-day
   dispute check replayed on day 62. (payments) Security windows use
   `commitTime`.
3. **Ruling 8 blocks atomic cross-principal money** — dest-debt recovery is a
   resource fact over rows the sender cannot read. (payments) Hidden probes.
4. **The blanket randomness ban** makes a fair shuffle inexpressible while a
   client-supplied "random" is admitted. (game) Committed system randomness.
5. **"There is no other kind" is false again**: `exportStream`, `sequence`,
   `job`, and (pending) `room`.

### Writing the design in a strict language found the designs' bugs, again

FX and write-off signs (ledger, wave 1) have wave-2 cousins: grace window vs.
closer ordering and season-vs-lifetime eligibility (game); exact-mode
dashboards that never label the population they summarize, and a privacy
budget that cannot sustain the design's own dashboard count (BI); link expiry
that does not expire grants already minted (semantic, and workspace in wave
1); a derived cache that is a second ledger (payments); one op log that cannot
serve both "the complete document" and "members-only comments" (editor).

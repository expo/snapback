## 1. Why this document exists

Progressive web startup (LLP 3000.002.000) landed on 2026-10-30 and was made the
web default the same day. Seventeen bug-ironing rounds followed, each one a codex
lane fixing the previous round's blind three-family review findings. Rounds 1–14
are on `main` (`bdbd1e5516`). The feature is stable: the startup path, the
single dispatcher per document, custody decision at open, and the default flip
have had no new findings since round 11.

One area has not converged. Every round since 11 has produced one or two High
findings in the same place: where a write's **receipt evidence** meets
**authority**, **program generation**, **store identity**, **peer completion**,
**lifetime state** and **persistence**. The fixes are correct one at a time and
each opens the next gap, because the rules live in two forms that cannot be
checked for completeness:

- **Prose sentences appended per round** to RFC §6. That section now carries
  roughly forty rule-sentences of the form "an A-bound request refused by B
  cannot fence the already-adopted B, but still retires and recovers its
  private dispatcher", "the unqualified list becomes empty, not unknown",
  "the evidence map never evicts the entry just inserted or an outstanding
  response's evidence". Each is true. Nothing says whether the set is complete.
- **Scattered conditionals** at four sites: the journal fetch wrapper in
  `local.ts` (store refusal fence, dispatcher recovery, metadata adoption,
  receipt registration, live preservation — about eighty lines, six boolean
  inputs), the settlement callback in `local.ts` (cut, preserved list and
  footprint derived again from the same inputs), the receipt/completed/admitted/
  responding rings in `progressive-writes.ts`, and the gate/admission path in
  `app-online.ts`.

The review record shows the pattern (verified findings only; "=" marks the same
defect raised independently by more than one family):

| Round | Area | Findings that survived verification |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | lifetime | pause during nested install closes the device (Grok); post-await retirement recheck (Astra = Fable = Grok); completed-id ring forgotten (Grok) |
| 12 | settlement, refusal | refusal naming the adopted store retires the journal with no reopen (Fable); late own `preserved: []` dropped after peer completion (Astra); known list → unknown under rotated authority (Fable); refusal fixture not the wire shape (Astra = Fable = Grok) |
| 13 | ordering, lifetime | fresh writes without capture authority overtake restored intents (Astra = Fable); retained close permits capture (Astra) |
| 14 | settlement, refusal | recovery gated on `current()` (Astra = Fable); preservation gated on `current()` after peer completion (Astra = Fable = Grok); refusal metadata adopted without shrink witness or program boundary (Fable = Grok); evidence map evicts the just-inserted id (Grok); pause drops `recoverOpening` with no re-arm (Grok) |
| 15 | lifetime, gate | releasing the new opening gate admits a write after close (Astra = Fable = Grok, Blocker); retained close after recovery leaves the transport open (Astra = Fable); closed flag precedes the device fence (Fable = Grok) |
| 16 | settlement, refusal | qualified receipt's revocation waits behind a stalled save (Astra); generation-mismatched footprint widened by columns, not tables (Astra); selective retirement tells the caller `failed` while the recovered dispatcher re-sends the kept intent (Fable); the settle-once dedupe drops the only invalidation for a qualified receipt whose id was never admitted (Fable); the shrink witness inside `onlineEvidence` retires every intent before the selective retirement runs, and a lagging `onlineStore` misclassifies a same-store refusal (Grok, Blocker) |

Six rounds, flat at one to three Highs each, in one area. The orchestration
rule for that shape is to stop patching and redesign the area as a whole.

Two facts the panel established change what "the area" is:

- **Half the surviving Highs were extraction bugs, not rule bugs** (Fable).
  "Recovery gated on `current()`", "preservation gated on `current()`", "closed
  flag precedes the device fence" and "fixture not the wire shape" all had a
  correct rule applied to inputs computed wrongly from `json`, the request and
  client state. A design that tabulates the rules and leaves extraction inside
  the executors keeps the defect class.
- **The resumable pause has no production caller** (Fable; verified by the
  orchestrator against `app.ts` and `app-persona.ts`). `start()` is called once
  at app start (`app.ts:869`, `app-persona.ts:173`). Every production `pause()`
  is the first step of scope retirement (hide, release, fence, logout, persona
  store change). `onlineCustody.pause()` sets `active = false` terminally by
  LLP 3000.023 §3.4 and `start()` no-ops afterwards. The pause-then-`start()`
  sequences that rounds 14 and 16 hardened exist only in fixtures.

## 2. Decision layer (landed at `e10be4eef7`, HF2 repairs below; Draft)

The implementation has **one per-write reducer with two decision points, one
refusal decision over the code's own predicates, one terminal lifetime, and pure
extraction**, with the witnesses in §2.5. Side-effecting sites execute emitted
effects; ownership, read scheduling, persistence and history trimming remain
executor duties. Parent RFC §6 keeps its observation principles and those
executor obligations, and points here for the decisions. §7 records the lanes
and any remaining difference between the proposal and code; status stays Draft.

### 2.1 Extraction is a pure function

`settlement-extract.ts` implements `extract(json, request, snapshot) → Facts`.
The caller supplies a plain snapshot pinned at one instant; extraction never
reads a client, getter or ambient state. Its fields are `closed`, `sessionDead`,
`credentialRevision`, `incarnation`, `storeIdentity`, `onlineStore`, `generation`
(the backend's), `onlineHead`, `onlineGeneration`, `dispatchStore`,
`dispatchIncarnation`, `dispatchWatermark`, `dispatchSendRevision`,
`requestRevision` and `requestStore`. The dispatch values survive arrival:
`dispatchWatermark = max(dispatchStore === onlineStore ? onlineHead : 0, seq)`
is captured at send time, never recomputed from arrival heads. Online
head/generation stay separate from the backend generation.

The request carries `kind: response | observed`, `source`, `id`, `op`,
`footprintKnown` and `restoreConfirmed`. The 25-field `Facts` record always
contains `snapshot`, `outcome`, `certificate`, `kind`, `capture`,
`onlineMetadata`, `refusal`, `storeId`, `namedStoreId`, `head`, `generation`,
`storeRelation`, `generationRelation`, `authorityCurrent`, `authorityParts`,
`preservedKind`, `footprintKnown`, `op`, `source`, `code`, `retryable`,
`storeNamed`, `requestStore`, `authority` and `restoreConfirmed`; unknown
optional values are `undefined`.

`authorityParts` separates `lifetimeLive`, `credentialCurrent`,
`incarnationCurrent` and `dispatchStoreCurrent`. `authorityCurrent(now?)` pins
the dispatch values and accepts a fresh plain snapshot for settlement; without
an argument it answers for arrival. It never closes over mutable client state,
and inspected receipts never supply preservation authority. Store cuts require
the device identity; `onlineStore` is only the fallback for dispatch authority.
`storeRelation` is `same | other | unknown`; `generationRelation` is
`same | differs | unknown`. A certificate carries `outcome`, `storeId`,
`generation`, `preserved`, `op` and that authority predicate. The original
`namedStoreId`, `head` and `generation` retain validated direct wire fields.

`capture` is `{ store_id: dispatchStore ?? null, watermark: dispatchWatermark,
send_revision: dispatchSendRevision }`. `onlineMetadata` carries validated
`storeId`, `head` (wire head, otherwise non-replayed sequence) and the wire's
serving `generation`. An own replay's serving generation does not certify its
execution generation: `executionGeneration` leaves it unknown. Independently
persisted execution metadata can prove a generation; observation-time metadata
cannot manufacture that proof. A legacy inspected receipt has an outcome but
no certificate; additive receipts with store, generation and operation can
supply a certificate, without preservation.

Only a known terminal outcome supplies an own certificate. Retryable refusals
and `E_AUTH` / `E_CLOSED` retain refusal facts but supply neither terminal
outcome nor certificate: custody survives for retry or the next sign-in
(LLP 3000.013.000 §4.6). A failed outcome requires `extractRefusal` to validate a
nonempty string code, string family/message and absent or boolean retryability;
malformed failed bodies supply neither outcome nor certificate. For `E_AUTH`,
authority is `credentialCurrent ? (lifetimeLive ? current : lifetime-ended) :
obsolete`: rotation outranks lifetime end. Metadata still requires full dispatch
authority. `restoreConfirmed` means a confirmed terminal `E_STORE_RESTORE`,
never the wrapper's broader `confirmedJournalRefusal` flag.

Only own successful certificates carry preservation. `preservedKind` is
`list | empty | absent`; null/absent evidence stays unknown, malformed non-null
evidence becomes empty. Validation shares `validPreserved` with
`change-evidence.ts` (at most 256 names of at most 512 characters).
`footprintKnown` is caller-supplied metadata, refreshed at settlement rather
than inferred from the wire. The executors consume these facts for refusal,
metadata and settlement decisions. `settlement-extract.test.mjs` checks the
rounds' actual wire fixtures field by field, including HTTP-200 store refusals,
credential rotation, replay and inspected peer receipts.

### 2.2 Settlement is a per-write reducer with two decision points

`settlement-reducer.ts` implements `step(state, event, facts) → { state,
effects }`, replacing the separate receipt/admitted/completed/responding rings.
The executor creates a tracked state at admission or dispatch. `observed` and
`persisted` are reduced only for an existing state: historical receipts may
enter the public lookup history without publishing new changes on a cold open.

| Event | Source |
|---|---|
| `admitted(op, footprintKnown, initial?)` | inspection or admission observes pending intent; `initial` suppresses a redundant opening-prefix invalidation |
| `dispatched` | each fetch for this id, including overlapping sends |
| `transportFinished` | each fetch's idempotent `finally`, after processing or failure, including retries |
| `response(certificate)` | this tab's terminal live reply |
| `observed(receipt, certificate?)` | inspection/notification learns a terminal receipt, including a retained outcome after a failed save |
| `persisted(receipt?, certificate?)` | this runtime's `settle()` removed the intent from its in-memory journal pending set, before the durable save; a no-op uses observation instead |
| `evicted` | executor requests history trimming |

State is `{ phase: admitted | responded | settled, certificate, episodeStarted,
cutInstalled, published, op, footprintKnown, storeId, outcome, outcomeDelivered,
responseOutstanding }`. Unknown evidence is `undefined`. An identity-wait entry
has no store; the first identified admission/certificate fills its binding
without overwriting a known one. `initialState()` starts with zero response
pins; `dispatched` adds one and only `transportFinished` removes one. Neither
response processing nor peer completion releases a fetch's pin.

The reducer's six properties are:

1. **P1 — Revocation starts at arrival.** A live same-store certificate with a
   known list (including empty) emits `startEpisode(preserved, scope)` immediately;
   no receipt save gates it. Absent lists emit no episode. The executor uses the
   existing non-renewing deadline and attempts `scheduleRead` with `changed=true`,
   the emitted scope and preservation. Pending intersections park that read;
   arrival still starts the withdrawal bound, including during an in-flight read.
2. **P2 — Publish once, when the tracked id leaves pending.** `persisted` or
   `observed` with a known outcome emits `settle` and one `publish`, carrying the
   in-memory cut. A bare acknowledgement proves nothing. A first non-opening
   `admitted` emits `retain(invalidate=true)`; repeated admission does not invent
   a new barrier. Arrival and pending-exit read attempts coalesce through the
   existing dirty-watch machinery. The executor emits `persisted` inside the
   journal mutation, before awaiting `store.set`; the name denotes pending exit,
   not a durable-save acknowledgement. Durable storage never gates that
   publication effect or its covering read attempt. A rejected save leaves the
   durable intent recoverable; `retain` keeps the known terminal outcome and
   emits `observed`, which cannot publish the settled id again. A retry can save
   the receipt, and a later open can replay the original id. Ordinary custody
   uncertainty and read-coverage checks still govern display: a concurrent
   journal inspection may withhold a fetched answer until its fence clears.
   The arrival bound in P1 remains independent.
3. **P3 — Stored response evidence beats an inspected placeholder.** Inspection
   uses the retained certificate, not its own unknown generation. With no known
   execution certificate, a peer success installs a conservative all-table cut.
   An own response is retained if evidence is absent, or if its success replaces
   a failure or has a sequence at least as high as the stored success.
   Equal-sequence updates may refine preservation; 22 followed by 21 cannot
   lower the pending-exit cut or restore the older list. Each response's arrival
   effects are independent of this retention choice. Exception: on `observed`,
   a retained failed store-refusal certificate (`E_STORE_REPLACED`, `E_DEV_RESET`
   or `E_STORE_RESTORE`) yields to a `sent` receipt. That receipt supplies outcome
   and cut; its certificate replaces the refusal certificate, or is absent for
   a legacy receipt. Refusal metadata cannot qualify that success's execution
   footprint. Other stored certificates retain the existing precedence.
4. **P4 — A late own response revokes without settling again.** In `settled`,
   `response` may emit `startEpisode` and a refusal decision, but no second
   settlement, publication, reducer outcome delivery or retained certificate.
   Its episode still attempts a replacement read, including after the peer's
   replacement already finished. The transport separately delivers that
   invocation's full live outcome under LLP 3000.013 §6.
5. **P5 — Requalify at settlement.** `atSettlement` uses facts pinned at pending
   exit, rechecking lifetime, device store, backend generation and dispatch
   authority. The certificate's `authorityCurrent(now)` takes that fresh
   snapshot; it has no separate receipt incarnation stamp. A foreign or
   ended-lifetime completion emits a wake-only publication; missing evidence
   never grants current authority.
6. **P6 — Protect unpublished evidence and outstanding own responses.** `evicted`
   refuses every non-settled, unpublished or response-pinned state. The executor
   counts only settled states against `WRITE_HISTORY_LIMIT`, protects the id just
   inserted and all ids in the latest complete retained receipt snapshot, and
   trims only after consuming that whole snapshot. It evicts the oldest eligible
   settled entry; protected states may overflow without a second cap. Trimming
   repeats on transport completion and complete observations. Old orphan-evidence
   cap assertions are superseded by this completed-history bound.

The two pure decision points are:

- `atArrival(certificate, facts) → { episode?, retire? }`: preservation is the
  list only for current authority and same generation in a live same-store
  lifetime; otherwise a known list becomes `[]`. Unknown/different generation
  or unknown footprint uses `every-watch`; same-generation obsolete authority with a known footprint
  uses `all-columns-of-known-tables`; qualified known effects use `qualified`.
  Refusal decisions attach only to own failed response facts, never peer failures.
- `atSettlement(state, facts) → { invalidate, cut, preserved, footprint }`:
  same-store live success contributes its sequence; known execution generation
  matching current metadata qualifies its operation footprint, otherwise the
  footprint is unknown. Failed receipts have no executed cut and may retain their
  known operation footprint across generations. Qualified preservation stays a
  list; unqualified known preservation becomes empty. Foreign/ended-lifetime
  completion returns `invalidate=false`, no cut and no preservation. The executor
  installs any cut before scheduling, and a wake-only publication uses the
  unchanged read-scheduling path without inventing a change.

`deliverOutcome` marks the owner's first known terminal outcome even after
lifetime end; progressive transport owns public delivery, so executing that
logical effect must not duplicate a public receipt. The wrapper is the single
progressive refusal actor and applies its decision before response registration;
`retire` acknowledges that decision without executing it again.

`app-online.ts::receiptOf` persists additive `storeId`, `generation` and `op`
when known, using response execution metadata carried privately by the transport
and falling back to the intent for store/operation only. Older journal readers
ignore extra fields. Replayed serving generation is never substituted; legacy
receipts remain valid. Durable receipts omit arguments, result values,
preservation and refusal details. Public pending-write values strip store
bindings; receipt lookup, same-id recovery and notifications also strip execution generation/operation metadata;
private inspection and dispatch short-circuits retain them. Live invocation
outcomes retain their full result/refusal details. Witnesses are
`settlement-reducer.test.mjs` and `settlement-receipt.test.mjs`; executor
publication, history and scheduling are also covered by
`progressive-writes-s2b.test.mjs` and `progressive-writes-s3a.test.mjs`.

### 2.3 The refusal decision uses the code's predicates

`decideRefusal(facts) → { reads, dispatcher, reopen, metadata, notify, transport }`
over the predicates the wrapper and the raw `onWrite` path branch on today —
not a reinterpretation of them (Grok: "named: adopted" as the draft defined it
is not the already-on-B path; that path is `store_id === dispatchStore` with
`requestStore ≠ dispatchStore`):

| Input | Values |
|---|---|
| `source` | `journal-wrapper` · `raw-onWrite` · `dispatch-binding` (Fable: three actors decide today; the raw path retires everything, the binding must retire nothing) |
| `code` | `E_STORE_REPLACED` · `E_DEV_RESET` · `E_STORE_RESTORE` · `E_AUTH` · other |
| `retryable` | yes · no |
| `storeNamed` | `equals-dispatch` · `differs` · `absent` |
| `requestStore` | `equals-dispatch` · `differs` · `absent` |
| `authority` | `current` · `obsolete` · `lifetime-ended`; full dispatch authority for store refusals, credential freshness for `E_AUTH` |
| `authorityCurrent()` | full dispatch authority, independently required for metadata (including `E_AUTH`) |
| `restoreConfirmed` | yes · no |

| Output | Values |
|---|---|
| `reads` | `fence` · `keep` |
| `dispatcher` | `retire-all` · `retire-foreign` (keep intents bound to the adopted store) · `keep` |
| `reopen` | `allowed` · `terminal` (Astra: confirmed restore retires without replay; store identity equality does not prove execution history survived) |
| `metadata` | `adopt-directly` · `through-online-evidence` · `none` |
| `notify` | `once` · `none` |
| `transport` | `settle` · `retry` (the `E_AUTH`-after-rotation retry at `local.ts:1939` becomes a row, not an exception) |

`transport: settle` means pass the reply through normal transport handling,
not permission to create a terminal receipt; extraction still gates outcome and
certificate construction.

Rows follow the wrapper's predicates. A replacement naming a different or
absent store with current authority fences reads, retires all, adopts directly
and requests recovery once. A replacement naming the dispatch store recovers
(`retire-foreign`, `reopen: allowed`) without fencing and runs online evidence,
including shrink and generation checks. **It still requests recovery**:
`onlineEvidence` notifies only on replacement, so it cannot reopen an
already-adopted store's dispatcher. The executor deduplicates a recovery
notification that online evidence already made.

A current reset/restore with equal **or absent** request store fences reads and
retires all. A server restore fence itself confirms terminal retirement, even
when the input `restoreConfirmed` was false; reopening would strand the next
send behind provisional restore validation. An unfenced, unconfirmed restore
keeps the wrapper dispatcher, as does a restore under obsolete authority.
Confirmed current restores retire terminally. Restore uses `refuseRestore`,
never `onStoreChange`; its `notify` remains `none`. This is an explicit terminal
restore exception to the recovery rule. Obsolete replacement/reset refusals
still retire foreign intents and request recovery but adopt no metadata.
`lifetime-ended` keeps everything. Current ordinary failures, retryable auth
refusals and retryable store
refusals run online evidence too, only under full dispatch authority. Terminal
auth retirement suppresses metadata.

The raw `onWrite` row retires all on terminal store refusals, including restore;
it is not the wrapper's unfenced-restore row. Raw rows have `notify: none`: raw
custody has no `onStoreChange` handle; progressive mode uses the wrapper as its
single refusal actor. `dispatch-binding` retires
nothing. `reachable()` separately exposes impossible actor combinations and
their reason; `decideRefusal()` is total. A contradictory `restoreConfirmed`
is ignored when deciding the real code, so it cannot suppress a fence or
recovery. Other impossible actor combinations return keep without throwing.
Refusal decisions
attach only to response facts, never inspected peer failures. A caller whose
intent is kept by `retire-foreign` is told `pending`, never `failed` (round 16,
Fable). The wrapper and raw executor port and terminal lifetime are implemented as
recorded in §7. `refusal-decision.test.mjs` witnesses these rows.

### 2.4 The lifetime is terminal

`ProgressiveWrites` owns one document lifetime with one `transition()`:

```
opening ──denied──▶ denied ──recover──▶ recovering ──installed──▶ installed
   │                  │                    │                         │
   └──────────── retire (pause · close · signOut · viewer changed) ──┘
                                   │
                                   ▼
                              retiring ──cleanup settled──▶ retired
```

`opening` may also install directly; failed recovery returns to `denied`.
Absent edges are idempotent no-ops. There is no resumable pause. `pause()` is the retire event (Fable; verified in
§1). `onlineCustody` keeps its terminal `active` as "this dispatcher object is
dead" and is never resumed (LLP 3000.023 §3.4). If a resumable opening pause is
ever wanted, it is one additional state and needs a named production caller
first. The fixture-only pause/resume witnesses in rounds 14–17 are retired
under this section, retaining terminal admission, preservation and once-per-streak
reporting coverage (orchestrator ruling, 2026-10-31, §5.1 and §7).

Invariants the machine enforces in one place:

1. **Fence before notify.** The machine records `retiring` before invoking the
   device fence
   (`original.close()` / `original.signOut()`, which set `closed`/`sessionDead`
   synchronously) before any custody `pause()` or listener notification
   (round 15). `start()` cannot reactivate a retired handle. Fresh progressive
   mutations after pause/close return `E_CLOSED`; sign-out/viewer retirement
   returns `E_AUTH`. Sign-out retains its synchronous session fence and delayed
   device close so session notifications survive; the common drain does not
   wait on the sign-out promise that itself awaits that drain. The sign-out
   promise reports a deletion/media rejection once and always calls `endDevice`
   in `finally`, including viewer-triggered retirement; a rejected cleanup cannot
   strand `ending`, the response hold or the transition to `retired`.
2. **No new intake after retirement; accepted work is preserved.** Every
   fresh admission path re-reads the state inside the journal transaction after
   its read. For uncaptured work, a readiness wait is returned out of that
   transaction: release the lock, wait, then retry the read and state check.
   Resolving a readiness promise grants no authority. `start()` issues the
   restored prefix and waits for its admissions to reach dispatch, not for
   network replies, before publishing installed readiness. Retirement wakes
   waiters so the close drain can finish them. A fresh
   non-captured intent in `retiring`/`retired` gets the state's typed refusal
   (round 15 Blocker). Committed journal custody is **kept**, with `pending` or
   its known outcome
   for already-admitted work, and is recovered on the next open. A click already
   holding durable capture custody is preserved subject to HF1's authority rule.
   An uncommitted capture still goes through `admitCaptured`'s locked authority
   check: an ended authority cancels it with `E_AUTH`; restored ids already
   committed to the journal keep custody (round 17 HF1).
   If store refusal arrives during that locked admission, an unknown id returns
   a marker out of the transaction and enters `refusedCapture()` after releasing
   the lock. Its capture is cancelled only when `refusedStore()` returns the
   terminal refusal; a rejected retirement returns `pending` and keeps the
   record for the next open (LLP 3000.013.000 §4.2, §4.7).
3. **One retirement, through the machine.** A retained close callback from any
   earlier frame calls `transition("retire")`, which tears down whatever is
   installed now (rounds 13, 15).
4. **Cleanup owns the response hold and drains opening work.** `holdClose` is
   taken on entry to `retiring` and released only when every cleanup has
   settled: the installed custody's close, the private transport, and any
   in-flight opening recovery or inspection read (`recovering`, the locked
   journal read), so no quarantine or journal write lands after close resolves
   (LLP 3000.013 §6; round 17, Astra).
5. **The machine spans nested installs.** Denial, recovery and the installed
   custody all read the same state; a retirement during an awaited nested
   install closes exactly the resources that install created (rounds 11, 13).
   Successful quarantine always reports its side key. A pause-retired opening
   reports its final preservation-failure streak once; other unpublished retired
   openings suppress those failures. Installed custody retains normal persistence
   error reporting through cleanup.

### 2.5 Witnesses

- **Extraction:** `settlement-extract.test.mjs`, a table test over the rounds' wire fixtures, asserting the
  `Facts` record field by field.
- **Reducer:** `settlement-reducer.test.mjs`, generated **event sequences** (every interleaving of `dispatched`, `admitted`,
  `response`, `observed`, `persisted`, `transportFinished`, `evicted` up to length
  five, with at most one `admitted` and one `persisted` per sequence, crossed
  with certificate qualification: authority × generation × store × preserved)
  asserting the six properties of §2.2. A Cartesian product of attributes
  cannot prove ordering, once-only publication or safe eviction (Astra); the
  sequences can.
- **Refusal:** `refusal-decision.test.mjs`, the seven categorical axes of §2.3 (3·5·2·3·3·3·2 =
  1,620 rows), plus extraction-to-decision crossings that independently move
  credential, incarnation, store and lifetime authority. Unreachable rows assert
  the reason; contradictory confirmations follow the real code, other actor
  contradictions keep. A read fence always

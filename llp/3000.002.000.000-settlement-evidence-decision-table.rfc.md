## 1. Why this document exists

Progressive web startup (LLP 3000.002.000) landed on 2026-10-30 and was made the
web default the same day. Seventeen bug-ironing rounds followed, each one a codex
lane fixing the previous round's blind three-family review findings. Rounds 1–14
are on `main` (`bdbd1e5516`). The feature is stable: the startup path, the
single dispatcher per document, custody decision at open, and the default flip
have had no new findings since round 11.

One area has not converged. Every round since 11 has produced one or two High
findings in the same place: where a write's **receipt evidence** meets
**authority**, **program generation**, **store identity**, **peer completion**,
**lifetime state** and **persistence**. The fixes are correct one at a time and
each opens the next gap, because the rules live in two forms that cannot be
checked for completeness:

- **Prose sentences appended per round** to RFC §6. That section now carries
  roughly forty rule-sentences of the form "an A-bound request refused by B
  cannot fence the already-adopted B, but still retires and recovers its
  private dispatcher", "the unqualified list becomes empty, not unknown",
  "the evidence map never evicts the entry just inserted or an outstanding
  response's evidence". Each is true. Nothing says whether the set is complete.
- **Scattered conditionals** at four sites: the journal fetch wrapper in
  `local.ts` (store refusal fence, dispatcher recovery, metadata adoption,
  receipt registration, live preservation — about eighty lines, six boolean
  inputs), the settlement callback in `local.ts` (cut, preserved list and
  footprint derived again from the same inputs), the receipt/completed/admitted/
  responding rings in `progressive-writes.ts`, and the gate/admission path in
  `app-online.ts`.

The review record shows the pattern (verified findings only; "=" marks the same
defect raised independently by more than one family):

| Round | Area | Findings that survived verification |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | lifetime | pause during nested install closes the device (Grok); post-await retirement recheck (Astra = Fable = Grok); completed-id ring forgotten (Grok) |
| 12 | settlement, refusal | refusal naming the adopted store retires the journal with no reopen (Fable); late own `preserved: []` dropped after peer completion (Astra); known list → unknown under rotated authority (Fable); refusal fixture not the wire shape (Astra = Fable = Grok) |
| 13 | ordering, lifetime | fresh writes without capture authority overtake restored intents (Astra = Fable); retained close permits capture (Astra) |
| 14 | settlement, refusal | recovery gated on `current()` (Astra = Fable); preservation gated on `current()` after peer completion (Astra = Fable = Grok); refusal metadata adopted without shrink witness or program boundary (Fable = Grok); evidence map evicts the just-inserted id (Grok); pause drops `recoverOpening` with no re-arm (Grok) |
| 15 | lifetime, gate | releasing the new opening gate admits a write after close (Astra = Fable = Grok, Blocker); retained close after recovery leaves the transport open (Astra = Fable); closed flag precedes the device fence (Fable = Grok) |
| 16 | settlement, refusal | qualified receipt's revocation waits behind a stalled save (Astra); generation-mismatched footprint widened by columns, not tables (Astra); selective retirement tells the caller `failed` while the recovered dispatcher re-sends the kept intent (Fable); the settle-once dedupe drops the only invalidation for a qualified receipt whose id was never admitted (Fable); the shrink witness inside `onlineEvidence` retires every intent before the selective retirement runs, and a lagging `onlineStore` misclassifies a same-store refusal (Grok, Blocker) |

Six rounds, flat at one to three Highs each, in one area. The orchestration
rule for that shape is to stop patching and redesign the area as a whole.

Two facts the panel established change what "the area" is:

- **Half the surviving Highs were extraction bugs, not rule bugs** (Fable).
  "Recovery gated on `current()`", "preservation gated on `current()`", "closed
  flag precedes the device fence" and "fixture not the wire shape" all had a
  correct rule applied to inputs computed wrongly from `json`, the request and
  client state. A design that tabulates the rules and leaves extraction inside
  the executors keeps the defect class.
- **The resumable pause has no production caller** (Fable; verified by the
  orchestrator against `app.ts` and `app-persona.ts`). `start()` is called once
  at app start (`app.ts:869`, `app-persona.ts:173`). Every production `pause()`
  is the first step of scope retirement (hide, release, fence, logout, persona
  store change). `onlineCustody.pause()` sets `active = false` terminally by
  LLP 3000.023 §3.4 and `start()` no-ops afterwards. The pause-then-`start()`
  sequences that rounds 14 and 16 hardened exist only in fixtures.

## 2. Proposal (revision 1)

Replace the prose rules and the scattered conditionals with **one per-write
reducer with two decision points, one refusal decision over the code's own
predicates, one terminal lifetime, and pure extraction** — each with a generated
witness. The side-effecting sites become executors of emitted effects. RFC §6
keeps its principles and points at this document for the rules.

### 2.1 Extraction is a pure function

`extract(json, request, snapshot) → Facts`, where `snapshot` is a plain record
of the client facts the rules need, pinned at one instant: `closed`,
`sessionDead`, `credentialRevision`, `incarnation`, `storeIdentity`,
`onlineStore`, `backend.generation`, and the dispatch-time `dispatchStore`,
`dispatchIncarnation`, `request.revision`, `requestStore`. `Facts` is the
enumerated record the decisions consume (§2.2, §2.3). Extraction is tested
directly against the rounds' **wire fixtures** (the HTTP 200 refusal shape with
`store_id`/`head`/`generation`, rotated-credential responses, inspected peer
receipts). This is where the executors' conditionals go; nothing in an executor
inspects `json` after extraction.

### 2.2 Settlement is a per-write reducer with two decision points

For each write id the dispatcher owns, a pure reducer
`step(state, event, facts) → { state, effects }` replaces the four rings
(`receipts`, `admitted`, `completed`, `responding`). Events, in the order the
code can see them (a peer may complete before this tab's own response):

| Event | Source today |
|---|---|
| `admitted(op, footprintKnown)` | inspection or admission observes the intent pending |
| `response(certificate)` | this tab's own live reply: outcome, store, generation, preserved, authority at arrival |
| `observed(receipt)` | inspection sees a persisted receipt, own or peer's |
| `persisted` | this runtime's `settle()` wrote the receipt |
| `evicted` | the write-history limit |

State per id: `{ phase: admitted | responded | settled, certificate?,
episodeStarted, cutInstalled, published }`. Properties the reducer enforces by
construction, replacing rings and flags:

1. **Revocation starts at arrival.** On `response` (or `observed` with a
   certificate) carrying a known list or a known empty list, emit
   `startEpisode(preserved, scope)` immediately. Durable `store.set` never gates
   it (round 16, Astra; all three seats on §5 Q2).
2. **Publish once, when the id leaves pending.** The one publishing
   `scheduleRead` with the in-memory cut is emitted when the reducer learns the
   id is no longer pending (`persisted` or `observed(receipt)`), never on
   arrival while the write still intersects (Grok: a wake while the id is in
   `current()` is parked by `journalIntersects` and a `settle: once` dedupe would
   then swallow the only wake that can publish). `store.set` lag never gates
   this either: the effect carries the in-memory cut.
3. **The stored certificate beats the placeholder.** `observed(receipt)` for an
   id in `responded` settles with the arrival certificate (store, generation,
   preserved), not inspection's unknown generation (Grok: today's
   `receipts.get(id) ?? admission.evidence`, asserted by the round-16 item-5
   witness). An inspected peer receipt with no certificate installs the
   conservative all-table cut, as today.
4. **A late own response after peer completion revokes but does not settle
   again.** `response` in `settled` emits `startEpisode` only.
5. **Settlement qualification is re-derived at the second decision point**
   from `facts` pinned then, not from arrival (Fable: store identity,
   incarnation and generation move between arrival and persistence; RFC §6
   requires the store check immediately before installing cuts). The arrival
   certificate carries `authorityCurrent` as the closure it is today.
6. **Eviction cannot drop an outstanding own response or an unpublished
   certificate**: `evicted` is refused by the reducer for any id not in
   `settled`, and the map may overflow (rounds 14, 16 Grok).

The two decision points are pure functions inside the reducer:

- `atArrival(certificate, facts) → { episode?: { preserved: list | [], scope },
  retire?: … }` — revocation scope and (for refusals) the §2.3 decision.
  Preservation is the list only when the certificate is qualified (authority
  current, generation same, store same); otherwise `[]` with scope
  `every-watch` when generation differs (the executed table set is unknown
  across a program boundary — Astra round 16), `all-columns-of-known-tables`
  when only authority differs.
- `atSettlement(state, facts) → publication` — one `{ cut, preserved,
  footprint: qualified | unknown }` (Grok: cut footprint and wake set are one
  `writes` value; failed receipts keep the operation footprint even when
  generation differs; a differing generation on a success passes `unknown`).

Optionally, and recommended by Fable, `settle()` persists `storeId`,
`generation` and `op` on the receipt record (`valid()` ignores unknown fields,
so older clients read it unchanged, and intents already persist `op`, `args`,
`storeId`). Then `observed(receipt)` carries its own certificate across tabs,
inspected peer receipts gain a known generation instead of the forced all-table
cut, and the reducer's in-memory state shrinks to ordering. This is additive
and can be the first implementation step.

### 2.3 The refusal decision uses the code's predicates and three outputs

`decideRefusal(facts) → { reads, dispatcher, reopen, metadata, notify, restore }`
over the predicates the wrapper and the raw `onWrite` path branch on today —
not a reinterpretation of them (Grok: "named: adopted" as the draft defined it
is not the already-on-B path; that path is `store_id === dispatchStore` with
`requestStore ≠ dispatchStore`):

| Input | Values |
|---|---|
| `source` | `journal-wrapper` · `raw-onWrite` · `dispatch-binding` (Fable: three actors decide today; the raw path retires everything, the binding must retire nothing) |
| `code` | `E_STORE_REPLACED` · `E_DEV_RESET` · `E_STORE_RESTORE` · `E_AUTH` · other |
| `retryable` | yes · no |
| `storeNamed` | `equals-dispatch` · `differs` · `absent` |
| `requestStore` | `equals-dispatch` · `differs` · `absent` |
| `authority` | `current` · `obsolete` (credential rotated or incarnation moved — same outputs everywhere here, all three seats on §5 Q3) · `lifetime-ended` |
| `restoreConfirmed` | yes · no |

| Output | Values |
|---|---|
| `reads` | `fence` · `keep` |
| `dispatcher` | `retire-all` · `retire-foreign` (keep intents bound to the adopted store) · `keep` |
| `reopen` | `allowed` · `terminal` (Astra: confirmed restore retires without replay; store identity equality does not prove execution history survived) |
| `metadata` | `adopt-directly` · `through-online-evidence` · `none` |
| `notify` | `once` · `none` |
| `transport` | `settle` · `retry` (the `E_AUTH`-after-rotation retry at `local.ts:1939` becomes a row, not an exception) |

Rows encode the rules the rounds produced: a replacement naming a different or
absent store with current authority fences reads, retires all, adopts
directly, notifies once; a replacement naming the dispatch store with a
differing request store recovers (`retire-foreign`, `reopen: allowed`) without
fencing and adopts only through online evidence; a reset or restore whose
request store equals the dispatch store fences; a confirmed restore is
`retire-all, reopen: terminal`; obsolete authority still retires a live
lifetime's dispatcher but adopts nothing; `lifetime-ended` keeps everything.
The raw `onWrite` path becomes an executor of the same decision (or is removed
once the wrapper always runs first), so there is one deciding actor. A caller
whose intent is kept by `retire-foreign` is told `pending`, never `failed`
(round 16, Fable).

### 2.4 The lifetime is terminal

`ProgressiveWrites` owns one document lifetime with one `transition()`:

```
opening ──denied──▶ denied ──recover──▶ recovering ──installed──▶ installed
   │                  │                    │                         │
   └──────────── retire (pause · close · signOut · viewer changed) ──┘
                                   │
                                   ▼
                              retiring ──cleanup settled──▶ retired
```

There is no resumable pause. `pause()` is the retire event (Fable; verified in
§1). `onlineCustody` keeps its terminal `active` as "this dispatcher object is
dead" and is never resumed (LLP 3000.023 §3.4). If a resumable opening pause is
ever wanted, it is one additional state and needs a named production caller
first. The rounds-14/16 pause-then-`start()` witnesses are retired with a note.

Invariants the machine enforces in one place:

1. **Fence before notify.** Entering `retiring` invokes the device fence
   (`original.close()` / `original.signOut()`, which set `closed`/`sessionDead`
   synchronously) before any custody `pause()` or listener notification
   (round 15).
2. **No new intake after retirement; accepted work is preserved.** Every
   admission path re-reads the state inside the journal transaction. A fresh
   non-captured intent in `retiring`/`retired` gets the state's typed refusal
   (round 15 Blocker). A click that already holds durable capture custody, or an
   intent already journaled, is **kept** and answered `pending` or its known
   outcome; it is recovered on the next open (Astra: capture acquires custody
   synchronously before the gate; refusing it abandons accepted work).
3. **One retirement, through the machine.** A retained close callback from any
   earlier frame calls `transition("retire")`, which tears down whatever is
   installed now (rounds 13, 15).
4. **Cleanup owns the response hold and drains opening work.** `holdClose` is
   taken on entry to `retiring` and released only when every cleanup has
   settled: the installed custody's close, the private transport, and any
   in-flight opening recovery or inspection read (`recovering`, the locked
   journal read), so no quarantine or journal write lands after close resolves
   (LLP 3000.013 §6; round 17, Astra).
5. **The machine spans nested installs.** Denial, recovery and the installed
   custody all read the same state; a retirement during an awaited nested
   install closes exactly the resources that install created (rounds 11, 13).

### 2.5 Witnesses

- **Extraction:** a table test over the rounds' wire fixtures, asserting the
  `Facts` record field by field.
- **Reducer:** generated **event sequences** (every interleaving of `admitted`,
  `response`, `observed`, `persisted`, `evicted` up to length five, crossed
  with certificate qualification: authority × generation × store × preserved)
  asserting the six properties of §2.2. A Cartesian product of attributes
  cannot prove ordering, once-only publication or safe eviction (Astra); the
  sequences can.
- **Refusal:** the full product of §2.3's inputs (4·5·2·3·3·3·2 = 2,160 rows;
  unreachable rows marked, not asserted vacuously — Fable) against the row
  properties: `reads: fence` only for `source: journal-wrapper` with current
  authority and a differing/absent store or an equal request store on
  reset/restore; `dispatcher ≠ keep` for every non-retryable replaced/reset/
  restore row with a live lifetime; `metadata: adopt-directly` only with
  `reads: fence`; `reopen: terminal` only for confirmed restore; `notify: once`
  exactly when the dispatcher retires and metadata is not through online
  evidence; `transport: retry` only for `E_AUTH` with obsolete credentials.
- **Lifetime:** a transition-table test (every state × every event) plus a
  model-based random walk interleaving retire events with held admissions,
  captured clicks and nested installs, asserting invariants 1–5.
- The seventeen rounds' scenario witnesses (about a hundred cases) are kept
  unchanged as regressions, except the fixture-only pause/resume cases, which
  are retired with a note naming this section.

### 2.6 What RFC §6 and §7.2 become

The rule-sentences in §6 that §2.2 and §2.3 encode are replaced by one
paragraph per decision naming its inputs, outputs and properties, with a
pointer to the module and the witness. §7.2 keeps the ownership and ordering
rules and points at §2.4 for retirement. Nothing about the startup path, the
dispatcher-per-document rule, the capture protocol, the journal format
(beyond the additive receipt fields) or custody decision at open changes.

## 3. Alternatives

- **Keep patching.** Six rounds at one to three Highs each in the same area is
  the evidence against it. Each patch is correct and each moves the gap.
- **Accept the residue and file tickets.** Viable if the inputs were exotic.
  They are not: credential rotation is scheduled (`scheduleRefresh`), peers
  completing one's own id is ordinary multi-tab use, and scope retirement is
  the normal app lifecycle. The combinations are rare per session and certain
  across a user base.
- **A stateless decision table over a flat input product** (this document's
  draft). Rejected by all three panel seats: it cannot express persistence
  phase, which effects already ran, or the stored-certificate preference, so
  the executors would keep the conditionals it meant to remove.
- **Rewrite the dispatcher as a whole.** Out of scope: the dispatcher, the
  journal format, the capture path and custody decision at open are stable.
  Only the decision layer, extraction and the lifetime flags move.

## 4. Plan

1. **Extraction and the reducer first**, with their witnesses, before touching
   the executors; one blind code review of that commit. A wrong row or
   property is cheap here and expensive later. Persisting `storeId`/
   `generation`/`op` on receipts is the first step if the lane confirms
   `valid()` tolerance.
2. **Port the executors.** The journal fetch wrapper, the settlement callback,
   the refusal block, the raw `onWrite` path and the lifetime sites become
   executors of emitted effects. The round witnesses must pass unchanged
   (except the retired pause/resume cases).
3. **Land as one commit** with the §6/§7.2 rewrite, then **one** blind
   three-family review round. A High in the decision layer means a property or
   a row is wrong, and the fix is there, not in an executor.
4. Stop. Remaining findings become tickets against this document.

Acceptance: all retained round witnesses green; the four generated witnesses
green; the review round reports zero Highs from at least two of three families;
RFC §6 shrinks by the rule-sentences this document absorbed.

## 5. Decisions the panel left to the author

1. **Pause is retirement** (orchestrator's reading, ~85%): the panel split
   2–1 (Fable: no production caller; Grok: keep one resumable document state
   for the round-16 cases; Astra: a shared controller). The code check in §1
   supports Fable. If a resumable opening pause is wanted later it is one
   state plus a named caller.
2. **Persist the certificate on the receipt** (Fable's §2.2 option): additive,
   compatible, shrinks in-memory state. Recommended as step 1 unless the lane
   finds a reader that rejects unknown receipt fields.

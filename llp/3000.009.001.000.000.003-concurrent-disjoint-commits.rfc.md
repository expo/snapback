## 1. Summary

Reads win 6/6 S1 cells; W5 exact (A, B) and W5 mix/A win; W4/B and W5
mix/B tie at 1,600 (G7.3 plus CPU cuts and prompt release target them);
**W4/A loses, 2,262 against the Supabase advocate's 3,200**. With 32
clients the next rung, 4,525/s, needs a mean residence of at most 7.07 ms
(about 6.4 ms at a passing 90% occupancy). The attribution measured about
15 ms and found no ordered lever that reaches 7.07 ms.

This RFC designs **option C soundly: batches whose complete footprints are
disjoint from every open batch commit concurrently and independently
on separate writer connections**, so PostgreSQL's group commit can merge
their flushes, while every Snapback reader, `/changes`, sync, receipt,
cache and claimer still sees only a contiguous prefix of one revision
order. Four mechanisms carry it:

1. **A footprint router and a phase rule** (§3.1–§3.3). A batch enters the
   concurrent lane (the *C lane*) only if every member's conservatively
   closed footprint is disjoint from every *open* batch (dispatched and not yet covered by
   the watermark); conflicting
   members are held until their blocker resolves, non-commuting work runs
   on the accepted ordered lane (G7.2/G7.3, the *O lane*), and **the two
   lanes are never open at the same time**.
2. **In-process ranges, a durable batch record and an in-process watermark**
   (§3.4–§3.5). Each C batch gets its revision, sequence and attempt range
   at dispatch and inserts its own `_batches` row in its first message; the
   published watermark **W** is the largest revision below which every batch
   is resolved. C batches never touch `_head`, which becomes a durable
   low-water mark that ordered commits and off-path catch-ups advance.
3. **Two-image rows and a bound watermark on every read** (§3.6, §4). A C
   write keeps the row's previous image beside the new one, stamped with
   its revision and dispatch watermark. Every reader statement binds W and
   reads the image visible at `V = GREATEST(W, _head.revision)`, raising
   for a resample in the rare case no image at V survives. Hot reads keep
   one flight and never wait on a writer.
4. **Voids by a key race** (§5). A hole is closed by inserting a void with
   the same `_batches` key; PostgreSQL's unique-index wait makes a late
   commit and a void mutually exclusive and doubles as the drain. Recovery
   and takeover reconcile every hole before serving.

**The non-PG budget decides the verdict** (§7). From the measured stage
costs, C alone (two flights, today's CPU) is about **13.2 ms** at W4/A, no
better than today; with per-slot preparation, smaller batches, prompt
release and halved CPU and tail costs, about **9.4 ms** (≈3,400/s, a tie at
3,200 at best): concurrency removes Q and the ticket wait, but independent
commits pay a flush *residual* the chain does not. Only a one-flight form,
**C-1F**, interpreting on warm facts validated inside the final fenced
statement, gets to **7.3–7.7 ms**; **6.6 ms** needs flush-aligned pacing,
N≈4 and ten slots, every term at its floor. **4,525/s on A is not a
planning target** (a qualified pass below 20%). B is comfortable: **10.3–11.2
ms** for W4/B and W5 mix/B at 2,262 (limit 14.15).

Because the √2 ladder jumps from 3,200 to 4,525, §8 proposes, **for the
author's ruling**, a pre-registered quarter-octave rung (3,805 on A, 1,903
on B) for every tied system, controls re-run in the same campaign. At 3,805
(8.41 ms) C-1F is plausible; it wins only if Supabase fails that rung.
Rulings R1–R6 are in §12. The reader layer can ship default-on; the C lane
stays opt-in until its S1 rerun and witness battery qualify (§9.4).

Citations use paths relative to `snapback4/` at `5fc6e6449b`. Bare names
are in `crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/` or its `postgres/`. §7 labels
every number measured, inferred, assumed or modelled.

### 2.2 What concurrency removes, and what it cannot

C removes **Q's chain-driven part, the ticket wait and the serialization of
preparation CPU**. It removes **none of a request's own flights or its
flush**, and independence **adds a flush residual**: a chained commit
starts its flush as the predecessor's ends, so S pays almost exactly F
(2.70 against a 2.64 ms sync), while an independent commit landing during a
flush waits out the remainder and then a full flush. G7.0's arm C reached
6.93 ms at N=16 on two connections with **sync/batch 1.003**: its gain was
the missing chain, not merged flushes, which appear only with more
committers (0.705 at N=8 on four). Supabase shows the regime C enters:
≈0.115 syncs per commit at 3,200/s on A, about 370 flushes/s on a device
that cannot do many more, p50 8.05 ms, failure at 4,525.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Two lanes and the phase rule

- **O lane.** The accepted G7.2 pipeline (G7.3 once it lands), barriers and
  the solo route, unchanged: ticket chain, nonce CAS on `_head`, fold,
  in-order release, drain before recovery.
- **C lane.** Up to K writer slots, each running one independent
  transaction. No ticket, no `_head` access, no fold: a C batch reads
  nothing an open batch writes. A batch is **open** from dispatch until
  the watermark W covers it (§3.5); resolution alone does not close it.
- **Phase rule.** At any instant every open batch belongs to one
  lane. Switching lanes drains the lane in use: every batch resolved
  (confirmed or voided) and W published. **Barriers drain both lanes**, as
  they drain the pipeline today, and write `_head` (§3.5).
- **Why exclusive.** An O commit advances `_head` with its rows, so a
  reader that sees it takes V at or above it and would read a lower,
  uncommitted C batch's rows at their old images; and an O cascade
  re-seeds sequences from `_head` (parent §4.2), colliding with C ranges
  committed above it. Interleaving per key could avoid both; the cells at
  stake do not need it.

### 3.2 Footprints and commutativity

A member's footprint is computed **before dispatch** from the G3b bounded
closure plan (`program_batch::bounded_plan`, `committer/closure.rs`), which
already enumerates a conservative finite closure of possible reads,
including rule probes, hidden examined rows and alternative control-flow
paths (controls §3.3, "Full-dependency independence rule"). Two batches
**commute** if neither writes anything the other reads or writes. Entries
are row keys where the plan derives the key from arguments or level-0
facts, and **whole tables** (or index spans) where a dependent level's keys
depend on fetched data; a table entry meets every row of that table.
Read-read overlap is fine. Coarsening can lose concurrency, never a
dependency.

| Dependency | Footprint entry | C lane, first slices | Later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rows read by key, including rule probes and absent keys | Row key (absence is the key) | Admitted | — |
| Rows written | Row key | Existing rows, unchanged index keys (G3b's selector) | Inserts and deletes need versioned index entries (§10) |
| Dependent-level reads | Table or index span | Admitted as table entries | Span entries |
| Ranges, scan boundaries, uniqueness | Index span | Read-only spans admitted; no writer changes a key | — |
| Maintained totals | Owner row read; source writes excluded | Reads admitted; maintained writes excluded | Commutative class (committer §4.5) |
| Caps, `_sequences`, allocations | — | Excluded | — |
| Client receipts `(principal, op id)` | Receipt key | Excluded (unkeyed only) | Slice C6 with seq-bounded receipt reads |
| Effects, outbox, presence marks | — | Excluded | Slice C6; claimers stay barriers |
| Authority: sessions, identity, generation, `_meta` | Shared read | Admitted; never written in the C lane (its writers are barriers) | — |
| Time | — | Time-independent programs; no `not before` schema (`program_pipeline.rs:183–187`) | — |
| Presence and ephemeral reads | — | Excluded | — |
| Engine entropy | Counter-mode by revision (§3.4) | Programs drawing no entropy until counter mode lands | — |
| Minted ids, `_change_evidence` prune | Unaffected; prune intervals disjoint by seq | Admitted | — |

Within a batch, members may still conflict: the overlay serializes them in
revision order as today, and the batch's footprint is the union. A
member whose plan cannot bound its closure is ineligible.

### 3.3 Routing, holds and lane switching

- **The open index U** maps each row key and table in an open C batch's
  footprint to that batch, split into reads and writes. Keying U by
  *open* rather than *unresolved* is essential: a batch that committed
  while a lower one is still out is not yet in any reader's V, and a
  successor that read or rewrote its rows would act on an image no prefix
  contains (§3.6).
- **Formation.** The committer scans its mailbox in FIFO order. An
  eligible member disjoint from U joins the forming batch. A conflicting
  member is **held**. A held member blocks later members **of the same
  principal**; others may overtake it, since revision order is assignment
  order and members of different clients have no arrival-order guarantee.
  A held member joins the first batch formed after W covers its blockers,
  which is also when their callers are answered; a hot key therefore moves
  one batch per watermark advance, every queued writer of it in that batch.
- **Dispatch.** A batch is dispatched when a slot is free, the mailbox is
  non-empty and at least g has elapsed since the previous dispatch. g is
  adaptive, aiming at the N* of §7.3 (about 4–6 on both links), with N ≤ 32.
  **Back-pressure:** no dispatch while `next_revision − W.r > 512` or 2K
  batches are open, which keeps the in-flight sequence span far below
  `_change_evidence`'s 1,000-sequence prune window (`group_tail.rs:23`).
- **Switching.** C→O when held or ineligible members exceed 25% of
  arrivals over 50 ms (exact W5); O→C when at least 90% are eligible and
  unconflicted over 50 ms; minimum dwell 100 ms. W5 exact therefore stays
  on G7.2, which already wins it; W4 and W5 mix stay on C.
- **Idle members.** An idle C-eligible member is a one-member C batch; the
  solo route stays for ineligible members, as a barrier.

### 3.4 Revisions, sequences, attempts and the batch record

The committer's sequencer (parent §3.6) assigns each C batch, at dispatch
and in dispatch order, a revision range `[r_lo, r_hi]`, a public sequence
range `[s_lo, s_hi]` (empty if no member publishes), a fresh attempt from
the monotone counter, its effective time `at = max(T, pin)` from its
predecessor in revision order (committer §6) and an entropy key. One
sequencer serves both lanes, so revision order is total.

```sql
CREATE TABLE _batches (
  rev_lo BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, rev_hi BIGINT NOT NULL,
  seq_lo BIGINT, seq_hi BIGINT, attempt TEXT NOT NULL,
  state TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('committed','void')),
  at BIGINT NOT NULL);
```

**The record is the batch's lock and its durable commit record.** A C
batch inserts its row with `state='committed'` as the first write after
the owner fence in its first message. Its uncommitted tuple is what a void
waits on (§5.1); its committed row is the proof the batch committed, read
with its attempt so a reused key cannot be mistaken for it. `_commits`,
`_log`, `_change_evidence` and `_outbox` stay keyed by public sequence and
are written by the final message as today. Rows below `_head.revision` are
pruned in bounded steps, like evidence.

**Entropy** becomes counter-mode: each member draws from a keyed stream
indexed by its revision, so concurrent preparation needs no chaining and a
voided batch's draws are simply never used. Until that lands, C-eligible
members are those whose plans draw none. Minted ids already tolerate gaps
(`Invocation::minted`).

### 3.5 The watermark W and `_head`

**W** is the pair (W.r, W.s): the largest revision such that every revision
at or below it belongs to a batch that is **resolved**, either committed
and confirmed on its own connection, or voided and the void confirmed, and
the public sequence at that revision. W advances only on resolutions, in
the committer, under the publication lock, which in one step installs
G5/G6 publication for the newly covered batches, wakes `/changes` and
long waits, and releases replies and continuations in revision order.
Readers sample W from one atomic pair.

**`_head` is a durable low-water mark** under C (amending store-port §7.2),
written by O commits (the G7.1 nonce CAS), barriers, recovery and
**catch-ups**: a fenced `UPDATE _head SET seq=$s, revision=$r, attempt=$a
WHERE singleton AND attempt=$last` about every 1,000 revisions or 100 ms
of a C phase, on a free slot, off every request's path. A catch-up may
commit asynchronously: losing one leaves an older mark and recovery scans
further. Invariants: `_head.revision ≤ W.r` in a C phase, equality after
each drain, and **the durable prefix (`_head` plus the contiguous resolved
`_batches` above it) is never below W**, which advances only on confirmed
synchronous commits. The first O transaction after a C phase CASes against
the last `_head` the committer wrote, starts sequences at W.s+1, and leaves
`_head` at its end; the tail's contiguity check (`group_tail.rs:149`) takes
W.s as its base.

### 3.6 Two-image rows: how a commit stays hidden above W

`r` gains three nullable columns: `orev BIGINT`, the revision of the last C
write; `owm BIGINT`, that batch's dispatch watermark; and `odata`, the type
of `data`. Adding nullable columns without a default changes only the
catalog; none is indexed, so updates stay HOT. A C batch's image update
replaces `group_tail.rs:10–11` with:

```sql
UPDATE r SET odata = r.data, data = w.data, orev = w.rev, owm = $w
FROM <images> AS w(t, id, data, rev)
WHERE r.t = w.t AND r.id = w.id AND (r.orev IS NULL OR r.orev <= $w)
```

followed by a raising count check. O-lane and barrier writes keep writing
`data` alone.

**Invariant.** For every row x: `data` is x's latest committed image; and
if x's last writer is a C batch, `odata` is x's image at every revision in
`[owm(x), orev(x))`. *Proof.* When c wrote x, x was in c's write
footprint, so no other open batch read or wrote x, and every earlier
writer of x was covered by c's dispatch watermark W_c = owm(x).
Hence `data` at c's write was x's image at W_c, and no batch between W_c
and c wrote x: it would have been open alongside c and touched x. O
writes run only when no C batch is open and advance `_head`, so the
stale stamps they leave lie below every reader's V (§4.1).

**The `orev ≤ $w` predicate is a tripwire.** A misclassified footprint
whose conflicting writer has already committed raises and aborts the batch
as a known abort. It cannot catch a conflict whose writer has not committed
yet, so the router, not the tripwire, is the mechanism, and the
differential in §9.2 is what qualifies it.

**Costs.** Stamps and `odata` exist only on C-written rows, whose updates
write about twice the tuple bytes to WAL; rows over 2 KiB are ineligible at
first, keeping TOAST copies out. No cleanup is needed; doctor reports them.

### 3.7 The flights of a C batch

**P1, one message:** `BEGIN … READ COMMITTED READ WRITE` with `SET LOCAL
synchronous_commit=on`, the settings guard (`flight.rs:44`), the raising
owner fence, the `_batches` insert, `pg_backend_pid()` and
`pg_current_xact_id()`, the generation and identity check, and the composed
authority and level-0 statements (`batch.rs`), every row read through
§4.1's expression at the dispatch W. CPU then runs on the slot's thread.
**Final message:** timeouts from the earliest member deadline; the
generation and identity re-check; the two-image apply and vector tail, each
count a raising assertion; timeouts reset to zero; COMMIT. **Two flights
per batch for every N**: G7.3's shape under its ruling-4 conditions. C-1F
folds P1 into the final message (§7.2).

**Locks.** A C transaction holds the fence's key share, its own rows and
its own `_batches` key, none shared with another C transaction, so C
transactions never wait on each other and their commits can share flushes.

### 3.8 Why the committed history is serial-equivalent

Order all batches by revision. A C batch c reads its facts at its dispatch
watermark W_c. Every lower batch was either covered by W_c, or
open at c's dispatch and therefore touches nothing in c's footprint.
So **c's facts equal the state after every lower batch, restricted to c's
footprint**, and its members run exactly as the serial reference `run`
would at their revisions, with the same rules, refusals, meters, images,
times and sequence positions; the overlay's in-batch induction (controls
§3.3) is unchanged. A void is a no-op in the reference and no higher batch
depended on it, so **a known abort never cascades to another C batch**,
unlike G7.2 (parent §4.2). Survivors rerun at new ranges above everything
dispatched; nobody has been answered, so moving them later is allowed. The
committer §3.5 oracle replays committed members in revision order, voids as
no-ops, transcripts carrying entropy, minted ids and `at`. O phases keep the
parent's argument; phase changes are drains, so the arguments compose.

## 4. Visibility on every reader path

### 4.1 The reader rule

Every Postgres reader statement or snapshot binds the pair (W.r, W.s),
sampled immediately before dispatch, and computes in its own snapshot its
**visible revision V** and every row's image:

```sql
V.r = GREATEST($w_r, (SELECT revision FROM _head WHERE singleton))
image(x) = CASE WHEN x.orev IS NULL OR x.orev <= V.r THEN x.data
                WHEN x.owm <= V.r THEN x.odata
                ELSE sb4_stale() END
```

with V.s likewise for sequence-keyed tables. `sb4_stale()` is a VOLATILE
function, so the planner never folds it, raising a named SQLSTATE.
**Lemma 1:** every batch at or below W.r is visible to the snapshot. A
synchronous commit becomes visible after its local flush and before its
reply; W advances only after that reply; the reader samples W, then sends
its statement, whose snapshot is taken later. **Lemma 2:** every image
returned is the row's image at V. An O commit is visible only with the
`_head` it advanced, so V covers it and every stamp it left; a row whose
last C writer is at or below V shows `data`; one whose writer is above V
shows `odata`, valid on `[owm, orev)`, which contains V when `owm ≤ V`.
**The third branch** covers a reader delayed between sampling W and taking
its snapshot long enough for a batch dispatched after its sample to rewrite
a row it examines: no image at V survives, so the statement raises and the
reader resamples W (now at least that `owm`) and retries, at most three
times before a retryable `E_STORE`. **So every Snapback read is exactly the
state at V**, a prefix of revision order, and reports V as its revision and
V.s as its sequence. A
REPEATABLE READ snapshot binds once for all its statements, the way arm E's
transport binds its head (`flight.rs:58–61`).

**Coverage is structural.** Reader SQL builders take a `Watermark` token;
nothing can name `r`, `_commits`, `_log`, `_change_evidence`, `_ops` or
`_outbox` without one, and a census test rejects reader SQL naming `r.data`
outside the image expression. The token plays the part of arm E's `Bound`
envelope (`early_release.rs:241–251`), without a hold.

### 4.3 Cost on reads

One more int8 bind parameter; one `_head` singleton InitPlan where a
statement does not already read the head (most do); one `CASE` per `r` row
over columns already on the page. No extra flight, no wait, no added lock.
Tables the C lane never writes (S1's messages and rooms) keep NULL stamps,
so the first branch always holds. The stale branch needs a reader delayed
by more than a whole C write cycle (≈6–9 ms) on a row it examines; C0 and
the probe count it. Expected cost is well under 1% of W1–W3 reader CPU;
C1 re-measures W1/W2/W3 at their qualified rungs before anything else lands
(§9). The connection budget is the larger read-side risk (§6, R2).

### 4.4 Raw SQL, inspection, backups and graduation

Inspect views select images through `_sb4_prefix()`, a stable function
returning `_head` plus the contiguous resolved `_batches` above it, so
`snapback sql` and doctor see a consistent prefix. Owner and core roles
reading `r` directly see commits above W out of revision order, **all
durable**: unlike arm E, nothing a raw reader sees can be lost; the deploy
guide states the window. A backup may capture a committed C batch above an
uncommitted one; restore reconciles (§5.4) and loses only unacknowledged
writes, since nothing is acknowledged before W covers it. Graduation drains
writes and exports at the durable prefix.

## 5. Holes, outcomes and recovery

### 5.1 The void and the key race

A **void** for batch c is one fenced, synchronous transaction in one
message: `BEGIN; SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on; <fence>; INSERT INTO
_batches (rev_lo, …, state) VALUES (c.r_lo, …, 'void')`, then a void
`_commits` row for each of c's sequences (`kind='void'`, no touched tables,
zero writes, c's assigned `at`) and an empty-footprint `_change_evidence`
row for each, then COMMIT, with raising counts. It is idempotent and keyed
on c's range.

**Mutual exclusion.** A unique-index insert that meets a tuple inserted by
an in-progress transaction waits for that transaction to end, then raises
23505 if it committed and proceeds if it aborted. c inserted its key in
P1. So:

- c committed: the void raises 23505, and the row's attempt proves it is
  c's: c is **committed**.
- c in progress: the void **waits**, which is the drain, then lands in one
  of the other cases.
- c aborted, or its P1 has not run: the void commits, and a P1 still in
  flight raises 23505 at its insert: c **can never commit**.

So a void never buries committed work. It is issued only for a batch
classified aborted or unknown; its wait is bounded by `lock_timeout`, after
which the committer terminates c's backend by the PID from P1, re-checking
role, tag and token (store-port §5.2), and retries; if termination fails
the store pauses for fenced reacquisition, as G6's sticky rule requires. A
void whose own outcome is unknown is retried: 23505 against a `void` row
bearing its attempt shows the first one committed. **On the wire** a void
is a sequence with no events and an empty footprint, which `/changes` and
evidence certification read as "nothing changed"; R5 asks whether every
client accepts that.

### 5.2 Outcome table

C batches are classified per batch, on their own connection, as controls
§3.1 and parent §4.1 specify; what changes is that **no outcome of one C
batch reclassifies another**.

| Evidence for C batch c | Classification | c's callers | W |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COMMIT tag after passed assertions, ReadyForQuery `I`, no warning | Confirmed | Released in revision order once W covers c | Resolved |
| Error before COMMIT with ROLLBACK acknowledged (raised assertion, tripwire, 55P03/57014 under the deadline, 40P01, 23505 on its own `_batches` key) | Known abort | Controls §3.1: remove a named failed member and rerun the rest, or each alone, within deadlines; fresh ranges | Void, then resolved |
| Lost reply, error or warning after COMMIT was sent, connection loss after the final message | Unknown | `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, no automatic rerun (R3 would let the void's result decide) | Void race decides; resolved either way |
| Lease lost before the final message | Not dispatched; rolled back | `E_STORE_OWNERSHIP` for started members | Void if P1 ran |
| Lease lost after the final message | Actual result if received, else unknown | As above | The new owner reconciles (§5.4) |

### 5.3 Unknown outcomes and the drain

After an unknown, the void race is both the drain and the classification,
so **nothing waits for a ticket probe and nothing is re-seeded**: W simply
stalls at c until the void returns, while batches above c keep committing
within back-pressure. Their replies wait for W. Under ruling 3's present
"no", c's callers stay unknown even though the database answered. **R3**
asks whether a won void (c can never commit) is explicit abort evidence
permitting §3.1's bounded rerun, and whether a lost void (c's own committed
record) may release c's results. The evidence is about c itself, not a
successor's inference, which is what ruling 3's dissent wanted.

### 5.4 Crash recovery and takeover reconciliation

Before an owner serves after start, restart or takeover:

1. Acquire ownership (store-port §5.2). Takeover's `_owner FOR UPDATE` is
   granted only after every old key share is gone, so every old C
   transaction has ended and its `_batches` row is final; a restarting
   owner first terminates its old token's tagged backends.
2. Read `_head` (H) and scan `_batches` above `H.revision` (at most the
   catch-up window plus 512 revisions). Walking up from H, committed and
   void rows extend the prefix; a gap between rows is a hole whose
   transaction ended uncommitted: **void it**, fenced and synchronous.
   Numbering resumes above the highest row.
3. **Exact counts:** each committed record has one `_commits` and one
   `_change_evidence` row per sequence and no void row; each void record
   only void rows; no `_commits` row above `H.seq` outside a record; no
   `orev` inside a void range. Failure refuses to serve with
   `SNAPBACK_C_LANE_RECONCILE` and the evidence.
4. Write `_head` to the reconciled prefix with a fresh attempt, fenced and
   synchronous, and serve with W equal to it.

The old owner's unresolved callers already got unknown or ownership loss;
keyed retries resolve through receipts once the new W covers them (C6).
The old owner, unable to void without its fence, releases nothing above
its last W and refuses.

### 5.5 Fencing, deadlines and back-pressure

- **Fencing.** Every C transaction, void and catch-up takes the owner fence
  first, as a raising command; key shares coexist with each other and with
  renewal, and takeover terminates every tagged writer (store-port
  §5.1–§5.2). Unlike arm E's marker, nothing is unfenced.
- **Deadlines.** The final message's timeouts come from the earliest
  member's remaining deadline; a timeout before COMMIT is a known abort with
  COMMIT skipped; COMMIT runs with both at zero and is never cancelled.
- **Back-pressure.** §3.3's bounds keep a stalled void from growing W's lag
  without limit; past a 250 ms stall the lane stops admitting and reports
  `E_STORE` retryably.

## 6. Interactions

- **G6 read grants and the publication gate.** Closure hides the gap
  between PostgreSQL visibility and in-process installation (controls
  §4.1); Snapback readers cannot see an unpublished C batch at all, so **C
  batches neither close the gate nor count in `inflight`**, and publication
  (including a committed unknown's known write set) installs with each W
  advance. O batches keep the parent's count and sticky uncertainty; a C
  unknown is settled by its void without revoking the grant epoch.
- **Arm E (opt-in).** E releases locks before durability and holds readers
  until D covers their head; C's synchronous commits need neither, and two
  release rules on one read path are not worth it. **R6: `commit=early` and
  the C lane are mutually exclusive**; a store URL selecting both refuses.
- **G7.2 and G7.3.** The O lane is unchanged and keeps W5 exact. G7.3's
  raising assertions and ruling-4 conditions are prerequisites for C's
  co-dispatched COMMIT; C does not need G7.3's chain.
- **Write levers.** Non-authorizing ingress hints and batch plan reuse apply
  unchanged; the plan inventory now also feeds footprints. The CPU and tail
  halvings and prompt release are §7's levers.
- **Receipts and idempotent replay.** First slices admit unkeyed members
  only. In C6 the receipt key joins the footprint, so two copies of one id
  never run concurrently; preflight and the serial re-check read `_ops` at
  seq ≤ V.s and wait on a key an open batch holds. Store binding and
  replay-before-judgment (round-trip §3.1, §7) are unchanged.
- **Deadlines and the G2/G3 table.** §5.2 is the per-batch table; COMMIT
  stays uncancellable; ambiguity stays unknown unless R3 says otherwise.
- **SQLite is unchanged.** Its write lock spans `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` through
  the WAL fsync, so two writer transactions cannot be in flight, there are
  no network flights to overlap, and G3 already dominates at about 7.9 ms
  per fsync. Its readers keep reading `data` (per-store reader SQL); it
  stays the differential's reference; the C lane is `postgres`-only.

## 7. The non-PG budget

### 7.1 What the lane does to Q, X and release

Of W4/A's 15.5 ms, about 8 ms is outside PostgreSQL's final flights: Q
4.2, X 3.66 (1.91 of it the P1 flight, 1.75 committer CPU), release 0.3 and
HTTP 0.63. Under C, **Q** becomes half the dispatch gap plus any slot wait,
no longer tracking a chain; **preparation** leaves the committer thread and
runs in parallel per slot; **release** waits for W, adding head-of-line
time only when a lower batch finishes later (rare at 1–2 ms dispatch gaps;
budgeted at 0.1 ms).

### 7.2 Levers

1. **Per-slot preparation.** At dispatch the committer builds an owned
   batch context (invocations, closure plans, ranges, `at`, entropy key, an
   `Arc` of schema, IR, programs and ceilings, expected generation and
   identity). The slot thread runs P1, interpretation on the shared G3b-par
   pool, authority resolution (a pure function of facts and a clock
   sample), merge, tail construction and the final message. The committer
   keeps collection, routing, assignment and ordered release, about 0.1–0.15
   ms per batch: committer §3.7's owned commands, finished for this class.
2. **Smaller batches with more slots.** Per-batch PG server cost is about
   2.4 ms plus 0.09 ms per member (inferred, §7.3). Smaller N cuts Q and the
   per-member terms but raises commits per second, PG CPU and flush
   contention; the model's best is N≈4–6 at A with K≈8–10.
3. **CPU halving** (≈146 → 73 µs per member, parallel wall included) and
   **tail-executor halving** (≈1.0 → 0.5 ms per batch): the attribution's
   lever targets, owned by the parallel lane.
4. **Prompt release** (0.35 → 0.25 ms): the committer blocks on one select
   over its mailbox and every slot's completion instead of polling (arm E
   measured 0.4–0.5 ms done-to-poll) and releases inside the W advance.
5. **HTTP intake** (0.63 → 0.45 ms): trim intake, admission and encode; the
   injected link delay is not removable work.
6. **C-1F, one flight per batch (conditional, R4).** A C footprint is
   written only through this committer while it owns the store, so a
   bounded write-through mirror of images it committed and W covers
   (dropped wholesale at every barrier, phase change, unknown outcome or
   ownership event), plus the non-authorizing ingress facts, lets the slot
   interpret **before any flight**. The single final message **validates**
   every input inside the fenced transaction: base images by equality in
   the apply's `WHERE` (a mismatch fails the raising count), other read rows
   by equality assertions, session facts by digest against what the
   resolver used, the clock sampled just before dispatch. A mismatch is a
   known abort that reruns two-flight with fresh facts; the authorizing
   facts are still those in the final fenced statement. Cold rows take the
   two-flight form.
7. **Flush-aligned pacing (conditional).** Hold each final message so its
   commit record lands as the running flush ends, predicted from COMMIT
   replies: about F + 0.3 ms of flush wait instead of F plus the residual.
   Unproven, cheap to try, stopped if it does not deliver.

### 7.3 Stage model and inputs

`L = H + Q + P1 + CPU + R + Val + T + A + W_F + rel + S_w` (validation, tail
server, assertions, flush, release, slot wait), at arrival rate λ, batch
size N, commit rate a = λ/N and K slots.

- **Measured** (attribution, G7.0, one-host controls): R = 0.56 (A) and
  1.10 ms (B); F = 2.6 ms pure WAL sync; HTTP 0.63; release 0.30.
- **Inferred from the measured product means:** P1 server = 1.07 +
  0.025N ms (1.35 at N=11.2 on both links); tail server = 1.32 + 0.061N
  ms (2.0 at N≈11; G7.0's slope); preparation CPU = 0.146N ms on the
  critical path (1.63 at N=11.2).
- **Assumed:** C's tail extras (two images, no CAS) +0.12 ms; raising
  assertions 0.10 ms; C-1F validation 0.12–0.15 ms; Q = N/(2λ) + 0.1 ms,
  plus an M/D/1 wait when preparation stays on the committer; slot wait
  from Erlang C, halved for deterministic service.
- **Modelled flush wait:** with unlimited merging, the device idles after
  a flush with probability e^(−aF), so its busy fraction is
  F / (F + e^(−aF)/a), and `W_F = F + busy·F/2 − 0.3 ms`. The calibration
  term makes the model reproduce G7.0's measured arm-C mean of 6.93 ms at
  N=16, A, 4,525/s. W_F rises from 3.1 ms at N=16 to 3.6 ms at N=4.

Nothing is iterated to self-consistency; read every row as ±15%.

### 7.6 Verdict

- **B is reachable with margin.** C + levers puts W4/B and W5 mix/B near
  11 ms against 14.15 ms, about 2.3 ms better than the ordered lane's 13.3
  ms forecast with the same levers. C alone is a 93% occupancy edge.
- **A at 4,525/s (7.07 ms) is not reachable on any column a planner should
  rely on.** Two-flight C with every lever stalls at about 9.4 ms. Only
  C-1F gets under 8 ms (7.3–7.7), and only the optimistic column, with
  pacing working, N=4, ten slots and every lever at target, reaches 6.6
  ms (93% occupancy). The residual flush wait (3.4–3.6 ms against S's 2.8),
  P1 and tail server time (about 2.4 ms per batch, regardless of N), and a
  request's own two link traversals remain. I put a qualified 4,525 pass
  below 20%.
- **A at 3,805/s (8.41 ms) is plausible with C-1F**: 7.3–7.7 ms, 87–92%
  occupancy. It is a strict win only if R1 is accepted and Supabase,
  re-run in the same campaign, fails 3,805; Supabase at 3,200 already has
  p50 8.02 ms on a saturated flush device, so that is likely but must be
  measured.
- **What would change the A verdict:** cutting PG's fixed per-batch server
  time (the settings guard, raising fence, JSON record sets and CTE tail
  cost about 2.4 ms per batch on A), which is controls §10.2–§10.3
  territory (typed layout, lowering to SQL), not this RFC's.

## 8. Measurement fairness: a pre-registered quarter-octave rung

**This is a method amendment to S1 §4 and §6 and needs the author's
agreement (R1).** The registered write ladder is 100·2^(k/2): …, 1,600,
2,262, 3,200, 4,525, …. A system whose ceiling lies anywhere in
[3,200, 4,525) reports 3,200; so a strict win over a 3,200 control
requires 4,525 even when both systems' real ceilings differ by 30%.
Supabase's own curves show the gap: W4/A passes 3,200 and fails 4,525
(p50 1,005 ms); W4/B and W5 mix/B pass 1,600 and fail 2,262.

Proposed, registered before any C-lane product result:

1. **Quarter rungs** 100·2^((2k+1)/4): 951, 1,345, 1,903, 2,691, 3,805,
   5,382 per second, interleaved with the existing rungs.
2. **When.** In any cell where systems' √2 ceilings are equal, every tied
   system, controls included, is tested at the quarter rung above and
   re-tested at the tied rung. A quarter rung cannot turn a full-rung loss
   into a win, so other cells need nothing new.
3. **How.** The unchanged runner, fixtures, links, pinning, SLO, error and
   counter rules and three repetitions; advocate configurations as
   published, changes admitted under S1 §4's existing rule before
   measurement. Controls run in the **same campaign window as Snapback,
   interleaved by repetition**: the 2026-10-24 controls predate host drift
   the attribution saw (the historical A/2,262 S pass did not reproduce).
4. **Verdict.** A system's ceiling is its highest union-ladder rung at which
   all three repetitions pass; strict wins compare ceilings; equal ceilings
   stay ties; both ladders and every failure are published.

Here that means 3,805 on W4/A and 1,903 on W4/B and W5 mix/B. The rule binds
every system alike and is fixed before measurement, so ladder spacing
decides no verdict in either direction.

### 9.2 Witnesses

1. **Randomized serial-equivalence differential against N=1 in revision
   order**, extending G3b's paired oracle (seeds, 192 members, both
   stores): random batch boundaries; K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}; adversarial
   completion order across slots; disjoint, hot-key and 1% mixes; refusals,
   barriers and lane switches; comparing outcomes, meters, rows, images,
   sequences, change evidence, log, head, revisions and attempts. Voids
   count as no-ops in the reference.
2. **Adversarial footprint collisions.** Pairs that share a hidden rule
   probe, an absent key, a dependent-level table, a scan boundary or a
   maintained owner row, generated against the closure planner, must be
   held or routed to O. A planted footprint bug must be caught by the
   differential, and by the tripwire when the writer has committed.
3. **Faults per flight with concurrent batches:** P1 failure, apply or

   COMMIT, socket loss after the final message, a hung backend that forces

5. **Reader prefix witnesses, one per §4.2 class:** concurrent readers
   assert every image and sequence-keyed row is the state at V, V never
   decreases in a session, and an acknowledged write is visible to every
   read that starts after its reply; a hook that delays a reader between
   its W sample and its snapshot across two writes of one row must hit the
   stale branch, never a wrong image; G6 hits racing W serve only installed
   publication.
6. **Locks, RT0 and order:** zero `pg_stat_database.deadlocks` delta and no
   wait between C transactions except a void on its own batch; two flights
   per C batch for every N (one under C-1F), one per void, read budgets
   unchanged; release order equals revision order under every completion
   order.

### 9.4 Stop rules and default-on

Stop the C lane on any serial-equivalence, prefix or release-order
violation, a lost acknowledged write, a void and a commit both committed
for one key, a single-store deadlock, a read-rung regression (revisit R2
first), C0's stop rule, W4/B not at least 2 ms below the ordered lane with
the same levers, or PG CPU per write up more than 30%. **Default-on:** C1
ships default-on once its reads re-measure clean, since with W ≡ `_head` it
changes no behavior. The C lane is opt-in (`lane=disjoint` in the store
URL, refused with `commit=early`) until C3 and C4 pass §9.2 and qualify on
S1; then it becomes the Postgres default with the router choosing lanes.

## 10. Alternatives considered

- **The naive variant** (parent §7): unsound as recorded there; §2.3 maps
  each defect to its repair.
- **A publication epoch gate on every reader.** Readers cannot be kept from
  seeing in-place rows, so a gate can only stall a read until the committed
  set is a prefix again, which under continuous out-of-order commits is
  most of the time. Arm E's similar hold cost 4/6 read cells under
  concurrent writes. Rejected.
- **Full versioned rows and index entries.** General, but it changes every
  index scan, adds retention and vacuum work and migrates storage. The
  two-image row is the one-deep case the disjoint-update class needs; full
  versioning is the extension if C must cover inserts and deletes.
- **Private intents with an ordered publisher** (controls §10.4's first
  redesign). Durable intents commit concurrently; one connection installs
  them in order with an asynchronous commit, safe because intents are
  durable. Sound, but every request pays a second final flight and the
  install, about +1 ms on A. Kept as the fallback if C0 rejects two images.
- **Two-phase commit with ordered `COMMIT PREPARED`.** `COMMIT PREPARED`
  always flushes, so each batch flushes twice, and enabling
  `max_prepared_transactions` changes the matched configuration. Rejected.
- **Exported snapshots taken at prefix moments.** Under out-of-order commits
  such moments may not exist, and each import needs a live exporter.
  Rejected. **SERIALIZABLE instead of footprints** gives no revision order
  and retries on conclusive abort (controls §10.4). Rejected.
- **Arm E with small batches and the same levers.** An ordered rival with
  no holes and no visibility layer; its chain allows about 660 batches/s,
  but its reads hold for durability and a raw reader can see rows a crash
  loses. C0 measures it beside C.
- **Writes on borrowed reader-pool connections** (Supabase's shape). Better
  connection economy, but it reverses ruling 6 and needs exclusive custody
  across two flights. Deferred in favour of R2's elastic dedicated slots.

## 11. Risks

- **Census incompleteness.** A reader that reads `data` directly serves a
  non-prefix state; the `Watermark` token, the census and the prefix hammer
  are the defenses, and later readers inherit them.
- **Footprint misclassification.** A dependency outside the closure plan
  breaks serial equivalence silently unless its writer has committed; the
  differential and adversarial generators are mandatory, as for G3b.
- **Model error.** The flush residual, small-N PG CPU and SMT sharing on the
  four app logical CPUs can each exceed §7's inputs; then C helps B, not A.
- **Connections, WAL and the wire.** Eight to ten slots leave 21–23
  readers; C-written rows roughly double their WAL bytes; empty void
  sequences must be harmless to every client (R5).
- **Complexity.** Two lanes, two release paths and reconciliation; phase
  exclusivity keeps the argument small, and relaxing it needs its own RFC.

## 12. Open questions and rulings needed

**Rulings (author or orchestrator):**

- **R1, the quarter-octave rung (author; S1 §4 and §6 method amendment).**
  Adopt §8 before any C-lane product result? Recommended: yes.
- **R2, connections.** Elastic dedicated writer slots, K from 1 to 10,
  drawn from the 31 non-owner connections while the C lane is active and
  returned after 5 s idle, with a 20-reader floor at which W1/W2 are
  re-measured. Recommended: yes.
- **R3, void-resolved outcomes.** Is a won void explicit abort evidence
  (bounded rerun allowed), and may a lost void against c's own committed
  record release c's results? Recommended: the abort side after §9.2
  items 3–4 pass, the commit side after a release-path witness; ruling 3's
  "no" holds until then.
- **R4, C-1F freshness.** Is a decision on carried facts, validated by
  equality inside the final fenced statement with the clock sampled just
  before dispatch, round-trip §7's and committer §3.6's fresh final check?
  Recommended: yes, with round-trip §7's authority tests on that path.
- **R5, void sequences.** Accept a public sequence with no events and an
  empty footprint as wire-compatible? Recommended: yes, after an SDK
  `/changes` and sync conformance test; otherwise voids need a design that
  keeps them out of the public sequence.
- **R6, C and arm E exclusive.** Recommended: yes.

**Open questions:** whether pacing holds flush waits near F under proxy
jitter; the best N and K, and whether one adaptive g serves both links;
whether hot-key holds hurt W5 mix's p99; `odata`'s cost on wide rows; P2
would need W from `_sb4_prefix()`; whether PG's per-batch server time
(§7.6) can be cut inside the current layout.

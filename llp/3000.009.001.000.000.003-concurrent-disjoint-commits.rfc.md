## 1. Summary

Reads win 6/6 S1 cells; W5 exact (A, B) and W5 mix/A win; W4/B and W5 mix/B
tie at 1,600; **W4/A loses, 2,262 against the Supabase advocate's 3,200**. A
strict win needs **W4/A at 4,525/s, a mean of at most 7.07 ms with 32 clients
(≈6.4 at a passing 90% occupancy), and W4/B and W5 mix/B at 2,262/s, at most
14.15 ms**; Supabase fails both rungs; the product measures about 15 and 17 ms.

**Accepted as a staged, gated program** (§3.1), each stage built only when its
own gate passes: **Stage P** (§3.2, in progress, P0 first) attributes and cuts
per-batch server and committer cost on both lanes; **L′** (§3.3), only under
its freshness protocol, validates warm facts in one fenced flight before an
absolute deadline; **C0′** (§3.5), a product-shaped harness with measured
costs injected, gates **C** (§4–§6: footprint-disjoint concurrent commits,
readers kept on a prefix by two-image rows and a bound watermark, holes closed
by a key race), **ordered C** (§3.6: arm E's early release, two-image reads at
the durable point) and **L** (§3.4, a fully charged lowered flight), each
needing 4,525/s on A under S1's criteria with a transfer margin. **Budgets**
(§8; judgment): B is reachable on the ordered lane, Stage P alone marginal to
likely (11.3–14.3 ms), L′ with margin (9.3–12.3); A's best shape, C at N=16,
K=2–3, on fast L′, is 6.8–8.9 ms against a practical 6.4, about **one in six
to eight**. R1 is the author's pending decision (§9); R2–R6 are decided (§13).
Citations use paths relative to `snapback4/` at `5fc6e6449b`; bare names are
in `crates/snapback4-core/src/`, its `engine/` or `engine/postgres/`.

### 2.2 What a strict W4/A win requires

With 32 clients at the edge of capacity, Little's law sets the bar: a mean of
at most 32/4,525 = **7.07 ms**, about 6.4 ms with the margin three passing
repetitions need (G7.0's arm C passed at 6.93 ms, worst intended p99 84 ms
against 100). HTTP 0.45–0.63 ms, 0.56 ms of link per flight, the flush (2.64
ms sync, 2.70 ms PG commit) and release 0.25–0.35 ms are fixed, so **one
flight leaves about 3 ms for CPU, server work, phasing and the flush residual;
a second leaves 2.5.**

- **Supabase is flush-bound too**: one `set_config` and one SECURITY DEFINER
  RPC per transaction; at 3,200/s ≈98% of the device, p50 8.02 ms; it fails
  4,525. The contest is everything else.
- **The shapes** (G7.0, SQL only): arm C at N=16 on two connections, 6.93 ms
  at 4,525/s, sync/batch 1.003; E2, early release at N=16, 7.01–7.03 ms,
  **though product arm E qualified only 1,600/s** (§3.5); N=8 on four merged
  flushes (0.705) was slower, 7.74 ms; ordered S at N=32, 8.39–8.73 ms. The
  product adds HTTP, interpretation and authority CPU, a slower P1, phasing
  and release.
- **The ordered lane's limit.** At depth d a request spans about d chains
  (≈4.7 ms after G7.3), so depth two cannot pass 4,525; at depth one with N≈32
  the per-member tail matters as much as concurrency. The best shape's floor,
  every term low, is ≈6.8 ms (§8.2); the raw-PG token reference measures p50
  5.43 ms on W5 mix/B at 4,525/s ([one-host
  final](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261024-s1-onehost-final/README.md)).

### 3.2 Stage P: Postgres per-batch server cost

**P0 first, attributing both sides.** The ≈2.4 ms is a fitted intercept
(flight minus RTT against N: P1 server 1.07 + 0.025N, tail 1.32 + 0.061N ms),
not an attribution (Fable). Before any cut is credited, on W4/A, W4/B and W5
mix/B, P0 measures **server time per statement** (`pg_stat_statements` with
planning, nested `auto_explain`, per-batch clocks); **the per-member slope by
relation** (the `r` image, `_commits`, `_change_evidence`, the prune, keyed
`_ops`; Fable), since a post-P slope near 0.05 ms/member is about 23 times
controls §10.2's loopback `r_only` marginal of 2.15 µs and says nothing about
image bytes until P0 names its cause (grok); **committer CPU by phase** on
per-thread clocks (Fable), splitting today's 0.51 ms parallel wall, 1.12 ms
pre-residual and 0.29 ms per batch of callbacks, against which §8 books L′;
and **a depth-one cohort-hold run** on today's ordered lane (§4.3's rule, h ∈
{0.2, 0.4, 0.6} ms), settling the phasing row for O1 and C. P can buy at most
the gap between product PG CPU of 263–300 µs per write at N≈11 and G7.0 arm
C's 134 at N=16 on the product's own tail SQL. **Suspects:** the `DO`-block
guard and fence (`flight.rs:44`, `ownership.rs:74`), planned on every flight,
and the nine-CTE tail (`group_tail.rs:7–47`), parsing `$1::text::jsonb` seven
times with empty CTEs for W4.

**Changes, keeping today's semantics:** (1) guard and fence as **check
functions** called by prepared `EXECUTE`, each still raising S4001 or S4002
before any data command in its message, the fence VOLATILE, taking `FOR KEY
SHARE` on `_owner`, still the first lock and its own command (round-trip §7,
store-port §5.1); (2) **typed arrays** (`unnest` over `text[]` and `int8[]`)
instead of seven JSON parses, ahead of binary Bind (round-trip §3.6); (3)
**shape-specialised tails**, each present CTE keeping its raising count; (4)
**set-based per-member work** (grok), generic plans confirmed, P1's statements
fused where shapes allow, and **no prune on any batch's path** (§6.4); (5)
**the batch-granular commit index** (Fable), if P0 finds `_commits` and
evidence leading the slope: one `_commits` row and one evidence row per batch
over [s_lo, s_hi] instead of one per member (`group_tail.rs:9–21`), per-member
fields and footprints as arrays, and readers (`footprint_between`,
`/changes`, sync evidence, the committer §3.5 oracle, recovery's counts,
inspect views) decoding by sequence. Two of three per-member writes go on both
lanes, a void becomes one row, and a row is pruned only once its whole span is
below the floor; as a history-representation change it ships behind a `_meta`
marker the opening path checks, one release after the check, with a
differential over histories and R5's conformance test. **The typed layout
removes no `_commits` or evidence work (controls §10.2), so the relation split
decides between them:** a slope still near 0.05 with images leading means
prototyping the typed layout for this fragment before C1 (astra).

**Function lifecycle** (astra, grok, Fable): digest-named
(`sb4_fence_<digest>`), schema-qualified functions are created only if
absent, never replaced, by the opening transaction under the head lock
(`postgres/store.rs:284`), fenced by the inline `DO` fence; older binaries
keep the `DO` form, a binary starting mid-lease adds a name, and old versions
drop only after a takeover. Lease acquisition and doctor validate signature,
owner, `search_path`, `SECURITY INVOKER`, VOLATILE, language and body digest,
dropping the lease on failure; prepared state resets on upgrade and
reconnect; a missing function raises 42883 before data, refusing like S4001;
privileged mid-lease alteration stays outside the boundary (controls §6).
**Prediction (assumed, pending P0):** fixed server 2.4 → 1.2–1.7 ms, slope
0.086 → 0.052–0.069 ms per member; every fault still raises before data;
oracles and difftest unchanged.

### 3.3 L′: one flight on validated warm facts

The slot interprets each member before any flight, in parallel, on a bounded
mirror and ingress-carried session facts; the final message validates every
consumed fact inside the fenced transaction, and any failure **raises and
aborts the attempt**, the batch rerunning two-flight. **L′ is built only with
the freshness protocol below** (astra); without it the two-flight path stays.

- **Mirror:** images this committer published plus **validated read facts**
  (S1 never writes `memberships`; Fable), dropped at every barrier, lane
  change, unknown outcome and ownership event; rows an unresolved batch writes
  are cold, and a stale entry costs only a miss.
- **Targets: a locked probe** classifies each row (absent, hidden, rule
  refusal, image mismatch, ok) against the predicted class, so a changed row
  never commits as `NOT_FOUND` (grok).
- **Reads:** points and absences by key; **a span only if re-read whole under
  a phantom-rejecting lock** (grok: the O lane's ticket or a C phase's frozen
  `i`), such as `exists memberships[.room, viewer]`, a `key::encode` prefix
  over `i.k` (`rows.rs:156`) asserted with count and ids as `k >= $p AND k <
  $p_next` (Fable), one row by uniqueness.
- **Stored authority facts:** generated from every `SessionFacts` field the
  shared resolver consumes (`identity/facts.rs:7`), absent metadata asserted
  absent; `ordinary` kinds only, others taking the two-flight resolver; a
  census fails on any unasserted input.
- **Host authority inputs** (astra). The resolver also consumes `ServiceSample`:
  `now` and the in-memory **supervisor revision** (`identity/facts.rs:33`;
  sampled at ingress, `crates/snapback4/src/auth.rs:192`), which failed worker
  retirement advances with database facts unchanged
  (`crates/snapback4/src/dev_worker.rs:822–831`, `fence_revision` at `:331`).
  **The slot resamples it (an Acquire load) after interpretation, immediately
  before writing the final message, and a member resolved under another value
  misses**, keeping the order of the two-flight path's in-transaction sample
  (`mutation.rs:65`); a fence after it linearizes after the request, as today.
- **Time: an absolute deadline** (astra). Relative SQL timeouts start only when
  PostgreSQL runs the message and `at` can lag the authority clock (committer
  §3.6), so a message sent before expiry could execute after it against
  unchanged facts. **The message's last authority command raises a named
  SQLSTATE unless `clock_timestamp()` precedes a bound**: the earliest
  authority deadline consumed (session and read-cap expiry, claim windows),
  mapped to the server's clock by a measured offset, minus a margin ε for its
  uncertainty and drift. Run after every assertion, a pass proves validation
  preceded each deadline on committer §6's clock while the mapping holds
  within ε. **ε is qualified:** an RTT-bounded offset sample at lease
  acquisition and every renewal, ε = 50 ms to start; L′ falls back to two
  flights while the sample is stale or wider than ε, or after a clock step
  outside G6's bounds. A deadline passed at dispatch, or a raise, is a **miss
  resolved afresh** on two flights, where an expired session refuses as
  today. Rows' lifetime edges keep committer §6's rule (`at` before B).
- **Keyed members join on the O lane** (Fable: the SDK always sends `{id,
  args}`, `packages/snapback4/src/client.ts:1013`): receipt absence is a point
  assertion after the head CAS (round-trip §3.5). C stays unkeyed (C4).

**Cost and keep rule.** Preparation is 0.146 ms per member on today's critical
path; L′ interprets in parallel during collection, targeting 20–30 µs per
member (Fable), plus ≈0.12–0.15 ms of validation at N=16; **C0′ uses the CPU
L′ measures, never the target.** Kept if one flight beats G7.3 + P by ≥0.5 ms
of W4/A mean at 3,200/s, differential and §10.2 item 8 clean, on W4, W5 mix
and keyed W4.

### 3.4 Stage L, conditional: lowering the point update

W4 reads a counter (`! else NOT_FOUND`) and returns `update counters[c.id] {
n: c.n + 1 }` under `.owner = viewer or (.owner = null and exists
memberships[.room, viewer])` ([S1
schema](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/snapback-app/snapback/schema.q)).
Lowered, one statement per batch would lock and classify each target, update
`ok` members and return classifications, probes and images. **Eligibility:**
one keyed read `! else E`, optional `require`, one `update` of that row; rules
compiling to SQL over the row, viewer, arguments and keyed probes on unwritten
tables; no index move, maintained write, effect, presence, time, entropy or
`ctx.sql`; images under 2 KiB; meters covering the fixed trace, else fallback
before execution (controls §10.3); one member per row key; unkeyed.

**Its true cost** (Fable). `n` lives in the JSON image (grok), so the statement
must produce `r.data` byte for byte (canonical tagged JSON with sorted keys,
`value.rs:378`; the i53 model, `value.rs:118`; omitted literal defaults,
`rows.rs` `row_image`; `key::encode` probe prefixes, which `jsonb` cannot
stand in for) and footprint JSON in-statement: a second executor and encoder.
For W4, where worker w increments only counter w
(`experiments/scale-controls/src/load.rs:139–147`), L′'s mirror is always warm
and its server work equals L's, so **L buys only interpreter CPU**, which L′
attacks directly; a C-L arm skipping this encoding authorizes nothing (§3.5).
**Contract if built** (astra): predicates judge both the old and the next
image; Quarry's null and overflow rules, constraints, returned-image
visibility and the complete meter trace; eligibility restricted where these,
sync and log production or lifecycle cannot be reproduced; authority as L′;
lost replies per §6.3; controls §10.3's differential, boundary sweeps and
planted fault; `snapback check` names each fallback. A general compiler
optimisation (grok, astra), **built only if C0′'s fully charged C-L arm passes
the gate where C on measured L′ does not**, else deferred behind the typed
layout (controls §10.2).

### 3.5 Stage C0′: the product-shaped harness and its gate

G7.0's harness, with no product edit, sends the product's messages (checks,
fence, session validation and deadline check, timeouts, raising counts and
COMMIT, as built today **and** as cut by P; `_batches`, two-image apply and
tripwire; L′'s probes and validation; for C-L, a hand-lowered W4 statement),
built now beside P. **Injected costs keep their contention and correlation**
(astra): HTTP, critical-path CPU and fill are replayed per batch from the
post-L′ ledger's joint distribution as CPU work on the product's pinned app
cores, not as independent delays, since C's two slots keep the device ≈75%
busy and gain only in phase (Fable). It measures ordered release behind W,
formation under §4.3's cohort rule, catch-up and void flushes, and W5 mix's
validation misses. **Arms:** O1 (one flight, depth one and two, N ∈ {8, 16,
32}); C2f; C-L′ at **measured** per-member CPU, and at 20–30 µs **as
sensitivity only**; C-L, whose statement **pays the full canonical image
encoding** (§3.4), else also sensitivity; ordered C (§3.6); C at N ∈ {1, 2,
4, 8, 16}, K ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16}, pacing on and off with **its hold
inside residence**. It reports mean, p50, p99, syncs per batch, flush waits,
PG CPU, stale raises, a prefix hammer, the void race under kill and reader
statement cost, with three open-loop confirmations at 4,525/s on A and
2,262/s on B.

**The gate** (grok, astra, Fable). Only an **authorizing arm**, on measured L′
CPU or a fully charged C-L statement, can start C, ordered C or L, and only if
its best configuration, costs injected, (1) **passes 4,525/s on A in all three
confirmations under S1's unchanged arrival, error and p99 criteria**, which
decide (a mean ≤7.07 ms is necessary, never sufficient); (2) **clears a
transfer margin**, every confirmation's mean ≤6.4 ms or a pass with every
injected term at its high end; and (3) beats the best O1 measured the same way
by ≥1.0 ms of mean, or passes where O1 fails. **Arm E's precedent is why**
(Fable): G7.0's E2 passed 4,525/s at 7.01–7.03 ms, yet product arm E qualified
only 1,600/s on W4/A, below S's 2,262 ([S1 arm E
final](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261026-s1-final-e/README.md)):
the harness lacked HTTP, interpretation, authority, owner renewal and the
release protocol and ran two flights where the product ran three, which added
a ≈0.5 ms marker flight and a 0.4–0.5 ms poll delay. Injection answers the
omissions; the margin covers the rest and keeps C3's 0.5 ms tolerance (§10.1)
a pass. §8.2's G7.0 re-derivation is **not a second measurement** and
authorizes nothing (grok, astra). If O1 meets (1) and (2), O1 is what the
product qualifies; if no arm does, A is out of reach on the √2 ladder. **B_A**
(7.07 minus post-L′ HTTP, critical-path CPU and release) is ≈4.5 ms today
(0.63; 0.51 + 1.12; 0.30), ≈5.7–6.2 at L′'s target.

### 3.6 Ordered C: a candidate C0′ measures beside C

**Ordered C** (Fable) keeps the O lane's revision order and arm E's early lock
release (data at `synchronous_commit=off`, a synchronous marker before
acknowledgement; parent §3.8), but replaces E's reader hold with C's reader
layer (§5) bound at **the durable point D**: two-image rows (§4.6) with `owm`
the D at dispatch, and V = GREATEST($d, durable head). WAL order makes every
crash loss a **suffix**, so it needs **no holes, voids, key race, tombstones
or reservations** and inherits E's recovery identity (parent §3.9). **Only
write-write overlap among uncertified batches holds a member** (a second
uncertified writer would overwrite the only old image); reads need no hold,
since a successor is lost whenever its predecessor is. **V's GREATEST reads a
durable head written only by synchronous writers** (marker, barriers,
recovery), since an asynchronously written `_head` can be visible yet not
durable. G7.0 prices E2 at 7.01–7.03 ms against C's 6.93–6.94, and E's
recovery is built and SIGKILL-tested; **its prerequisite is removing E's
measured overheads**, the ≈0.5 ms marker flight and 0.4–0.5 ms
completed-before-poll delay (attribution "Scheduling, durability and
release"). It shares C1's reader work, is exclusive with the C lane (R6) and
passes only §3.5's gate; if both pass, the one with more margin is built.

## 4. Stage C: the concurrent lane

### 4.1 Two lanes, the phase rule and the drain

- **O lane:** G7.2 (G7.3 once it lands, with P and L′), barriers and the solo
  route, unchanged. **C lane:** up to K writer slots, each one independent
  transaction, no ticket, `_head` access or fold. A batch is **open** from
  dispatch until the watermark W covers it (§4.5).
- **Phase rule.** Every open batch belongs to one lane; switching drains the
  lane in use; **barriers drain both** and write `_head`, because an O
  commit's `_head` would expose a lower, uncommitted C batch's rows at their
  old images, and an O cascade re-seeds sequences from `_head` (parent §4.2)
  into C ranges above it.
- **The C→O drain**: every C batch resolved and W published; **every catch-up
  resolved** (reply received, or backend terminated and confirmed gone); then
  one fenced synchronous transaction checks `_head` is at or below W with an
  attempt this committer wrote and **writes `_head` = (W.s, W.r, a fresh
  attempt)**, which the first O CAS names; sequences resume at W.s + 1, so no
  O commit aims at a stale catch-up or jumps `_head` over unscanned records.

### 4.2 Footprints and eligibility

A member's footprint is computed **before dispatch** from the G3b bounded
closure plan (`program_batch::bounded_plan`, `committer/closure.rs`), which
covers rule probes, hidden examined rows and alternative paths (controls
§3.3). Batches **commute** if neither writes what the other reads or writes;
entries are row keys, or **whole tables** where a dependent level's keys
depend on fetched data. Coarsening loses concurrency, never a dependency.

| Dependency | Footprint entry | C lane, first slices | Later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rows read by key, including rule probes and absent keys | Row key (absence is the key) | Admitted | — |
| Rows written | Row key | Updates of existing rows that change no `i` entry, hard-checked | Inserts and deletes need versioned index entries (§11) |
| Dependent-level reads | Table or index span | Admitted as table entries | Span entries |
| Ranges, scan boundaries, uniqueness, counts over `i` | Index span | Read-only; `i` is never written in the C lane | — |
| Maintained totals | Owner row read; source writes excluded | Reads admitted; maintained writes excluded, hard-checked | Commutative class (committer §4.5) |
| Caps, `_sequences`, allocations | — | Excluded | — |
| Client receipts `(principal, op id)` | Receipt key | Excluded (unkeyed only) | C4, receipts filtered by creating revision |
| Effects, outbox, presence marks | — | Excluded | C4; claimers stay barriers |
| Authority: sessions, identity, generation, `_meta` | Shared read | Admitted; never written in the C lane (its writers are barriers) | — |
| Time | — | Time-independent programs; no `not before` schema (`program_pipeline.rs:183–187`) | — |
| Presence and ephemeral reads | — | Excluded | — |
| Engine entropy | Counter-mode by revision (§4.4) | Programs drawing none until counter mode lands | — |
| Minted ids | — | Admitted; gaps tolerated (evidence is pruned only by catch-ups, §6.4) | — |

**`i` is frozen, by enforcement.** Index entries live in a second, unversioned
table (`store.rs`: delete plus insert on `(t, ix, k)`), so a C write moving an
`i` key would let a scan find a payload showing its old image, unseen by the
tripwire. The router admits only plans with no `i` mutation or maintained
write, the slot sends a member with such a delta to the O lane, and a census
asserts the C apply path issues neither, so scans, uniqueness probes and
counts need no versioning. The overlay serializes members within a batch as
today; an unbounded closure is ineligible.

### 4.3 Routing, holds, formation and lane switching

- **The open index U** maps each row key and table in an open batch's
  footprint to that batch. Keying U by *open*, not *unresolved*, is essential:
  a committed batch above an unresolved one is in no reader's V, and a
  successor reading its rows would act on an image no prefix has.
- **Formation.** In FIFO order, an eligible member disjoint from U joins the
  forming batch; a conflicting member is **held**, blocking only later members
  of its principal, until W covers its blockers.
- **Dispatch: the cohort rule** (Fable). A slot frees at confirmation while its
  cohort is still in release and HTTP (≈0.6–0.9 ms); dispatching on the first
  return sends a nearly empty batch and leaves the rest a slot interval in Q,
  which is how Q reaches ≈0.78 I. **After a slot frees, the batch dispatches
  once a cohort of N has joined (32/N slots active) or a bound h has passed**,
  at most 32 members; h, N and K come from C0′, h starting near the release
  spread (0.3 ms); O1's fill hold is the same rule, and revision 3's undefined
  g is withdrawn. **Back-pressure:** no dispatch while `next_revision − W.r >
  512`, 2K batches are open, or `_head` lags W by over four catch-up intervals.
- **Switching.** C→O when held or ineligible members exceed 25% of arrivals
  over 50 ms; O→C at 90% eligible and unconflicted; minimum dwell 100 ms. W5
  exact stays ordered; an idle eligible member is a one-member C batch.

### 4.4 Ranges, reservations and the batch record

At dispatch, in dispatch order, one sequencer serving both lanes assigns each
C batch **one revision and one public sequence per member** (`[r_lo, r_hi]`,
`[s_lo, s_hi]`, never empty), a fresh attempt, `at = max(T, pin)` (committer
§6) and an entropy key. **Reservations:** the final message publishes
committed members on the lowest reserved sequences and writes, for each unused
one (a refused or failed member), a void `_commits` row (`kind='void'`, zero
writes, the batch's `at`) and an empty-footprint evidence row, so every
reserved sequence has exactly one of each (one row per batch under §3.2's
batch-granular index); a whole-batch void does the same for its range (§6.1).

```sql
CREATE TABLE _batches (
  rev_lo BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, rev_hi BIGINT NOT NULL,
  seq_lo BIGINT NOT NULL, seq_hi BIGINT NOT NULL, attempt TEXT NOT NULL,
  state TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('committed','void')),
  at BIGINT NOT NULL);
```

**The record is the batch's lock and durable commit record**, inserted with
`state='committed'` as the first write after the fence: a void waits on its
uncommitted tuple (§6.1), and its committed row, read with its attempt, proves
the commit. Ranges are never empty and are reserved in lockstep, so **a hole's
reservation is reconstructible from its durable neighbours** (between the
record or `_head` below and the record above), with no descriptor of the lost
batch and no reservation fsync. **Rows are pruned in bounded steps only once
below `_head.revision`**, void rows also under §6.1's tombstone rule (Fable).

**Receipts carry their creating revision**, since refusals and receipt-only
successes store an older sequence (`mutation.rs:241`): `_ops` gains `rev
BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, metadata-only on Postgres 16 and 17. Older rows
read 0 and stay visible at every V, correctly, since takeover ends every older
transaction before a C phase (§6.5); this binary's lanes write `rev`
explicitly, as the census checks, so C1 stays behaviour-neutral (grok).
**Entropy** becomes counter-mode, keyed by revision, so a voided batch's draws
are never used; until then eligible members draw none. Minted ids tolerate
gaps.

### 4.5 The watermark W and `_head`

**W** is the pair (W.r, W.s): the largest revision at or below which every
batch is **resolved** (committed and confirmed, or voided with the void
confirmed), and its public sequence. W advances only on resolutions, under the
publication lock, which in one step installs G5/G6 publication, wakes
`/changes` and long waits, and releases replies in revision order; readers
sample it as one atomic pair.

**`_head` is a durable low-water mark** under C (amending store-port §7.2),
written by O commits, barriers, recovery, the drain and **catch-ups**: a
fenced `UPDATE _head … WHERE singleton AND attempt=$last` that also prunes
evidence behind it (§6.4), about every 1,000 revisions or 100 ms, off every
request's path; a lost one only lengthens recovery's scan and retention.
**Catch-ups and voids have their own slot** (Fable): at K=3 the cohort rule
leaves the third idle (32/N = 2 active at N=16); at any K a due catch-up or
void takes the next free slot before a batch, so `_head`'s lag is bounded by
one interval plus one flight, never by load. Their ≈10 flushes/s against ≈283
batch flushes/s are charged in C0′. Invariants: `_head.revision ≤ W.r` in a C
phase, equality after each drain, and **the durable prefix (`_head` plus the
contiguous resolved `_batches` above it) is never below W**.

### 4.6 Two-image rows: how a commit stays hidden above W

`r` gains nullable, unindexed columns (updates stay HOT): `orev`, the revision
of the last C write; `owm`, that batch's dispatch watermark; and `odata`. A C
batch's image update replaces `group_tail.rs:10–11` (or is Stage L's
statement in this form):

```sql
UPDATE r SET odata = r.data, data = w.data, orev = w.rev, owm = $w
FROM <images> AS w(t, id, data, rev)
WHERE r.t = w.t AND r.id = w.id AND (r.orev IS NULL OR r.orev <= $w)
```

followed by a raising count check; O-lane and barrier writes keep writing
`data` alone. **Invariant.** For every row x, `data` is x's latest committed
image, and if its last writer is a C batch, `odata` is its image at every
revision in `[owm(x), orev(x))`. *Proof.* x was in c's write footprint, so no
other open batch touched it, and every earlier writer was covered by W_c =
owm(x); so `data` at c's write was x's image at W_c, and no batch between W_c
and c wrote x. O writes run only when no C batch is open and advance `_head`,
so their stale stamps lie below every reader's V (§5.1). **The `orev ≤ $w`
predicate is a tripwire**: a misclassified footprint whose conflicting writer
has committed raises a known abort, one whose writer has not escapes it, so
the router is the mechanism and the differential (§10.2) qualifies it.
**Costs:** stamps and `odata` only on C-written rows, about twice their tuple
bytes in WAL at lower page density; rows over 2 KiB are ineligible at first,
keeping TOAST copies out; doctor reports stamps.

### 4.7 The flight of a C batch

**One flight, the form C is built in:** `BEGIN … READ COMMITTED READ WRITE`;
`SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on`; the settings check and owner fence in Stage
P's form; the `_batches` insert; L′'s authority assertions, locked probes,
validation and deadline check (§3.3), or the lowered statement if L is built
(§3.4), applied as two images with the tripwire, every read through §5.1's
expression at the dispatch W; the vector tail with reservation voids; raising
counts; timeouts from the earliest member deadline, reset to zero; COMMIT,
under **G7.3's ruling-4 conditions** (every check raises before it; it is
never cancelled). Slots record backend PID and start time at connect. **Two
flights are the fallback** for cold rows, misses and other members. A C
transaction holds only the fence's key share, its own rows and its own key,
so C transactions never wait on each other and can share flushes.

### 4.8 Why the committed history is serial-equivalent

A C batch c reads its facts at W_c; every lower batch was covered by W_c or
open at c's dispatch and so touches nothing in c's footprint. Hence **c's
facts equal the state after every lower batch, restricted to its footprint**,
and its members run as the serial reference `run` would at their revisions
(the overlay's induction, controls §3.3). A void is a no-op no higher batch
depended on, so **a known abort never cascades to another C batch** (unlike
G7.2); survivors rerun at new ranges before anyone is answered. The committer
§3.5 oracle replays voids as no-ops; O phases keep the parent's argument, and
phase changes are drains.

## 5. Visibility on every reader path

### 5.1 The reader rule

Every Postgres reader statement or snapshot binds the pair (W.r, W.s), sampled
immediately before dispatch, and computes in its own snapshot its **visible
revision V** and every row's image:

```sql
V.r = GREATEST($w_r, (SELECT revision FROM _head WHERE singleton))
image(x) = CASE WHEN x.orev IS NULL OR x.orev <= V.r THEN x.data
                WHEN x.owm <= V.r THEN x.odata
                ELSE sb4_stale(x.orev) END
```

with V.s likewise for sequence-keyed tables. `sb4_stale(orev)` raises a named
SQLSTATE and is **STABLE**: it takes the row's stamp, so it is never
constant-folded, and `CASE` evaluates it only when reached; VOLATILE would
block subquery pull-up and qual pushdown, and C1 compares plans (§5.3).

**Lemma 1:** every batch at or below W.r is visible: a synchronous commit is
visible after its flush and before its reply, W advances after that reply, and
the reader's snapshot follows its W sample. **Lemma 2:** every image returned
is the row's image at V: an O commit is visible only with the `_head` it
advanced, so V covers it; a row whose last C writer is at or below V shows
`data`, and one above V shows `odata`, valid on `[owm, orev)`. **So every
Snapback read is exactly the state at V**, a prefix of revision order; a
REPEATABLE READ snapshot binds once for all its statements, as arm E's
transport binds its head (`flight.rs:58–61`).

**Every dependent use goes through the image** (projections, predicates, join
and closure keys, rule probes, hidden authorization rows, maintained owner
rows, keys derived from fetched data). **The stale branch restarts the whole
snapshot**: a reader delayed while a later batch rewrote a row it examines
discards **everything accumulated from that snapshot** (rows, closure results,
G5 fill candidates, any partial response), resamples W and restarts, at most
three times before a retryable `E_STORE`; streaming readers restart before
emitting or end with a retryable error. **Coverage is structural**: reader SQL
builders take a `Watermark` token, nothing can name `r`, `_commits`, `_log`,
`_change_evidence`, `_ops` or `_outbox` without one, and a census rejects
`r.data` outside the image expression or `_ops` without its `rev` bound, like
arm E's `Bound` envelope (`early_release.rs:241–251`) without a hold.

### 5.3 Cost on reads, and raw SQL

A bind parameter, a `_head` InitPlan and a `CASE` per row are noise; the real
costs are measured: wider tuples and lower page density on C-written rows,
their WAL and vacuum traffic, stale restarts, plan changes and the
connections slots take (R2). **C1 qualifies all six read cells at their rungs,
solo and under the arm-E report's 400/s writer, and compares EXPLAIN plans for
W1–W3, G4a closures and fallbacks, before any writer slot is reserved**; C3
repeats it with the C lane writing; any read-rung regression or plan change
stops C. Inspect views read through `_sb4_prefix()` (`_head` plus the
contiguous resolved `_batches` above it), so `snapback sql` and doctor see a
prefix; raw `r` readers see commits above W out of order, **all durable**, as
the deploy guide states. Restore reconciles (§6.5); graduation exports at the
durable prefix.

## 6. Holes, outcomes and recovery

### 6.1 The void and the key race

A **void** for batch c is one fenced, synchronous transaction in one message:
`BEGIN; SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on; <fence>; INSERT INTO _batches
(rev_lo, …, state) VALUES (c.r_lo, …, 'void')`, a void `_commits` row and an
empty-footprint evidence row for every sequence c reserved, then COMMIT, with
raising counts. It is idempotent, keyed on c's range, and prunes nothing.

**Mutual exclusion.** A unique-index insert meeting an in-progress
transaction's tuple waits for it, then raises 23505 if it committed and
proceeds if it aborted; c inserted its key first. If c committed, the void
raises 23505 and the row's attempt proves c **committed**; if c is in
progress, the void **waits**, which is the drain; if c aborted or its message
has not run, the void commits and a late message raises 23505 at its insert: c
**can never commit**, **provided the void row outlives every attempt that
could still execute**. **Tombstone retention** (grok, Fable): the row stays
until `_head` has passed its range **and** the backend that received c's
message (PID and start time, recorded at connect) has finished it, shown by a
later transaction **on that same backend**, or is gone from
`pg_stat_activity`, or the fence would refuse c's token. A reconnected slot
proves nothing, since the old backend may still hold c's message; a void
pruned above a lagging `_head` would reappear to recovery as a gap whose
re-void inserts `_commits` and evidence rows that already exist.

A void is issued only for a batch classified aborted or unknown; past
`lock_timeout` the committer terminates c's backend by its recorded PID,
re-checking role, tag and token (store-port §5.2), and retries, else the store
pauses for fenced reacquisition (G6's sticky rule). A retried void meeting
23505 on its own `void` row shows the first committed; on the wire a void is
"nothing changed" (R5).

### 6.2 Outcome table

Per batch, on its own connection; **no outcome reclassifies another**.

| Evidence for C batch c | Classification | c's callers | W |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COMMIT tag after passed assertions, ReadyForQuery `I`, no warning | Confirmed | Released in revision order once W covers c | Resolved |
| Error before COMMIT with ROLLBACK acknowledged (raised assertion, tripwire, probe or validation mismatch, deadline check, index or maintained delta, 55P03/57014 under the deadline, 40P01, 23505 on its own `_batches` key) | Known abort | Controls §3.1: remove a named failed member and rerun the rest, or each alone, within deadlines; fresh ranges | Void, then resolved |
| Lost reply, error or warning after COMMIT was sent, connection loss after the final message | Unknown | `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` until R3's witnesses pass; then the void's result decides (§6.3) | Void race decides; resolved either way |
| Lease lost before the final message | Never sent; an open two-flight attempt rolls back when takeover terminates it | `E_STORE_OWNERSHIP` for started members, retryable as unsent intent | Not the old owner's to void, its fence refusing; only the new owner's reconcile closes a hole (§6.5) |
| Lease lost after the final message | Actual result if received, else unknown | As above | The new owner reconciles (§6.5) |

### 6.3 Unknown outcomes and the drain

After an unknown, the void race is both drain and classification: **nothing
waits for a ticket probe and nothing is re-seeded**; W stalls at c while
batches above it keep committing within back-pressure. **R3 is yes on both
sides once its witnesses pass** (§13): a won void proves c can never commit and
permits controls §3.1's bounded rerun; 23505 against c's own record and
attempt proves c committed, and **a proven commit is never rerun** (astra).
Its exact results are released only where the committer still holds them, as
for interpreted and L′ members, computed in Rust before the final message and
kept until c resolves; a lowered member's results exist only in the lost
reply, so it returns `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` unless Stage L persists them
(§3.4). Until the witnesses pass, ruling 3's "no" holds.

### 6.4 Evidence retention

The tail today deletes evidence at the committing sequences minus 1,000
(`group_tail.rs:23`), which under C deletes evidence a client at W is owed,
and `footprint_between` reads a gap as revocation. A prune at the dispatch
watermark (revision 2) fails a **delayed reader** that samples W = 1,000 while
`_head` stalls at 1,000 and opens its snapshot after a batch prunes through
1,200 (astra, grok, Fable). **Evidence is pruned only behind `_head`, inside
the catch-up that advances it** (astra): a catch-up writing `_head` = H
deletes `(H_prev.s − 1,000, H.s − 1,000]`; on the O lane a fenced maintenance
transaction does the same about every 1,000 sequences, bounded by the
committed `_head` it reads. No batch, void or reservation prunes (grok).
**Lemma 3:** a reader's V.s is at least the `_head` its snapshot shows (§5.1),
and a deletion commits with, or after, the head that bounds it; so a snapshot
that sees a deletion has V.s ≥ H.s, and **the 1,000 sequences below every
reader's V keep their evidence**. Retention is 1,000 plus `_head`'s lag behind
W, bounded by §4.5's catch-up slot; recovery's floor is the same (§6.5).

### 6.5 Crash recovery and takeover reconciliation

Before an owner serves after start, restart or takeover:

1. Acquire ownership (store-port §5.2): takeover's `_owner FOR UPDATE` is
   granted only after every old key share is gone, so every old C transaction
   has ended; a restarting owner first terminates its old token's tagged
   backends.
2. Read `_head` (H) and walk `_batches` up from it: committed and void rows
   extend the prefix; a gap is a hole whose transactions all ended
   uncommitted: **void it** as one record keyed at the gap's start, fenced and
   synchronous, its reservation rebuilt from its neighbours (§4.4). A lost
   batch with `rev_lo` inside the gap would not collide with that key, which
   is safe **only because step 1 comes first** (Fable): old backends are gone,
   and the fence refuses a late message under an old token before its insert.
3. **Exact counts, inside the retained window:** every reserved sequence has
   one `_commits` row (void for a void record or unused reservation) and,
   above the floor P = H.s − 1,000, below which alone anything was pruned
   (§6.4), one evidence row; no `_commits` row above `H.seq` lies outside a
   record; no `orev` inside a void range. Failure refuses with
   `SNAPBACK_C_LANE_RECONCILE`; a lagging H does not trip the counts.
4. Write `_head` to the reconciled prefix with a fresh attempt, fenced and
   synchronous, and serve with W equal to it.

### 6.6 Fencing, deadlines and back-pressure

Every C transaction, void and catch-up takes the owner fence first as a
raising command; takeover terminates every tagged writer (store-port
§5.1–§5.2). Timeouts come from the earliest member deadline; a timeout before
COMMIT is a known abort; COMMIT runs with both at zero; past a 250 ms void
stall the lane stops admitting and reports `E_STORE`.

## 7. Interactions

- **G6.** Readers cannot see an unpublished C batch, so **C batches neither
  close the gate nor count in `inflight`**; publication installs with each W
  advance; a C unknown is settled by its void.
- **Arm E, ordered C, G7.2 and G7.3.** Two release rules on one read path are
  unsafe as well as costly: **R6, `commit=early` and the C lane are mutually
  exclusive**, and ordered C, if built, replaces E's hold and excludes the C
  lane likewise. G7.3's raising assertions, exact completion checks and
  uncancellable COMMIT govern every co-dispatched COMMIT here.
- **Barriers and generality.** Claimers, sweeps, session and identity writers,
  activation, adoption and ineligible members drain the C lane; a
  barrier-heavy workload lives in O phases (S1's W4 has none): a generality
  limit, not a correctness gap. Ingress hints and plan reuse **establish
  neither footprint independence nor authority**.
- **Receipts and replay (C4).** The receipt key joins the footprint; preflight
  and the serial re-check read `_ops` at `rev ≤ V.r` and wait on a key an open
  batch holds. **SQLite is unchanged**, the differential's reference; P, L′,
  L, ordered C and the C lane are `postgres`-only.

### 8.4 Verdict

**B: the ordered lane suffices**, Stage P alone marginal to likely, L′ with
margin. **A strict W4/A win** needs a mean in practice ≤6.4 ms: one flight,
Stage P substantially delivered, measured preparation near 20–30 µs per
member, honest phasing and concurrency at N≈16 on two to three slots, or a
batch-granular index cutting the slope enough for the ordered lane.
**Likelihoods** (judgment): C on fast L′ (or L) about **one in six to eight**
(Fable), its terms flooring at 6.8 ms against 6.4 with only harness evidence,
and E's transfer record, below that; astra and grok put no number on it beyond
judgment, the G7.0 re-derivation not being independent. C on halved CPU and
the ordered lane at N≈32 are below one in ten, two-flight C below one in
twenty. All move with Stage P's cut, each 0.5 ms of shortfall or surplus
moving every A row by about 0.5 ms, so the program is about **one in six to
eight**. At 3,805/s (R1) every one-flight C shape is plausible, if Supabase,
re-run in the same window, fails it.

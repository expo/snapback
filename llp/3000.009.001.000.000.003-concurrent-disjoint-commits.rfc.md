## 1. Summary

Reads win 6/6 S1 cells; W5 exact (A, B) and W5 mix/A win; W4/B and W5
mix/B tie at 1,600; **W4/A loses, 2,262 against the Supabase advocate's
3,200**. A strict win needs the next √2 rung: **W4/A at 4,525/s, a mean
residence of at most 7.07 ms with 32 clients (about 6.4 ms at a passing
90% occupancy), and W4/B and W5 mix/B at 2,262/s, at most 14.15 ms**;
Supabase fails both rungs. The product measures about 15 ms and 17 ms.

Revision 1 offered one lever, concurrent disjoint commits (option C),
which its own model put below one chance in five; the panel named two
levers it had set aside. **This RFC is now the program for the remaining
write cells, in four gated stages, C last** (§3.1):

1. **Stage P, Postgres per-batch server cost, both lanes** (§3.2): ≈2.4 ms
   per batch independent of N (two `DO` blocks compiled per flight, a
   tail parsing its JSON seven times and running empty CTEs, per-member
   rows), cut by prepared checks, typed arrays and shape-specialised
   tails, with no reader change; measured on the ordered lane first.
2. **Stage L, one flight per batch** (§3.3–§3.4): an eligible point-update
   fragment, W4's `increment` among it, compiles to one fenced `UPDATE …
   SET n = n + 1 WHERE <rule> RETURNING` preserving authorization,
   refusals, overflow, meters, images, receipts and evidence — a general
   compiler optimisation, never a change to the benchmark's program. Its
   comparator, built first, is one-flight interpretation on **validated
   warm facts** (L′), which keeps one executor.
3. **Stage C0′, a product-shaped harness** (§3.5). **C proceeds only if
   its best mean at 4,525/s on A meets 7.07 ms minus the product's measured
   HTTP, CPU and release (≈5.6 ms today) and materially beats the improved
   ordered lane.**
4. **Stage C, the concurrent lane** (§4–§6): batches with complete
   footprints disjoint from every open batch commit concurrently;
   two-image rows and a bound watermark keep every reader on a prefix of
   one revision order; voids close holes by a key race. Built one-flight,
   after its reader layer is re-measured on all six read cells under
   concurrent writes.

**Budget verdicts** (§8; inputs ±15%; likelihoods are judgment): **B is
reachable on the ordered lane**: Stage P brings W4/B and W5 mix/B to about
10.5–13.3 ms and Stage L to 8–11, against 14.15. **A at 4,525/s needs one
flight, most of Stage P's cut, and probably C**: C with a lowered final
5.7–7.6 ms (about one in three); the ordered lane with one lowered flight
at N≈32, 6.3–8.5 (one in five); C on validated facts 6.3–8.7 (one in
six); two-flight C 6.9–9.6 (below one in ten). The program as a whole:
about two in five. **R1, a finer tie-break rung (3,805 on A), is the
author's decision and pending** (§9), never a planning target; R2–R6 are
decided (§13). Citations use paths relative to `snapback4/` at
`5fc6e6449b`; bare names are in `crates/snapback4-core/src/engine/` or its
`postgres/`.

### 2.2 What a strict W4/A win requires

With 32 clients at the edge of capacity, Little's law sets the bar: a mean
of at most 32/4,525 = **7.07 ms**, about 6.4 ms with the margin three
passing repetitions need (G7.0's arm C passed at 6.93 ms with a worst
intended p99 of 84 ms against 100). HTTP 0.45–0.63 ms, 0.56 ms of link per
flight, the flush (2.64 ms sync, 2.70 ms PG commit) and release 0.25–0.35
ms are fixed, so **one flight leaves about 3 ms for CPU, server work,
phasing and the flush residual; a second flight leaves 2.5.**

- **Supabase is flush-bound too.** One `set_config` and one RPC, a
  SECURITY DEFINER increment with an explicit authorization predicate, in
  one transaction (G7.0 protocol trace); at 3,200/s ≈0.115 syncs per
  commit, about 370 flushes/s × 2.64 ms ≈ 98% of the device, p50 8.02 ms;
  it fails 4,525. The contest is everything other than the flush.
- **The shapes** (G7.0, SQL only: no HTTP, interpreter, guard or fence):
  arm C at N=16 on two connections, 6.93 ms at 4,525/s, sync/batch 1.003,
  its gain the missing chain; **N=8 on four connections merged flushes
  (0.705) and was slower, 7.74 ms**; ordered S at N=32, three flights,
  8.39–8.73 ms. A product mean adds HTTP, interpretation and authority
  CPU, a P1 ≈0.45 ms slower than G7.0's fused opener, and release.
- **The ordered lane's limit.** At depth d a request spans about d chains
  (≈4.7 ms after G7.3), so depth two cannot pass 4,525; at depth one with
  N≈32 residence is one batch cycle, so a cheap per-member tail matters as
  much as concurrency. **The floor** of the best shape, every term low, is
  ≈**5.7 ms** (§8.2; Fable's 5.4–5.8); the raw-PG token reference, one
  statement per request, measures p50 5.43 ms on W5 mix/B at 4,525/s
  ([one-host final](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261024-s1-onehost-final/README.md)).

### 3.1 Order and gates

| Stage | Lane | For | Starts when | Kept or stopped by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **P**, server cost | Both | Every write cell; B on its own | Now | Each cut kept if it lowers measured per-batch server time with oracles and difftest unchanged |
| **L′**, validated warm facts | O first | W4, W5 mix; A | P measured on O | Kept if one flight beats G7.3 + P by ≥0.5 ms of W4/A mean at 3,200/s |
| **L**, lowered fragment | O first | Same | L′ measured | Kept only if ≥0.4 ms below L′ on W4/A at 3,200/s, or a rung on any cell, differential clean (§3.4) |

P and L touch no reader; C changes every reader, recovery and the
connection budget, so it must beat the **improved** ordered lane.

### 3.2 Stage P: Postgres per-batch server cost

**Where the fixed time is** (attribution fits: P1 server 1.07 + 0.025N
ms, tail server 1.32 + 0.061N ms): the **settings guard** (`flight.rs:44`)
is an anonymous `DO` block before every flight's data commands, compiled
and planned afresh each time; the **owner fence** (`ownership.rs:74`) is
another, its `FOR KEY SHARE` read planned on every transaction; the
**tail** (`group_tail.rs:7–47`) is one prepared nine-CTE statement in
which `$1::text::jsonb` appears seven times, so the payload is parsed
seven times, and its log, effect, presence and receipt CTEs run on empty
arrays for W4, which writes none; **per member**, the image update, a
`_commits` row, an evidence row and the prune step.

**Changes, all keeping today's semantics:** (1) settings guard and owner
fence as **provisioned statement-level checks**, PL/pgSQL functions
created with the store's schema (owner role, pinned `search_path`) and
called by prepared `EXECUTE`, so plans are cached per session; each still
raises S4001 or S4002 before any data command in the same message, the
fence still the first lock and its own command (round-trip §7, store-port
§5.1), and lease acquisition and doctor check their body digests, a
missing or altered function refusing before any write; (2) **typed
arrays** per tail collection (`unnest` over `text[]` and `int8[]`
literals) instead of seven JSON parses, ahead of binary Bind, which would
change the transport round-trip §3.6 decides by measurement; (3)
**shape-specialised tails**, prepared variants chosen by which
collections are non-empty, each present CTE keeping its raising count;
(4) **cheaper per-member work**: set-based inserts, committer-assigned
prune intervals (§6.4), a confirmed generic plan per variant, and P1's
identity, authority and level-0 statements fused where shapes allow.

**Prediction (assumed):** fixed per-batch server 2.4 → 1.2–1.7 ms,
per-member slope down 20–40%, measured on the ordered lane (W4/B, W5
mix/B, W4/A) with per-batch server clocks before any C-lane work. Drift,
stale-token, wrong-store and tampered-function faults still raise before
data; oracles and difftest are unchanged. Stage P is controls §10.2's
cheaper sibling: the same cost without a migration.

### 3.3 Stage L: one flight for the ordinary point update

W4 reads a counter (`! else NOT_FOUND`) and returns `update
counters[c.id] { n: c.n + 1 }` under the rule `.owner = viewer or (.owner
= null and exists memberships[.room, viewer])` ([S1 schema](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/snapback-app/snapback/schema.q)).
Every fact its outcome depends on can be evaluated by the statement that
writes it, as for many first-party mutations (set, toggle, increment).

**Eligibility**, checked by the compiler at deploy and by the router per
member: (1) one keyed read `x = T[k] ! else E` with `k` from the
arguments, optional `require` predicates, one `update T[x.id] { … }` and a
return of x's new image or a projection, nothing else read or written;
(2) `T`'s rules and every `require` compile to SQL predicates over x's
columns, the viewer, the arguments and keyed point or `exists` probes on
tables the batch does not write; (3) assigned expressions are arithmetic
or comparison over x's fields and arguments, Quarry's null and overflow
rules compiled as guarded `CASE` expressions whose guard runs first; (4)
no inserted, deleted or moved index key, no maintained source written, no
effects, presence, time-dependent rules, `not before` schema, entropy or
`ctx.sql`, images under 2 KiB; (5) the meter budget covers the fragment's
fixed trace, else the member falls back **before execution**, never after
a possibly committed lowered write (controls §10.3); (6) at most one
member per row key in a batch, since sibling data-modifying CTEs cannot
update a row twice (controls §10.3), so W5 exact stays interpreted on the
ordered lane, which already wins it; (7) unkeyed at first, keyed members
once their receipts join the tail (C4).

**The statement.** One data-modifying statement per batch over the
members' argument arrays locks and reads each target row, classifies each
member in the program's evaluation order (absent or hidden by the read
rule → E; update rule false → refusal; guard failure → refusal; else the
new image), updates only `ok` members, and returns per member the
classification, probe results and old and new images. The tail in the
same message derives `_commits` and evidence rows from the classification,
numbering sequences densely over `ok` members on the ordered lane (the
contiguity check, `group_tail.rs:149`, moves into SQL) and filling the
reservation on the C lane (§4.4). Rust rebuilds each outcome, logical
meter trace (rows read, hidden examined rows, probes), wire result and
publication from the returned rows.

**Authority.** The shared Rust resolver still decides session authority;
SQL never returns an `allowed` bit for it (controls §10.3, round-trip
§3.1). The resolver runs on session facts carried from ingress, and the
statement **re-validates them inside the fenced transaction**: the session
row by digest, family, generation and store identity by equality, and
expiry against the member's deadline, since equality cannot validate
elapsed time. A revocation or expiry committed first refuses the write,
which reruns two-flight on fresh facts (R4a, §13). Table rules are
evaluated on the locked row, so lowering carries **no program facts** and
needs no R4b.

**Lanes.** On the ordered lane a batch is one message (`BEGIN`, settings
check, fence, ticket, head CAS, identity and session validation, the
statement, the tail, raising counts, `COMMIT`) under G7.3's ruling-4
conditions. On the C lane the `_batches` insert replaces ticket and head,
probes read through the image at the dispatch watermark, and the update is
the two-image apply with its tripwire (§4.6–§4.7).

**Evidence and ergonomics.** A compiled-on/off executor arm in the
oracles and difftest (controls §10.3's list), boundary sweeps (overflow,
null owner, hidden and absent rows, revocation and expiry races) and a
planted lowerer fault. Agents write nothing new: `snapback check` names
each mutation's path and why it fell back, and the guide documents the
fragment as a performance contract with identical outcomes either way.

### 3.4 L′: one flight on validated warm facts

The panel's alternative (astra) keeps one executor. The slot interprets
**before any flight** on a bounded write-through mirror of images this
committer committed and published (dropped wholesale at every barrier,
lane change, unknown outcome and ownership event) plus ingress-carried
session facts. The final message validates every input inside the fenced
transaction: base images by equality in the apply's `WHERE`; every other
read, **including absences and rule probes**, by point-key equality or
absence assertions (a span read is ineligible unless an explicit
predicate validates it); session facts as in §3.3. A mismatch, or a cold
row, takes the two-flight form. L′ needs R4 in full and pays interpreter
CPU ahead of the flight (0.5–1.2 ms at N=16, halved) plus ≈0.12–0.15 ms of
validation. **Choice rule:** L′ is built first, as the equal-flight
comparator controls §10.3 requires before a second executor; L is kept
only if it beats L′ by ≥0.4 ms on W4/A at 3,200/s, or by a rung.

### 3.5 Stage C0′: the product-shaped harness

G7.0's harness, with no product edit, sending the product's messages
(settings check, owner fence, identity check, `SET LOCAL
synchronous_commit=on`, timeouts, raising counts and COMMIT as built today
**and** as cut by Stage P; the `_batches` insert, two-image apply and
tripwire; the lowered statement and L′'s validation). Arms: **O1**, the
improved ordered lane in one flight, depth one and two, N ∈ {8, 16, 32},
with a bounded fill hold; **C2f**, two-flight C; **C-L′** and **C-L**,
one-flight C; C at N ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}, K ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16},
pacing on and off **with its hold counted in residence**. It reports
mean, p50, p99, syncs per batch, flush waits, PG CPU, stale raises, a
prefix hammer, the void race under kill and reader statement cost with
and without the image expression, with three open-loop confirmations at
4,525/s on A and 2,262/s on B.

**Stop rule.** B_A is 7.07 ms minus the product's HTTP, critical-path CPU
and release as measured after Stages P and L (with today's 0.63, 0.5 and
0.30, **B_A ≈ 5.6 ms**). C proceeds only if its best configuration meets
B_A in all three confirmations at 4,525/s **and** materially beats the
best O1 in the same window: a mean at least 1.0 ms lower, or meeting B_A
where O1 misses. If O1 meets B_A, O1 goes to the product and C is not
built for S1; if neither does, A is out of reach on the √2 ladder.

## 4. Stage C: the concurrent lane

### 4.1 Two lanes, the phase rule and the drain

- **O lane:** G7.2 (G7.3 once it lands, with Stages P and L), barriers
  and the solo route, unchanged. **C lane:** up to K writer slots, each
  one independent transaction, no ticket, `_head` access or fold. A batch
  is **open** from dispatch until the watermark W covers it (§4.5).
- **Phase rule.** At any instant every open batch belongs to one lane;
  switching drains the lane in use; **barriers drain both** and write
  `_head`. Exclusive because an O commit's `_head` would expose a lower,
  uncommitted C batch's rows at their old images, and an O cascade
  re-seeds sequences from `_head` (parent §4.2) into C ranges above it.
- **The C→O drain**: every C batch resolved and W published; **every
  catch-up resolved** (reply received, or backend terminated and confirmed
  gone); then one fenced synchronous transaction checks `_head` is at or
  below W with an attempt this committer wrote and **writes `_head` =
  (W.s, W.r, a fresh attempt)**. The first O CAS names that attempt and
  sequences resume at W.s + 1, so no O commit aims at a stale catch-up or
  jumps `_head` over records recovery would not rescan.

### 4.2 Footprints and eligibility

A member's footprint is computed **before dispatch** from the G3b bounded
closure plan (`program_batch::bounded_plan`, `committer/closure.rs`),
which covers rule probes, hidden examined rows and alternative paths
(controls §3.3). Batches **commute** if neither writes what the other
reads or writes; entries are row keys, or **whole tables** where a
dependent level's keys depend on fetched data. Coarsening loses
concurrency, never a dependency.

| Dependency | Footprint entry | C lane, first slices | Later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rows read by key, including rule probes and absent keys | Row key (absence is the key) | Admitted | — |
| Rows written | Row key | Updates of existing rows that change no `i` entry, hard-checked | Inserts and deletes need versioned index entries (§11) |
| Dependent-level reads | Table or index span | Admitted as table entries | Span entries |
| Ranges, scan boundaries, uniqueness, counts over `i` | Index span | Read-only; `i` is never written in the C lane | — |
| Maintained totals | Owner row read; source writes excluded | Reads admitted; maintained writes excluded, hard-checked | Commutative class (committer §4.5) |
| Caps, `_sequences`, allocations | — | Excluded | — |
| Client receipts `(principal, op id)` | Receipt key | Excluded (unkeyed only) | C4, receipts filtered by creating revision |
| Effects, outbox, presence marks | — | Excluded | C4; claimers stay barriers |
| Authority: sessions, identity, generation, `_meta` | Shared read | Admitted; never written in the C lane (its writers are barriers) | — |
| Time | — | Time-independent programs; no `not before` schema (`program_pipeline.rs:183–187`) | — |
| Presence and ephemeral reads | — | Excluded | — |
| Engine entropy | Counter-mode by revision (§4.4) | Programs drawing none until counter mode lands | — |
| Minted ids; `_change_evidence` prune | Prune intervals assigned from W (§6.4) | Admitted | — |

**`i` is frozen, by enforcement.** Index entries live in a second,
unversioned table (`store.rs`: delete plus insert on `(t, ix, k)`); a C
write that moved an `i` key would let a scan find a payload still showing
its old image while the old key misses, unseen by the tripwire. So the
router admits a member only if its plan shows no `i` mutation and no
maintained write; the slot computes each member's index and maintained
deltas before the final message and sends a member with either non-empty
to the O lane; and a writer census asserts the C apply path issues no `i`
or maintained statement. `i` is then identical across a C phase, so scans,
uniqueness probes and counts need no versioning. Within a batch the
overlay serializes members as today; an unbounded closure is ineligible.

### 4.3 Routing, holds and lane switching

- **The open index U** maps each row key and table in an open batch's
  footprint to that batch. Keying U by *open*, not *unresolved*, is
  essential: a committed batch above an unresolved one is in no reader's
  V, and a successor reading its rows would act on an image no prefix has.
- **Formation.** In FIFO order, an eligible member disjoint from U joins
  the forming batch; a conflicting member is **held**, blocking only later
  members of its principal, until W covers its blockers.
- **Dispatch** when a slot is free, the mailbox non-empty and g elapsed;
  g and K start at C0′'s best measured configuration (smaller N is not
  assumed), N ≤ 32. **Back-pressure:** no dispatch while `next_revision −
  W.r > 512` or 2K batches are open, bounding W's lag (retention no
  longer depends on it, §6.4).
- **Switching.** C→O when held or ineligible members exceed 25% of
  arrivals over 50 ms; O→C at 90% eligible and unconflicted; minimum
  dwell 100 ms. W5 exact stays ordered; an idle eligible member is a
  one-member C batch.

### 4.4 Ranges, reservations and the batch record

At dispatch, in dispatch order, one sequencer serving both lanes assigns
each C batch **one revision and one public sequence per member**
(`[r_lo, r_hi]`, `[s_lo, s_hi]`, never empty), a fresh attempt, `at =
max(T, pin)` (committer §6), an entropy key and a prune interval (§6.4).

**Reservations.** The final message publishes committed members on the
lowest reserved sequences and, for each sequence it does not use (a
refused or failed member), writes a void `_commits` row (`kind='void'`,
zero writes, the batch's `at`) and an empty-footprint evidence row, so
every reserved sequence has exactly one of each; a whole-batch void does
the same for its whole range (§6.1).

```sql
CREATE TABLE _batches (
  rev_lo BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, rev_hi BIGINT NOT NULL,
  seq_lo BIGINT NOT NULL, seq_hi BIGINT NOT NULL,
  prune_hi BIGINT NOT NULL, attempt TEXT NOT NULL,
  state TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('committed','void')),
  at BIGINT NOT NULL);
```

**The record is the batch's lock and durable commit record**, inserted
with `state='committed'` as the first write after the fence: its
uncommitted tuple is what a void waits on (§6.1), and its committed row,
read with its attempt, proves the commit. Because ranges are never empty
and are reserved in lockstep, **a hole's reservation is reconstructible
from its durable neighbours** (the revisions and sequences strictly
between the record or `_head` below and the record above), with no
descriptor of the lost batch and no reservation fsync. Committed rows
below `_head.revision` are pruned in bounded steps; void rows only under
§6.1's retention rule.

**Receipts carry their creating revision.** `_ops` gains `rev`, written
by both lanes: refusals and receipt-only successes store an older public
sequence (`mutation.rs:241`), so only the revision tells whether a
receipt's batch is in a reader's prefix (§5.2). **Entropy** becomes
counter-mode, keyed by revision, so a voided batch's draws are never used;
until then eligible members draw none. Minted ids tolerate gaps.

### 4.5 The watermark W and `_head`

**W** is the pair (W.r, W.s): the largest revision at or below which
every batch is **resolved** (committed and confirmed, or voided with the
void confirmed), and its public sequence. W advances only on resolutions,
under the publication lock, which in one step installs G5/G6 publication,
wakes `/changes` and long waits, and releases replies in revision order;
readers sample it as one atomic pair.

**`_head` is a durable low-water mark** under C (amending store-port
§7.2), written by O commits, barriers, recovery, the drain and
**catch-ups** (a fenced `UPDATE _head … WHERE singleton AND
attempt=$last` about every 1,000 revisions or 100 ms, on a free slot, off
every request's path; a lost one only lengthens recovery's scan).
Invariants: `_head.revision ≤ W.r` in a C phase, equality after each
drain, and **the durable prefix (`_head` plus the contiguous resolved
`_batches` above it) is never below W**.

### 4.6 Two-image rows: how a commit stays hidden above W

`r` gains nullable, unindexed columns (updates stay HOT): `orev`, the
revision of the last C write; `owm`, that batch's dispatch watermark; and
`odata`. A C batch's image update replaces `group_tail.rs:10–11` (or is
Stage L's statement in this form):

```sql
UPDATE r SET odata = r.data, data = w.data, orev = w.rev, owm = $w
FROM <images> AS w(t, id, data, rev)
WHERE r.t = w.t AND r.id = w.id AND (r.orev IS NULL OR r.orev <= $w)
```

followed by a raising count check. O-lane and barrier writes keep writing
`data` alone.

**Invariant.** For every row x, `data` is x's latest committed image, and
if its last writer is a C batch, `odata` is its image at every revision in
`[owm(x), orev(x))`. *Proof.* x was in c's write footprint, so no other
open batch touched it, and every earlier writer was covered by W_c =
owm(x); so `data` at c's write was x's image at W_c, and no batch between
W_c and c wrote x. O writes run only when no C batch is open and advance
`_head`, so their stale stamps lie below every reader's V (§5.1).

**The `orev ≤ $w` predicate is a tripwire.** A misclassified footprint
whose conflicting writer has already committed raises a known abort; a
conflict whose writer has not committed yet escapes it, so the router is
the mechanism and the differential (§10.2) qualifies it. **Costs:** stamps
and `odata` exist only on C-written rows, whose updates write about twice
the tuple bytes to WAL and lower those pages' density; rows over 2 KiB are
ineligible at first, keeping TOAST copies out; doctor reports stamps.

### 4.7 The flight of a C batch

**One flight, the form C is built in.** One message: `BEGIN … READ
COMMITTED READ WRITE`; `SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on`; the settings
check and owner fence in Stage P's form; the `_batches` insert; the
generation, identity and session checks (§3.3); the lowered statement
(§3.3) or L′'s validated results (§3.4), applied as two images with the
tripwire, every read through §5.1's expression at the dispatch W; the
vector tail with reservation voids and the prune interval; each count a
raising assertion; timeouts from the earliest member deadline, reset to
zero; COMMIT, under **G7.3's ruling-4 conditions**: every check raises
before it, and it is never cancelled. Slots record their backend PID and
start time at connect. **Two flights are the fallback** for cold rows,
validation mismatches and other members (P1 carries the checks, the
`_batches` insert and facts read at the dispatch W). A C transaction
holds only the fence's key share, its own rows and its own key, so C
transactions never wait on each other and can share flushes.

### 4.8 Why the committed history is serial-equivalent

A C batch c reads its facts at W_c; every lower batch was covered by W_c
or open at c's dispatch and so touches nothing in c's footprint. Hence
**c's facts equal the state after every lower batch, restricted to its
footprint**, and its members run as the serial reference `run` would at
their revisions (the overlay's induction, controls §3.3, unchanged; a
lowered statement held to the same reference by its differential). A void
is a no-op no higher batch depended on, so **a known abort never cascades
to another C batch** (unlike G7.2); survivors rerun at new ranges before
anyone is answered. The committer §3.5 oracle replays voids as no-ops; O
phases keep the parent's argument, and phase changes are drains.

## 5. Visibility on every reader path

### 5.1 The reader rule

Every Postgres reader statement or snapshot binds the pair (W.r, W.s),
sampled immediately before dispatch, and computes in its own snapshot its
**visible revision V** and every row's image:

```sql
V.r = GREATEST($w_r, (SELECT revision FROM _head WHERE singleton))
image(x) = CASE WHEN x.orev IS NULL OR x.orev <= V.r THEN x.data
                WHEN x.owm <= V.r THEN x.odata
                ELSE sb4_stale(x.orev) END
```

with V.s likewise for sequence-keyed tables. `sb4_stale(orev)` raises a
named SQLSTATE and is **STABLE**: it takes the row's stamp, so it is never
constant-folded, and `CASE` evaluates it only when reached. Revision 1's
VOLATILE would have blocked subquery pull-up and qual pushdown through
every output containing it; C1 compares plans (§5.3).

**Lemma 1:** every batch at or below W.r is visible: a synchronous commit
is visible after its flush and before its reply, W advances after that
reply, and the reader's snapshot is taken after its W sample. **Lemma 2:**
every image returned is the row's image at V: an O commit is visible only
with the `_head` it advanced, so V covers it; a row whose last C writer is
at or below V shows `data`, and one above V shows `odata`, valid on
`[owm, orev)`. **So every Snapback read is exactly the state at V**, a
prefix of revision order. A REPEATABLE READ snapshot binds once for all
its statements, as arm E's transport binds its head (`flight.rs:58–61`).

**Every dependent use goes through the image**: projections, predicates,
join and closure keys, rule probes, hidden authorization rows (`exists
memberships[…]`), maintained owner rows and keys derived from fetched data;
`data` outside the expression is a census failure. **The stale branch
restarts the whole snapshot.** A reader delayed between its W sample and
its snapshot, while a later batch rewrote a row it examines, raises; it
discards that snapshot and **everything accumulated from it** (rows,
closure results, G5 fill candidates, any partial response), resamples W
and restarts the logical request, at most three times before a retryable
`E_STORE`. Resampling inside the old snapshot would splice two prefixes;
streaming readers restart before emitting or end with a retryable error.

**Coverage is structural.** Reader SQL builders take a `Watermark` token;
nothing can name `r`, `_commits`, `_log`, `_change_evidence`, `_ops` or
`_outbox` without one, and a census rejects reader SQL naming `r.data`
outside the image expression or `_ops` without its `rev` bound, like arm
E's `Bound` envelope (`early_release.rs:241–251`) without a hold.

### 5.3 Cost on reads: a hypothesis to qualify

A bind parameter, a `_head` InitPlan and a `CASE` per row are noise. The
real costs are measured, not assumed: wider tuples and lower page density
on C-written rows, their WAL and vacuum traffic, stale restarts, plan
changes, and the connections slots take (R2). **C1 qualifies all six read
cells at their rungs, solo and under the arm-E report's 400/s writer, and
compares EXPLAIN plans for W1–W3, G4a closures and fallbacks, before any
writer slot is reserved**; C3 repeats it with the C lane writing. Any
read-rung regression or plan-shape change stops C.

### 5.4 Raw SQL, inspection, backups and graduation

Inspect views read through `_sb4_prefix()` (`_head` plus the contiguous
resolved `_batches` above it), so `snapback sql` and doctor see a prefix.
Raw `r` readers see commits above W out of order, **all durable**; the
deploy guide states the window. Restore reconciles (§6.5), losing only
unacknowledged writes; graduation exports at the durable prefix.

## 6. Holes, outcomes and recovery

### 6.1 The void and the key race

A **void** for batch c is one fenced, synchronous transaction in one
message: `BEGIN; SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on; <fence>; INSERT INTO
_batches (rev_lo, …, state) VALUES (c.r_lo, …, 'void')`, a void
`_commits` row and an empty-footprint evidence row for every sequence c
reserved, c's prune interval (§6.4), then COMMIT, with raising counts. It
is idempotent and keyed on c's range.

**Mutual exclusion.** A unique-index insert meeting an in-progress
transaction's tuple waits for it, then raises 23505 if it committed and
proceeds if it aborted; c inserted its key first. If c committed, the void
raises 23505 and the row's attempt proves c **committed**; if c is in
progress, the void **waits**, which is the drain; if c aborted or its
message has not run, the void commits and a late message raises 23505 at
its insert: c **can never commit**. A void never buries committed work,
**provided its row outlives every attempt that could still execute**.
**Tombstone retention:** a void row stays until c's slot connection has
completed a later transaction, or c's backend (PID and start time) is gone
from `pg_stat_activity`, or ownership has passed and the fence would
refuse it; pruning earlier would let a delayed insert commit a voided range.

A void is issued only for a batch classified aborted or unknown; past
`lock_timeout` the committer terminates c's backend by its recorded PID,
re-checking role, tag and token (store-port §5.2), and retries; if that
fails the store pauses for fenced reacquisition, as G6's sticky rule
requires. A void of unknown outcome is retried: 23505 against a `void` row
bearing its attempt shows the first committed. **On the wire** a void is a
sequence with no events and an empty footprint ("nothing changed", R5).

### 6.2 Outcome table

Per batch, on its own connection; **no outcome reclassifies another**.

| Evidence for C batch c | Classification | c's callers | W |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COMMIT tag after passed assertions, ReadyForQuery `I`, no warning | Confirmed | Released in revision order once W covers c | Resolved |
| Error before COMMIT with ROLLBACK acknowledged (raised assertion, tripwire, validation mismatch, index or maintained delta, 55P03/57014 under the deadline, 40P01, 23505 on its own `_batches` key) | Known abort | Controls §3.1: remove a named failed member and rerun the rest, or each alone, within deadlines; fresh ranges | Void, then resolved |
| Lost reply, error or warning after COMMIT was sent, connection loss after the final message | Unknown | `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` until R3's witnesses pass; then the void's result decides (§6.3) | Void race decides; resolved either way |
| Lease lost before the final message | Not dispatched; rolled back | `E_STORE_OWNERSHIP` for started members | Void if the record insert ran |
| Lease lost after the final message | Actual result if received, else unknown | As above | The new owner reconciles (§6.5) |

### 6.3 Unknown outcomes and the drain

After an unknown, the void race is both drain and classification:
**nothing waits for a ticket probe and nothing is re-seeded**; W stalls at
c while batches above it keep committing within back-pressure. **R3 is yes
on both sides once its witnesses pass** (§13): a won void permits
controls §3.1's bounded rerun, and 23505 against c's own record and
attempt releases c's results; until then ruling 3's "no" holds.

### 6.4 Evidence retention

The tail today deletes evidence at the committing sequences minus 1,000
(`group_tail.rs:23`). Under C, with W.s = 1,000 and a commit at 1,512,
that deletes sequences through 512 while a client at W is owed 1,000, and
`footprint_between` reads the gap as revocation. **The prune is anchored
at W instead**: at dispatch each batch, in either lane, gets the next
contiguous interval `(p_prev, W_c.s − 1000]` (an O batch uses its own end
sequence), recorded as `prune_hi` and deleted by its tail or its void.
Every later reader has V.s ≥ W_c.s, so **at least 1,000 sequences below
every reader's V stay retained**, and disjoint intervals never contend.
(Retaining 1,000 plus the in-flight span would also be correct.)

### 6.5 Crash recovery and takeover reconciliation

Before an owner serves after start, restart or takeover:

1. Acquire ownership (store-port §5.2): takeover's `_owner FOR UPDATE` is
   granted only after every old key share is gone, so every old C
   transaction has ended; a restarting owner first terminates its old
   token's tagged backends.
2. Read `_head` (H) and walk `_batches` up from it: committed and void
   rows extend the prefix; a gap is a hole whose transactions all ended
   uncommitted: **void it**, fenced and synchronous, its reservation
   reconstructed from its neighbours (§4.4) and its prune interval run.
3. **Exact counts, inside the retained window:** every reserved sequence
   has one `_commits` row (void for a void record or unused reservation)
   and, above the retained floor P (the largest `prune_hi` among records at
   or above H, or H's own prune bound), one evidence row; no `_commits` row
   above `H.seq` lies outside a record; no `orev` inside a void range.
   Failure refuses with `SNAPBACK_C_LANE_RECONCILE`; a long C phase with a
   lagging H no longer trips the counts.
4. Write `_head` to the reconciled prefix with a fresh attempt, fenced and
   synchronous, and serve with W equal to it.

### 6.6 Fencing, deadlines and back-pressure

Every C transaction, void and catch-up takes the owner fence first as a
raising command; takeover terminates every tagged writer (store-port
§5.1–§5.2). Timeouts come from the earliest member deadline; a timeout
before COMMIT is a known abort; COMMIT runs with both at zero. Past a
250 ms void stall the lane stops admitting and reports `E_STORE`.

## 7. Interactions

- **G6.** Readers cannot see an unpublished C batch, so **C batches
  neither close the gate nor count in `inflight`**; publication installs
  atomically with each W advance. O batches keep the parent's count and
  sticky uncertainty; a C unknown is settled by its void.
- **Arm E.** Two release rules on one read path are unsafe as well as
  costly: **R6, `commit=early` and the C lane are mutually exclusive**.
- **G7.2 and G7.3.** The O lane keeps W5 exact and gains Stages P and L;
  G7.3's raising assertions, exact completion checks and uncancellable
  COMMIT govern every co-dispatched COMMIT here.
- **Barriers and generality.** Claimers, sweeps, session and identity
  writers, activation, adoption and ineligible members drain the C lane;
  a workload with frequent barriers lives in O phases and gains little
  from C (S1's W4 has none). A generality limit, not a correctness gap.
- **Write levers.** Ingress hints and plan reuse stay optimisations;
  **neither establishes footprint independence nor authority**.
- **Receipts and replay (C4).** The receipt key joins the footprint;
  preflight and the serial re-check read `_ops` at `rev ≤ V.r` and wait on
  a key an open batch holds; replay-before-judgment is unchanged.
- **SQLite is unchanged**: one writer transaction at a time, every
  program interpreted, the differential's reference. Stages P and L and
  the C lane are `postgres`-only.

### 10.2 Witnesses

1. **Serial-equivalence differential against N=1 in revision order**,
   extending G3b's paired oracle (seeds, 192 members, both stores): random
   boundaries, K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}, adversarial completion order, disjoint,
   hot-key and 1% mixes, refusals, barriers, lane switches, lowered,
   validated and interpreted paths; comparing outcomes, meters, rows,
   images, sequences, reservations, evidence, head and attempts.
2. **Adversarial footprint collisions** (shared hidden probe, absent key,
   dependent table, scan boundary, maintained owner row) are held or
   routed to O; `i` or maintained mutations hit the hard check; a planted
   footprint bug is caught by the differential, and by the tripwire once
   its writer has committed.
3. **Faults per flight with 0–7 other C batches in flight**, both forms
   (insert, apply, validation or assertion failure; 55P03 at a deadline;
   termination around COMMIT; socket loss; a hung backend forcing
   termination; a lost void reply): §6.2's row, no reply above W, no
   cascade, exact counts, `pg_xact_status` agreement.
4. **Crash and kill matrix with holes** of one and several batches,
   takeover mid-phase, a `_head` lagging past the prune window and a lost
   catch-up: every acknowledged write present, every hole a void rebuilt
   from its neighbours, §6.5's counts passing.
5. **Reader prefix witnesses, one per §5.2 class:** state at V; V
   monotone per session; read-your-writes; a reader delayed across two
   writes of one row restarts with nothing from its first snapshot;
   dependent probes read the image; receipts only at `rev ≤ V.r`; G6 hits
   racing W serve only installed publication.
6. **Retention:** a delayed insert after its void raises 23505 while the
   tombstone stands; under adversarial completion order 1,000 sequences
   below every reader's V keep their evidence.
7. **Locks and order:** no deadlocks, no waits between C transactions
   except a void on its own batch, one flight per C batch, release order
   equal to revision order.

### 10.4 Stop rules and default-on

Stop the C lane on any serial-equivalence, prefix or release-order
violation, a lost acknowledged write, a void and a commit both committed
for one key, a deadlock, a read-rung regression or plan change (revisit
R2 first), C0′'s stop rule, or PG CPU per write up over 30%; stop Stage L
on any differential mismatch it cannot name and fall back for. Stages P
and L and C1 (W ≡ `_head`, no behaviour change) ship default-on once
clean; the C lane is opt-in (`lane=disjoint`, refused with
`commit=early`) until C3 passes §10.2 and qualifies on S1.

## 11. Alternatives considered

- **Rejected:** the naive variant (parent §7; §2.3 maps each defect); a
  publication epoch gate on every reader (it stalls reads most of the
  time; arm E's hold cost 4/6 read cells); `commit_delay` (the one G7.0
  cell that merged flushes got slower); two-phase commit (two flushes per
  batch); exported snapshots (prefix moments may not exist);
  SERIALIZABLE (no revision order).
- **Deferred:** full versioned rows and index entries, the extension if C
  must cover inserts and deletes; a typed per-table layout (controls
  §10.2), reopened by Stage P's residue; writes on borrowed reader-pool
  connections (Supabase's shape), which reverse ruling 6.
- **Fallbacks:** private intents with an ordered publisher (controls
  §10.4), about +1 ms on A, if C0′ rejects two images; arm E with small
  batches is bounded by §2.2's chain and holds reads.

## 12. Risks

- **Stage P drift** (a check function missing or altered) must refuse,
  never skip: digest checks at lease acquisition and in doctor. **Stage
  L** is a second executor for its fragment: differential, planted fault,
  fallback before execution and the L′ comparator.
- **Census incompleteness and footprint misclassification** break the
  prefix or serial equivalence silently: the `Watermark` token, census,
  prefix hammer, differential, adversarial generators and hard checks.
- **Model error** in Stage P's cut, the per-member slope, the flush
  residual or SMT sharing leaves A lost on the √2 ladder; B still wins.
- **Connections, WAL, wire, complexity:** measured reader floor (R2),
  doubled WAL on C-written rows, harmless void sequences (R5), two lanes.

## 13. Rulings and open questions

**R1, the finer tie-break rung (author; S1 §4 and §6 method amendment):
pending**, with §9's conditions; not a planning target.

**Decided after the round-1 panel**, as all three panelists answered:

- **R2, connections: yes.** Elastic dedicated slots within the 32, K from
  C0′'s measured N, returned after 5 s idle and before pure read cells;
  the reader floor measured on W1/W2 under mixed reads, not 20 by
  default. Conditions: astra (floor from mixed-read qualification), grok
  (measured floor; idle slots returned before pure read cells), Fable (K
  from measured N; W1/W2 re-measured at the floor).
- **R3, void-resolved outcomes: yes, both sides**, once §10.2 items 3, 4
  and 6, the fencing tests and a release-path witness pass (§6.3).
  Conditions: astra (after fencing, retention and release witnesses), grok
  (after the fault witnesses; a void row and 23505 against the batch's own
  attempt are direct evidence), Fable (once items 3–4 pass).
- **R4, one-flight freshness: yes, in two clauses.** **R4a:** ingress
  session facts validated in the fenced statement by equality, expiry
  checked against the member's deadline, are round-trip §7's fresh final
  check (L and L′). **R4b:** mirror program facts validated by equality,
  probes and absences included, at point keys only (spans need a
  validating predicate), are committer §3.6's fresh check (L′ only).
  Authority and time writers still drain C. Conditions: astra (complete
  facts, absence, identity; expiry preserved, since equality cannot
  validate elapsed time), grok (image at the dispatch watermark with probes
  and absences; authority and time writers drain), Fable (point keys only;
  lowering makes R4 unnecessary for program facts, as R4b's scope records).
- **R5, void sequences: yes.** Every reserved sequence has a void
  `_commits` row and an empty-footprint evidence row ("no change"; a
  missing row is revocation; a gap is never acceptable); where cursors are
  opaque the server skips void sequences; an SDK `/changes` and sync
  conformance test is the gate. Conditions: astra (allocation, recovery
  and conformance agree), grok (load-bearing; conformance the gate), Fable
  (drop server-side if cursors are opaque, else conformance).
- **R6, C and arm E exclusive: yes** (all three).

**Open questions:** Stage P's actual cut and whether typed arrays suffice
before binary Bind; whether a fill hold makes depth-one N≈32 viable; the
best N and K once per-batch cost falls; hot-key holds and W5 mix's p99;
`odata` on wide rows; how far the lowerable fragment should grow; P2 would
need W from `_sb4_prefix()`.

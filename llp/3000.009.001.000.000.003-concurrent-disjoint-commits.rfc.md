## 1. Summary

Reads win 6/6 S1 cells; W5 exact (A, B) and W5 mix/A win; W4/B and W5 mix/B
tie at 1,600; **W4/A loses, 2,262 against the Supabase advocate's 3,200**. A
strict win needs the next √2 rung: **W4/A at 4,525/s, a mean residence of at
most 7.07 ms with 32 clients (about 6.4 ms at a passing 90% occupancy), and
W4/B and W5 mix/B at 2,262/s, at most 14.15 ms**; Supabase fails both rungs.
The product measures about 15 ms and 17 ms.

This RFC is the program for the remaining write cells (§3.1). **The plan:
Stage P, in progress as its own lane; then L′ on the ordered lane; then C0′,
its harness built beside P; then C1–C3 if C0′ gates C in. Stage L is
conditional.** **P** (§3.2) attributes, then cuts, the ≈2.4 ms fitted
per-batch server intercept on both lanes. **L′** (§3.3) interprets before the
flight on a mirror; one fenced message validates every consumed fact and
aborts on any mismatch. **C0′** (§3.5) is a product-shaped harness with the
product's measured distributions injected; **C proceeds only if it passes
4,525/s on A under S1's criteria and materially beats the improved ordered
lane.** **C** (§4–§6) commits footprint-disjoint batches concurrently;
two-image rows and a bound watermark keep every reader on a prefix of one
revision order; voids close holes by a key race. **L** (§3.4), lowering the
point update, is built only if C0′ shows a lowered flight is what meets A;
otherwise it waits behind the typed layout.

**Budget verdicts** (§8; likelihoods are judgment; server time on Stage P's
own formula): **B is reachable on the ordered lane** (Stage P about 10.5–13.3
ms, L′ 8.5–11.4, against 14.15). **A needs one flight, most of Stage P's cut
and probably C.** The best shape, C at N=16, K=2–3, on L′ with per-member
preparation at 20–30 µs in parallel, is 6.6–8.6 ms by terms and 6.2–7.4 from
G7.0's arm C; the ordered lane at N≈32 is 7.3–9.5. The program: **about one in
four**, down from revision 2's two in five, whose server rows sat below Stage
P's formula. **R1, a finer tie-break rung, is the author's decision and
pending** (§9); R2–R6 are decided (§13). Citations use paths relative to
`snapback4/` at `5fc6e6449b`; bare names are in `crates/snapback4-core/src/`,
its `engine/` or `engine/postgres/`.

### 2.2 What a strict W4/A win requires

With 32 clients at the edge of capacity, Little's law sets the bar: a mean of
at most 32/4,525 = **7.07 ms**, about 6.4 ms with the margin three passing
repetitions need (G7.0's arm C passed at 6.93 ms with a worst intended p99 of
84 ms against 100). HTTP 0.45–0.63 ms, 0.56 ms of link per flight, the flush
(2.64 ms sync, 2.70 ms PG commit) and release 0.25–0.35 ms are fixed, so **one
flight leaves about 3 ms for CPU, server work, phasing and the flush residual;
a second flight leaves 2.5.**

- **Supabase is flush-bound too.** One `set_config` and one RPC (a SECURITY
  DEFINER increment with an explicit authorization predicate) in one
  transaction; at 3,200/s about 370 flushes/s × 2.64 ms ≈ 98% of the device,
  p50 8.02 ms; it fails 4,525. The contest is everything else.
- **The shapes** (G7.0, SQL only: no HTTP, interpreter, guard or fence): arm C
  at N=16 on two connections, 6.93 ms at 4,525/s, sync/batch 1.003, its gain
  the missing chain; **N=8 on four merged flushes (0.705) and was slower, 7.74
  ms**; ordered S at N=32, three flights, 8.39–8.73 ms. A product mean adds
  HTTP, interpretation and authority CPU, a P1 ≈0.45 ms slower than G7.0's
  fused opener, and release.
- **The ordered lane's limit.** At depth d a request spans about d chains
  (≈4.7 ms after G7.3), so depth two cannot pass 4,525; at depth one with N≈32
  residence is one batch cycle, so the per-member tail matters as much as
  concurrency. The best shape's floor, every term low, is ≈6.6 ms on Stage P's
  formula, 6.2 from G7.0 (§8.2); the raw-PG token reference measures p50 5.43
  ms on W5 mix/B at 4,525/s ([one-host
  final](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/results-20261024-s1-onehost-final/README.md)).

### 3.2 Stage P: Postgres per-batch server cost

**P0 first.** The ≈2.4 ms is a fitted intercept (flight minus RTT against N:
P1 server 1.07 + 0.025N, tail 1.32 + 0.061N ms), not an attribution (Fable).
P0 adds per-statement server attribution (`pg_stat_statements` with planning,
nested `auto_explain`, per-batch clocks) on W4/A, W4/B and W5 mix/B before any
cut is credited. What P can buy: product PG CPU of 263–300 µs per write at
N≈11, against G7.0 arm C's 134 at N=16 on the product's own tail SQL.
**Suspects:** the settings guard (`flight.rs:44`) and owner fence
(`ownership.rs:74`), `DO` blocks compiled and planned on every flight; the
nine-CTE tail (`group_tail.rs:7–47`), parsing `$1::text::jsonb` seven times
and running empty CTEs for W4; per member, the image, `_commits` and evidence
rows and the prune.

**Changes, keeping today's semantics:** (1) guard and fence as **check
functions** called by prepared `EXECUTE`, each still raising S4001 or S4002
before any data command in its message, the fence VOLATILE, taking `FOR KEY
SHARE` on `_owner`, still the first lock and its own command (round-trip §7,
store-port §5.1); (2) **typed arrays** (`unnest` over `text[]` and `int8[]`)
instead of seven JSON parses, ahead of binary Bind (round-trip §3.6); (3)
**shape-specialised tails**, each present CTE keeping its raising count; (4)
**set-based per-member work** (grok): images, `_commits` and evidence each one
statement over typed arrays, generic plans confirmed, P1's statements fused
where shapes allow, and **no prune on any batch's path** (§6.4).

**Function lifecycle** (astra, grok, Fable). Names carry the body digest
(`sb4_fence_<digest>`) and calls are schema-qualified. Functions are created
only if absent, never replaced, on today's schema-ensure path: the opening
transaction that installs every physical upgrade under the head lock
(`postgres/store.rs:284`), fenced by the inline `DO` fence. Older binaries
keep the `DO` form, so a mixed fleet is safe, and a newer binary starting
mid-lease adds a name instead of replacing a running owner's body; old
versions are dropped only after a takeover. Lease acquisition and doctor
validate signature, owner, `search_path`, `SECURITY INVOKER`, VOLATILE,
language and body digest from `pg_proc`, dropping the lease on failure;
prepared state is invalidated on upgrade and reconnect. A missing function
raises 42883 before data and refuses like S4001. Privileged alteration during
a running lease stays outside the supported boundary, like other runtime DDL
on managed relations (controls §6).

**Prediction (assumed, pending P0):** fixed server 2.4 → 1.2–1.7 ms, slope
0.086 → 0.052–0.069 ms per member, on the ordered lane before any C-lane work;
every fault still raises before data; oracles and difftest unchanged. **A
measured slope near 0.05 ms per member makes per-member image work the slope's
kind and reopens the typed layout (controls §10.2) for this fragment** (grok).

### 3.3 L′: one flight on validated warm facts

The slot interprets each member before any flight, in parallel, on a bounded
mirror and ingress-carried session facts; the final message validates every
consumed fact inside the fenced transaction, and any failure **raises and
aborts the attempt**, the batch rerunning two-flight.

- **Mirror:** images this committer committed and published, plus **validated
  read facts** (probes, absences and spans a committed batch validated: S1
  never writes `memberships`, so write-through alone leaves W5 mix's hot
  member cold; Fable), dropped at every barrier, lane change, unknown outcome
  and ownership event. A row an unresolved batch writes is cold; a stale entry
  costs only a miss.
- **Targets: a locked probe** classifies each row (absent, hidden, rule
  refusal, image mismatch, ok), and a raising check requires the predicted
  class; equality in the apply's `WHERE` alone would conflate a changed row
  with an absent one and commit `NOT_FOUND` (grok).
- **Reads:** points and absences are asserted by key; **a span only if re-read
  whole under a phantom-rejecting lock** (grok), the O lane's ticket or a C
  phase's frozen `i`. A unique-key probe such as `exists memberships[.room,
  viewer]` is such a span over `i.k` (`key::encode` of the components, then
  the id, `rows.rs:156`), asserted with its count and ids as `t = $t AND ix =
  $ix AND k >= $p AND k < $p_next`, the prefix's successor bounding it
  (Fable); uniqueness keeps it to one row (grok).
- **Authority:** the assertion is generated from **everything the shared
  resolver consumed** (astra), every `SessionFacts` field
  (`identity/facts.rs:7`): sessions with identity, family, kind, scope and
  digest, principal generation, worker family and revision, family revision,
  store generation and id, backend generation, schema and activation hashes
  and device generation, absent metadata asserted absent. Only `ordinary`
  kinds are eligible, every other kind falling back to the two-flight
  resolver; a census fails on any unasserted input.
- **Time:** committer §6's clock. A member is judged at its `at`; the
  statement asserts every lifetime edge it observed (session and read-cap
  expiry, pending openings, examined rows' edges) lies beyond E, its deadline
  on that clock, and dispatch enforces `at < E` with timeouts from E. A
  session expiring before E is a **miss resolved afresh**, never an
  expired-session refusal; unchanged bytes never validate elapsed lifetime
  (astra).
- **Keyed members join on the O lane** (Fable: the SDK always sends `{id,
  args}`, `packages/snapback4/src/client.ts:1013`): receipt absence is a point
  assertion, its own command after the head CAS (round-trip §3.5); a hit is a
  mismatch. C's first slices stay unkeyed (C4).

**Cost and keep rule.** Preparation is 0.146 ms per member on today's critical
path; L′ interprets members in parallel as they are collected, overlapping the
previous flight, targeting 20–30 µs per member (Fable), plus ≈0.12–0.15 ms of
validation at N=16. Kept if one flight beats G7.3 + P by ≥0.5 ms of W4/A mean
at 3,200/s, differential clean, on W4, W5 mix and keyed W4.

### 3.4 Stage L, conditional: lowering the point update

W4 reads a counter (`! else NOT_FOUND`) and returns `update counters[c.id] {
n: c.n + 1 }` under `.owner = viewer or (.owner = null and exists
memberships[.room, viewer])` ([S1
schema](../snapback4/experiments/scale-controls/snapback-app/snapback/schema.q)).
Lowered, one statement per batch would lock and classify each target in
evaluation order, update `ok` members and return classifications, probes and
images. **Eligibility:** one keyed read `! else E`, optional `require`, one
`update` of that row; rules compiling to SQL over the row, viewer, arguments
and keyed probes on unwritten tables; guarded arithmetic with Quarry's null
and overflow rules; no index move, maintained write, effect, presence, time,
entropy or `ctx.sql`; images under 2 KiB; meters covering the fixed trace,
else fallback before execution (controls §10.3); one member per row key, so W5
exact stays interpreted; unkeyed.

**Its true cost** (Fable). `n` lives in the JSON image (grok), so the
statement must produce `r.data` byte for byte: canonical tagged JSON with
sorted keys and compact serde (`value.rs:378`), the i53 model and its overflow
(`value.rs:118`), images that may omit literal defaults (`rows.rs`,
`stored_index_keys` → `row_image`) and `key::encode` probe prefixes (`jsonb`
reorders keys and whitespace, so it cannot stand in), plus footprint JSON
in-statement, since evidence is written before Rust sees the images: a second
executor and encoder. For W4, where worker w increments only counter w
(`experiments/scale-controls/src/load.rs:139–147`), L′'s mirror is always warm
and its server work equals L's: **L buys only interpreter CPU**, which L′'s
per-member cut attacks directly.

**Contract if built** (astra): update predicates judge **both the old and the
next image**; constraints; returned-image visibility; the complete meter
trace; eligibility restricted where these, sync and log production or
lifecycle behaviour cannot be reproduced; authority as L′; lost replies per
§6.3; controls §10.3's differential, boundary sweeps and planted fault;
`snapback check` names each fallback, and agents write nothing new. It is a
general compiler optimisation (grok, astra), never a change to the benchmark's
program. **When** (grok, Fable): only if C0′'s hand-lowered C-L arm passes the
A bound where C on fast L′ does not; otherwise it waits behind the typed
layout (controls §10.2), where images are columns. Revision 2's ordered-lane
keep rule is withdrawn: it judged L on a shape that does not decide A.

### 3.5 Stage C0′: the product-shaped harness

G7.0's harness, with no product edit, sends the product's messages (checks,
fence, session validation, timeouts, raising counts and COMMIT as built today
**and** as cut by P; `_batches`, two-image apply and tripwire; L′'s probes and
validation; for C-L, a hand-lowered W4 statement). **It is built now, beside
P** (Fable); only its stop bound waits for P and L′. It **injects the
product's measured HTTP, critical-path CPU and fill distributions** from the
post-L′ ledger instead of subtracting means, since C's two slots keep the
device ≈75% busy and gain only in phase (Fable), and it measures **ordered
release behind W**, formation from realistic arrivals with holds, and
validation misses at W5 mix's rates (astra). Arms: **O1** (one flight, depth
one and two, N ∈ {8, 16, 32}, a bounded fill hold); **C2f**; **C-L′** at
measured and at 20–30 µs per-member CPU; **C-L**; C at N ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}, K
∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16}, pacing on and off with its hold counted. It reports
mean, p50, p99, syncs per batch, flush waits, PG CPU, stale raises, a prefix
hammer, the void race under kill and reader statement cost, with three
open-loop confirmations at 4,525/s on A and 2,262/s on B.

**Stop rule.** C proceeds only if its best configuration, distributions
injected, **passes 4,525/s on A in all three confirmations under S1's
unchanged arrival, error and p99 criteria** (a mean ≤7.07 ms is necessary, not
sufficient; astra) **and** beats the best O1 measured the same way by ≥1.0 ms
of mean, or passes where O1 fails. If O1 passes, O1 ships and C is not built
for S1; if neither does, A is out of reach on the √2 ladder. **B_A**, the
screening form (7.07 minus post-L′ HTTP, critical-path CPU and release), is
≈4.5 ms on today's terms (0.63; 0.51 + 1.12; 0.30), not revision 2's 5.6
(grok), and ≈5.7–6.2 at L′'s target.

## 4. Stage C: the concurrent lane

### 4.1 Two lanes, the phase rule and the drain

- **O lane:** G7.2 (G7.3 once it lands, with P and L′), barriers and the solo
  route, unchanged. **C lane:** up to K writer slots, each one independent
  transaction, no ticket, `_head` access or fold. A batch is **open** from
  dispatch until the watermark W covers it (§4.5).
- **Phase rule.** Every open batch belongs to one lane; switching drains the
  lane in use; **barriers drain both** and write `_head`. Exclusive because an
  O commit's `_head` would expose a lower, uncommitted C batch's rows at their
  old images, and an O cascade re-seeds sequences from `_head` (parent §4.2)
  into C ranges above it.
- **The C→O drain**: every C batch resolved and W published; **every catch-up
  resolved** (reply received, or backend terminated and confirmed gone); then
  one fenced synchronous transaction checks `_head` is at or below W with an
  attempt this committer wrote and **writes `_head` = (W.s, W.r, a fresh
  attempt)**, which the first O CAS names; sequences resume at W.s + 1, so no
  O commit aims at a stale catch-up or jumps `_head` over records recovery
  would not rescan.

### 4.2 Footprints and eligibility

A member's footprint is computed **before dispatch** from the G3b bounded
closure plan (`program_batch::bounded_plan`, `committer/closure.rs`), which
covers rule probes, hidden examined rows and alternative paths (controls
§3.3). Batches **commute** if neither writes what the other reads or writes;
entries are row keys, or **whole tables** where a dependent level's keys
depend on fetched data. Coarsening loses concurrency, never a dependency.

| Dependency | Footprint entry | C lane, first slices | Later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rows read by key, including rule probes and absent keys | Row key (absence is the key) | Admitted | — |
| Rows written | Row key | Updates of existing rows that change no `i` entry, hard-checked | Inserts and deletes need versioned index entries (§11) |
| Dependent-level reads | Table or index span | Admitted as table entries | Span entries |
| Ranges, scan boundaries, uniqueness, counts over `i` | Index span | Read-only; `i` is never written in the C lane | — |
| Maintained totals | Owner row read; source writes excluded | Reads admitted; maintained writes excluded, hard-checked | Commutative class (committer §4.5) |
| Caps, `_sequences`, allocations | — | Excluded | — |
| Client receipts `(principal, op id)` | Receipt key | Excluded (unkeyed only) | C4, receipts filtered by creating revision |
| Effects, outbox, presence marks | — | Excluded | C4; claimers stay barriers |
| Authority: sessions, identity, generation, `_meta` | Shared read | Admitted; never written in the C lane (its writers are barriers) | — |
| Time | — | Time-independent programs; no `not before` schema (`program_pipeline.rs:183–187`) | — |

**`i` is frozen, by enforcement.** Index entries live in a second, unversioned
table (`store.rs`: delete plus insert on `(t, ix, k)`), so a C write that
moved an `i` key would let a scan find a payload showing its old image while
the old key misses, unseen by the tripwire. The router admits only plans with
no `i` mutation or maintained write, the slot sends a member with a non-empty
index or maintained delta to the O lane, and a census asserts the C apply path
issues neither; `i` is then identical across a C phase, so scans, uniqueness
probes and counts need no versioning. The overlay serializes members within a
batch as today; an unbounded closure is ineligible.

### 4.3 Routing, holds and lane switching

- **The open index U** maps each row key and table in an open batch's
  footprint to that batch. Keying U by *open*, not *unresolved*, is essential:
  a committed batch above an unresolved one is in no reader's V, and a
  successor reading its rows would act on an image no prefix has.
- **Formation and dispatch.** In FIFO order, an eligible member disjoint from
  U joins the forming batch; a conflicting member is **held**, blocking only
  later members of its principal, until W covers its blockers. A batch of at
  most 32 members dispatches when a slot is free, the mailbox non-empty and g
  elapsed, g and K starting at C0′'s best measured configuration.
  **Back-pressure:** no dispatch while `next_revision − W.r > 512` or 2K
  batches are open.
- **Switching.** C→O when held or ineligible members exceed 25% of arrivals
  over 50 ms; O→C at 90% eligible and unconflicted; minimum dwell 100 ms. W5
  exact stays ordered; an idle eligible member is a one-member C batch.

### 4.4 Ranges, reservations and the batch record

At dispatch, in dispatch order, one sequencer serving both lanes assigns each
C batch **one revision and one public sequence per member** (`[r_lo, r_hi]`,
`[s_lo, s_hi]`, never empty), a fresh attempt, `at = max(T, pin)` (committer
§6) and an entropy key. **Reservations:** the final message publishes
committed members on the lowest reserved sequences and writes, for each unused
one (a refused or failed member), a void `_commits` row (`kind='void'`, zero
writes, the batch's `at`) and an empty-footprint evidence row, so every
reserved sequence has exactly one of each; a whole-batch void does the same
for its range (§6.1).

```sql
CREATE TABLE _batches (
  rev_lo BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, rev_hi BIGINT NOT NULL,
  seq_lo BIGINT NOT NULL, seq_hi BIGINT NOT NULL, attempt TEXT NOT NULL,
  state TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('committed','void')),
  at BIGINT NOT NULL);
```

**The record is the batch's lock and durable commit record**, inserted with
`state='committed'` as the first write after the fence: its uncommitted tuple
is what a void waits on (§6.1), and its committed row, read with its attempt,
proves the commit. Ranges are never empty and are reserved in lockstep, so **a
hole's reservation is reconstructible from its durable neighbours** (the
revisions and sequences strictly between the record or `_head` below and the
record above), with no descriptor of the lost batch and no reservation fsync.
Committed rows below `_head.revision` are pruned in bounded steps; void rows
only under §6.1's rule.

**Receipts carry their creating revision**, since refusals and receipt-only
successes store an older sequence (`mutation.rs:241`). `_ops` gains `rev
BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` among the opening transaction's physical upgrades,
metadata-only on the supported Postgres 16 and 17. Existing rows, and any an
older binary writes before it loses ownership, read 0, visible at every V:
correct, since each committed before any C phase could begin (takeover ends
every older transaction, §6.5). This binary's lanes write `rev` explicitly, as
the census checks, so C1 stays behaviour-neutral (grok: an unfilled column
would hide every receipt, `NULL <= V.r` not being true). **Entropy** becomes
counter-mode, keyed by revision, so a voided batch's draws are never used;
until then eligible members draw none. Minted ids tolerate gaps.

### 4.5 The watermark W and `_head`

**W** is the pair (W.r, W.s): the largest revision at or below which every
batch is **resolved** (committed and confirmed, or voided with the void
confirmed), and its public sequence. W advances only on resolutions, under the
publication lock, which in one step installs G5/G6 publication, wakes
`/changes` and long waits, and releases replies in revision order; readers
sample it as one atomic pair.

**`_head` is a durable low-water mark** under C (amending store-port §7.2),
written by O commits, barriers, recovery, the drain and **catch-ups**: a
fenced `UPDATE _head … WHERE singleton AND attempt=$last` that also prunes
evidence behind the head it writes (§6.4), about every 1,000 revisions or 100
ms, on a free slot, off every request's path; a lost one only lengthens
recovery's scan and retention. Invariants: `_head.revision ≤ W.r` in a C
phase, equality after each drain, and **the durable prefix (`_head` plus the
contiguous resolved `_batches` above it) is never below W**.

### 4.6 Two-image rows: how a commit stays hidden above W

`r` gains nullable, unindexed columns (updates stay HOT): `orev`, the revision
of the last C write; `owm`, that batch's dispatch watermark; and `odata`. A C
batch's image update replaces `group_tail.rs:10–11` (or, if Stage L is built,
is its statement in this form):

```sql
UPDATE r SET odata = r.data, data = w.data, orev = w.rev, owm = $w
FROM <images> AS w(t, id, data, rev)
WHERE r.t = w.t AND r.id = w.id AND (r.orev IS NULL OR r.orev <= $w)
```

followed by a raising count check; O-lane and barrier writes keep writing
`data` alone. **Invariant.** For every row x, `data` is x's latest committed
image, and if its last writer is a C batch, `odata` is its image at every
revision in `[owm(x), orev(x))`. *Proof.* x was in c's write footprint, so no
other open batch touched it, and every earlier writer was covered by W_c =
owm(x); so `data` at c's write was x's image at W_c, and no batch between W_c
and c wrote x. O writes run only when no C batch is open and advance `_head`,
so their stale stamps lie below every reader's V (§5.1).

**The `orev ≤ $w` predicate is a tripwire**: a misclassified footprint whose
conflicting writer has committed raises a known abort, while one whose writer
has not escapes it, so the router is the mechanism and the differential
(§10.2) qualifies it. **Costs:** stamps and `odata` exist only on C-written
rows, which write about twice the tuple bytes to WAL at lower page density;
rows over 2 KiB are ineligible at first, keeping TOAST copies out; doctor
reports stamps.

### 4.7 The flight of a C batch

**One flight, the form C is built in.** One message: `BEGIN … READ COMMITTED
READ WRITE`; `SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on`; the settings check and owner
fence in Stage P's form; the `_batches` insert; the generation, identity and
session assertions, L′'s locked probes and validation (§3.3), or the lowered
statement if L is built (§3.4), applied as two images with the tripwire, every
read through §5.1's expression at the dispatch W; the vector tail with
reservation voids; raising counts; timeouts from the earliest member deadline,
reset to zero; COMMIT, under **G7.3's ruling-4 conditions** (every check
raises before it; it is never cancelled). Slots record backend PID and start
time at connect. **Two flights are the fallback** for cold rows, validation
misses and other members. A C transaction holds only the fence's key share,
its own rows and its own key, so C transactions never wait on each other and
can share flushes.

### 4.8 Why the committed history is serial-equivalent

A C batch c reads its facts at W_c; every lower batch was covered by W_c or
open at c's dispatch and so touches nothing in c's footprint. Hence **c's
facts equal the state after every lower batch, restricted to its footprint**,
and its members run as the serial reference `run` would at their revisions
(the overlay's induction, controls §3.3). A void is a no-op no higher batch
depended on, so **a known abort never cascades to another C batch** (unlike
G7.2); survivors rerun at new ranges before anyone is answered. The committer
§3.5 oracle replays voids as no-ops; O phases keep the parent's argument;
phase changes are drains.

## 5. Visibility on every reader path

### 5.1 The reader rule

Every Postgres reader statement or snapshot binds the pair (W.r, W.s), sampled
immediately before dispatch, and computes in its own snapshot its **visible
revision V** and every row's image:

```sql
V.r = GREATEST($w_r, (SELECT revision FROM _head WHERE singleton))
image(x) = CASE WHEN x.orev IS NULL OR x.orev <= V.r THEN x.data
                WHEN x.owm <= V.r THEN x.odata
                ELSE sb4_stale(x.orev) END
```

with V.s likewise for sequence-keyed tables. `sb4_stale(orev)` raises a named
SQLSTATE and is **STABLE**: it takes the row's stamp, so it is never
constant-folded, and `CASE` evaluates it only when reached. Revision 1's
VOLATILE would have blocked subquery pull-up and qual pushdown through every
output containing it; C1 compares plans (§5.3).

**Lemma 1:** every batch at or below W.r is visible: a synchronous commit is
visible after its flush and before its reply, W advances after that reply, and
the reader's snapshot follows its W sample. **Lemma 2:** every image returned
is the row's image at V: an O commit is visible only with the `_head` it
advanced, so V covers it; a row whose last C writer is at or below V shows
`data`, and one above V shows `odata`, valid on `[owm, orev)`. **So every
Snapback read is exactly the state at V**, a prefix of revision order; a
REPEATABLE READ snapshot binds once for all its statements, as arm E's
transport binds its head (`flight.rs:58–61`).

**Every dependent use goes through the image** (projections, predicates, join
and closure keys, rule probes, hidden authorization rows, maintained owner
rows, keys derived from fetched data); `data` outside the expression is a
census failure. **The stale branch restarts the whole snapshot**: a reader
delayed between its W sample and its snapshot, while a later batch rewrote a
row it examines, discards that snapshot and **everything accumulated from it**
(rows, closure results, G5 fill candidates, any partial response), resamples W
and restarts the logical request, at most three times before a retryable
`E_STORE`; streaming readers restart before emitting or end with a retryable
error. **Coverage is structural.** Reader SQL builders take a `Watermark`
token; nothing can name `r`, `_commits`, `_log`, `_change_evidence`, `_ops` or
`_outbox` without one, and a census rejects reader SQL naming `r.data` outside
the image expression or `_ops` without its `rev` bound, like arm E's `Bound`
envelope (`early_release.rs:241–251`) without a hold.

A bind parameter, a `_head` InitPlan and a `CASE` per row are noise; the real

## 6. Holes, outcomes and recovery

### 6.1 The void and the key race

A **void** for batch c is one fenced, synchronous transaction in one message:
`BEGIN; SET LOCAL synchronous_commit=on; <fence>; INSERT INTO _batches
(rev_lo, …, state) VALUES (c.r_lo, …, 'void')`, a void `_commits` row and an
empty-footprint evidence row for every sequence c reserved, then COMMIT, with
raising counts. It is idempotent, keyed on c's range, and prunes nothing.

**Mutual exclusion.** A unique-index insert meeting an in-progress
transaction's tuple waits for it, then raises 23505 if it committed and
proceeds if it aborted; c inserted its key first. If c committed, the void
raises 23505 and the row's attempt proves c **committed**; if c is in
progress, the void **waits**, which is the drain; if c aborted or its message
has not run, the void commits and a late message raises 23505 at its insert: c
**can never commit**, **provided the void row outlives every attempt that
could still execute**. **Tombstone retention** (grok): the row stays until the
backend that received c's message (PID and start time, recorded at connect)
has finished it, shown by a later transaction completing **on that same
backend**; or that backend is gone from `pg_stat_activity`; or the fence would
refuse c's token. A later transaction on a reconnected slot proves nothing:
the old backend may still hold c's message and would commit a voided range.

A void is issued only for a batch classified aborted or unknown; past
`lock_timeout` the committer terminates c's backend by its recorded PID,
re-checking role, tag and token (store-port §5.2), and retries, else the store
pauses for fenced reacquisition (G6's sticky rule). A retried void meeting
23505 against a `void` row with its own attempt shows the first committed.
**On the wire** a void is "nothing changed" (R5).

### 6.2 Outcome table

Per batch, on its own connection; **no outcome reclassifies another**.

| Evidence for C batch c | Classification | c's callers | W |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COMMIT tag after passed assertions, ReadyForQuery `I`, no warning | Confirmed | Released in revision order once W covers c | Resolved |
| Error before COMMIT with ROLLBACK acknowledged (raised assertion, tripwire, probe or validation mismatch, index or maintained delta, 55P03/57014 under the deadline, 40P01, 23505 on its own `_batches` key) | Known abort | Controls §3.1: remove a named failed member and rerun the rest, or each alone, within deadlines; fresh ranges | Void, then resolved |
| Lost reply, error or warning after COMMIT was sent, connection loss after the final message | Unknown | `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` until R3's witnesses pass; then the void's result decides (§6.3) | Void race decides; resolved either way |
| Lease lost before the final message | Not dispatched; rolled back | `E_STORE_OWNERSHIP` for started members | Void if the record insert ran |
| Lease lost after the final message | Actual result if received, else unknown | As above | The new owner reconciles (§6.5) |

### 6.3 Unknown outcomes and the drain

After an unknown, the void race is both drain and classification: **nothing
waits for a ticket probe and nothing is re-seeded**; W stalls at c while
batches above it keep committing within back-pressure. **R3 is yes on both
sides once its witnesses pass** (§13). A won void proves c can never commit
and permits controls §3.1's bounded rerun. 23505 against c's own record and
attempt proves c committed, and **a proven commit is never rerun** (astra);
its exact results are released only where the committer still holds them, as
for interpreted and L′ members, whose outcomes are computed in Rust before the
final message and kept until c resolves. A lowered member's results exist only
in the lost reply, so it returns `E_COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` unless Stage L
persists them (§3.4). W advances past c either way; until the witnesses pass,
ruling 3's "no" holds.

### 6.4 Evidence retention

The tail today deletes evidence at the committing sequences minus 1,000
(`group_tail.rs:23`), which under C deletes evidence a client at W is owed;
`footprint_between` reads a gap as revocation. Revision 2 anchored the prune
at the dispatch watermark, claiming every later reader has V.s ≥ W_c.s:
**false for a delayed reader** (astra, grok, Fable), one that samples W =
1,000 while catch-ups stall at `_head` = 1,000 and opens its snapshot after W
reaches 2,200 and a batch prunes through 1,200; its V is 1,000, and `/changes`
examines no row that could raise `sb4_stale`.

**Evidence is pruned only behind `_head`, inside the catch-up that advances
it** (astra): a catch-up writing `_head` = H deletes `(H_prev.s − 1,000, H.s −
1,000]`. On the O lane, where every batch advances `_head`, a fenced
maintenance transaction does the same about every 1,000 sequences, bounded by
the committed `_head` it reads. No batch, void or reservation prunes, so
pruning leaves the critical path (grok). **Lemma 3:** a reader's V.s is at
least the `_head` its snapshot shows (§5.1), and a deletion commits with, or
after, the head that bounds it; so a snapshot that sees a deletion has V.s ≥
H.s, and **the 1,000 sequences below every reader's V keep their evidence**.
Retention is 1,000 plus `_head`'s lag behind W (grok's in-flight span),
bounded by the catch-up cadence; recovery's floor is the same boundary (§6.5).

### 6.5 Crash recovery and takeover reconciliation

Before an owner serves after start, restart or takeover:

1. Acquire ownership (store-port §5.2): takeover's `_owner FOR UPDATE` is
   granted only after every old key share is gone, so every old C transaction
   has ended; a restarting owner first terminates its old token's tagged
   backends.
2. Read `_head` (H) and walk `_batches` up from it: committed and void rows
   extend the prefix; a gap is a hole whose transactions all ended
   uncommitted: **void it** as one record keyed at the gap's start, fenced and
   synchronous, its reservation rebuilt from its neighbours (§4.4). A lost
   batch whose `rev_lo` lies inside the gap would not collide with that key;
   this is safe **only because step 1 comes first** (Fable): every old backend
   is gone, and the fence refuses any later message under an old token before
   its insert.
3. **Exact counts, inside the retained window:** every reserved sequence has
   one `_commits` row (void for a void record or unused reservation) and,
   above the floor P = H.s − 1,000, below which alone anything was pruned
   (§6.4), one evidence row; no `_commits` row above `H.seq` lies outside a
   record; no `orev` inside a void range. Failure refuses with
   `SNAPBACK_C_LANE_RECONCILE`; a lagging H does not trip the counts.
4. Write `_head` to the reconciled prefix with a fresh attempt, fenced and
   synchronous, and serve with W equal to it.

### 6.6 Fencing, deadlines and back-pressure

Every C transaction, void and catch-up takes the owner fence first as a
raising command; takeover terminates every tagged writer (store-port
§5.1–§5.2). Timeouts come from the earliest member deadline; a timeout before
COMMIT is a known abort; COMMIT runs with both at zero. Past a 250 ms void
stall the lane stops admitting and reports `E_STORE`.

## 7. Interactions

- **G6.** Readers cannot see an unpublished C batch, so **C batches neither
  close the gate nor count in `inflight`**; publication installs atomically
  with each W advance. O batches keep the parent's count and sticky
  uncertainty; a C unknown is settled by its void.
- **Arm E, G7.2 and G7.3.** Two release rules on one read path are unsafe as
  well as costly: **R6, `commit=early` and the C lane are mutually
  exclusive**. The O lane keeps W5 exact and gains P and L′; G7.3's raising
  assertions, exact completion checks and uncancellable COMMIT govern every
  co-dispatched COMMIT here.
- **Barriers and generality.** Claimers, sweeps, session and identity writers,
  activation, adoption and ineligible members drain the C lane; a
  barrier-heavy workload lives in O phases (S1's W4 has no barriers): a
  generality limit, not a correctness gap. Ingress hints and plan reuse stay
  optimisations, **establishing neither footprint independence nor
  authority**.
- **Receipts and replay (C4).** The receipt key joins the footprint; preflight
  and the serial re-check read `_ops` at `rev ≤ V.r` and wait on a key an open
  batch holds; replay-before-judgment is unchanged.
- **SQLite is unchanged**: one writer transaction at a time, every program
  interpreted, the differential's reference; P, L′, L and the C lane are
  `postgres`-only.

W5 mix pays 0.2–0.5 ms more than W4: its shared-counter batches add the
membership flight (0.13–0.16 ms averaged) where the mirror is cold, and its 1%
hot-key members are held or interpreted. On B, where each flight costs 1.10
ms, one flight is worth about as much as Stage P. C is never needed for B;
keyed W4 is measured beside these cells (§3.3).

### 8.4 Verdict

- **B: the ordered lane suffices.** After Stage P, W4/B and W5 mix/B sit near
  11–12 ms; L′ adds 2–3 ms of margin.
- **A strict W4/A win** needs 4,525/s in all three repetitions under S1's
  criteria, a mean in practice 6.4–6.8 ms: **one flight, Stage P substantially
  delivered, per-member preparation near 20–30 µs, and concurrency at N≈16 on
  two to three slots**; on Stage P's slope the ordered lane at N≈32 does not
  reach it.
- **Likelihoods** (judgment, §8.2): C on fast L′ (or L) about one in four; C
  on halved CPU one in ten; the ordered lane at N≈32 below one in ten;
  two-flight C below one in twenty; two-flight ordered, none. All move with
  Stage P's cut, so the program is about **one in four**, not the sum; each
  0.5 ms of P shortfall or surplus moves every A row by about 0.5 ms. At
  3,805/s (R1) every one-flight C shape is plausible, if Supabase, re-run in
  the same window, fails it.

## 9. Measurement fairness: a finer tie-break rung (R1, pending the author)

On the registered ladder 100·2^(k/2) a ceiling anywhere in [3,200, 4,525)
reports 3,200, so beating a 3,200 control needs 4,525 even when ceilings

- **R3, void-resolved outcomes: yes, both sides**, once §10.2 items 3, 4 and
  6, the fencing tests and a release-path witness pass (§6.3); a void row and
  23505 against the batch's own attempt are direct evidence; a proven commit

  session facts are round-trip §7's fresh final check when the fenced
  statement asserts every resolver input by equality or absence and every
  lifetime edge beyond the member's deadline on committer §6's clock, a miss
  taking fresh resolution (L′, and L if built). **R4b:** mirror program facts
  are committer §3.6's fresh check when validated by a locked probe, by key

  lock for spans (L′). Authority and time writers still drain C.
- **R5, void sequences: yes.** Every reserved sequence has a void `_commits`
  row and an empty-footprint evidence row ("no change"; a missing row is
  revocation; a gap is never acceptable); where cursors are opaque the server
  skips void sequences; an SDK `/changes` and sync conformance test, with
  allocation and recovery agreeing, is the gate.
- **R6, C and arm E exclusive: yes.** **Open questions:** what P0 attributes
  and Stage P's actual cut; typed arrays versus binary Bind; whether a fill
  hold makes depth-one N≈32 viable; the best N and K once per-batch cost
  falls; L′'s miss rate, hot-key holds and p99 on W5 mix; `odata` on wide
  rows; P2 would need W from `_sb4_prefix()`.

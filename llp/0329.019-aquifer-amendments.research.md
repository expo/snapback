## Summary

Asked whether Aquifer (Exact LLP 0498) should be amended or rethought for
Snapback 2.

**Amend. Rethinking would mean re-deriving conclusions Aquifer already reached
correctly** — and, notably, reached *independently of* the Snapback 2 panel while
arriving at the same place: one Rust engine, capabilities at the edge, a single
transition table pinned by a conformance corpus, receipts out the side, and
Snapback named as a provider rather than as the architecture. Two programs
converging on that shape without coordinating is evidence, not coincidence.

Four amendments follow. **A1 is time-critical**: the seam it touches is being
versioned breakingly right now.

These are proposed *to* Aquifer's §8 ("Joint rulings, amendments, and proposed
dispositions at acceptance"), which exists for exactly this. They are not edits
to another repository's RFC.

**Reading scope, stated up front:** I have read LLP 0498's Summary, §4.4, §4.6,
§4.7, §8 (partially), §9, and §10, plus its section structure — roughly a third
of 1,046 lines. §4.1–4.3, §4.5, and §5–7 are unread. Any amendment below may
already be answered in a section I did not reach.

## A1 — Give `ProviderEvent` a completeness field (time-critical)

> **Withdrawn as a mechanism, 2026-08-29.** A three-family panel
> ([LLP 0329.020](./0329.020-where-the-client-data-tier-lives.research.md))
> rejected this. Its C3 separates three layers that must stay distinct — the
> authoring *declaration* states the contract required, *admission* checks whether
> an implementation supplies it, *runtime evidence* reports what happened — and
> rules that **"the third cannot substitute for the first two."** A completeness
> field is layer 3 doing layer 1's job. Grok put the practical objection more
> bluntly: *"the thing that would ship is the field,"* regardless of the hedges
> around it.
>
> **What survives:** runtime evidence is still needed at layer 3. What it must not
> be is the thing distinguishing a receipt-backed result from a fetched one — that
> belongs in **non-coercible result types** reached by **different constructors**,
> so a remote result has no `Fresh`, `Predicted`, or covered-absence case to read.
>
> The section is retained unrevised below as the record of the proposal and its
> refutation.

§4.4 records a **decided, breaking** change to the provider seam: version RFC
0391 with "a real typed `ProviderEvent` channel (rows-arrived, request-failed,
request-in-flight, subscription-ended); the engine derives every canonical
transition from those events," retiring the legacy `DataCellV2` channel
"outright — no compatibility adapter, no untrusted-status bridge." AQ-C owes an
event-normalization table as "the *only* ingestion path."

None of those four events can say **how much of the answer this is**.

That matters because §4.7 makes offline "cells served from the local store with
staleness stated truthfully," and constraint 6 is "honesty under degradation." A
locally-served cell can be *stale* or *incomplete*, and these are different
claims:

- **stale** — I hold the whole answer, as of a past moment;
- **incomplete** — I do not hold the whole answer, and cannot know it without a
  fetch.

Only the backend knows the second. Snapback 2 computes it exactly:
`spikes/bounded-programs/src/client.rs` carries
`LocalState::{Fresh, Predicted, Partial(Vec<String>)}` where the vector is the
precise partial site ids, and the closure receipt carries coverage, halo, and
the N+2 key. At a rows-and-writes boundary **all of it is discarded**, and
Aquifer's own honesty constraint becomes unsatisfiable for a local-first
provider.

**Proposal:** `rows-arrived` carries an optional completeness descriptor —
minimally `complete | partial{ reason } | unknown`. Absent means `unknown`,
which is what every non-Snapback provider reports and what today's behaviour
already is, so the amendment is additive and backend-neutral.

**Why now:** the channel is being defined this workstream, breakingly, having
just declared no compatibility adapter. Adding a field now costs one line in the
normalization table. Adding it after AQ-C lands costs a second breaking change to
a surface that just spent its breaking-change budget.

## A2 — Split OQ2's "partial" into two states

OQ2 asks "row-chunk arrival vs. value-patch arrival, and what the view grammar
exposes for **partially arrived** cells."

There are two partials and one word:

| | resolves itself? | offline? | user-visible meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| partially **arrived** | yes, transiently | n/a | "still loading" — a spinner |
| partially **held** | **no** — needs a fetch | may never resolve | "showing what I have; reconnect for the rest" |

A single `partial` conflates a progress indicator with a completeness disclosure.
The second is the one LLP 0329.015 K2 found three model families independently
getting wrong — a filtered or truncated set rendered as if whole, which is the
silent-wrongness class this whole family treats as disqualifying.

**Proposal:** OQ2 resolves to two grammar positions, not one. Snapback 2's
`Partial(site ids)` is the second kind and is the natural source for it.

## A3 — Decide OQ4 jointly with the provider's store

OQ4 asks what retention/eviction vocabulary the declaration can express "(LRU,
TTL, pinned, route-scoped) and what stays engine-internal."

§4.7 gives cells SQLite-backed persistence through the engine, with
`exact-sqlite` as a policy adapter. Snapback 2's client is *itself* a
receipt-seeded range-indexed store. If both persist the same rows, there are two
stores with two eviction policies over one dataset, and a concrete failure
follows: **Aquifer evicts a row under its LRU while Snapback's receipt still
claims coverage of the range that row was in.** The completeness claim of A1 then
becomes false without either side doing anything wrong.

**Proposal:** a provider that supplies a completeness receipt **owns its store**.
Aquifer's cell policy declares *intent* — pinned, TTL, route-scoped — and
delegates enforcement to that provider, rather than maintaining a parallel copy.
Providers without a receipt keep today's engine-owned persistence unchanged.

This is the same shape as A1: the engine states policy, the provider states
truth.

## A4 — OQ5 needs no amendment, only a recorded mechanism

OQ5 ("when a client's replayed cells and the server's current cells disagree, who
wins") already answers itself: "likely provider-delegated with a declared
default." That is right, and worth recording that at least one provider has the
mechanism rather than designing the default in a vacuum — Snapback 2 resolves it
with an authorization epoch plus dependency recheck on reconnect, fail-closed,
removing newly-unauthorized materialization before it is exposed as fresh.

## The structural tension worth naming

Aquifer's constraint 2 exists so that "Aquifer never blocks on" Snapback, and
§9 records the cross-repo coordination cost honestly. That is correct
*scheduling* discipline and I would not change it.

But it has a design consequence: **the seam gets specified without a local-first
provider in the room.** A1 is precisely the kind of field that goes missing that
way — not because anyone decided against it, but because the four events chosen
are exactly the four a fetch-shaped provider needs, and a fetch-shaped provider
has no completeness story to tell.

The mitigation is cheap and does not violate constraint 2: review the AQ-C
event-normalization table against one local-first provider's needs before it
freezes. Not blocking on Snapback, and not designing the only edge without
consulting the case that stresses it hardest, are compatible.

## Why not rethink

The calls Aquifer has already made are the ones I would want:

- **a Rust cell engine** — matches LLP 0329.014's "one Rust IR interpreter…
  JavaScript not in the authoritative path";
- **providers as the only edge**, with Snapback "named as itself, behind it";
- **one transition table pinned by a conformance corpus both engines pass** — the
  same instrument Snapback 2 uses for its 75-case cross-engine corpus;
- **receipts as typed records** on the write side, already;
- and it explicitly names "Snapback's language-neutral core (LLP 0329)" as a Rust
  provider on `native-total`, which is the arm LLP 0329.018 F1 argues should run
  Rust rather than the TypeScript twin.

That last point is worth stating plainly: **Aquifer already contemplates the
no-JS native Snapback provider.** The thing I thought needed proposing is
present.

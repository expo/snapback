# LLP 3000.016: Following on a bounded device

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Draft
**Systems:** Snapback 4 — the materialized Following view (timeline admission, delivery, backfill, device reads), sync horizons, the client's server fallback, the feeds card
**Author:** Claude Opus 5.5 (for Charlie Cheever)
**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Revised:** 2026-09-25 (initial)
**Related:** [LLP 3000.013](./3000.013-quarry-states-intent.rfc.md) §4 (Following without caps, which this amends) and §12.2 (an unavailable answer is never data; the server fallback), [LLP 3000.007](./3000.007-relaxed-quarry-and-explicit-residency.rfc.md) (horizons, completeness), [calibration #20](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-25-snapback4-clique-astra-0.4.1/README.md)

## Summary

Calibration #20 (0.4.1) charged three of its five weakest categories for the same thing: the Following view.

- `queries_and_live` scored 82: "Following's 1,000-event backfill limit and full-source-sync requirement; `E_TIMELINE_SHAPE` rejected bounded acquisition".
- `scale_and_limits` scored 78: "cold multi-route browser flow took 42.324 seconds; required full public sync and rejected acquisition bounds remain material".
- `client_integration` scored 83: "acquisition, admission and online fallback as demanding integration boundaries; M8 needed readiness UX".

The builder kept the materialized view that 0.4.1 made fast to write. The view then made every device acquire every public post, repost and follow, because its three sources must declare `sync public` without a horizon. The builder tried `sync public last 5000 by .at` on posts and was refused.

This RFC lets a device hold only what it reads. Nothing about the server's delivery changes.

1. **Sources may be bounded.** `posts` and `reposts` may declare a sync horizon (`sync public last N by .at`) or `online only`. The follow edges keep full public sync.
2. **The device answers from what it holds.** A Following page on a device reads the reader's own inbox copies. It shapes each copy with the card the device holds. A copy whose card is past the horizon makes the page incomplete, and the client's existing server fallback (LLP 3000.013 §12.2) answers it.
3. **No history window.** A follow backfills every event of the followed author, not the newest 1,000, in the same durable, chunked background work. A reader's pages are `complete: false` while a backfill for that reader is pending, as today.

## 1. Evidence

- **Calibration #20** ([run record](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-25-snapback4-clique-astra-0.4.1/README.md)). The builder's `check` refused the bounded posts table:

  ```
  schema.q:91:1 — E_TIMELINE_SHAPE: posts must be explicitly public, fully synced, non-expiring application data
  ```

  Its essay: "The compiler explicitly refused my attempt to give posts a bounded acquisition horizon … a full public corpus has a cold-device cost. My initial screen-wide acquisition gate made that cost the user's entire startup experience."
- **Calibration #19** (0.4.0) had the opposite problem. The materialized view was slow to write, so the builder abandoned it for an application-owned projection. 0.4.1 fixed the write path, and the builder kept the view. Its remaining cost is acquisition.
- **The scale fixture** has 50,228 public posts, 1,004 reposts and 5,921 follow edges over 1,008 users. Every cold device acquires all of those posts and reposts, and the whole follow graph, before its Following page is complete offline. The posts dominate. The edges are about a tenth of them, which is why this RFC leaves the edges at full sync.

## 2. What changes

### 2.1 Admission (`bind/timeline.rs`)

| Source | 0.4.1 | This RFC |
|---|---|---|
| posts (originals) | public, `sync public`, no horizon, no expiry | public, and `sync public`, `sync public last N by .<time>`, or `online only`; no expiry |
| reposts | the same | the same as posts |
| follows (edges) | public, `sync public`, no horizon | unchanged: full public sync |

Every other rule is unchanged: owner write authority, immutable mapped fields, one-way removal, the total unique pair on edges and reposts, no expiry on sources. A horizon's order column must be the mapped event time (`at`), so the horizon keeps the newest events, and the Following inbox orders by that same time.

### 2.2 Device reads

A device holds:
- the reader's own inbox copies (the owned `Feed` rows), in the reader's partition, as today;
- the follow edges, which are fully public, as today;
- the posts and reposts inside their horizons.

`following last 20 by .at after c` on a device:
1. reads inbox copies in order, applying the view's filter (the actor is the viewer, or is still followed) exactly as today;
2. for each copy, shapes the page with the event's card: the post row, and for a repost, the repost row and its original;
3. marks the page **incomplete** when a card row is past the device's acquired horizon for its table. The horizon machinery already makes such a read an unknown fact. The client's server fallback then answers the page, and it is never answered with a missing card or a hole.

A page whose cards are all held is complete when its inbox coverage is. So with a horizon of 5,000 posts, the first pages of an ordinary reader's feed answer offline, and a deep scroll falls back online.

With `online only` sources, a device holds no cards. Every Following page falls back to the server, and offline it is `loading` (LLP 3000.013 §12.2).

Predictions are unchanged. A predicted post makes its author's own inbox copy on the device. A card the device just predicted is held.

### 2.3 No history window (`engine/timeline/online.rs`, `work.rs`)

`HISTORY_WINDOW` (1,000 events per follow) is removed.
- A follow still copies the author's newest 50 events in the writing transaction.
- Durable background backfill then copies every older event, 256 per chunk, with the same epoch binding, idempotent `(reader, event)` admission, and per-chunk meters.
- `complete` stays false for the reader until the backfill finishes, as it does today for the window.
- `READER_ACTOR_COPIES` and `EVENT_COPIES`, the per-reader and per-event copy bounds that exist only to bound the window, go too. Their chunked equivalents remain.
- `doctor` shows pending backfills with their remaining counts.

The server's storage grows by the events of each followed author, as it already does for every new post of every followed author.

### 2.4 The card

The feeds card shows the bounded form first:

```quarry
table posts:
  ...
  sync public last 5000 by .at
table reposts:
  ...
  sync public last 5000 by .at
```

It states that a device answers the first pages offline and falls back online past its horizon, and that full `sync public` remains available for an app that must scroll the whole feed offline. It names the cost of each choice in one sentence.

## 3. Semantics worth stating

- **Delivery is unchanged.** Copies are made on the server for every follower, whatever any device holds.
- **Completeness is per page.** A Following page is complete on a device only when every card it shapes is held and the reader's inbox coverage for the page is complete. Otherwise it is incomplete, and the fallback answers it. A device never renders an inbox copy without its card.
- **Nothing is disclosed.** Sources stay public, and the inbox stays private to its reader. A horizon changes what a device holds, never who may read it.
- **Removal.** A removed original's copies are filtered by the tombstone, as today. A tombstone for a post past the device's horizon arrives with the post's partition event. Until then, the fallback's server answer already omits it.

## 4. Increments

1. Admission of horizons and `online only` on posts and reposts, and the device's incomplete-on-missing-card rule. Tests: a device with a 50-post horizon and 500 followed events answers its first page complete offline, and a deep page incomplete with the server fallback returning it; the cold acquisition size and time at the scale fixture, before and after.
2. Removal of the history window, with backfill of 5,000 events for a new follower under the meters, `complete: false` until it finishes, and restart mid-backfill.
3. The card, `check --cost` lines for the bounded form, and `doctor`.

## 5. How we will know

- The bounded feeds program compiles with no refusal, and its cold acquisition at the scale fixture is at least 10× smaller than the full-sync program's. Record the bytes and the time.
- A new follower of an author with 5,000 events sees all of them after the backfill, and a page during it is `complete: false`.
- The next calibration does not charge the Following model's acquisition or backfill window.

## Non-claims

This RFC does not change delivery, the inbox, prediction, or the fan-out costs LLP 3000.013 §4 measured. It does not bound the follow edges; a device still acquires the whole public follow graph. That is the next candidate if a calibration charges it.

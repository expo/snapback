## Summary

Calibration #20 (0.4.1) charged three of its five weakest categories for the same thing: the Following view.

| Category | Score | The scorer's charge |
|---|---:|---|
| `queries_and_live` | 82 | "Following's 1,000-event backfill limit and full-source-sync requirement; `E_TIMELINE_SHAPE` rejected bounded acquisition" |
| `scale_and_limits` | 78 | "cold multi-route browser flow took 42.324 seconds; required full public sync and rejected acquisition bounds remain material" |
| `client_integration` | 83 | "acquisition, admission and online fallback as demanding integration boundaries" |

The view requires its sources to declare `sync public` with no horizon. Its generated event table does the same. So every cold device acquires every public post, repost and event before a Following page is complete offline. At the scale fixture that is 50,228 posts and as many events.

This RFC changes only **acquisition**. Delivery, the inbox's contents, the history window and prediction of an author's own post are unchanged.

1. **Sources may be bounded.** `posts` and `reposts` may declare `sync public last N by .at` or `online only`. The owned event table, a small row per event with no body, stays fully synced.
2. **The inbox may be bounded per reader.** The reader's owned inbox syncs with a per-reader floor of the newest M copies. By default, M equals the history window.
3. **A page never shows a hole.** On a device, a Following page, or any query that shapes it with cards, is either answered from held rows or withheld. Online, the withheld page is answered by the server. Offline, it is `loading`. A null card is never published as data.

## 1. Evidence

- **Calibration #20** ([run record](../benchmarks/runs/2026-09-25-snapback4-clique-astra-0.4.1/README.md)). The builder's `check` refused the bounded posts table:

  ```
  schema.q:91:1 — E_TIMELINE_SHAPE: posts must be explicitly public, fully synced, non-expiring application data
  ```

  Its essay: "a full public corpus has a cold-device cost. My initial screen-wide acquisition gate made that cost the user's entire startup experience."
- **Calibration #19** had the opposite problem: the view was slow to write, and the builder abandoned it. 0.4.1 fixed the writes. What remains is acquisition.
- **The scale fixture**, with 1,008 users:

  | Data | Rows | Held by a device today |
  |---|---:|---|
  | posts | 50,228 | all |
  | reposts | 1,004 | all |
  | owned events | one per original and repost | all |
  | follows | 5,921 | all |
  | owned tombstones | small | all |
  | the reader's inbox | the followed authors' events, up to the window per author | the reader's own |

## 2. What changes

### 2.1 Admission (`bind/timeline.rs`)

| Table | 0.4.1 | This RFC |
|---|---|---|
| posts (originals) | public, `sync public`, no horizon | public, and `sync public`, `sync public last N by .at`, or `online only` |
| reposts | the same | the same as posts |
| follows (edges) | public, `sync public`, no horizon | unchanged |
| owned events (`@T@Posts`) | `sync public` | unchanged: full sync. A horizon cannot say "this event is absent because it was never admitted or was deleted", and repost prediction and refusal depend on that absence being known |
| owned tombstones (`@T@RemovedEvents`) | `sync public` | unchanged: full sync, because removal must always reach a device (§3) |
| owned inbox (`@T@Feed`) | `sync` to its reader | `sync` to its reader, with a floor of the newest M copies over `byReaderPage`, the index the Following scan uses. M defaults to the history window, 1,000, as one merged suffix per reader, not per author |

- **A floor is global and non-sliding.** `sync public last N by .at` makes a cold device acquire the newest N rows of the whole table, whoever it follows. Nothing evicts a row after acquisition, and the floor is fixed at acquisition time; this is how horizons already behave.
- **A source horizon's index must be the source's mapped time column plus `id`.** This applies to `posts` and `reposts` only. An `id`-ordered or other index is refused, with a rewrite, because the floor must be the newest events. The inbox floor is always on `byReaderPage`, because a horizon page is locally known only when the floor's index is the scan index.
- **Everything else is unchanged:** owner write authority, immutable mapped fields, one-way removal, the unique pairs, and no expiry on sources.

### 2.2 Device reads

The paved Following page is the **inbox projection**: event id, post, actor, author, repost flag and time. It reads the reader's inbox, the follows filter and the tombstones. It needs no posts. A page has **known inbox coverage** when its scan stays within the acquired inbox floor. Such a page is answered on the device, whatever the posts horizon. Pending backfill or cleanup, and meter cuts, keep their existing `complete: false` meaning: they do not make a page unavailable.

A **card** is the application's shape, for example `{ *, card: posts[.post] }`. It is a separate point read.
- When a card read misses because its row is past the posts floor, **or when the inbox scan reaches past the inbox floor**, the whole query is **unavailable** on the device: never a page with a null card, never a short page, and never a continuation past it. Both unknowns take the same path.
- The client's existing fallback (LLP 3000.013 §12.2) then retries the query's **original input**, cursor included, on the server when online.
- Offline, the query is `loading`.
- While a predictable write is pending, the withheld page stays withheld. The server's page replaces the local one only after the pending write is observed. That keeps the rule that a predicted row never disappears under a server answer.

So the promise is precise. A device answers a Following page offline when every row it shows is held: the copies within the inbox floor, and every card the application shapes within the posts and reposts floors. A quiet followed author's older post, or a new repost of an old original, can put a page past those floors, and then the server answers it.

With `online only` sources, cards are never held, and a shaped Following page is always answered by the server.

### 2.3 Prediction

- An author's own post predicts as today: the local row is the card, and the author's own inbox copy is written.
- These are **necessary** conditions for a repost to predict: its source post, the original's owned event row in `byActorPost`, and the tombstone probe must all be known. The owned events are fully synced, so that row's presence or absence is always known. The ordinary uniqueness proof of a paved `insert … if absent` also applies, and a new insertion into a horizoned `reposts` table can still queue. Otherwise the repost queues without a local row.
- `check` classifies every implicit timeline input by its actual residency, so the mutation's summary says when it may queue.
- **`online only` sources do not predict.** A write to an `online only` source is a server site, so the mutation is `predicts: never` and queues without a local card or inbox copy, as residency already classifies it. The own-post guarantee above is for synced sources, full or horizoned.
- Follow and unfollow already queue without prediction.

### 2.4 Adoption

- Changing a source between full sync, a horizon and `online only` is an ordinary generation change. The device clears its acquisition and re-snapshots the partition, as it does for any sync change. A device never trims in place.
- Changing from a horizon to full sync re-acquires the whole table.
- The inbox floor is the same kind of change.

## 3. Semantics worth stating

- **Delivery is unchanged.** Copies are made on the server for every follower, whatever any device holds.
- **Removal is unchanged.** The owned tombstone table stays fully synced. A copy is hidden by `not exists RemovedEvents[eventId]`, whether or not its post is held.
- **Nothing is disclosed.** Sources, events and tombstones stay public, and the inbox stays private to its reader. A floor changes what a device holds, never who may read it.
- **The history window stays.** A follow copies the author's newest 1,000 events, as today. Removing it needs a design of its own: a fixed enumeration domain, fairness between background tasks, per-page completeness, an upgrade for existing follows, and a stated storage cost. The reviews of revision 1 laid all of that out. It is not in this RFC. The feeds card states the window plainly.

## 4. Increments

1. **Admission** of source horizons and `online only`, the derived floor on owned events, and the per-reader inbox floor.
2. **The withheld-page rule** for a card read past a floor, on the device and in the client's fallback, including the pending-write case.
3. **Repost prediction** gated on a held original, and its horizon-site classification.
4. **The card:** the bounded form first, the global-floor wording and the window. `check --cost` lines. A measured inventory.

## 5. How we will know

- **A whole-partition inventory at the scale fixture,** before and after, in rows and bytes, with the cold acquisition time on the web device: posts, reposts, events, tombstones, follows, the inbox, profiles and work. The target is at least 5× less. Say what dominates afterward.
- **Device tests:**
  - a page with known inbox coverage and held cards is answered offline;
  - a page whose scan reaches past the inbox floor is withheld offline and answered online;
  - a page with a repost of an original past the floor is withheld offline and answered by the server online, from the original cursor;
  - a pending predicted post is never replaced by the server page before it is observed;
  - a quiet author's old post is answered online.
- The bounded feeds program compiles without refusal, and the next calibration does not charge the full-source-sync requirement.

## 6. Non-claims

This RFC does not bound the follow edges or tombstones on a device, change delivery or the history window, or make a Following page complete offline beyond the floors. Its claim is smaller acquisition, and a page that is never shown with a hole.

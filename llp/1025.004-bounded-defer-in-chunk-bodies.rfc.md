**Status:** Shelved

**Revised:** 2026-09-12 (commission review: shelved; no remaining digest dependency)
**Related:** [LLP 1025](./1025-digest-final-emission-at-hub-scale.research.md) §3.3 and F4, [LLP 1025.001](./1025.001-timers-drain-before-chunks.rfc.md) (which removes the digest's need for this), LLP 1016 D2 (a job is a sequence of sweeps; park, never stop), D3 (`_jobs`, FIFO, restart), D6 (rehearsal)

## Commission disposition — 2026-09-12

The user delegated evaluation, refinement and implementation of worthwhile
changes in this family. This proposal is **not selected for implementation**.
LLP 1025.001 removes the digest's due-timer guard. No other exercised
customer here establishes a need for a new durable retry state machine.

The retained design needs more than a choice of constants. A row rollback
must restore its writes, generated IDs, effect attempts, trace and receipt
accounting while preserving earlier successful rows; that work is not free
as D4 claims. A deferred primary-key fetch is not automatically covered by a
certificate for an indexed sweep with particular provenance. A bounded key
list also needs per-row refusal codes and a precisely atomic overflow rule.
Three immediate passes do not wait for an external condition to change.
Under the existing single-head FIFO, a job waiting for a later job can run
all three passes without ever giving that dependency a turn. These are
semantic questions, not implementation details that the digest must pay.

Reopen only with a reproduced customer and an explicit progress/retention
contract. No implementer or delivery date is assigned while shelved. The
proposal below is historical, does not bind code, and does not authorize a
new diagnostics registry under Snapback 2's rules.

## Summary

A chunk body may say `defer else CODE`. The current row's writes roll back,
its key is appended to the sweep's durable deferred list with the progress
write, and the cursor advances. When the sweep reaches its upper key the
driver revisits the deferred keys as extra chunks of the same body, at most
three passes; what still defers parks the job with `E_JOB_DEFERRED` naming
`CODE` and the rows. Capacity is bounded by the chunk size. This is the
per-row grain that "automatic progress" needs: `if` loses the row, `require`
parks the whole job, and neither revisits.

## Motivation

The digest assessment's counterexample is the general shape: a final guard
sees a fact that will be true soon (a due timer, another job's output, a
provider's back-pressure) and has two bad choices. With 1025.001 the digest's
own guard disappears, so this RFC is not on the requirement's path; it is
the job machinery's answer for the next recipe whose guard depends on
something the driver does not order.

## Design

### D1 — Grammar

`defer else CODE` is a statement in a chunk body, with `require`'s shape and
placement rules. It is refused outside a chunk body and inside a `proc`
(`E_JOB_SHAPE`, existing family).

### D2 — Semantics at the row

Executing `defer` ends the current row's iteration: its writes roll back,
the row's primary key is appended to `_jobs.deferred` (a JSON list), and
the chunk continues with the next row. The progress write commits the
cursor, the chunk count and the deferred list together, so a crash cannot
lose a deferral or replay it.

Capacity: at most `4 × N` keys per sweep, where `N` is the chunk's `first
N`. The next `defer` parks the job with `E_JOB_DEFER_CAPACITY` and the
rewrite "fix the condition or reduce the sweep"; nothing already written is
lost.

### D3 — The deferred pass

When a sweep's cursor reaches its captured upper key and `deferred` is not
empty, the driver runs a **deferred chunk**: the same certified body over up
to `N` deferred keys read by primary key in list order, reading current
rows (a deleted row is skipped, as in an ordinary sweep). Rows that complete
leave the list; rows that defer again stay for the next pass. After three
passes over a sweep, remaining keys park the job with
`E_JOB_DEFERRED { code: CODE, rows: [≤ 10 ids], remaining }`; `trace` shows
the pass count and the rows; `run --resume` re-runs the deferred pass, not
the sweep.

### D4 — Pricing

A chunk body with `defer` is priced as today; a rollback costs nothing
extra. The deferred chunk executes the same body over at most `N` point
reads by `id`, which costs no more than the sweep's chunk over `N` index
rows; the certificate is shared. `_jobs.deferred` is bounded by `4 × N ×`
the declared identity width and is an engine write already counted.

### D5 — Restart, succession, rehearsal

`deferred` is durable with the cursor; `running → queued` keeps it. A
changed declaration parks as today (`E_JOB_GENERATION`). `--dry-run` runs
the deferred passes on the copy and reports `deferred: { rows, passes }` in
its receipt.

### D6 — Diagnostics

`E_JOB_DEFERRED`, `E_JOB_DEFER_CAPACITY`, and the existing `E_JOB_SHAPE`
site for a misplaced `defer`; all in the registry with `check-inventories`.

## Where it fits

- With 1025.001, a digest never defers.
- A backfill whose row depends on an asset the sweep has not released yet, a
  reconciliation that waits for another job's output, an effect-driven job
  under provider back-pressure: each writes `defer else CODE` where it would
  otherwise choose between silence and a parked job.

## Alternatives rejected

- `require` plus automatic resume: whole-job grain; resumes the wrong thing
  and hides which rows were the problem.
- A separate retry job enqueued by the guard: the grammar has no
  chunk-level enqueue, and adding one crosses LLP 1016 D2's "no plain
  statement stages".
- `if` plus a later sweep: loses the row for that occurrence.

## Acceptance

1. `tests/jobs.rs`: a sweep where every third row defers; three passes;
   parked with the row list; `--resume` completes after the condition
   clears.
2. Crash between the row's writes and the progress write: no deferral lost
   or duplicated (the existing atomicity test extended).
3. Capacity refusal at `4 × N + 1`.
4. Dry-run reports deferrals and writes nothing.
5. Guide `job.md`: the paragraph and a five-line example.

## Non-claims

No cross-job state, no retry policy for effects (LLP 1003 owns that), no
change to the cursor's stability property, no scheduling of the deferred
pass on a clock.

## Open questions

1. Three passes and `4 × N` capacity are proposals; a customer's numbers
   should set them.
2. Whether a deferred pass may run before the sweep ends when the list
   fills, so a long sweep does not hold deferrals for hours.

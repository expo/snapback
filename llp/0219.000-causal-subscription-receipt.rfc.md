## Summary

Every Snapback lane in the 0.1.29 Clique matrix hit the same dark loop: a
write settled, a one-shot query saw the row, and an already-open live hook did
not change. Unable to tell *where* the chain broke — the query's dependency,
the authority fence, the transport, the client's applied revision, or the
renderer — the lanes spent hours on destructive guesses: refresh-argument
nonces, 250–400 ms polling, alternating `skip` cycles, revision rows,
presence hacks, reloads, and snapshot-mutating workarounds (LLP 0317 O15).
The L1 diagnosis later showed that every one of those cases had a precise,
different answer (LLP 0317 O14 r2.4–r2.6), and that the facts needed to reach
it were observable — but only by an author who could read
`demos/_kit/tests/live-query-invalidation-e2e.mjs` and the engine.

This RFC commissions the **causal subscription receipt**: a bounded,
redacted, machine-readable set of facets, computed on the client from what
the protocol already delivers, plus one **primary finding** derived from them
by a published truth table, answering "did subscription *S* observe committed
version *V*, and if not, which link is responsible?" Each finding binds to one
guide repair. It adds no server route. It needs at most one optional wire
field and one advertised number. Its automatic producer — an own write that a
continuously attached subscription has not covered inside the server's
advertised envelope — is the dark-loop detector LLP 0219 was built to host.

The claim being made is deliberately narrower than r1's. LLP 0200 §2.1
delivers **coverage**: for every registered subscription, the server's
watermark reaching ≥ V means the client holds every content ≤ V it is
entitled to, with a `current_as_of(V)` envelope when the evaluation skipped or
found no change (`crates/snapback/tests/live_coalesce_v0.rs`
`skip_and_no_change_advance_subscription_watermark`). Coverage without
content at V is the server's own statement that *it published nothing new for
this subscription at V*. That is enough to stop the workaround spiral —
"do not poll; the server already answered" — and it is the fact none of the
lanes had. It is **not** a proof that the query is semantically independent
of the write: the matcher's skip is the server's classification of the
registered read set, watermarks coalesce, and later content supersedes a
pending watermark. The receipt says exactly what it knows.

## Motivation

The evidence is the matrix itself:

- `sol-snapback-familiar` `1787857038`: own settled write absent from the
  same client's hook for 3 s. Finding, had the receipt existed: `converged`
  inside the envelope (the Linux rerun measured 30–40 ms), i.e. nothing to
  fix; the lane instead added polling.
- `sol-snapback-explicit` `1787861040`: "neither the JS conversation query
  nor a dedicated declarative reactions subscription emitted an update" while
  a fresh one-shot read saw the row. Finding: `covered-no-visible-change`
  (the server evaluated V for those subscriptions and published nothing) or
  a `shared-custody` comparison result (LLP 0317 F2's Node/headless partition
  collision). Either ends the "fanout is broken" line of attack in seconds.
- `grok-snapback-explicit` M3: an unscoped query "went silent" on a large
  thread. Finding: `refused` — `SNAPBACK_QUERY_IMPLICIT_LIMIT_REACHED`, bound
  1,024, repair `.take(n)` — which since `9f7eb7f5d` is exactly what
  `handle.refusal` carries.
- `sol-snapback-familiar` `1787857472`: cross-context staleness while the
  same client's projection updated. Finding: whichever of `pending`,
  `not-attached` or `fenced` the second context was actually in — the point
  is that it was one of three, and the lane had no way to name it.

O15 is LLP 0317's highest-leverage row for the reason its priority synthesis
states: the missing causal observability turned *one* uncertainty into hours
of workaround exploration, and the workarounds themselves (subscription
churn, `SNAPBACK_SUBSCRIPTION_CONNECTION_LIMIT`) created new failures. LLP
0219 already holds the channel, the redaction rules, the active-condition map
and the dev-only reporter; what it lacks is a producer for this loop and the
receipt the producer would carry.

## Options considered

1. **A server-side explain route** returning the engine's per-subscription
   evaluation ledger. Most complete — it could name the matcher's decision per
   version exactly — but it adds an authenticated dev route, a new authority
   surface, and a second history to redact; LLP 0219 rules out channel-owned
   routes, and every fact the agent needs to *choose a repair* is already
   delivered to the client.
2. **A client-composed receipt from delivered facts** (chosen). Joins the
   settlement version, the subscription's coverage and last delivered content,
   the authorization fence state and generations, the typed refusal, the
   transport/attachment continuity, the client's applied revision and the
   registry's publication disposition into facets, then derives one finding.
   Cost: a versioned receipt contract, the per-handle bookkeeping
   inventoried in §Design, a detector with an eligibility interval, and one
   advertised server number.
3. **Export the engine spike trace** (`SNAPBACK_SPIKE_TRACE` JSONL fences).
   Already exists; server-only, env-gated, and useless from inside an app or
   an agent's headless client. Stays as the engineer's tool.
4. **Log every frame to the console.** No finding, unbounded noise, and the
   lanes had console access already; what they lacked was a *diagnosis*.

## Decision

Build option 2 as a producer into the LLP 0219 channel plus explicit query
surfaces, with the facets, the finding truth table and the guide ladder as the
contract. The receipt is computed from held client state in constant time,
carries no rows, arguments, principals, raw identifiers or credentials, and
is available in every client arm (headless, React, Expo) because the channel
already is.

## Design

### What the client holds today, and what the receipt adds

| Link in the lanes' question | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Was my write committed, and at which version?" | direct settlement `MutationOutput.version`; queued/replayed/recovered settlement's settled record and its version | LLP 0043; `packages/snapback-client/index.d.mts` (`MutationOutput`, the `settled` disposition) | held |
| "Is this subscription registered and continuously attached?" | `subscriptionId`, `connection`, `transport` (`stream` / automatic poll fallback / manual poll), `paused`, the `resubscribed` subscription event, the subscription generation | LLP 0095 | held; generation continuity needs a per-handle timestamp of the last `resubscribed` |
| "Did the server cover V for this subscription?" | the greater of the last content frame version and the last `current_as_of` version | LLP 0200 §2.1 (watermark ≥ V ⇒ every content ≤ V held) | **new bookkeeping** — see below |
| "Did the server classify V for this subscription?" | `current_as_of.disposition` when present and exact for V | §Server changes | new, optional |
| "Is the authority fence holding the result?" | fence disposition (`retain`/`quarantine`/`delete`), whether the fence is **holding** the result — an unresolved obligation covering the current revision, the same predicate the fence uses for its own unresolved disposition / renderability (an applied-but-unacknowledged entry is not holding) — projected as `authority.holding`, the fence snapshot's authority generation and query generation | LLP 0053; `packages/snapback-contract/query-authorization.mjs` (the fence carries both generations) | held on the runtime handle; not yet on the public type |
| "Was I denied?" | `handle.authorizationDenial` | LLP 0053 | held |
| "Was the reevaluation refused?" | `handle.refusal` | LLP 0053 2026-08-28 amendment; `9f7eb7f5d` | held |
| "Did the client apply it?" | `handle.authoritativeRevision`, `lastDeliveryAtMs`, `deliveryLagMs`, `freshness`, `answerClass` | client handle | held |
| "Did the registry publish it to consumers?" | per-delivery publication disposition on the React/Expo arm | `packages/snapback-react/implementation.mjs` (value-equal suppression, LLP 0278) | **new**: recorded per delivery, bound to the delivery version, not a lifetime counter |
| "Are my two clients actually two custodies?" | `storagePartition` digest, custody kind, client-instance pseudonym | LLP 0059 custody; the harness's `partitions` witness | held |

**New per-handle bookkeeping.** Today `packages/snapback-client/index.mjs`
handles `current_as_of` by advancing `handle.version` and delivering
`onUpdate` with `kind: 'current_as_of'` — the same route content takes — so
`authoritativeRevision` and `lastDeliveryAtMs` advance on watermarks too.
Those fields keep exactly that meaning. The receipt adds, per handle, and
these are the **only** additions: `lastContentVersion`, `lastContentKind`
(normalized over every authoritative content delivery the client applies —
`live_replace`, `live_delta`, `authorization_snapshot`, and the subscribe-time
initial snapshot/registered boot deliveries), `lastContentAtMs` (which
`lastDeliveryAtMs` cannot supply, since that advances on watermarks),
`lastWatermarkVersion`, `lastWatermarkExact` — an optional `{ version,
disposition }` retained only when the envelope carried a disposition for the
exact version it stamps — and `lastResubscribedAtMs` beside the subscription
generation; on the React/Expo arm, a **bounded ring of publication records** `{ atVersion,
disposition }` (the last 32 deliveries, a tunable) plus the latest record, so
the receipt can find the delivery that first made the applied content version
reach an asked version; `explainAfter` registers its ask so the crossing is
captured even if the ring later evicts it. When the crossing has been evicted
and no ask was registered, the publication facet is `'unknown'` for that
version — the receipt never reconstructs history it did not keep. The receipt
stays constant-time: the ring is scanned only for the asked version. `coverage.through` is
`max(lastContentVersion, lastWatermarkVersion)`. Every finding below is
computed from these and the other facets, never from `authoritativeRevision`.

**Redaction is structural**, as in LLP 0219, and stricter than r1: no rows,
no arguments (canonical digest only), no principals in any form, no row
counts, and no raw wire identifiers — the subscription id is a bearer
capability and the connection id is caller-supplied, so both appear only as
pseudonyms produced by the client's existing inspection pseudonymizer
(`inspectionPseudonymV1` in `packages/snapback-client/index.mjs`: deterministic
JCS + SHA-256, not salted per instance, not rotated) whose supported use is
process-local inspection; the client-instance token is a separate random
per-instance value. Neither is anonymization: the receipt is a development
artifact consumed in-process by the author or agent and by the LLP 0219
channel; it never crosses a telemetry or export boundary, and the lane states
that scope on the type. Custody comparison needs no principal
token — the collision the lanes hit is a *partition* collision, so
`shared-custody` is decided on partition digest plus distinct client instance.
When the receipt rides the channel its `details` are the receipt's flat
subset (finding, asked version, coverage through / last content version /
`contentAtAsked`, authority disposition, refusal code, connection, transport,
paused, generation) under keys that pass the channel's structural filter (the
key is `authority`, never `authorization…`), never a nested object beyond
depth two.

### The primary finding: a truth table

Let `V` be the asked version, `T` = `coverage.through`, `C` =
`lastContentVersion`, `X` = `coverage.exactForAskedVersion` with its
`classification`, `P` the publication record of the delivery that first made
`C ≥ V` (React/Expo arm only; taken from the bounded ring or the registered
ask, `'unknown'` when evicted; the facet also carries the latest publication
record, which `P` is not). The finding is the **first matching row** of an ordered decision
list: the predicates of rows 1–6 overlap by design (a refusal can coexist with
a fence, a denial pauses the handle), and precedence selects the cause; the
later rows also rely on precedence (`applied-not-published` before
`converged`, `pending-past-envelope` before `pending`). The selected finding
is unique and total because every state matches at least one row and the
first match is taken; the lane lands the list as a test that enumerates every
predicate combination — including elapsed time against the envelope — and
asserts the selected finding and its precedence.

| # | Finding | Row predicate | What the agent does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `refused` | `refusal !== null` | Apply the refusal's repair (`.take(n)` for the implicit ceiling). Takes precedence over a fence: the refusal is the cause, the quarantine its fail-closed shape. |
| 2 | `denied` | `authority.denial !== null` | A server-answered denial paused the handle; authority, not transport. |
| 3 | `paused` | `subscription.paused` (`pause()` unsubscribes and reports `disconnected`) | Resume; nothing else is wrong. |
| 4 | `not-attached` | `subscriptionId` null, or `connection` ∉ {`live`, `polling`} | Wait for reattachment; if it persists, lifecycle — LLP 0095 ladder. |
| 5 | `generation-ended` | `resubscribedAfterAsk` (a `resubscribed` event after `askedAtMs`; the handle may be live on a new generation) | The original subscription generation ended after the ask, so no coverage claim is made for it; ask again against the new generation. This is O14's no-`resubscribed` witness as a finding. |
| 6 | `fenced` | `authority.disposition` ∈ {`quarantine`, `delete`} or `authority.holding` (LLP 0200 suppresses standalone watermarks while a quarantine-class obligation is unresolved, so no coverage row is reachable here) | Authority changed or is being re-proven; read the disposition and generations; never add a refresh argument. |
| 7 | `no-question` | `asked === null` | Status only; ask with a version for a causal finding. |
| 8 | `applied-not-published` | `C ≥ V` and `P` is known and either `P` = `value-equal-suppressed` **and** the LLP 0219 churn detector holds a live site for this hook, or `P` = `not-published` | Renderer/hook identity — LLP 0219's churn repair. A value-equal suppression alone is healthy (LLP 0278) and is **not** this finding. |
| 9 | `converged` | `C ≥ V` | Nothing is wrong at the client. `contentAtAsked` (`true` / `false` / `'unknown'`, below) says what is known about V itself; on the React arm the facet says whether the crossing's publication is known or evicted. |
| 10 | `matcher-skipped` | `T ≥ V`, `C < V`, `X` with `classification: 'matcher-skipped'` | The server's matcher classified this subscription's registered read set as disjoint from V. Predicate, index window, or a nested/child dependency the registration does not cover. |
| 11 | `evaluated-no-change` | `T ≥ V`, `C < V`, `X` with `classification: 'no-change'` | The query was re-evaluated at V and its result was unchanged. |
| 12 | `covered-no-visible-change` | `T ≥ V`, `C < V`, no exact classification (coalesced, superseded, or the field absent) | The server covered V and published nothing new for this subscription. Do not poll; do not add a refresh argument. Either nothing the query reads changed at V or the query does not read those rows. |
| 13 | `pending-past-envelope` | `T < V`, the handle is eligible (rows 1–6 unmatched, stream or automatic-poll transport, no active negotiated pressure condition), an envelope is advertised, and the elapsed time since `askedAtMs` exceeds `envelope.advertisedMs` plus the transport allowance | Either the server is over capacity (LLP 0200's bound holds only within capacity — check the server's overload/pressure counters) or delivery is stuck; this is the detector's warning finding, and it never claims which. |
| 14 | `pending` | `T < V` (row 13 unmatched: inside the envelope, or no envelope advertised, or an ineligible handle) | The receipt reports elapsed time against `envelope.advertisedMs` when one exists. Wait — never poll. |

Notes on the algebra. `matcher-skipped`, `evaluated-no-change` and
`covered-no-visible-change` are worded as **server classifications**,
never as semantic truth about the application's query: a matcher skip asserts
that the registered read set is disjoint, which is exactly what O14 shape
(c)'s nested-dependency allegation questions, so the receipt reports the
classification and the guide step says what to check. **`contentAtAsked` is
tri-state and only ever reports what the client can observe:** `true` when
`lastContentVersion === V`; `false` when the client holds coverage of V
without content and no later content (`C < V ≤ T`); `'unknown'` when later
content has arrived (`C > V`) **or when V is not yet covered (`T < V`)**,
because LLP 0200 §2.3's latest-value collapse
means the client cannot tell whether V itself brought content. Supersession
is therefore row 9: a skip at V followed by content at W > V is `converged`
with `contentAtAsked: 'unknown'` — the correct answer to "did S observe V"
(it holds everything ≤ V) that does not pretend to know more. **Exactness
(`X`) follows one rule, retain-latest:** a `current_as_of` envelope may carry
a classification only for the version it stamps; when pending watermarks
coalesce, the surviving envelope keeps its own stamp's classification and
every earlier version it covers has none; when content supersedes a pending
watermark, no classification survives for it. So a coalesced envelope covering
V from a later stamp yields row 12 for V, and rows 10–11 fire only for the
stamped version itself — the client never invents a classification it did not
receive for that version. Custody comparison is not a finding of either receipt:
`compareReceipts(a, b)` returns a separate `CustodyComparison` (`partition:
same|different`, `clientInstance: same|different`, `custody: same|different`,
`verdict: shared-custody | distinct-custody | same-client`), where
`shared-custody` requires the same partition digest **and** different client
instances.

### Surfaces

- **Headless (`LiveQueryHandle<Rows>`):** `handle.explain({ version? })`
  returns the receipt synchronously from held state (no I/O); with `version`
  omitted it uses this client instance's latest own settled version, and with
  none it returns `no-question`. `await handle.explainAfter({ version } |
  { mutation }, { timeoutMs?, signal? })` resolves when `coverage.through`
  reaches the version, the handle reaches a terminal finding (rows 1–6 or
  13), the timeout elapses, or the signal aborts — and on dispose it resolves with the
  terminal lifecycle receipt. It never resubscribes, never polls, never issues
  a one-shot query, never adds demand and never changes polling cadence.
  `compareReceipts(a, b)` is a pure function beside them. The v1 type surface
  adds `explain`, `explainAfter`, `refusal`, `authorizationDisposition` and the
  fence generations to `LiveQueryHandle<Rows>` (the JS handle already carries
  the disposition; `index.d.mts` does not).
- **React/Expo:** `useSnapbackQueryReceipt(reference, args)` projects the bound
  handle's receipt for the same descriptor `useSnapbackQuery` uses, adding the
  `publication` facet — a per-delivery disposition recorded by the registry
  when it publishes (or suppresses) a delivery, bound to that delivery's
  version. "Publication" is the registry's consumer-visible publication, not a
  React commit or a painted frame; the receipt does not claim more. When the
  query is `skip`ped or has no registry store it returns `not-attached` and
  starts nothing. Row 8 exists only on this arm.
- **Channel producer (the dark-loop detector).** Code
  `SNAPBACK_LIVE_QUERY_UNCONVERGED`, an LLP 0219 *active condition* keyed by
  `(subscriptionPseudonym, generation, version)` and computed by the **same
  function as `explain()`** so the console can never disagree with the handle.
  *Own write* is any settlement this client instance observes through its
  commit-observation path — direct result, queued replay, or recovered
  durable settlement — normalized to its version; the lane enumerates the
  arms and the acceptance covers each. It **arms** only when `explain({
  version })` is `pending` (row 14) on an eligible handle: one continuous subscription
  generation, `transport` = `stream` or automatic poll fallback (never
  manual-poll headless handles, whose owner polls on their own cadence), no
  refusal, no fence, not paused — or, when the first observation of an
  own write is already row 13, it arms and escalates in the same step. From
  arming until escalation the condition
  is **producer-local state** (`coverage-pending`, visible on the receipt and
  through `useSnapbackQueryReceipt`, not a channel event — the v2 carrier
  admits `error` and `warning` only), and it
  **escalates** to `warning` only when the receipt reaches row 13 — elapsed
  time past `envelope.advertisedMs` plus the transport allowance (zero on the
  stream lane; LLP 0095's polling cadence and backoff on the automatic poll
  lane); when no envelope is
  advertised it never escalates and the condition stays informational and
  unbounded. The warning is honest about capacity: LLP 0200's bound holds
  only within capacity, and ordinary subscriptions receive no pressure event
  (the live-delivery-pressure carrier is a draft, opt-in amendment of LLP
  0219), so the warning says "over capacity or stuck" and routes to the
  server's overload counters rather than alleging a fanout defect. It
  **clears** on coverage and on every lifecycle cut — pause, detach,
  resubscribe (new generation), offline/retained transition, fence, refusal,
  dispose — because those are findings, not dark loops. Where the pressure
  carrier *is* negotiated, pressure activation and clearance are eligibility
  cuts: the condition is suppressed while pressure is active and the
  interval restarts from a fresh deadline when it clears, never resuming an
  old elapsed count. Dev-positive only, per LLP 0219's
  `positiveDevelopmentEnvironment()` seam; the code rides the v2 carrier.
  Cross-client cases are not detected automatically — the client does not
  know the peer's version — and are covered by `explainAfter` with a version
  obtained out of band (the peer's settlement, or a bounded control query such
  as a `latestVersion` read), which the guide ladder names.
- **Not in v1:** a CLI/dev-loop projection and any overlay. Both are
  consumers of the channel and can follow without changing this contract.

### Server changes (additive, optional)

1. **`current_as_of.disposition`** — one optional field on the existing
   envelope: `"no-change" | "matcher-skipped"`, present only when the
   envelope is exact for the version it stamps. The engine already
   distinguishes the two on the publish path (`live_coalesce_v0.rs` exercises
   both; the skip branch is the engine's `proved_disjoint`), so the cost is
   carrying one enum onto an envelope it already emits. **Coalescing rule
   (retain-latest, the one rule this RFC states everywhere):** an envelope's
   disposition classifies only the version it stamps. When pending watermarks
   coalesce, the surviving envelope keeps its own stamp's classification (a
   mixed skip/no-change burst classifies the stamp as whatever its own
   evaluation was) and carries none for the earlier versions it now covers;
   when content supersedes a pending watermark, no classification survives
   for the superseded version. This adds no per-commit ledger and does not
   strengthen the coalescer's contract; it only stops the client from
   mislabeling. Compatible within wire v1 (LLP 0093 D1); absent from every
   other variant. Without it every no-content case is row 12.
2. **Advertised envelope** — the server publishes `liveEnvelopeMs`, its
   server-computed LLP 0200 §2.5 within-capacity bound **excluding
   transport**, computed from the live tunables as
   `max_hold + max_eval_turn + max_eval_turn` — the second `max_eval_turn`
   being the in-flight evaluation turn that §2.5 allows a commit to wait out
   (with the served defaults of 5 s and 30 s that is 65,000 ms, the sample's
   value); the server never emits a gloss — as one integer on the public,
   handshake-exempt
   `/health` document that already carries binary identity. The live client
   starts reading it: once per connection grant lineage, cached with the
   grant and refreshed on reconnect; the receipt records the value, its
   source and the grant it belongs to. The client adds only LLP 0095's
   cadence and backoff on the poll lane. Capacity classification stays the
   server's (LLP 0200); the warning's wording above carries that caveat.

No other server change. This RFC relies on, and adds an acceptance for,
LLP 0200 §2.1's existing promise: a registered subscription the matcher skips
still observes `current_as_of ≥ V` within the service bound, on both the
stream and the poll lanes.

### Cost and hot-path posture

The receipt reads fields the handle and registry already maintain plus the
per-handle additions inventoried above (content version/kind/time, watermark
version and exact classification, last-`resubscribed` time beside the
generation, the bounded publication ring plus registered asks on the React
arm) and the fence generations the runtime handle already tracks; the detector's armed set is
bounded by the number of in-flight own settlements and cleared on coverage or
on any terminal finding. No timers per frame; the detector uses the channel
owner's single deadline scheduler (LLP 0219). Observers never add demand,
retain a handle, reopen transport, or alter polling cadence.

### The diagnostic ladder (guide)

`guide/realtime/live-queries.md` gains "Explain a live query that did not
update": step 1 is always `handle.explain()` / `useSnapbackQueryReceipt`;
the remaining steps are the truth table's rows, one repair each, worded as
conservatively as the receipt: "the server covered V without publishing
changed content". The guide never says "your query is independent of V";
even `matcher-skipped` is worded as the server's classification of the
registered read set, with the checks that follow from it. The section states the rule O15 asked for in so many words: **do not
add a refresh argument, a nonce, a `skip` cycle or a poll before reading the
receipt.** Retrieval-eval cases use the lanes' own phrases ("live query
didn't update after mutation", "hook stale but query sees the row",
"cross-client live update missing", "is fanout broken").

## Non-goals

- A server explain route or any new authenticated surface.
- Backend telemetry or fleet aggregation (LLP 0154/0156 territory); the
  receipt's pseudonyms never leave the process.
- Changing settlement, watermark, coalescing or fence semantics (LLP 0200,
  LLP 0053 own them; this receipt only reads their outcomes, and the coalescer
  is unchanged — the surviving envelope's own stamp keeps its classification,
  and no per-commit ledger is introduced).
- Diagnosing the server's matcher internals beyond its own classification.
- Production-default emission; the detector is dev-positive like every
  LLP 0219 producer.
- A visual overlay; a painted-frame or React-commit claim.

   predicates (including refusal+fence, denial+pause, detach+fence,
   resubscribe-after-ask on a live handle, an applied-but-unacknowledged fence
   entry that is not holding, active pressure past the envelope, a first
   observation already past the envelope, no-V, coalesced vs exact
   classification, content-superseded watermark) and asserts the single
   selected finding and its precedence for each.
2. **Harness parity.** Under `demos/_kit/tests/live-query-invalidation-e2e.mjs`
   the receipt alone yields: shapes (a) and (c) → `converged` with
   `contentAtAsked: true` and the content kind/version recorded (the writer is

   `converged` with `deliveryLagMs` recorded; shape (d) past the ceiling →
   `refused` naming `SNAPBACK_QUERY_IMPLICIT_LIMIT_REACHED`, bound 1,024 and
   the `.take(n)` repair; a cross-client `explainAfter({ version })` case
   using the writer's settled version. Ten consecutive fresh-server runs each.
3. **Coverage classes, separately:** a subscription whose registered read set
   is disjoint from the write (`matcher-skipped` with the disposition, row 12
   without it); an evaluated no-change (`evaluated-no-change` / row 12); six
   coalesced rowless watermarks (row 12 with `exactForAskedVersion: false` for
   the five earlier versions, and row 10 or 11 for the stamped sixth when the
   disposition ships); a **mixed** burst of skips and evaluated no-changes
   whose surviving stamp was a no-change (row 11 for the stamp, row 12 for
   every earlier version — the survivor keeps only its own class); a skip at V
   superseded by content at W > V (row 9 with
   `contentAtAsked: 'unknown'`); content at exactly V (`contentAtAsked: true`);
   on both the stream and the poll lane.
4. **Custody.** Two headless clients on the default partition →
   `shared-custody`; distinct partitions → `distinct-custody`; two handles on
   one client → `same-client`.
5. **Publication (React arm).** A healthy rowless watermark republishing
   equal rows stays `converged`; LLP 0219's churn fixture → row 8 with the
   site; an injected publication failure → row 8 with `not-published`; an
   ask for a version whose crossing the ring has evicted, with no registered
   ask → row 9 with publication `'unknown'`, never row 8; a registered
   `explainAfter` ask survives eviction.
6. **Detector.** Positive control: a stalled-but-continuously-attached drain
   with an advertised envelope raises `SNAPBACK_LIVE_QUERY_UNCONVERGED` after
   envelope + allowance with the flat receipt details, and clears on coverage.
   Negative controls, each asserting silence — no channel event of any
   severity; producer-local `coverage-pending` state may exist and is not an
   event — for: an own write the matcher skips;
   `pause()`; detach; resubscribe (new generation); offline/retained
   transition; a fenced handle; a refused handle; a manual-poll headless
   handle; automatic poll fallback within cadence and backoff; no advertised
   envelope (condition stays informational, never escalates); an active
   pressure condition, and a pressure clear that restarts the interval rather
   than warning immediately; a positively-identified production environment;
   and every harness shape. The envelope value is read once per grant lineage
   and refreshed on reconnect, asserted by a test that changes it between
   grants. Arming is covered for direct, queued/replayed and
   recovered settlement on headless, React and Expo.
7. **Redaction.** A receipt serialized from a fixture with known rows,
   arguments, principals, partitions, subscription and connection ids
   contains none of them and no row count (structural test over every field,
   including nested refusal details), and the channel event's details survive
   the depth-2 and forbidden-key filters intact.
8. **Bookkeeping invariance.** `handle.version`, `authoritativeRevision` and
   `lastDeliveryAtMs` keep their pre-receipt semantics across every harness
   shape; the inventoried fields are the only additions.
9. **Guide and O15 retrieval.** The ladder lands with CI-verified snippets and
   retrieval-eval cases; and a scored evaluation gives an agent only a receipt
   plus the guide for each of O14's four shapes and passes only if the agent
   names the responsible link and introduces none of the recorded workarounds
   (nonce, poll, `skip` cycle, reload, snapshot mutation).
10. **P6 scope.** Evidence names platform, profile and client arm; the
    browser arm is proven through
    `issues/20260829-o14-real-browser-arm-harness.md`'s runner by reading the
    receipt from the page.

## The settled position

### Three resource kinds, deliberately distinct

| Kind | Authority | States | Capabilities |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`snapback resource`** | a Snapback server, **exclusive writer of its database** | `Fresh` / `Predicted` / `Partial(site ids)` | offline, local re-execution, prediction, local authorization, OCC |
| **`remote resource`** | the foreign system (Rails, GraphQL, HTTP) | `Cached` / `Stale` / `Failed` | fetch, cache, retry, queued commands. Existence, uniqueness, membership and authorization guards execute **at that backend** |
| **`local resource`** | the device | explicit, declared limits | genuinely device-originated state — *not* a replica of mutable foreign data |

The third kind is codex's round-3 addition and closes a real gap: data that
legitimately originates on the device previously had nowhere to live that was not
a degraded replica.

Aquifer, if retained, schedules these, scopes them to routes and principals, and
binds their **unchanged** states into views. It does not store Snapback rows,
normalize the three state vocabularies, mint completeness, or run a second
optimistic layer.

### The migration path for an existing app

Start on `remote resource`. Choose a bounded domain that genuinely needs certified
offline execution. Use snapshot/CDC tooling to bootstrap and shadow-check it. Cut
**that domain's writes** over to Snapback. Then declare it receipt-backed.

The property that makes this safe, in codex's words: **"There is no moment when
the same declaration silently acquires stronger meaning."**

## What round 3 changed

### R1 — "Snapback in front of Rails" as integration is dead

Both panelists moved. Codex withdrew its own round-2 language:

> "I explicitly withdraw the breadth of my earlier 'conforming server-side adapter
> or gateway' language… A gateway that merely reads a Rails-owned database while
> Rails keeps writing it is not a weaker strong path; **it is unsound**. For the
> migrated domain, this is Snapback **adoption**, not backend integration."

Grok recorded the same movement against its own round-1 §4: "**Move — dead.**
Exclusive writer or read-only. Call it migration." It also retracted its round-1
claim that a server-in-front made the founder's direction recoverable:
"Dual-write server-in-front is silent `Fresh`."

This follows from [LLP 0329.021](./0329.021-where-the-snapback-server-sits.research.md)
P2: an external writer advances no commit version, joins no OCC, and applies no
maintained-aggregate delta, so a receipt over a shared-write database is unsound
in the silent direction.

### R2 — The client-side placement is rejected, and P5 is why

P5 established that a receipt absence means "no row exists," never "none visible
to you," and that a client holding a subset cannot make that claim. Codex:

> "Rows are only positive holdings. The facts that turn **omission into absence**,
> a prefix into a complete range, and a policy result into an authorization
> verdict all require an authority."

So an embedded Snapback over a foreign backend cannot answer any absence-dependent
query — uniqueness guards, membership checks, `exists`-shaped conditions — which
LLP 0329.008 measured as the largest single category of real mutations. Grok:
"not worth a positive-only salvage."

### R3 — CDC is a migration tool, not a runtime escape

Grok rejects it outright as a shared-write mechanism ("reintroduces two clocks…
ingest-for-cutover or watermarked replica only; a replica does not issue
receipts"). Codex calls it "a partial *read* escape, not a full P2 escape" and
puts it to work in the migration path above. Both agree it cannot make concurrent
foreign writes sound.

### R4 — The pitch shrinks, and both say so plainly

Codex's proposed form:

> "Exact 2 has a first-class local-first backend. Adopt Snapback for the domains
> that need certified offline execution, prediction, and reconciliation; use
> explicit remote resources for existing backends; **migrate domain by domain.**"

Grok names what to drop: "local-first regardless of backend," "Snapback in front
of Rails," "bridge," "embed the client," "one `useQuery`." And what it costs:
"the local-first product is Snapback-shaped apps on Snapback-owned data, and the
rest of the market is the other type. **That is correct. Treat it as scope, not as
a defect in the type split.**"

## The test to keep

Grok's, and worth quoting whenever this recurs:

> "The next time someone proposes Snapback in front of Rails, ask **who advances
> the commit version**. If the answer is 'both,' the receipts are unsound. If the
> answer is 'we will CDC the gap,' they have rebuilt the gap. If the answer is
> 'Rails will write through Snapback,' they are adopting Snapback and should say
> so."

## What remains open

1. **Possibly residual rather than live.** Grok still records "hold rejection" on
   Aquifer-as-public-tier, but codex withdrew that framing in round 2 and its
   round-3 position — "Aquifer, if retained, coordinates lifecycle and binding
   rather than data semantics" — reads as nearly grok's. They may be arguing past
   each other.
2. ~~**Genuinely live:** whether the two contracts may share a *parametric*
   container.~~ **Answered by reading the implementation —
   [LLP 0329.023](./0329.023-reading-aquifer-settling-the-container.research.md).**
   Aquifer is built; `CellDataState<Value>` is parametric with `Value: Clone` as
   its only bound, so codex's condition holds by construction and grok's objection
   is false for the payload. It is true for exactly one field, `empty: bool`, which
   is engine-derived and reaches the view — and which cannot distinguish
   "no row exists" from "no row held," the P5 distinction. The successor question
   is F4: **RFC 0498 §4.7's "local-first is a provider choice plus policy, not an
   app rewrite" contradicts this document's settled position directly**, and is a
   larger conflict than the container ever was.
3. **Uncontested but unexamined:** grok never saw codex's `local resource`
   proposal, which arrived in round 3.
4. The `remote resource` write path — "queued commands" — is named but not
   designed. Offline queueing against a backend with no OCC needs a stated
   conflict story, and nobody supplied one.

## A defect found by the panel

While source-checking P5, codex found that `assert_bounds` never validates
`receipt.absences` against `trace.absences`, although `SPEC.md` states the
invariant `receipt ⊆ trace ⊆ certificate`. Verified independently and filed as
`issues/shelved/20260829-bounded-programs-receipt-absences-unvalidated.md`. Benign today —
both fields are written on the same line at both call sites — but absences are
precisely the authority-only component, so an unvalidated one would be a forged
"does not exist."

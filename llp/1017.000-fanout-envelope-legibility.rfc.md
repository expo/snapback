## Summary

Two cold builders built the same Clique brief on the same published
`snapback2@0.0.20` on the same day and met the same wall: the brief's
celebrity envelope (u0500 follows 500 accounts, u0900 has 900 followers)
cannot be expressed as write-time fan-out, by design. One builder (codex
gpt-6-astra, medium) composed the existing paved shapes around the wall —
a read-side `merge` with a declared 500-lane bound, `maintain`
aggregates, and a resumable `chunk` job — and passed the full
167,157-action scale replay. The other (grok-4.6, xhigh) was refused
`E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT`, recorded — accurately — that a `cap` on
`followed` would refuse the celebrity population, found no statement of
where such work should live instead, called it "a platform gap, not an
authoring miss", separately generalized the diagnostic's echoed `50` and
the shipped `merge.md` exemplar into a believed 50-lane merge ceiling
that no refusal ever asserted, and did not run the scale replay.

The refusal was correct and complete as a *no*. It was incomplete as a
*where instead* — and the shipped guide reinforces the wrong inference at
both ends: `merge.md`'s only worked feed uses `first 50` and calls the
overflow "`capped`", and `bounds.md`'s escape sentence points at an LLP
number the published package cannot resolve. This RFC amends what the
refusal and the guide *teach*, not what the certifier refuses: an amended
LLP 1017 §3.3 table with numeric-provenance reason templates and a
retargeted guide page (§3.1), a shipped `fanout` topic that owns the
which-shape-for-which-need decision and replaces `merge.md`'s false
sentence (§3.2), and provenance rules for every echoed number (§3.3).

No rule, semantics, or metering changes. LLP 1017 §3.2's refusal and
§2.4's pricing stand exactly as landed, and the accurate limitation —
no synchronous write-time fan-out over an uncappable population — stays
stated plainly rather than papered over.

## 1. Field evidence: one wall, two records

The 2026-09-09 treatment rerun built the frozen social-messenger brief
from published `snapback2@0.0.20` and its installed docs only — no
source checkout, no prior lane, no steering. Lane records live under
`lanes/{grok,astra}-snapback2/` in the run directory named in the
header; the quotes below cite their files. The in-repo record copy is
pending; until it lands, open question 3 covers binding these quotes.

### 1.1 What the grok-4.6 lane recorded

The builder wanted the digest counter maintained fan-out-on-write: at
post-create, loop `follows` by `followed` and bump each follower's
`newPostsInFollowing`. The certifier refused (`E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT`,
"follows has no cap over followed") with the pasted rewrite
`cap 50 by followed` — 50 being the `first 50` the builder had written
on the loop, echoed by `fanout.rs` (`scan.take.maximum()`), exactly as
LLP 1017 §3.3 specifies ("`N` = the loop's literal bound").

Two distinct inferences follow in its records:

- **A correct one the platform never answered.** run-log: "platform gap:
  follower fan-out for digest newPostsInFollowing refused
  E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT without cap 50 by followed (celebrity 900 follows
  would refuse)." essay: write-time fan-out without that cap "cannot be
  expressed … which would then refuse the scale log. … That is a
  platform gap, not an authoring miss." diary: "Missing: no documented
  way to fan out digest `newPostsInFollowing` to an uncapped follower
  set." The builder is right that the cap is the wrong shape here; the
  refusal and the shipped docs never say what the right shape is.
- **An unsupported generalization no refusal asserted.** final report:
  "following-feed merge and any follower fan-out are capped at 50 …
  u0500's 500 follows and u0900's 900 followers cannot be projected as
  500/900 merge lanes." The structural rule is a mutation-loop check; a
  `merge` in a *query* never reaches `fanout.rs`, and the lane's feed
  (authored at `first 50` lanes, the same number as `merge.md`'s
  exemplar) was never refused at any bound. The 50-lane merge ceiling
  was pattern-matched from the diagnostic's echoed number and the guide
  exemplar, not observed.

The lane then recorded "Scale replay: Not executed" with the believed
bound as the stated reason. Its own scorer's category note reproduces
the same chain verbatim — "scale_and_limits 30: Following-feed merge and
write fan-out cap at 50; u0500's 500 follows and u0900's 900 followers
cannot be projected. Scale.log was not replayed" — so the score's link
to this belief is the scorer's own stated attribution, quoted, not this
document's arithmetic.

### 1.2 What the astra lane composed

The astra lane never fanned out on write. Its feed is merge-on-read with
an author-declared lane bound, and it certified:

```q
query followingFeed(c: cursor ?):
  page = merge feedEntries after c last 20 by byActorTime:
    [viewer]
    [f.followed] for f in follows[viewer] first 500 by byFollower
  return page entryCard
```

Its digest computes at period boundaries, not per write: a `maintain`
(`periodPosts = count posts by author, digestPeriod`) plus a resumable
`chunk` job (the LLP 1016 J1b admission, live since 2026-09-05) that
walks `follows` in bounded chunks at digest time and adds the maintained
count per follow row. Note the shapes are not interchangeable: the merge
bounds *followees per viewer* (feed projection); the digest counter over
*followers per author* — the population that must stay uncapped — is
carried by maintain-plus-job, and its freshness contract is the period
boundary, which is what the brief's digest requires. The lane's
`following500` probe passed in ~12 ms and the full 167,157-action replay
settled green with invariants.

### 1.3 Interpretation, separated from the observations

The observations above support: the refusal was correct; the paved
shapes existed in the package; the grok lane's records document a search
for them that the diagnostic and guide did not answer; and the specific
50-lane-merge belief was formed without any refusal asserting it. That
is evidence that the teaching surfaces are a *likely contributor* to the
documented misconception, and it is the strongest reading these lanes
support. It does not isolate the cause of the outcome difference: the
two lanes ran different models at different efforts (medium vs xhigh)
with different architectures, and N=1 discipline (LLP 0201) applies. The
effort split cuts one way worth recording — the higher-effort builder is
the one that did not find the door, which is consistent with an unmarked
door and inconsistent with "just think harder" — but it proves nothing
about relative model capability, and this RFC claims nothing about it.
The next treatment run is an intervention with an uncertain effect
(§4.2), not a promised score.

## 2. Root cause: three teaching failures, in load order

**(c) The paved alternatives are never named where the decision
happens.** This is the primary failure: the grok lane's records show a
builder who understood the refusal and searched for the sanctioned
shape. The three shapes that carried the astra lane — read-side `merge`,
`maintain`, `chunk` in a `job` — live in three separate guide topics,
none reachable from the refusal. `bounds.md`'s escape sentence is
"Fan-out past a cap uses LLP 1016's continuation": a corpus pointer,
meaningless in the published package. The §3.3 whole-table rewrite still
says "needs LLP 1016's continuation, not yet available" — stale since
J1b landed; the continuation a cold builder needs (`chunk` in a `job`)
shipped in the very package it was holding, invocable today
(`snapback2 run <job> --as system`, `run --file`), not only on
schedules.

**(a) The rewrite echoes the author's own number with no origin
sentence.** `fanout.rs`'s no-cap arm emits
`format!("cap {} by {}", scan.take.maximum().max(1), columns)`. A
builder who wrote `first 50` reads back `cap 50 by followed` with
nothing marking 50 as their own input. The echo is not always the loop's
literal, either: for an argument-valued bound `maximum()` is the
argument's declared max, and in the arms where a cap exists the number's
origin is the schema constraint — three different provenances, none
stated. Pasting must stay (LLP 1017 §3.3's executable-rewrite contract);
a bare pasted constant with no origin *is* a platform-shaped claim.

**(b) The shipped guide's only fan-out exemplar teaches the same number
and a false identification.** `merge.md`'s worked example is a following
feed at `first 50` lanes with the note "The recipe admits 50 follows;
more is `capped`." That sentence identifies a query-page bound with a
schema cap: extra follows are omitted from that query's lanes, never
refused at insert, and a larger `first` is the author's envelope until
the read budget refuses. A builder pattern-matching the guide reproduces
50 and then meets (a).

**What is *not* wrong.** The refusal itself. Write-time fan-out over an
uncappable population is inexpressible by design (LLP 1017 §3.2): a
schema `cap N by followed` is a data constraint (it refuses the N+1th
row in the group) and §2.4's metering prices every capped write by N, so
"just declare cap 900" is not a paved road and must not become one. That
limitation is accurate and stays stated as such (§3.2's topic says it
plainly). This RFC changes only whether the author can learn all of the
above without reading the certifier's source.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 D1

**The amended §3.3 table: templates, field assignment, and the guide join.**
This is a specification of the amended LLP 1017 §3.3 rows, not sample
prose. Three structural decisions first:

1. **Field assignment.** The parent wrapper — `` `for` over
   `<table>.<index>` at `<site>` may end before its range does:
   `<reason>` `` — is unchanged, and `<reason>` stays a short clause.
   Teaching paragraphs (the three shapes, metering, the celebrity
   anti-pattern) do **not** go into `<reason>` or `rewrite`; they live
   on the structured guide page the diagnostic already carries.
2. **The guide join.** `E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT`'s published guide page
   retargets from `bound/loop` to the new `fanout` topic; the family
   stays `bound`. Rows that change: `families.source.json` (page row),
   `diagnostics.ts` (guide map), the explicit arm in
   `protocol.rs::certification_family_guide`. `E_UNBOUND_LOOP` stays on
   `bound/loop`, whose text gains one sentence pointing loop-coverage
   fan-out questions at `guide fanout` so the two pages cannot teach two
   eras (see also §3.2's cross-links). A builder who follows either the
   reason's anchor or the structured guide field lands on the same page.
3. **Rewrite classes.** Every row is marked `executable` (pastes and
   certifies under stated preconditions) or `instructional` (a direction
   in imperative form; nothing to paste). Published diagnostic strings
   never name LLP numbers; anchors are `guide <topic>` only.

The amended rows. `{table}`, `{columns}`, `{index}`, `{n}` (the echoed
number), `{n+1}`, and `{cap}` interpolate; nothing is hardcoded:

| arm | reason template | rewrite | class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no cap, literal bound | `{table} has no cap over {columns}; {n} is this loop's bound, not a platform maximum. A cap refuses the {n+1}th row per group and prices writes by {n} (guide fanout)` | `cap {n} by {columns}` | executable |
| no cap, argument bound | same, with "{n} is the argument's declared maximum, not a platform maximum" | `cap {n} by {columns}` | executable |
| no cap, unpinned range (whole table) | `{table} has no cap over its unpinned range; a whole-table pass belongs in a job (guide job)` | `author a job with a chunk loop over {table} by {index}; run it with run <job> --as system` | instructional |
| bound below cap | `bound {n} is below cap {cap} ({cap} is {table}'s schema cap)` | `first {cap}` | executable |
| argument bound, cap exists | `the bound is an argument; {cap} is {table}'s schema cap` | `first {cap}` | executable |
| cursor / key range | `the loop reads after a cursor (or a range); a resumable pass belongs in a job (guide job)` | the pasted whole scan (as today), followed by the instructional job direction | executable + instructional tail |
| merge union | `a merge page has no union proof; loop one scan, or move the pass to a job (guide fanout)` | `loop one scan, not a merge` | instructional |
| non-uniform rule | unchanged | unchanged prefix repair | executable |
| budget-spend (non-uniform scan) | unchanged | unchanged prefix repair | executable |

Notes binding the table:

- The load-bearing correction at the refusal is the clause **"not a
  platform maximum"** with the number's stated origin; the full
  which-shape decision is one hop away on the `fanout` page. Acceptance
  (§4) does not claim the reason text alone suffices.
- An executable paste can expose the *next* refusal — pasting a cap onto
  a table whose rule is not uniform yields the prefix-repair arm on the
  following check. That sequence is intended fail-forward behavior;
  fixtures cover it (§4.1).
- Until LLP 1016 J3 lands, the alternatives stay guide-shaped by design:
  `merge`, `maintain`, and `chunk` are compositions, not one-line
  pastes, which is exactly why they are not `rewrite` material. The cap
  rewrite therefore remains the wrong action for an uncappable
  population, and the reason clause plus the `fanout` page is the
  correction path. When J3 lands, every arm gains the second pasted form
  `enqueue job { … }` exactly as parent §3.3 already specifies; this RFC
  neither preempts nor blocks that. (This closes what draft r0 left as
  an open question.)

### 3.2 D2

**The `fanout` guide topic, and replacement text where the guide currently
misteaches.** A new shipped topic `packages/snapback2/guide/fanout.md`
(registered in
`families.source.json`, budgeted in `build.mjs` per LLP 1011). Its core
is a **decision procedure keyed by need**, not a menu of mechanisms:

| the need | the shape | bounded dimension | freshness contract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| feed projection (what the viewer follows) | read-side `merge`, lanes over the viewer's follows | followees per viewer — the lane bound is the author's declared envelope, certified against the read budget | live, per query |
| per-group counter over a population you can cap (group members) | `cap` on the table + the covering mutation loop | the schema cap; writes priced by it (LLP 1017 §2.4) | per write |
| per-group counter over a population you must not cap (followers, likers) | `maintain` aggregate + a `chunk` job that folds it in at period boundaries | the chunk size per step; the job is resumable | the period boundary, not per write |
| whole-table pass, backfill, erasure | `chunk` in a `job`; runnable on demand (`run <job> --as system`, `run --file`) as well as on schedules | the chunk size per step | when the job completes |

The topic states the accurate limitation in its own voice: **synchronous
per-write fan-out over an uncappable population has no shape today**; a
product that needs it must wait for LLP 1016 D8/J3 or redesign around
one of the rows above. The celebrity paragraph works the digest example
end to end (maintain + chunk job — the astra lane's composition,
generalized) and says why `cap 900 by followed` is the wrong repair even
though it is the pasteable one. The worked feed example must itself hold
the topic to §4's standard: either a complete schema-plus-projection
whose 500-lane merge is demonstrated to certify, or the same example
carried to the read-budget refusal with `check`'s real numbers shown.
The 0.0.4 enriched-lane observation (~360 of 500 lanes certifying for a
heavier shape) is absorbed here as the worked illustration that the
budget, not a lane constant, is the binding surface.

Replacement text, specified as replacements — not additions beside the
text they contradict:

- **`merge.md`:** delete "The recipe admits 50 follows; more is
  `capped`." Replace with: "The lane bound is the author's declared
  envelope; 50 here is a choice, not a limit. Follows beyond the bound
  are omitted from this query's lanes — nothing is refused at insert. A
  larger `first` certifies until the read budget refuses; see guide
  fanout." The exemplar keeps a small bound only if that sentence sits
  directly on it.
- **`bounds.md`:** replace "Fan-out past a cap uses LLP 1016's
  continuation" with "Fan-out past a cap belongs in a job's `chunk`
  loop, or read-side; see guide fanout." `bounds.md` keeps ownership of
  cap/metering/loop-coverage mechanics; `fanout.md` owns the choice —
  cross-reference, don't absorb (resolving draft r0's open question 2).
- **`job.md`, `maintain.md`:** one cross-link each to `guide fanout`
  from their bulk-work and aggregate paragraphs.

### 3.3 D3

**Numeric provenance is a property, not a wording.**

Recorded separately because it amends parent §3.3's normative table
notes: **every number a diagnostic echoes states its origin.** The three
origins in this family — the loop's literal bound, an argument's
declared maximum, a schema cap — each carry their naming clause in the
§3.1 table, and ordinals derive from the interpolated value ({n+1}th,
never a hardcoded "51st"). The certifier-computed ceiling ("up to N
certifies here") that draft r0 floated is **withdrawn from this RFC**:
both reviews judged it new analysis surface with its own echo risk, and
one field run showed a missing shape, not a missing ceiling. If wanted
later it is its own proposal with its own validation.

### 3.4 Adjacent findings, routed not designed

- **`E_STEPS` on a membership-scoped fill job** (grok lane, M7): the
  runtime step refusal names the exceeded certificate but not the
  chunked-job pattern that avoids it; it should carry the `guide job`
  anchor. Follow-up ticket; jobs/diagnostics surface.
- **`E_OPERATOR_REQUIRED` forcing a duplicate client-callable mutation**
  for schedule targets (the lane's `dropTrending`/`hideTrending` pair):
  separate papercut, separate ticket.
- **Reason-length precondition:** before D1 lands, verify no published
  surface truncates diagnostic reason text (a 0.0.4 lane reported
  120-character truncation of test miss lines); if one does, the reason
  templates above are re-cut to fit, teaching stays on the guide page,
  and the truncating surface gets its own ticket. Open question 1 tracks
  the audit.

## 4. Acceptance

### 4.1 Ship gates (mechanical; block landing)

- Diagnostic fixtures assert the §3.1 reason templates with correct
  numeric provenance across bounds `n ∈ {1, 50, 500}` and all three
  origins (literal, argument max, schema cap); ordinals interpolate.
- Every `executable` rewrite pastes and certifies on a single-error
  fixture; the cap-then-uniformity two-step fixture yields the prefix
  arm on the second check, not a certify. `instructional` arms are
  marked as such in the table and asserted as non-paste text — the
  parent's "every rewrite pastes" claim is narrowed to executable arms.
- No published diagnostic string contains an LLP number.
- `guide fanout` resolves from the published package;
  `families.source.json`, `diagnostics.ts`, and `protocol.rs` agree the
  page is `fanout`; guide checks and LLP 1011 byte budgets pass.
- `merge.md` no longer contains "more is `capped`"; the replacement
  sentences of §3.2 are present verbatim or improved-in-place; the
  `fanout` topic contains either the certified 500-lane worked example
  (schema and projection included) or the real-numbers refusal
  transcript.

### 4.2 Validation (an experiment, not a gate)

The next cold Clique treatment run is read for four separately recorded
facts: the builder's stated interpretation of any `E_STRUCTURAL_FANOUT`
refusal, the architecture it chose for feed and digest, whether it
attempted the scale replay, and the result. A repeat of the r0 diary
sentence — "no documented way to fan out … to an uncapped follower set"
— is the anti-goal, now precise: the way *is* documented, and the
accurate limitation (no synchronous write fan-out) is stated rather
than discovered. One run remains N=1 (LLP 0201); no score movement is
predicted or claimed.

## 5. Non-claims

- No change to the fan-out rule, cap semantics, metering, successor
  admission, or any certificate budget.
- No claim that the three shapes are interchangeable, or that any one of
  them covers any refused fan-out: the mapping is §3.2's table, and the
  synchronous-uncapped case is covered by none of them today.
- No claim that `first 500` certifies for every lane shape; the bound is
  subject to the read budget and the topic demonstrates both sides.
- Does not implement LLP 1016 J3 (`enqueue job`) or alter its
  sequencing; does not implement the withdrawn computed ceiling.
- Does not claim the r0 wording alone caused the grok-4.6 outcome, nor
  that this revision would have changed it; §1.3's reading is
  "likely contributor", and §4.2 is an experiment.

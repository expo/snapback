## 0. Summary

A list such as

```quarry
query myTasks(c: cursor ?):
  return tasks where .owner = viewer or .assignee = viewer last 50 by .at after c
```

over `read <- .owner = viewer or .assignee = viewer` was never `complete`.
The engine walked one index per page; neither `[owner, at, id]` nor
`[assignee, at, id]` holds every row the caller may read, so the page was
planned on `[at, id]`, checked the rule per row, and could not tell "no more"
from "more you may not read". `check` printed `N_NEVER_COMPLETE` with a
verified per-party rewrite (LLP 3000.036 §12.2, §13.2, §16.1). Across six farm
waves 12, 19 and 16 lanes reported the shape.

Now the one-query form is complete. When a query page's predicate, or its
table's read rule, is a disjunction of branches each of which pins an index
prefix that decides the read rule, the planner reads it as a k-way merge of
the branch ranges in the page's order. A row matching several branches is
emitted once. Each branch's rows are decided by its own pins, so the merged
page is `complete` exactly when every branch's walk finishes. The cursor is
one position in the shared order, which every branch resumes strictly after
(inclusively, for an unfinished first returned shape); its token is a new
version naming the branch set. A meter cut continues from the frontier every
branch has examined. Devices run the same plan with the existing per-scan
coverage rules. The Lean model's C8 gains the rule, with a coverage proof.

Owner-or-staff (`.owner = viewer or exists staff[viewer]`) is the same plan
with one branch guarded by the readable grant of §12.1: held, that branch is
the whole page; not held, it contributes nothing and the owner branch is the
page. A staff page is complete for staff (as before) and an owner's page is
complete for everyone else (new). The guide's owner-or-staff and two-party
recipes are now single queries.

## 1. What the builders met

LLP 3000.036 records the shape in waves 2–6: `#12` (12 lanes), `#3b`
(19 lanes) and the §14.2 and §16.1 notes (16 lanes). The lanes either pasted
the per-party rewrite (two queries, two cursors, a client-side merge and
de-duplication, two loading states) or kept the one-query form and shipped a
list that always said "there may be more". The engine already merges ordered
lanes (LLP 3000 §7, 2026-09-13) and already has the position that makes a
merge resumable (LLP 3000.007.000 §1); this RFC lets one merge read several
indexes of one table.

## 2. The rule

Definitions, over the checked schema:

- An **equality pin** in a conjunction is a conjunct `.col = e` (either
  side) or a bare boolean column (`.flag`, `not .flag`), where `e` reads no
  row column, `.col` is a plain column of the table and not `id`. On a
  nullable column it counts only when `e` is `viewer`: a branch never seeks a
  null the original filter would reject.
- A **party disjunct** of a read rule is `.col = viewer` (either side) on a
  principal column of the row.
- A **guard disjunct** reads no column of the row (§12.1: a role probe, a
  seat such as `viewer = 'service:audit'`), or reads only columns the page's
  own pins fix (§16.3: `exists boards[.board, owner = viewer]` under `.board =
  b`). It is **usable** when `rules::page_guard_decision` takes it: no `not`,
  and every row it probes readable by whoever its key names.
- A prefix **decides** the read rule when every column the rule reads is
  pinned (with the self-witness proof of LLP 3000.007 §16), or the rule is
  true by the pins' form: `allow`, a `.col = viewer` disjunct pinned to
  `viewer`, a `.col = 'x'` disjunct pinned to `'x'`, and `and`/`or` of
  those. This is the core's `rules::rule_true`/`scan_visibility`, the
  binder's `intent_branches::decided` and the model's `decidedPins`.
- A predicate is **total** when it is comparisons (`=`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`,
  `>`, `>=`, `in`) and boolean operators over the row's own columns,
  arguments, literals and `viewer`: its evaluation never refuses and never
  reads. A member of anything but the row (`.meta.ok`, `m.ok`) is not: it
  binds to a read of the referenced row. CUT (2026-10-12) fixes interrupted
  single-index predicates by retrying their row inclusively (LLP 3000.007
  §20.24.1), but does not make such a predicate total: missing/hidden targets
  can still refuse or be unavailable, and distribution changes which reads
  short-circuit. Keep this exclusion and the merge's decided lane frontier.
  Arithmetic is not either,
  not even a time window (`now - 7d` overflows when the clock is at the
  integer bound), so such a page keeps one index. The Lean model has no `in`;
  the emitter names it unsupported.

A **query page** (`t where … first|last N by … [after c]` in a query program;
never in a mutation, job, shared helper or shape, which keep ordinary scans)
is **branch-decided** when all of these hold:

- it names no explicit index or key, does not search (`matching`), has no
  computed order, and reads an ordinary table (not a maintained view, row
  view, ephemeral or timeline table, a `public` declaration or a `read
  through` child), whose read rule is one `read` declaration;
- its own pins do **not** decide the rule (a decided page keeps its plan,
  index and cursor);
- its predicate is total;
- and either
  1. **branches in `where`:** exactly one top-level conjunct is an `or` of
     2–8 alternatives, each holding an equality pin, and each alternative
     with the other conjuncts decides the rule; or
  2. **branches in the read rule:** the rule is an `or` of at least two
     disjuncts, each a party or a usable guard, with at least one party.
     The branches are `base_j and .party_k = viewer` for every base branch
     (case 1's alternatives when `where` has such an `or`, else the whole
     `where`) and party, plus one **grant branch**, the whole `where`, read
     for a caller the rule's guards admit and for the system, which every
     rule admits (a rule of parties alone has no guard: the lane is the
     system's only);
- and every branch plans to an index of at most 8 components whose position
  after its pins is the page's order then `id` (a branch never pins an order
  column: a merge lane keeps such an equality as a seek), with at most 8
  branches.

Otherwise the page keeps its single-index plan and, when undecided,
`N_NEVER_COMPLETE` with its per-party rewrite.

A single party (`read <- .owner = viewer`) is not expanded: its complete form
is the pin the author writes, and expanding it would replan every unpinned
owner page (§12 Q1). 8 is both the index width limit and the point below
which the lazy merge reads a page per lane (`word_merge.rs`); every branch
may be a synthesized index.

### 2.1 Why the merged rows are exactly the readable matches

Write `W` for the predicate and `R` for the read rule; the page returns, in
order, the rows `r` with `W(r) ∧ R(r)`.

- Case 1: `W = rest ∧ (a_1 ∨ … ∨ a_k)`, so `W = ∨_j (a_j ∧ rest)`. Branch
  `j` reads the range its pins select and filters by the rest of `a_j ∧
  rest`; its prefix decides `R`, so every row of the branch is readable
  (verdict true) or none is (verdict false: the branch is empty). The union
  is `∨_j (a_j ∧ rest ∧ R) = W ∧ R`.
- Case 2: `R = ∨_k (.p_k = viewer) ∨ G` with `G` row-independent or reading
  only the page's pinned columns, so for every row the page selects and this
  caller `R(r) = ∨_k (r.p_k = viewer) ∨ g` with `g` one boolean (the guard
  with the pinned values), and
  `W ∧ R = ∨_{j,k} (b_j ∧ .p_k = viewer) ∨ (g ∧ W)`. Each party branch is
  decided true by its pin; the grant branch is all of `W` when `g` holds and
  nothing otherwise, and then it is a superset of every other branch, so the
  plan reads it alone. The Lean model proves the party half
  (`parties_cover_view`): every visible row carries the viewer in a party
  column.

The argument distributes `W` over its alternatives, so it needs `W` total:
with `or (.assignee = viewer and 1 / n = 1)`, a row in both parties skips the
division in the original and would evaluate it in its own branch (GPT
review, finding 1). A branch evaluates the predicate only on rows the
original also reads, so a total predicate gives the same rows.

A row in several branches has the same position in each (§5), so the merge
emits it once. No branch reads a row the caller may not read, so no such row
reaches the merge, the filter, the lookahead or the cursor.

## 3. The plan

**Planning** (`bind/intent_branches.rs`, called from `intent::plan` where the
other lane expansions run). Each branch is an ordinary planned read
(`Requests::plan_lane`): its pins form the prefix (a party pin first, so the
deciding pin is never left residual), the page's order then `id` the suffix;
the planner reuses a declared index with exactly those columns or synthesizes
one. The expansion is dropped, and the read planned as written, when a branch
does not decide the rule (by the binder's check), the branches' order columns
differ, an index would exceed 8 components, or a branch exceeds the portable 2,048-byte key or 512-byte label bound,
or planning a branch raised a diagnostic: a program that compiled before still compiles. The result is the
existing `MergeExpr` with planned lanes, each carrying the branch identity
`$or` (or `$or:grant`) in the field views use for theirs; the planned-merge
binding passes it through to the IR `MergeLane.branch`. No IR type gains a
field, so every existing program's IR and pinned digest is unchanged;
`Merge::branches()` recognises the plan.

**Binding check** (`program/views.rs`). With the core schema in hand, the
binder re-checks the plan with the runtime's own functions: every grant
lane's `rules::covering_grant` (every disjunct that is not a party is a
usable guard of `page_guard_decision` for the lane's pins, §12.1, §16.3) and `rules::branch_merge_lazy` (same table and order, scalar
pins, every non-grant lane `scan_visibility(..).uniform`, a total position,
identical position columns). `covering_grant` compares each non-party
disjunct after the same prefix simplification that produced the page guard;
`allow`, `deny`, `not` and required boolean pins therefore have one identity
through the coverage proof, while a nullable boolean's `null` is never
normalized to `false`. If it fails and the plan has a grant lane, the
read binds as its grant lane alone, which is the read as written: the grant
lane is planned exactly as the single-index page is (not as a merge lane), so
the fallback is 0.4.23's plan, page guard included; when that plan's order
differs from the party lanes' (an equality on the first order column becomes
a pin there), the page is not merged at all (a hidden
roster, `read <- deny` on `staff`, keeps today's plan and note); without a
grant lane the merge stays, sound and, for a lane that is not decided,
incomplete.

**Execution** (`engine/branch_merge.rs`). A branch merge that passes
`branch_merge_lazy` is a lazy merge whose lanes may read different indexes of
one table. Each lane's position is its index components after its prefix,
plus `id` when they do not end in it (LLP 3000.007.000 §1); the binding check
makes the positions comparable, and because they end in the row id, equal
positions are the same row. Grant lanes open first. A grant lane's guard is
decided once with the lane's pinned values (`grant_verdict`, charged as §12.1) and, when it holds, the lane
reads as a decided page (`scan_consumed`'s grant arm, through the runner's
`merge_grant`) and the other lanes are not opened; when it does not hold, the
lane is not read and does not mark the page non-uniform; for the system it
reads every row. Each lane opens with one read of `take + 1` rows; the heap
pops in position order, de-duplicates by id and refills a lane strictly after
its last popped position. A branch merge that is not lazy-eligible takes the
eager path with the same branch descriptor; it is sound and, with a lane
that is not decided, incomplete with no `next`.

**Page guards (LLP 3000.036 §16.3).** Without the merge, 0.4.23 decides a
single-index page once for a caller a pinned guard admits, and walks it per
row (incomplete) for everyone else. With it, a page whose rule is parties and
such guards merges the guard branch with the party branches, so the guard's
holder reads the guard branch alone and everyone else their party branches,
all complete. A page the merge does not cover keeps the single-index guard.

**Older engines.** Older devices refuse a merged backend by its capability
(§5). Were one to run the plan anyway, an engine that does not know the lane
identities (0.4.22) ignores them, so a backend compiled by this release still runs on an older
device: lanes over different indexes take the eager merge, which translates
one cursor across lanes with the same suffix (LLP 3000.007 §20.3) and
de-duplicates by id. It returns the same rows, mints its own cursor form, and
evaluates a grant lane as an ordinary page. For a plan without guards the
pages continue as here. A plan with a guard is only ever run by an older
*device* (servers compile their own IR), and a guard's table never syncs
except for a seat, which the device trusts: a role or parent-probe guard's
table is online only, so the older device's answer is unknown and the client
asks the server, never paging the device's incomplete answer (GPT round 2,
finding 2). `an_older_engine_reads_the_plan_as_an_eager_merge`
runs the plan with the identities stripped and compares every page.

## 4. Completeness and disclosure

- **Exact.** The page is `complete` when no lane is non-uniform, the meter
  did not cut, and the existing query-level conditions hold (position loss,
  device coverage, horizons). By §2.1 its rows are then exactly the first N
  readable matches after the cursor.
- **Nothing outside the rule.** Every lane is admitted by its own prefix
  verdict (`prefix_verdict`) or its grant before any row is read; a false
  verdict reads nothing. This is the admission a direct page on that prefix
  takes.
- **What `complete: true` and the meter reveal.** For a party or `where`
  branch, what a direct page on that prefix reveals: a verdict decided by
  facts the caller could read. For a grant branch, whether the grant holds,
  which §12.1 already reveals through a staff page's `complete` and which
  `grant_decision` restricts to grants whose every fact the caller can read.
  The number of lanes opened (one when a grant holds) carries the same bit.
  A grant charges the rule's static probe count on either verdict.
- **The token.** The version-4 descriptor hashes the branch set's index
  names and grant flags: schema facts. It does not encode row data.
- **Across pages.** The position is shared by every plan of the page, so a
  grant gained or lost between pages continues correctly from the same
  cursor; completeness is per page, as for every page.

## 5. The cursor

The continuation is one position: the shared position values (the order
columns, then `id`) of a row. Every lane resumes strictly after it on its own
index. One position suffices because it ends in the row id, so it totally
orders the union: the first N rows after `p` in the union are the first N
after `p` merged from each branch's rows after `p`, including a row several
branches hold.

The token is **version 4**, or **version 5** when inclusive:

```
[4 | 5, table, "$or:<branch-set SHA-256>", order, suffix, values, scope]
```

the layout of version 3 (an inclusive direct token), so the device decodes it
with the same code (sharing it saved 646 B of the web device). The index
field hashes the branch set in lane order: a domain marker, lane count, and
each index name with its byte length and grant flag, all unambiguously framed;
`scope` carries the argument-presence variant as other tokens do; the suffix
is the shared typed position. Encoding picks 4 or 5 exactly for a branch
descriptor, and decoding re-encodes and compares, so a version-4 or -5 token
without one, or a branch descriptor in an older version, is refused. The descriptor is bounded even when eight lanes reuse a legal 507-byte
index name: it is `$or:` followed by a SHA-256 digest, never the labels themselves.

- **Inclusive positions.** An unfinished first returned shape records its
  row's position inclusively (`shaping.rs`), on merges too (GPT review,
  finding 2). Version 5 carries it; every lane then seeks at or after
  the position, the row is re-read (and de-duplicated), and the existing
  no-progress check (`E_SHAPE_EXCEEDS_METER`) compares the canonical tokens.
- **Another plan's cursor restarts the read.** A token minted by this read's
  earlier one-index plan, or for another branch set, is refused with
  `E_CURSOR_INDEX`: "cursor belongs to another plan of this read; it now
  merges its branches (owner or another party): restart this read with a null
  cursor". This is new in the merge path; ordinary pages still refuse a
  foreign descriptor as `E_INPUT` (Muse review, finding 2).
- **The token is scoped to every lane's pins.** Its scope carries a SHA-256
  digest of the evaluated prefixes of all lanes, in lane order (their
  canonical encoding), and the generation, as a direct page's carries its
  prefix. The digest, not the prefixes: lanes that each fit the index key
  bound can together exceed the token's 4,096 bytes (three 1,600-byte folder
  pins; GPT round 4, finding 3). A cursor followed with other arguments,
  or by another viewer (a party pin is the viewer), refuses `cursor: …
  different prefix or generation` (`E_INPUT`) instead of resuming lanes over
  ranges it never saw (Muse round 3, finding 1).
- **Older engines refuse the token, and the plan.** A 0.4.22 engine or device
  decodes versions 4 and 5 as "cursor token version 4 is unsupported"
  (`E_INPUT`). A backend with a merged plan requires the schema capability
  `branch-merge-v1` (from the operation fact `branchMerge`, set when a
  merge sits anywhere in the program's IR, including a returned row
  shape's field; GPT round 4, finding 2), so an older
  device refuses the whole backend by name ("upgrade the engine/device")
  rather than running the plan or decoding a server's cursor (GPT round 3,
  finding 2).

## 6. Metering and ceilings

The meter charges what the plan does, in the existing units:

- each lane's opening is an ordinary scan: its prefix verdict (the rule's
  static probe count, once), one `examined` per candidate read (a batch of
  `take + 1` per lane), index entries as the store reports;
- one `step` when a lane's first head enters the heap and one per pop,
  including duplicate pops; a refill's push is not charged (as in every lazy
  merge; Muse review, finding 7);
- a grant charges the read rule's static probe count once, on either
  verdict.

With at most 8 lanes and a `take + 1` batch per lane, a lane never runs out
before the page finds its `(N+1)`th distinct row, so a cut can only happen
while the lanes open (GPT review, finding 4). The merge then keeps every row
it can prove is next: each lane records how far it examined (`through`: its
last returned row, or, when the cut fell inside its read, the last
rule-visible candidate it examined, which may be a filter reject;
`scan_consumed` reports it through the runner's `scan_frontier`, armed with
the lane's own table and index so a nested read's key never stands in). The
**frontier** is the least such position over the lanes still open. The page
emits only rows at or before the frontier, so it is a true prefix of the
merged order, and its `next` is the frontier: every lane has examined that
far, so resuming strictly after it loses nothing. When a lane examined nothing
past the cursor (it was never reached), the frontier is the cursor itself:
the page is empty, incomplete, without `next` (a cut while opening, as LLP
3000.007.000 §2). A page that fills before the frontier continues from its
last row as usual. Only the read meters (`examined`, `index_entries`,
`rule_probes`) cut; `steps` and `heap_bytes` abort the read, as everywhere.

## 7. Devices

The device runs the same interpreter over both stores, so the plan is the
same. Each lane applies the existing per-scan coverage rules: a lane over a
held partition is trusted (`trust_synced_read`) and complete when its group is
acquired and its horizon allows; a lane over an online-only table, an
unacquired group or beyond an acquired horizon marks the read unknown, and
the client asks the server (LLP 3000.028). A grant lane whose grant probes a
table is never decided on a device (§12.1): the read is unknown. A **seat**
(`viewer = 'service:audit'`) reads no table and is decided on a device too
(GPT review, finding 7): `read <- .owner = viewer or viewer = 'service:audit'`
syncs to `.owner` (service disjuncts leave the device audience), so the
owner's page answers from the held partition, complete, on both stores.

UNION integration (LLP 3000.036.000.000) admits synced party/group unions.
Those source tables retain ordinary per-arm scan planning: a grant lane that
probes membership would defer on a device despite the union's acquired
coverage. `intent_branches::branches` excludes sources whose sync needs contain
multiple arms. Online sources, held-group where-merges and service-seat plans
retain this RFC's merged-page behavior. The UNION simulator's snapshot-limit,
epoch and exhaustive owner/group worlds qualify the integration; the branch
merge suite continues to qualify its own surfaces.

The device oracle (`run-devices`) originally did not draw group-synced tables:
the first run that did found that a held group's pinned page charges one rule
probe on the server and none on a device, merged or not (the plain pin shows
it alone). That is a meter-parity gap older than this change. CUT (2026-10-12) repairs it:
delivered uniform pages reserve the server's logical prefix verdict
(LLP 3000.007 §20.24.6); the generator now draws group-synced tables.
The resolution is in
`issues/closed/20261012-snapback4-device-group-page-rule-probe-meter.md`.

## 9. The Lean model

OPEN C8 said "a page is uniform exactly when every row column its read rule
reads is pinned; a non-uniform page is never complete". With 0.4.23 (LN) it
says *decided*, per caller: every column pinned, or a page guard that holds
over the caller's view (`decided`, `granted`). This change adds two syntactic
verdicts (`Eval.lean`): `decidedPins` (`Rule.byForm`, the pins' form, so an
argument that merely equals the viewer decides nothing, as in the engine) and
`branchDecided` (§2's cases 1 and 2 over the model's surface syntax:
totality, eligible pins, first pin per column, no order or `id` pins, the
width bound, 2–8 alternatives, at most 8 branches; queries only, never in a
helper's body; case 2 takes usable guards, `Rule.usableGuard`, which mirrors

## 14. PAGE: capped owner-or-membership pages

PAGE resolves Q6 for the common rule

```quarry
read <- .owner = viewer or exists members[.parent, viewer]
```

when the membership lookup names an exact unique key and the membership table
declares a finite cap whose grouping columns are fixed by the viewer or other
row-independent expressions. More formally, the read rule's top-level
disjunction has at least one principal-party equality and at least one such
membership probe, and every disjunct is one of those two forms. Every
qualifying probe has one row-dependent exact-key component naming an ordinary
target column (not `id`) that the page has not already pinned, and otherwise
fixed components; its cap group is wholly among the fixed components and its
read rule is decided true by them. The §2–§3 exclusions still apply: total
page predicates, shared order and total cursor suffix, portable keys and
labels, at most eight physical components in both source and target indexes,
and fallback without speculative indexes. The planner first lowers
bare booleans and negated booleans to equality pins, as it does for every
other intent plan. It emits the party lanes, one descriptor per membership
probe, and one system lane. Their static descriptor count remains at most
eight; each declared membership cap bounds its descriptor's dynamic fanout
separately and may itself be greater than eight.

The dynamic descriptor scans the membership range to completion before the
page may emit a row. Each distinct parent in that result opens a target range
pinned to that parent. The membership row is the witness for the rule branch,
so target rows in that range need no second membership probe; their ordinary
target coverage, horizon and expiry checks still apply. The owner and parent
ranges merge in the requested order and `id`, deduplicate overlaps, and share
one version-4/5 position. Source discovery and every opened target scan use
the query's one store transaction/snapshot, so a membership cannot be revoked
between proving a lane and reading it. The system lane reads the whole
original range and suppresses every other lane for a system context.

The cursor descriptor includes the static lanes and the membership source's
table and index. Its scope includes the evaluated static pins and the source
range's fixed viewer/argument pins. It deliberately does not digest the set of
parents returned by that range: membership rows, like target rows, may change
between requests. This is ordinary non-snapshot pagination. The next request
answers completeness for its current remaining range strictly after the
shared position; it makes no claim about rows at or before that position. A
new membership therefore contributes only currently readable rows after the
cursor, and a removed membership contributes no further rows. Schema or
fixed-principal changes still restart because they change the descriptor or
scope.

The backend carries `branch-membership-v1` in addition to `branch-merge-v1`.
An older device understands the static branch cursor but not the mandatory
discovery-before-output step, so it refuses this backend instead of attempting
the eager merge. Handshake negotiation uses the ordinary schema-capability
path.

Membership discovery is an all-or-nothing admission step. Source rows,
distinct-parent retention and every dynamic lane descriptor charge the
ordinary examined, step and heap meters. A meter cut, cap
latch, unknown presence, incomplete source coverage or source horizon before
the source range is known complete returns an empty incomplete page and no
continuation. Minting either a normal continuation or a skip continuation at
that point could omit an undiscovered parent whose first row sorts before an
already emitted owner row. Once discovery finishes, ordinary per-range meter
cuts use the §6 frontier and may return a safe continuation.

The heap ledger applies to the whole membership merge, including owner and
system lane buffers as well as dynamic target buffers. It reserves source
distinct sets, evaluated prefixes and bounds, merge-position and heap slots,
deduplication, the emitted-page vector, frontier decode, and cursor/page-record
scratch before allocation. A source distinct key is first sized by the
allocation-free canonical JSON writer, then charged and encoded into one
bounded buffer of exactly that requested capacity; escaped parent text cannot
cause geometric growth after admission. Compiled scans remain shared; dynamic
descriptors borrow one compiled target scan rather than cloning its filter tree
per parent.

On a device the membership source and every opened target range retain their
normal per-scan coverage decisions. PAGE is an explicit exception to §7's
blanket synced-union exclusion for this proved source shape: it has a
system-only lane rather than a probe-grant lane, so acquired membership and
target union groups can use the same plan and produce the same complete page
as the server. An online or unacquired source makes discovery unknown and
returns the empty incomplete answer above. An unheld or horizon-incomplete
target range makes the result incomplete and triggers the ordinary server
fallback; no partial device result is marked complete. The plan does not
treat a merely uniform denial as an empty membership set: binding validates
that the source pins decide its read rule true. Expiry from membership
discovery joins target expiry at the existing minimum.

The planner and Lean classifier mirror point-probe resolution before accepting
the branch. A positional probe selects the one declared unique whose ordered
column types accept its arguments; equal-arity keys with different types do not
make it ambiguous, while multiple matching keys still refuse the positional
spelling. Named probe columns are duplicate-free and contain every column of a
duplicate-free declared unique, additional columns are fixed equalities, and a
cap covers the fixed discovery prefix. The descriptor bound counts each party
column once, matching runtime party-lane deduplication while retaining every
distinct membership branch. Its soundness lemma says every emitted
party or parent lane row is admitted under the branch-witness assumption; the
logical theorem does not prove that runtime discovery establishes that
witness. Its coverage lemma splits every admitted row into a party witness or
membership witness. Differential cases include
caps above eight and overlap. Runtime meter regressions cover discovery cuts;
simulator regressions include revocation between pages and missing acquisition;
server and both device stores exercise the same shared-cursor walk.

The implicit `byId` also proves an exact named probe containing `id`; any
additional named components remain required residual equalities. This does not
invent a multi-part positional key: positional probes still resolve through
their typed declared unique, apart from the ordinary single-ID spelling.

A fixed named component may remain an exact equality in the discovery scan's
residual filter rather than its capped prefix, as in
`members[id = 'm1', parent = .parent, principal = viewer]`. PAGE accepts that
component only when the equality is a necessary conjunct over the source row;
an alternative or computed implication is not witness evidence. The retained
filter therefore restricts discovery to `m1`, and a row with the same parent
and principal under another id cannot open a target lane. The planner checks
that every fixed probe component survives as a prefix pin or such a residual
before committing candidate indexes, and the bound-IR validator repeats the
same check without copying the filter. A denial, missing witness, expired
witness, incomplete source horizon or unsupported residual cannot expose an
unproved target lane. Uniform denial and unsupported residuals keep the
ordinary fallback without speculative PAGE indexes. A missing or expired
witness opens no lane under complete discovery, so the owner merge remains
complete; an incomplete source horizon returns the empty incomplete result
described above.

The intrinsic source `id` is always such a residual, even when fixed. It stays
in the exact logical witness and its equality must hold, but it is not a
discovery-prefix component and cannot satisfy the discovery cap. Thus `cap by
id` alone keeps the ordinary incomplete scan; a separate cap over fixed
discovery columns such as `principal` is required for PAGE.

An optional discovered parent may be `null` only when the exact point probe
identifies the source row without that parent component. For example,
`members[id = 'm1', parent = .parent, principal = viewer]` addresses `m1`
through `byId` and checks `parent = null` as a residual equality, so a matching
source row opens one null-parent target range. Discovery uses its ordinary
null-covering physical index, including the stored row-id suffix, and applies
the fixed residual before distinct-parent deduplication. If the varying parent
is instead a component of the probe's nullable unique key, a null argument has
the ordinary point result of no row and cannot open a PAGE lane. The same is
true when any other component of that point key is null. This rule neither
turns nullable uniques into total uniques nor changes their storage or
collision semantics.

A varying source field may be the discovered parent even when it is not the
next physical index component, provided the fixed discovery prefix covers a
plain total unique index. Such a prefix selects at most one source row, so PAGE
may project any field from that row: the implicit `id` in
`groups[id = .parent, principal = viewer]`, or an ordinary `parent` in
`groups[parent = .parent, principal = viewer]` under `unique principal`.
The total unique may be a proper subset of the probe's fixed source columns:
`unique principal` also admits
`groups[parent = .parent, slot = 0, principal = viewer]`. PAGE keeps `slot =
0` as an exact residual on the selected source row; a missing row, `slot =
null`, or any other slot does not open a target range. The provisional wider
discovery index is discarded before requests are committed.
The projected field may be optional under the null-witness rule above. The
source cap, readability, complete-discovery, resource and horizon checks still
apply. Every component of the one-row unique itself must be non-optional; a
nullable unique does not supply this proof and declines before PAGE indexes
are committed. In all other cases the parent remains the next physical
source-index component.

The Lean surface gate prices the same physical PAGE candidate before invoking
the logical oracle. Equality-prefix columns use the planner's canonical sorted
order followed by the exact scan-order suffix; the permutation allowed for an
extremum is not available to a PAGE discovery scan. A total declared unique in
that exact order may omit the trailing physical `id`, because its components
already identify the row. If two declared indexes both match the candidate and
the author selected neither, native optional planning declines the ambiguous
PAGE candidate and the emitter reports `page-membership-index-selection`
instead of asking an index-free logical model to choose one. Component, byte or
label overflow reports `page-membership-key-bound`; a reused nullable unique
that cannot prove the varying-`id` one-row case reports
`page-membership-source-unique`. Each stops before the index-free model.
